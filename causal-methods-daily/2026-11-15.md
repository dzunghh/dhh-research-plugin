## 1. Assessing algorithm fairness requires adjustment for risk distribution differences: reconsidering the equal opportunity criterion — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Biostatistics
- **Pub date**: 2026-01-20
- **Authors**: Hegarty SE et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Show that equal-opportunity (TPR parity) fairness is confounded by subgroup risk distributions and propose a risk-adjusted alternative.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Theoretical development, simulation studies, and EHR cohort application evaluating a 6-month mortality prediction model for palliative care referral.
  - **Estimand / target**: Adjusted true positive rate (aTPR) per subgroup and aTPR difference versus a reference subgroup.
  - **Identification**: Standardizes subgroup TPRs to the reference subgroup's true-risk distribution, so comparisons reflect equal treatment for equal risk.
  - **Estimator / model**: Risk-distribution-normalized TPR (reweighting by reference risk distribution); exact estimator details not reported in abstract.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulation scenarios with perfectly calibrated within-group models; real EHR cohort demonstration.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Even perfectly calibrated models show unequal TPRs at any threshold when risk distributions differ; aTPR difference removes this artefactual inequity.
- **Strengths**: Clear, formally motivated correction to a widely used fairness metric with direct clinical-AI relevance.
- **Limitations**: Requires estimating true risk distributions; choice of reference subgroup may influence conclusions.
- **Takeaway for practice**: When auditing clinical prediction models for fairness, standardize TPRs to a common risk distribution before declaring subgroup inequity.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/biostatistics/kxag032 / PMID:43034051

## 2. Reproducible Life-Expectancy Eligibility Criteria for Oncology Studies: Uncertainty Quantification for Machine Learning-Derived Survival Screening Rules — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-11
- **Authors**: Svetnik V et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Operationalize life-expectancy eligibility criteria via survival models with valid uncertainty for future-cohort screening performance.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Retrospective EHR application; >47,000 advanced NSCLC patients, Flatiron Health Research Database; independent right-censored calibration dataset.
  - **Estimand / target**: Cohort yield and positive predictive value as functions of screening threshold, for a random future cohort.
  - **Identification**: Calibration sample assumed representative of target population; handles right-censoring; models treated as black boxes.
  - **Estimator / model**: Bootstrap-based simultaneous confidence and prediction bands, jointly valid across models and thresholds; model-agnostic, no calibration requirement.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Applied across multiple survival models and prediction horizons.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Method yielded stable, interpretable screening rules with reliable cohort-level uncertainty quantification across models and horizons.
- **Strengths**: Simultaneous validity permits clinician-driven post-hoc model/threshold selection without invalidating inference.
- **Limitations**: Needs a separate representative calibration dataset; abstract reports no simulation coverage numbers.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Useful for emulating trial life-expectancy eligibility in RWD/target-trial studies with transparent, reproducible criteria.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70811 / PMID:43032892

## 3. Uptake of Bayesian robust information borrowing in Phase II clinical trials: a scoping review — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: BMC Medical Research Methodology
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-05
- **Authors**: Weru V et al.
- **Type**: review
- **Aims**: Describe how external information is borrowed, justified and reported in Phase II clinical trials.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Scoping review of PubMed Central, Cochrane CENTRAL plus citation search; 1282 records screened, 37 trials included.
  - **Estimand / target**: Frequency of borrowing methods, data-suitability assessment, and tuning-parameter specification/justification.
  - **Identification**: not applicable (descriptive review)
  - **Estimator / model**: Methods reviewed: power prior, meta-analytic predictive prior, robust mixture prior, Bayesian hierarchical models, fixed downweighting.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: not reported
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Fixed downweighting was the most common approach; only slightly over a third of studies detailed its implementation.
- **Strengths**: Quantifies the gap between robust-borrowing methodology and real trial practice.
- **Limitations**: Small included sample (N=37) and database scope may miss industry trials.
- **Takeaway for practice**: When borrowing external controls, prespecify and report the borrowing method, prior-data conflict checks and tuning parameters.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1186/s12874-026-03052-3 / PMID:43026506

## 4. Multiomics tissue segmentation via spatially-informed nested biclustering methods — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: Biometrics
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-07
- **Authors**: Denti F et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Jointly segment tissue pixels and cluster molecular signals across multiple MSI molecular classes sharing a pixel grid.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulations plus case study of multiomics MALDI-MSI (lipids, N-glycans, peptides) on clear cell renal cell carcinoma kidney tissue.
  - **Estimand / target**: Posterior tissue segmentation and within-class molecular signal clusters.
  - **Identification**: Separate exchangeability extended with Markov random field (Potts) spatial dependence.
  - **Estimator / model**: Poseidon: Bayesian nonparametric nested biclustering (shared-atoms Dirichlet process) with mean-field variational inference.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulated scenarios validating clustering performance.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Produced clear tissue segmentation and biomarker detection in ccRCC tissue; quantitative metrics not reported in abstract.
- **Strengths**: Scalable variational inference for high-dimensional spatial multi-dataset data.
- **Limitations**: Niche application; variational approximation may understate posterior uncertainty.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Relevant mainly for molecular-epidemiology teams analysing spatial omics imaging; limited direct use in population epi.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/biomtc/ujag177 / PMID:43021586

## 5. Evaluation of measurement properties of PROMs used in UK NIHR-funded musculoskeletal RCTs: A COSMIN-style systematic review — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: Journal of Clinical Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-09
- **Authors**: Borsky K et al.
- **Type**: review
- **Aims**: Appraise measurement properties of PROMs used as primary outcomes in recent NIHR-funded musculoskeletal RCTs.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Systematic review (PubMed, EMBASE, PsycINFO to March 2025); 26,598 records, 91 articles/180 studies on nine PROMs.
  - **Estimand / target**: COSMIN ratings (sufficient/insufficient/indeterminate) per measurement property and GRADE-graded evidence quality.
  - **Identification**: not applicable (measurement-validity appraisal)
  - **Estimator / model**: COSMIN risk-of-bias standards with modified GRADE; measurement invariance/DIF assessment.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Duplicate screening and COSMIN-guided extraction.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: No PROM met COSMIN criteria for unreserved recommendation; SPADI, OSS, MOXFQ, MSK HQ recommended against due to insufficient structural validity.
- **Strengths**: Comprehensive, standards-based appraisal directly tied to funded trial outcome choices.
- **Limitations**: Restricted to UK NIHR trials over two years; COSMIN thresholds are stringent.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Check structural validity and DIF of PROM outcomes before use; outcome mismeasurement can bias trial and observational effect estimates.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.jclinepi.2026.112585 / PMID:43003358
