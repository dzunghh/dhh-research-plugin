## 1. Heterogeneous treatment effect estimation with instrumental variable methods — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Statistical Methods in Medical Research
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-06
- **Authors**: Kazemi AA et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Estimate CATE for categorical treatments under unmeasured confounding by combining IV and covariate-adjusted estimators.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulation with nonlinear effect modification and unobserved confounding; rheumatoid arthritis TNF-alpha inhibitor comparison, observational registry data.
  - **Estimand / target**: Conditional average treatment effect given effect modifiers (age), multi-category treatment.
  - **Identification**: Valid IVs (relevance, exclusion, independence) plus parametric assumption on residual unobserved confounding effect; data-fusion logic.
  - **Estimator / model**: IV estimator combined with covariate-adjusted estimator, trial/observational data-fusion style; consistent and efficient CATE estimator.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulation versus competing IV and covariate-adjusted estimators on mean squared error.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Lower MSE than competing IV and adjusted estimators in simulation; RA analysis suggests smaller treatment differences in older patients.
- **Strengths**: Handles more than two treatments and effect modification, going beyond complier-only LATE.
- **Limitations**: Relies on parametric assumption about residual unobserved confounding; code availability not reported.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Consider when valid IVs exist and clinically relevant, population-level CATEs are needed rather than LATE.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1177/09622802261495081 / PMID:42992410

## 2. Nonparametric sensitivity analysis for unobserved confounding with survival outcomes — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Biometrics
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-07
- **Authors**: Hu R et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Provide assumption-light sensitivity analysis for unobserved confounding with right-censored time-to-event outcomes.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Numerical studies plus analysis of elective neck dissection in high-grade parotid carcinoma.
  - **Estimand / target**: Difference between observed and counterfactual survival curves; restricted mean survival time difference.
  - **Identification**: Nonparametric bounds on counterfactual survival under bounded unobserved confounding; no Cox or parametric confounder structure required.
  - **Estimator / model**: Semiparametric efficient estimators of bounds with influence-function-based inference and confidence bands.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Numerical simulation studies of estimator performance.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Valid bounds and inference without Cox or parametric confounder assumptions; applied to causal effect of neck dissection on mortality.
- **Strengths**: Avoids hazard-ratio causal interpretation problems by working on survival curve and RMST scales.
- **Limitations**: Bounds can be wide; sensitivity parameter choice requires subject-matter input.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use to stress-test survival analyses of observational data without committing to proportional hazards.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/biomtc/ujag184 / PMID:42989849

## 3. Drug Therapies and Incidence of Dementia Among Adults With Overactive Bladder — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Network Open
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-02
- **Authors**: Sheyn D et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Compare dementia risk across high-risk anticholinergic, low-risk anticholinergic and β3 agonist initiators for overactive bladder.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Retrospective new-user cohort, Epic Cosmos EHR 2012–2025, 830,393 adults aged 40–90.
  - **Estimand / target**: Hazard ratio and weighted 10-year dementia risk by first-prescribed drug class; landmark duration-response.
  - **Identification**: Conditional exchangeability given measured covariates; positivity; new-user design within 1 year of diagnosis.
  - **Estimator / model**: Inverse probability of treatment weighting with weighted Cox models, stratified by age and sex.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: E-values 1.29 and 1.20; exploratory landmark duration analysis.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: High-risk anticholinergics vs β3 agonists HR 0.90 (0.87-0.93); low-risk vs β3 HR 0.97 (0.93-1.01).
- **Strengths**: Very large new-user cohort with active comparators and explicit E-value reporting.
- **Limitations**: Dementia ascertained from codes/medications; small E-values mean modest residual confounding could explain results.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Active-comparator new-user design with IPTW can show prior drug-harm signals reflect confounding by indication.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.42026 / PMID:42993548

## 5. Improving interpretation of latent class models for diagnostic tests: distinguishing target condition from measurands using directed acyclic graphs — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: Biometrics
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-07
- **Authors**: Dendukuri N et al.
- **Type**: tutorial/guidance
- **Aims**: Use DAGs to separate target condition from test measurands and correctly label latent classes.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulation studies; reanalysis of pediatric tuberculosis and leptospirosis diagnostic datasets.
  - **Estimand / target**: Test accuracy and target-condition prevalence from latent class models.
  - **Identification**: DAG encoding tests, latent measurands, latent target condition, covariates and conditional dependence; effect on likelihood identifiability examined.
  - **Estimator / model**: Latent class analysis with more than two classes where measurands differ from target condition.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulations showing class mislabeling when measurands are ignored.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Ignoring distinct measurands can mislabel latent classes; two published analyses reinterpreted.
- **Strengths**: Brings DAG reasoning to diagnostic accuracy without a gold standard.
- **Limitations**: Diagnostic-accuracy focus; limited direct causal-effect content.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Draw a DAG of tests, measurands and target condition before fitting latent class models.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/biomtc/ujag185 / PMID:42989848
