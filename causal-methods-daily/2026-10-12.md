## 1. Generalizing Conditional Average Treatment Effects From Nested Randomized Trials to Trial-Eligible Individuals — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-10
- **Authors**: Wen L et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Estimate CATE functions in the target population of trial-eligible individuals under a nested trial design.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Nested trial design; simulation studies plus application to the Coronary Artery Surgery Study (CASS).
  - **Estimand / target**: CATE across pre-specified effect modifiers in the trial-eligible target population.
  - **Identification**: Generalizability assumptions for nested designs (exchangeability over trial participation, positivity); effect-modifier distribution differs between trial and target.
  - **Estimator / model**: Data-adaptive nuisance estimation, pseudo-outcomes from conditional influence functions, local linear kernel regression, sample splitting and cross-fitting.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Finite-sample simulations; asymptotic conditions under which nuisance estimation error is negligible.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Establishes asymptotic properties under cross-fitting; simulations assess finite-sample performance. No numeric results reported in abstract.
- **Strengths**: Unified, theory-backed approach to heterogeneity generalization, from a leading transportability group.
- **Limitations**: Abstract reports no code and no quantitative performance; kernel smoothing limits dimensionality of effect modifiers.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use when trial CATEs must inform decisions for the broader eligible population, not just enrolled participants.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70767 / PMID:42855171

## 2. Survival Outcomes Following Preemptive vs Nonpreemptive Kidney Transplantation — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Network Open
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-01
- **Authors**: Rampersad C et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Estimate mortality and graft outcomes of preemptive vs nonpreemptive kidney transplantation via target trial emulation.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Retrospective target trial emulation, Scientific Registry of Transplant Recipients, 2005–2023, N=32,770 first kidney-alone recipients.
  - **Estimand / target**: Observational-analogue hazard ratio for all-cause mortality; secondary graft failure, death-censored graft failure, delayed graft function.
  - **Identification**: Conditional exchangeability given DAG-selected confounders; lead-time bias addressed by left truncation at eGFR ≤20 wait-time start.
  - **Estimator / model**: Stabilized IPTW; weighted Kaplan-Meier; weighted left-truncated Cox; weighted logistic regression.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Attenuation analysis adjusting for delayed graft function; no other sensitivity analyses reported.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: No mortality benefit (HR 1.02, 95% CI 0.96–1.07); lower death-censored graft failure (HR 0.81, 0.74–0.88).
- **Strengths**: Explicitly handles lead-time and selection biases that confound preemptive-transplant literature.
- **Limitations**: Registry data with unmeasured confounding; no quantitative bias analysis reported in abstract.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Left truncation plus IPTW is a template for time-zero alignment in treatment-timing emulations.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.37964 / PMID:42848402

## 3. Maternal Influenza Infection During Pregnancy and Neuropsychiatric Disorders in Offspring — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Network Open
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-01
- **Authors**: Kong J et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Assess whether maternal influenza in pregnancy raises offspring neuropsychiatric risk, testing familial confounding with sibling comparison.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Binational birth cohorts: Korea NHIS discovery (2,564,435 pairs) and Japan JMDC replication (181,681 pairs), follow-up to 2023.
  - **Estimand / target**: Adjusted hazard ratio of any offspring neuropsychiatric disorder for maternal influenza in pregnancy.
  - **Identification**: Measured-confounder exchangeability for IPTW; sibling comparison controls shared familial confounders.
  - **Estimator / model**: Stabilized IPTW weighted Cox; exposure-discordant sibling-pair analysis; trimester and season subgroups.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Replication in independent Japanese cohort; sibling analysis as negative-control-like check.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: IPTW AHR 1.09 (1.04–1.14) discovery, 1.10 (1.02–1.19) replication; sibling AHR 0.98 (0.89–1.09), 1.04 (0.88–1.22).
- **Strengths**: Replication plus sibling design shows how population-level signal can vanish under familial control.
- **Limitations**: Claims-based exposure ascertainment; sibling-discordant subsets are small with wide intervals.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Pair IPTW with sibling comparisons in prenatal-exposure studies before inferring causation.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.37902 / PMID:42848403

## 4. Maternal Smoking and Risk of Childhood Cancer: A Report From the Childhood Cancer and Leukemia International Consortium — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: International Journal of Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-18
- **Authors**: Domingues A et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Re-evaluate maternal prenatal smoking and childhood cancer using record-linkage data with probabilistic bias analysis for exposure misclassification.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Pooled population-based record-linkage case-control studies (USA, Sweden, Finland, Denmark); >20,000 cases, >300,000 controls.
  - **Estimand / target**: Odds ratio of childhood cancer by cancer type for maternal prenatal smoking, bias-adjusted.
  - **Identification**: Prospectively recorded exposure; adjusted logistic regression; assumes validation-study sensitivity and specificity are transportable.
  - **Estimator / model**: Logistic regression; probabilistic bias analysis with biomarker-derived sensitivity and specificity; simulation intervals.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Probabilistic bias analysis (simulation intervals) is itself the sensitivity analysis for misclassification.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Mostly null; bias-adjusted OR 0.68 (0.47–0.98) for melanoma, 1.50 (1.21–1.84) for unspecified malignant tumours.
- **Strengths**: Large multinational sample with explicit quantitative bias analysis for misclassification.
- **Limitations**: Only exposure misclassification modeled; confounding and selection not bias-adjusted; subtype findings may reflect multiplicity.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Report bias-adjusted estimates with simulation intervals when exposure validation data exist.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/ije/dyag225 / PMID:42859918

## 5. A Bias-Adjusted Approach for Logistic Modeling With Predicted Exposures — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-10
- **Authors**: Arsham A et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Correct logistic regression bias when exposure is a prediction-model output measured with error.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Cohort designs; simulation study plus NHANES-type application relating glycohemoglobin to hypertension incidence.
  - **Estimand / target**: Logistic regression coefficients (log-odds ratio) for the true exposure.
  - **Identification**: Mixture measurement error model; linear regression calibration; assumes user-specified prediction model is adequate.
  - **Estimator / model**: Bias-adjusted estimators using bootstrap estimates of error-model parameters; Wald intervals with bootstrap standard errors.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulations under misspecified prediction models; ML vs parametric comparison; diagnostics in Supporting Information.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Accurate point estimates and near-nominal coverage; oversimplified prediction models cause severe bias, and ML models can underperform correct parametric ones.
- **Strengths**: Directly addresses the increasingly common pipeline of predicted-exposure regression, with misspecification stress tests.
- **Limitations**: Requires correct specification of the prediction model; cohort designs only; not reported for time-to-event outcomes.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Do not treat predicted exposures as observed; apply calibration-based correction and check prediction model fit.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70740 / PMID:42853115
