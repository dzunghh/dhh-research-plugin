## 1. Addressing Missing Data in Clinical Trials With a Hybrid Control Arm — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-11 (EDAT 2026-10)
- **Authors**: Wasserman J et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Propose treatment-effect estimators handling missing outcomes in both trial and external control data, even when missingness patterns differ.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Trials with hybrid control arm (randomized plus historical/registry controls); simulation plus one real-world trial application.
  - **Estimand / target**: Treatment effect (average effect) in trial population with missing outcomes in trial and external controls.
  - **Identification**: Outcomes missing at random (MAR) conditional on covariates, separately by data source; external-control exchangeability.
  - **Estimator / model**: Augmented weighted estimating equations; doubly robust under MAR; compared with a parametric modeling approach.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Pattern-mixture model sensitivity analysis for MAR violations; simulation comparison.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Estimators are doubly robust under MAR; numerical results not reported in abstract.
- **Strengths**: Addresses missing data and external borrowing jointly, a gap in hybrid-control guidance.
- **Limitations**: Abstract gives no quantitative performance or code availability.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use doubly robust estimators plus pattern-mixture sensitivity analyses when hybrid-control trials have source-specific missing outcomes.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70670 / PMID:42914162

## 2. Differences in SARS-CoV-2 rapid diagnostic testing usage by COVID-19 vaccination status can bias vaccine effectiveness estimates against symptomatic SARS-CoV-2 infection from a test-negative design — Score 4.5/6

- **Journal**: International Journal of Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-14
- **Authors**: Wiegand RE et al.
- **Type**: simulation
- **Aims**: Quantify bias in test-negative-design vaccine effectiveness (VE) when rapid test use differs by vaccination status.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Individual-level simulations seeded with US national COVID-19 surveillance data; 21 scenarios varying healthcare-visit and RDT-use probabilities.
  - **Estimand / target**: VE = 100 × (1 − odds ratio) against symptomatic infection; bias in percentage points versus no-RDT simulation.
  - **Identification**: Test-negative design; selection into testing depends on RDT result and vaccination status (selection bias mechanism).
  - **Estimator / model**: Odds ratio comparing vaccinated versus unvaccinated test-positives and test-negatives; Monte Carlo uncertainty intervals.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Scenario analysis across visit and RDT probabilities.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Bias ranged from about −6 pp to +8.4 pp (e.g., 8.4 pp, UI 5.6–11.3, when unvaccinated more likely to seek care after a positive RDT).
- **Strengths**: Quantifies bias direction under explicit, plausible selection mechanisms.
- **Limitations**: Simulation depends on assumed behavior probabilities, not observed RDT data.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Record or adjust for home RDT use and care-seeking behavior in TND VE studies.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/ije/dyag226 / PMID:42909463

## 3. Bayesian Methods Incorporating Baseline Covariates for Borrowing Historical Control Data in Randomized Clinical Trials: A Neutral Comparison Study — Score 4.5/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-11 (EDAT 2026-10)
- **Authors**: Jesse D et al.
- **Type**: simulation
- **Aims**: Neutrally compare covariate-adjusted Bayesian dynamic borrowing methods for historical controls in RCTs.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Extensive simulations for continuous outcomes plus two real RCT data examples.
  - **Estimand / target**: Treatment effect on continuous outcome with borrowing from historical controls.
  - **Identification**: Exchangeability of historical and current controls conditional on baseline covariates (propensity-score balance).
  - **Estimator / model**: Propensity score weighted commensurate prior; PS-weighted multi-source exchangeability models; Bayesian additive regression trees.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulation scenarios of heterogeneity; two real-data re-analyses.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Reveals potential issues with some approaches; provides method-choice recommendations (specific numbers not in abstract).
- **Strengths**: Neutral comparison design reduces developer-favoring bias.
- **Limitations**: Continuous outcomes only; three methods compared.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Check borrowing-method operating characteristics by simulation before adopting covariate-adjusted dynamic borrowing in rare-disease trials.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70785 / PMID:42914204

## 4. Measurement differences explain much of the variation in adherence and persistence after ischemic stroke, and standardized estimates are lower: a systematic review with meta-regression — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Journal of Clinical Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-22
- **Authors**: Jiang Y et al.
- **Type**: review
- **Aims**: Identify sources of heterogeneity in reported adherence and persistence and derive standardized estimates.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Systematic review, 141 studies; 101 (432 estimates, 976,020 participants) in synthesis.
  - **Estimand / target**: Pooled adherence and persistence proportions standardized to a reference measurement specification.
  - **Identification**: Model-based standardization: covariate-adjusted prediction at pharmacy/administrative data, moderate stringency, 9–15 month window.
  - **Estimator / model**: Three-level hierarchical Bayesian model; multivariable meta-regression on measurement, follow-up, drug class, region.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Variance partitioning across Assessment, Administration and Patient domains.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Pooled adherence fell from 74.1% unstandardized to 53.7% standardized; persistence from 76.8% to 48.2%.
- **Strengths**: Separates measurement artifact from true behavioral variation with a transparent reference framework.
- **Limitations**: Credible intervals wide for subgroup contrasts; observational heterogeneity remains.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Standardize adherence definitions (or model them) before comparing or pooling across studies.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.jclinepi.2026.112571 / PMID:42914064

## 5. Data-driven exploration of occupations and lung cancer risks using regularized regression models — Score 3.5/6

- **Journal**: International Journal of Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-14
- **Authors**: Ge C et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Agnostically identify occupations associated with lung cancer while accounting for known carcinogens.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Pooled 14 case-control studies; men 13,587 cases/16,410 controls; women 3,199 cases/4,408 controls.
  - **Estimand / target**: Odds ratio of lung cancer for ever-employment in each occupation.
  - **Identification**: Adjustment for age, study, smoking and known occupational carcinogen exposures; no explicit causal assumptions stated.
  - **Estimator / model**: LASSO and two-stage Bayesian hierarchical horseshoe selection, then unconditional logistic regression.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Agreement between two selection approaches ("consistently identified" occupations).
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Bricklayers OR 1.30 (1.12–1.52), miners 1.28 (1.06–1.54); secondary teachers OR 0.37 (0.22–0.60).
- **Strengths**: Two independent shrinkage approaches guard against selection instability in large pooled data.
- **Limitations**: Post-selection ORs may be optimistic; residual confounding by smoking intensity and SES possible.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use penalized selection for hypothesis-free exposure screens, then confirm in independent data.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/ije/dyag183 / PMID:42909464
