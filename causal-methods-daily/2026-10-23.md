## 1. Multivariable Mendelian Randomization With Weak Instruments: A Comparison of Bayesian and Frequentist Methods — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-10
- **Authors**: Grant AJ et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Evaluate MVMR-Pony, a Bayesian approach mitigating weak-instrument bias in multivariable Mendelian randomization, against frequentist methods.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulation studies with weak instruments from correlated genetic effects, measurement error and mediation; no applied dataset reported.
  - **Estimand / target**: Direct causal effects of each exposure conditional on other exposures in MVMR.
  - **Identification**: Standard MR assumptions (relevance, independence, exclusion) with conditional instrument strength per exposure; weak conditional instruments handled by modelling.
  - **Estimator / model**: Bayesian MVMR-Pony versus existing frequentist MVMR estimators.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulation scenarios varying correlation, measurement error and mediation; bias, coverage, type I error, power.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: MVMR-Pony outperformed frequentist approaches on bias, coverage, type I error and power across scenarios. Numerical values not reported in abstract.
- **Strengths**: Addresses conditional weak instruments, a known unresolved MVMR problem, across several realistic failure mechanisms.
- **Limitations**: Simulation-only evidence in abstract; software availability and real-data demonstration not reported.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Consider Bayesian MVMR-Pony when instruments are strong marginally but weak conditionally in multi-exposure MR.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70788 / PMID:42894289

## 2. Denosumab Therapy Duration and Osteonecrosis of the Jaw and Skeletal Events in Bone-Metastatic Prostate Cancer — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Network Open
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-01
- **Authors**: Kim S et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Estimate effects of denosumab treatment duration on 3-year ONJ and skeletal events via target trial emulation.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Nationwide Korean claims (HIRA) 2009–2024; 3307 men with bone-metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer initiating denosumab.
  - **Estimand / target**: Per-protocol 3-year cumulative incidence and risk ratios/differences for six duration strategies (≤6, 7–12, ≥13 months).
  - **Identification**: Target trial emulation; conditional exchangeability given baseline covariates; death handled as competing event.
  - **Estimator / model**: Clone-censor-weight with inverse probability of censoring weights.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Schedule-adherent subgroup analysis; radiotherapy-inclusive composite outcome as secondary.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: 7–12 vs ≤6 months: skeletal events RR 0.66 (0.45–0.97), ONJ RR 2.22 (1.21–4.11). ≥13 months: ONJ risk difference 8.93 points versus 7–12 months.
- **Strengths**: Clone-censor-weight handles immortal-time bias for duration strategies, with competing-risk handling.
- **Limitations**: Claims data with residual confounding risk; no quantitative bias analysis reported.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use clone-censor-weight when comparing treatment-duration strategies to avoid immortal-time bias.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.39765 / PMID:42905706

  - **Estimator / model**: Joint Bayesian hierarchical mixture model with random changepoints, fitted by MCMC.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulation under several covariate extensions; cross-validation in HALT-C.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Quantitative performance not reported in abstract; covariates evaluated for capturing unexplained biomarker heterogeneity.
- **Strengths**: Allows covariates at three model levels, with trial data validation.
- **Limitations**: Single-disease application; results and code availability not reported in abstract.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Consider covariate-adjusted changepoint models when designing multi-biomarker surveillance rules.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1177/09622802261496428 / PMID:42904922

## 5. Androgen Synthesis Inhibitors vs Androgen Receptor Inhibitors and Survival in Metastatic Hormone-Sensitive Prostate Cancer — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Network Open
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-01
- **Authors**: Gebrael G et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Compare overall survival with ASI (abiraterone) versus ARI (apalutamide/enzalutamide) intensification in mHSPC.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Retrospective cohort, Flatiron Health EHR-derived database, 3404 patients, 2013–2024.
  - **Estimand / target**: Hazard ratio for overall survival, ARI versus ASI (average effect in weighted population).
  - **Identification**: Conditional exchangeability given measured covariates; no randomization.
  - **Estimator / model**: Inverse probability of treatment weighting with Cox/Kaplan-Meier survival estimation.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Alternative index date at therapy initiation; restriction to index dates after December 2019.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: ARI had longer OS: HR 0.74 (95% CI 0.64–0.87); sensitivity HRs 0.73 and 0.67.
- **Strengths**: Large real-world cohort with consistent sensitivity analyses.
- **Limitations**: Unmeasured confounding and index-date issues remain; authors note only a randomized trial is definitive.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Treat as hypothesis-generating; check index-date alignment and consider E-values when using EHR comparative cohorts.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.39458 / PMID:42900475
