## 1. Causal vaccine effects on post-infection outcomes in the naturally infected — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Biometrics
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-07
- **Authors**: Codi A et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Define and estimate vaccine effects on post-infection outcomes among the "Naturally Infected" (infected if unvaccinated), rather than the always-infected Doomed stratum.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Theory plus simulations; reanalysis of a randomized rotavirus vaccine trial with antibiotic use as post-infection outcome.
  - **Estimand / target**: Principal-stratum causal effect of vaccination on post-infection outcomes in the Naturally Infected stratum.
  - **Identification**: Nonparametric bounds under minimal assumptions; point identification via exclusion restriction and/or partial principal ignorability; dose-based interpretation avoids cross-world parameters.
  - **Estimator / model**: Efficient one-step (influence-function) estimators, robust to some nuisance-model misspecification.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulations of bound validity/width and estimator performance; comparison against marginal and Doomed-stratum analyses.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Bounds valid but often wide. In the rotavirus trial, Naturally Infected analyses suggested a protective effect on antibiotic use, where marginal and Doomed analyses showed little effect.
- **Strengths**: Clinically meaningful estimand with a principled bounds-to-point-identification ladder and efficient estimators.
- **Limitations**: Point estimates rest on untestable principal ignorability/exclusion assumptions; bounds often uninformative.
- **Takeaway for practice**: For post-infection vaccine outcomes, report Naturally Infected estimands with bounds alongside Doomed-stratum effects to avoid understating vaccine benefit.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/biomtc/ujag179 / PMID:43021585

## 2. Selection Bias in Hybrid Randomized Controlled Trials Using External Controls: A Simulation Study — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-11
- **Authors**: Chiam HC et al.
- **Type**: simulation
- **Aims**: Quantify bias when historical external controls are selected in an outcome-dependent way during hybrid RCT planning.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulation study varying between-study heterogeneity, time trends, distributional shift, sample size, allocation ratio, number of studies; illustrative clinical trial example.
  - **Estimand / target**: Treatment effect versus control in the prospective trial; Type I error and bias of the test.
  - **Identification**: Assumes exchangeability of historical and prospective controls; violated by data-driven selection of external datasets.
  - **Estimator / model**: Test-then-pool (frequentist) and robust meta-analytic-predictive (MAP) prior (Bayesian) borrowing.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Multiple hypothetical selection mechanisms for choosing external controls.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Outcome-dependent selection of historical controls introduces bias even when controls are exchangeable; mitigation strategies discussed (abstract gives no numeric bias/Type I error figures).
- **Strengths**: Isolates an underexplored design-stage bias in external-control borrowing across Bayesian and frequentist methods.
- **Limitations**: Simulation-only; abstract reports no quantitative results or code.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Prespecify external control datasets before viewing historical outcomes when designing hybrid RCTs or external-comparator analyses.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70819 / PMID:43023650

## 3. Comparison of estimated personalized treatment rules under varying interference structures and treatment rates using dynamic weighted ordinary least squares — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: BMC Medical Research Methodology
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-18
- **Authors**: Mossman A et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Extend dWOLS to estimate dynamic treatment regimes under within- and between-group interference in nested hierarchical networks.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulation of nested hierarchical networks varying overall treatment rate and proportion of egos with assigned alters.
  - **Estimand / target**: Optimal personalized treatment rule (blip parameters) under interference.
  - **Identification**: No unmeasured confounding plus correctly specified exposure mapping for within- and between-group interference.
  - **Estimator / model**: Dynamic weighted ordinary least squares (doubly robust) with interference-aware exposure mappings vs mis-specified mappings.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Comparison across exposure-mapping misspecifications and treatment-rate scenarios.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Treatment rate and proportion exposed to neighbours affect dWOLS performance; interference-aware dWOLS performed better across all scenarios.
- **Strengths**: Addresses a realistic but neglected violation of SUTVA in DTR estimation.
- **Limitations**: Simulation-based; no applied data or numeric performance reported in abstract.
- **Takeaway for practice**: When estimating treatment rules in household/clinic networks, model both within- and between-group interference rather than standard dWOLS.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1186/s12874-026-02936-8 / PMID:43021072

## 4. Comparing performance of three propensity score weighting methods for continuous exposures in nutritional epidemiology — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: BMC Medical Research Methodology
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-18
- **Authors**: Muramatsu Y et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Compare IPTW, generalized overlap weighting and generalized matching weighting for continuous nutrient exposures, and choose quantile bin numbers.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Reanalysis of Japan Diabetes Complications Study cohort, N=1,414 type 2 diabetes patients; nutrient intake vs complications.
  - **Estimand / target**: Risk ratios for diabetes complications; IPTW targets whole population, GOW/GMW target overlap populations.
  - **Identification**: Conditional exchangeability given covariates; positivity concerns addressed via overlap-targeting weights.
  - **Estimator / model**: Generalized propensity score via quantile binning (2–5+ bins); IPTW, GOW, GMW weighting.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Covariate balance, weight variability, precision of risk ratios across bin numbers and overlap levels.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: 2 or 5 bins favourable; methods similar with good overlap, but with reduced overlap GOW/GMW balanced better and GOW had smallest weight variability.
- **Strengths**: Practical guidance on positivity for continuous exposures using real cohort data.
- **Limitations**: Single empirical dataset, no simulation ground truth; binning discards exposure information; estimands differ across methods.
- **Takeaway for practice**: For continuous exposures with poor overlap, consider generalized overlap weights with ~5 quantile bins, noting the shifted target population.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1186/s12874-026-02971-5 / PMID:43021127

## 5. Generalized point process additive models — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the Royal Statistical Society Series B
- **Pub date**: 2026-04-13
- **Authors**: Lee KY et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Regress a scalar response on high-dimensional point-process predictors (e.g., EHR event times) via additive models.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Theory, simulations, and an electronic health record data application.
  - **Estimand / target**: Additive component functions linking point-process predictors to a scalar response (not causal).
  - **Identification**: not applicable (predictive regression); sparsity and reduced-basis structural assumptions.
  - **Estimator / model**: Penalized likelihood with kernel embedding of random counting measures (RKHS), additive model, sparsity penalty.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Estimation and selection consistency with diverging number of predictors; simulations.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Estimator is consistent for estimation and variable selection as predictors diverge; performance illustrated in simulations and EHR data.
- **Strengths**: Principled framework for using irregular EHR event histories as predictors.
- **Limitations**: Predictive, non-causal; applied relevance to epi depends on available implementation.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Consider for feature construction from longitudinal EHR event streams instead of ad hoc counts, pending software release.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jrsssb/qkag061 / PMID:43022224

## Appraisal notes

Design-stage bias from data-driven external-control selection and positivity issues for continuous exposures are recurring practical pitfalls. Code availability was not reported for any pick.
