## 1. Estimate Optimal Dynamic Treatment Regimes with Partial Compatibility: An Application to data from a TREAT Asia HIV Observational Database (TAHOD) site in Pune, India — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: American Journal of Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-13
- **Authors**: Si C et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Stabilise dynamic treatment regime value estimation in small samples by letting near-compatible trajectories contribute information.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Observational HIV cohort, one TAHOD site in Pune, India, 2013-2024; adults living with HIV; N not reported.
  - **Estimand / target**: Expected outcome (value) under threshold-based regimes using baseline CD4 count and weight.
  - **Identification**: Sequential exchangeability, positivity and consistency; regime compatibility relaxed to partial compatibility.
  - **Estimator / model**: Generalized compatibility weighting (GCW) and bootstrap compatibility windowing (BCW), extending IPW/AIPW; information borrowing diminishes as sample size grows.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Compared with standard IPW-based estimators across candidate thresholds; variability of regime values assessed.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: GCW and BCW gave more stable regime values and more reliable identification of empirically optimal thresholds than standard IPW. No numeric results in abstract.
- **Strengths**: Addresses the practical problem of extreme weights when strict regime compatibility leaves few valid trajectories.
- **Limitations**: Single-site application; bias-variance trade-off from borrowing near-compatible trajectories not quantified in the abstract.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Consider partial-compatibility weighting when evaluating treatment rules in small cohorts where standard IPW weights are unstable.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/aje/kwag254 / PMID:42865085

- **Type**: tutorial/guidance
- **Aims**: Explain the law of iterated expectation and its role in identifying causal effects through the g-formula.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Tutorial with three numerical examples: time-fixed binary confounder, time-fixed mixed confounders, and two-timepoint time-varying.
  - **Estimand / target**: Standardised (marginal) causal effects, including time-varying treatment strategies.
  - **Identification**: Causal consistency, positivity and conditional exchangeability.
  - **Estimator / model**: G-formula in non-iterative conditional expectation (NICE) and iterative conditional expectation (ICE) forms.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Not applicable; the two g-formula forms are shown to be nonparametrically equivalent.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: NICE and ICE forms are shown to be nonparametrically equivalent, with worked numerical examples of increasing complexity.
- **Strengths**: Makes opaque integration notation accessible through progressively complex examples.
- **Limitations**: Tutorial only; no new estimator or empirical evaluation.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use as teaching or onboarding material before implementing parametric g-computation or ICE estimators.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/aje/kwag258 / PMID:42865042

## 3. Randomization-Based Covariate Adjustment for Randomized Block-Factorial Experiments With Missing Baselines — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-10 (month only)
- **Authors**: Zhao Z et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Develop Lasso-based covariate adjustment for block-factorial randomized experiments with high-dimensional covariates and missing baselines.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Randomized block-factorial experiments; simulations plus one empirical application; data details not reported.
  - **Estimand / target**: Factorial effects in a finite population under randomization-based inference.
  - **Identification**: Randomization; inference robust to model misspecification; some missingness strategies need treatment-invariant baseline missingness.
  - **Estimator / model**: Three Lasso-adjusted estimators (projection, joint projection, stratum-specific) with Neyman-type conservative covariance; three missing-covariate strategies.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulation studies and empirical application; efficiency comparisons under nested projection spaces.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Adjusted estimators are consistent and jointly asymptotically normal. Asymptotic variance is no larger than unadjusted; missingness indicators can add efficiency.
- **Strengths**: Needs no parametric outcome or missingness model and handles high-dimensional covariates.
- **Limitations**: Efficiency comparisons require nested linear spaces; some strategies need treatment-invariant missingness.
- **Takeaway for practice**: In factorial trials with incomplete baselines, add missingness indicators to the adjustment set when they are informative.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70761 / PMID:42869575

## 4. Bootstrap Calibration of Likelihood-Ratio Inference for the Hypergeometric-Normal Random-Effects Model in Rare-Event Meta-Analysis — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-10 (month only)
- **Authors**: Noma H et al.
- **Type**: simulation
- **Aims**: Improve finite-sample calibration of likelihood-ratio inference for rare-event meta-analysis using parametric bootstrap procedures.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Extensive simulation under rare-event conditions; application to rosiglitazone and myocardial infarction meta-analysis.
  - **Estimand / target**: Pooled effect (log odds ratio) with between-study heterogeneity in a random-effects model.
  - **Identification**: Hypergeometric-normal model with exact conditional likelihood; no continuity correction needed for zero cells.
  - **Estimator / model**: LR-based inference with parametric bootstrap mean calibration and direct bootstrap of the LR statistic's empirical distribution.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Coverage across sparse scenarios, including extremely rare events.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Uncorrected LR intervals already gave stable coverage; bootstrap mean calibration was most consistent in the most extreme rare-event settings.
- **Strengths**: Avoids Haldane +0.5 corrections and Wald approximations that fail with zero cells.
- **Limitations**: Computationally heavier; effect estimate numbers and software availability not reported in abstract.
- **Takeaway for practice**: For sparse-event meta-analyses, prefer HGN likelihood-ratio intervals over Wald; add bootstrap mean calibration when events are extremely rare.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70776 / PMID:42869622

## 5. The effect of hypothetical interventions to limit weight gain on incident type 2 diabetes mellitus among people with HIV initiating antiretroviral therapy in the United States, 2012-2021 — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: American Journal of Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-14
- **Authors**: Lesko CR et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Estimate the effect of hypothetical interventions capping BMI gain after ART initiation on 7-year type 2 diabetes risk.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: NA-ACCORD clinical cohorts; 13,822 people with HIV, BMI 18.5-29.9, initiating ART 2012-2021.
  - **Estimand / target**: 7-year T2DM risk ratio under BMI-capping interventions versus no intervention.
  - **Identification**: Conditional exchangeability, positivity and consistency for time-varying BMI; hypothetical interventions on BMI.
  - **Estimator / model**: Parametric extended g-formula.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Three intervention scenarios compared; further sensitivity analyses not reported.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Baseline 7-year T2DM risk 5.6% (4.8-6.3). Preventing any weight gain gave RR 0.81 (0.72-0.92); cap at BMI 29.9, RR 0.88 (0.80-0.97).
- **Strengths**: Large multi-cohort sample and clearly specified hypothetical interventions via the g-formula.
- **Limitations**: Interventions on BMI are ill-defined (multiple versions); the authors call the evidence preliminary.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use g-formula to quantify population impact of graded intervention targets while acknowledging weak consistency for BMI.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/aje/kwag255 / PMID:42869644
