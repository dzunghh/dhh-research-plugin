## 1. Causal effect estimation for ordinal outcomes: a performance comparison of G-computation, IPW, and TMLE-based methods — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: BMC Medical Research Methodology
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-23
- **Authors**: Park A et al.
- **Type**: simulation
- **Aims**: Define the ATE for ordinal outcomes and compare nine causal estimators under correct and misspecified nuisance models.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulation scenarios varying outcome/treatment model misspecification; real-data illustration with PBC dataset (D-penicillamine).
  - **Estimand / target**: ATE for an ordinal outcome (expected-score and threshold-based contrasts).
  - **Identification**: Conditional exchangeability, positivity and consistency, with correct specification of at least one nuisance model for doubly robust estimators.
  - **Estimator / model**: Adjusted estimator, parametric and Super Learner G-computation, IPW, threshold-based and continuous-score TMLE (GLM plus random forest library).
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Bias, empirical SE and RMSE across misspecification scenarios; model-, bootstrap- and influence-function-based CIs.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Threshold-based TMLE was most stable under single-model misspecification; IPW showed high variability; all methods degraded when both models were misspecified.
- **Strengths**: Systematic misspecification grid covering G-computation, IPW and TMLE for a neglected outcome type.
- **Limitations**: Results limited to the specific data-generating mechanisms; the real example is a randomized trial with little confounding.
- **Takeaway for practice**: For ordinal outcomes, prefer threshold-based TMLE over plain IPW or G-computation when model specification is uncertain.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1186/s12874-026-02951-9 / PMID:42897458

## 2. A pragmatic workflow for handling missing PROM data in registry studies: a methodological scoping review and simulated worked example — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: BMC Medical Research Methodology
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-19
- **Authors**: Eriksson J et al.
- **Type**: tutorial/guidance
- **Aims**: Audit missing-data handling in registry PROM studies and propose a pragmatic IPW plus tipping-point workflow.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Scoping review of 61 registry PROM studies (PRISMA-ScR); simulated registry-like cohort, N=8,000.
  - **Estimand / target**: Mean PROM difference (points) under non-response, with MNAR tipping-point delta.
  - **Identification**: MAR given covariates for IPW; MNAR explored via delta adjustment, separating never-responders from dropout.
  - **Estimator / model**: Single- and two-stage IPW, standardized mean difference diagnostics, delta-adjusted tipping-point analysis.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Tipping-point δ* with robustness classification across two simulated scenarios.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: 0/61 studies reported tipping-point analysis; 39% stated no strategy. Complete-case 1.21 (0.69–1.72) vs two-stage IPW 1.23 (0.67–1.79); δ*=7.2.
- **Strengths**: Quantifies a reporting gap and offers a workflow usable without specialist MNAR modelling.
- **Limitations**: Worked example is simulated, and the robustness thresholds are illustrative.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Pair IPW for non-response with a delta tipping-point analysis as routine sensitivity reporting in registry PROM studies.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1186/s12874-026-03058-x / PMID:42897463

## 3. Leukocytes and age-related macular degeneration — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: European Journal of Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-21
- **Authors**: Sandau N et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Test whether leukocyte subtype counts are causally associated with clinically diagnosed AMD.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Three prospective cohorts (CGPS, UKB, MVP), ~792,000 European-ancestry adults, 27,130 incident AMD cases.
  - **Estimand / target**: Hazard ratio (observational) and OR per 1-SD genetically determined leukocyte count.
  - **Identification**: IV relevance, independence and exclusion restriction for GWAS-derived instruments; multivariable MR to handle correlated cell types.
  - **Estimator / model**: Cox regression meta-analysed; one-sample MR and MVMR in UKB and MVP, meta-analysed.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Cross-cohort replication and meta-analysis; pleiotropy-robust methods not reported in abstract.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Eosinophils: MR OR 1.07 (1.00–1.15), MVMR OR 1.12 (1.03–1.21). Observational HR for leukocytes 1.07 (1.05–1.09).
- **Strengths**: Very large sample combining observational and genetic evidence across three cohorts.
- **Limitations**: Modest MR effect with borderline CI; European ancestry only; pleiotropy diagnostics not described.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use MVMR to disentangle correlated blood-cell traits; treat marginal single-trait MR signals cautiously.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1007/s10654-026-01471-6 / PMID:42902106

## 4. Calibrating Machine Learning Approaches for Probability Estimation Without Calibration Data — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-10
- **Authors**: Carluccio ED et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Propose SimCal and compare calibration approaches when no target-population outcome data exist.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulation study; stroke functional-outcome data and Cleveland Clinic coronary artery disease re-analysis.
  - **Estimand / target**: Calibrated outcome probability in a structurally different target population.
  - **Identification**: Synthetic data from development data matched to target marginal statistics; expert judgments for EJM.
  - **Estimator / model**: SimCal, expert judgment modeling, similarity-binning averaging, adaptive calibration of predictions, Elkan calibration.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulation comparison under correct and misspecified parameters; two real datasets.
- **Software**: R code provided.
- **Key findings**: No approach performed well everywhere; SimCal did well when model parameters were correctly specified; EJM failed on stroke data.
- **Strengths**: Honest head-to-head comparison with code, addressing a common transportability problem.
- **Limitations**: Inconsistent performance; authors call for further research.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Treat target-population calibration without outcome data as exploratory; try SimCal when marginal target statistics are credible.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70751 / PMID:42894315

## 5. Federated Time-to-Event Data Analysis Using Piecewise Exponential Regression in DataSHIELD — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-10
- **Authors**: Svennblad B et al.
- **Type**: tutorial/guidance
- **Aims**: Show how to run federated Poisson-based time-to-event analysis across cohorts without moving individual data.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Multi-centre federated individual patient data analysis within DataSHIELD infrastructure.
  - **Estimand / target**: Piecewise-constant hazard rates and rate ratios.
  - **Identification**: Standard regression assumptions (non-informative censoring, correct hazard specification); no causal identification.
  - **Estimator / model**: Piecewise exponential regression implemented as federated Poisson regression on split survival data.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: not reported
- **Software**: DataSHIELD (R).
- **Key findings**: Federated Poisson regression yields time-to-event estimates without data transfer, an alternative to pooling or two-step meta-analysis.
- **Strengths**: Enables individual-level analysis across cohorts under data-sharing restrictions.
- **Limitations**: Abstract reports no performance comparison against pooled analysis.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use federated piecewise exponential models when cohorts cannot share data but one-stage survival modelling is needed.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70789 / PMID:42894295
