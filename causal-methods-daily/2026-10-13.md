## 1. Methylphenidate and Long-Term Risk of Cardiovascular Diseases: A Nationwide Cohort Study — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Pharmacoepidemiology and Drug Safety
- **Pub date**: 2026-10
- **Authors**: Berthod C et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Estimate long-term cardiovascular risk of methylphenidate, using negative control outcomes to calibrate for residual confounding.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: New-user cohort, French National Health Data System 2014–2023; 212,216 users matched 1:3 to 636,648 nonusers on sex and birth month/year.
  - **Estimand / target**: Hazard ratio of incident cardiovascular disease (ICD codes or antihypertensive use) for methylphenidate versus nonuse.
  - **Identification**: Adjustment for demographics and clinical covariates; exclusion of prior CVD; negative control outcomes to detect and correct residual confounding.
  - **Estimator / model**: Cox proportional hazards with adjusted HRs; empirical calibration of HRs and confidence intervals using negative control outcomes.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Negative control outcome calibration is the main bias assessment; other sensitivity analyses not reported.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Adjusted HR 2.37 (2.21–2.54); calibrated HR 1.85 (1.55–2.21), showing attenuation from residual confounding.
- **Strengths**: Very large nationwide cohort with explicit empirical calibration rather than relying on adjustment alone.
- **Limitations**: Matching only on age and sex, no active comparator; outcome includes antihypertensive use, risking surveillance and detection bias.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Pair large claims-database cohorts with negative control outcome calibration to quantify residual confounding, and prefer active comparators.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/pds.70493 / PMID:42860840

## 2. Comparative effectiveness of amlodipine versus nifedipine prescribed at discharge in patients with ischemic stroke or transient ischemic attack: A nationwide 5-year follow-up registry study — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: PLoS Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-12
- **Authors**: Li X et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Compare 5-year functional outcome between amlodipine and nifedipine prescribed at discharge after ischemic stroke or TIA.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Observational analysis of Third China National Stroke Registry (2015–2018); 2,859 patients, 1,847 amlodipine and 1,058 nifedipine after IPTW.
  - **Estimand / target**: Common odds ratio for the 5-year modified Rankin Scale distribution, amlodipine versus nifedipine.
  - **Identification**: Conditional exchangeability given age, sex, BMI, admission systolic BP and onset date; positivity and consistency assumed.
  - **Estimator / model**: Inverse probability of treatment weighting with ordinal logistic regression for mRS distribution.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Sensitivity analyses reported as consistent; specific methods not reported in the abstract.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Amlodipine had lower 5-year mRS: OR 0.76 (0.65–0.89); mRS>0 OR 0.71 (0.61–0.83).
- **Strengths**: Ordinal full-distribution outcome with IPTW and long 5-year follow-up in a national registry.
- **Limitations**: Few adjustment covariates, observational design, high loss to follow-up, and no stated handling of informative censoring.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Treat as a hypothesis-generating comparative effectiveness estimate; it needs richer confounder sets and censoring weights, or a target trial framing.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1371/journal.pmed.1005277 / PMID:42862855

## 3. Development and Validation of an Adult Medical Urgency Score for US Donor Heart Allocation — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: JAMA
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-12
- **Authors**: Lyden GR et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Build and validate a physiology-plus-support-device risk score for heart transplant waitlist mortality to replace the 6-status system.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: US transplant registry, adult candidates 2019–2025; temporal split into training 21,859, validation 7,897 and test 8,256.
  - **Estimand / target**: Six-week waitlist mortality without transplant, estimated as a hypothetical outcome under transplant-censoring.
  - **Identification**: Marginal structural model weighting handles informative censoring at transplant, assuming no unmeasured predictors of both transplant and death.
  - **Estimator / model**: Marginal structural model (US-CRS) with five laboratory values and four device types; eight candidate models compared.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Held-out time-dependent AUC, calibration by status, and a match-run simulation over 45,530 donor hearts.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Time-dependent AUC 0.807 versus 0.693 for the current system; first-ranked candidate mortality 15.7% versus 11.2%.
- **Strengths**: Temporal validation plus policy simulation links a weighted prediction model directly to allocation impact.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective registry with policy-influenced data; simulation cannot capture changed clinician and center behavior under a new policy.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use MSM weighting for dependent censoring (for example transplant) when modeling waitlist risk, and validate with temporal splits plus decision simulation.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jama.2026.17403 / PMID:42862588

## 4. Joint Analysis for Multivariate Longitudinal and Event Time Data With a Change Point Anchored at Interval-Censored Event Time — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-10
- **Authors**: Zhan Y et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Jointly model multivariate longitudinal biomarkers and interval-censored onset, with trajectory change points anchored at onset.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulation study plus PREDICT-HD multisite observational cohort of prodromal Huntington's disease (sample size not reported).
  - **Estimand / target**: Effects of biomarkers on interval-censored onset time and biomarker trajectory changes after onset.
  - **Identification**: Shared-parameter joint model; correctness depends on model specification and noninformative interval-censoring assumptions.
  - **Estimator / model**: Joint multivariate longitudinal and event-time model with change point; inference via asymptotic normality.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Finite-sample simulation of estimator performance; details not reported.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Simulation demonstrates finite-sample performance; PREDICT-HD application characterizes cognitive-motor interplay; numbers not reported.
- **Strengths**: Handles interval censoring and multiple biomarkers with an onset-anchored change point, a realistic neurodegenerative structure.
- **Limitations**: Not a causal estimand; abstract lacks performance numbers and code availability.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Consider for natural-history studies with interval-censored onset; interpret associations descriptively, not causally.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70765 / PMID:42855169

## 5. Multivariate Analysis of Data From Crossover Clinical Trials — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Biometrical Journal
- **Pub date**: 2026-10
- **Authors**: Jiang Y et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Provide multivariate mixed models for multiple-outcome crossover trials, including tests of intra-subject variance matrices.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Crossover designs with multiple outcomes; simulation studies and two real data examples (sizes not reported).
  - **Estimand / target**: Treatment mean differences across outcomes and equality of intra-subject variance matrices between treatments.
  - **Identification**: Randomized sequences; carryover assumptions determine which of the three proposed models applies.
  - **Estimator / model**: Three multivariate linear mixed-effects models; ML and REML with closed-form variance components and exact tests for uniform-within-sequence designs.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulations comparing power against the usual univariate approach.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Simulations show superior performance of the multivariate approach for treatment effects; numeric results not reported.
- **Strengths**: Closed-form estimators and exact tests, plus rarely addressed testing of intra-subject variability.
- **Limitations**: Restricted to normal linear models; closed-form results need uniform-within-sequence designs; no code reported.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use when a crossover trial has several correlated outcomes or interest in within-subject variability; model choice should follow carryover assumptions.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/bimj.70193 / PMID:42860476
