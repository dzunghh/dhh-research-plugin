## 1. Comparison of modeling strategies to study the effect of an exposure on changes in continuous outcomes: alcohol and cardiometabolic markers in NAKO — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: International Journal of Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-14
- **Authors**: Bouka M et al.
- **Type**: simulation
- **Aims**: Compare change-score, baseline-adjusted and Oldham's models for baseline exposure effects on longitudinal change in continuous outcomes.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Prospective German National Cohort (NAKO), ~140,000 with 4–5-year follow-up; alcohol categories versus five cardiometabolic markers.
  - **Estimand / target**: Effect of baseline drinking category on 5-year change in TG, SBP, DBP, HDL-C, HbA1c.
  - **Identification**: DAG-informed adjustment; exposure precedes baseline outcome, so baseline outcome is a mediator/collider risk if adjusted.
  - **Estimator / model**: Change-score regression versus baseline-adjusted ANCOVA versus Oldham's (average of baseline and follow-up) adjustment.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Tests of baseline-level dependence of change; comparison of estimates across three specifications and five markers.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Change-score and Oldham's models agreed across all markers; baseline-adjusted models differed for all five. Baseline-dependence tests did not signal when adjustment matters.
- **Strengths**: Very large cohort and clear DAG rationale for a common, contested modelling choice.
- **Limitations**: Empirical comparison in one cohort and exposure; no simulated ground truth.
- **Takeaway for practice**: When exposure precedes baseline outcome measurement, avoid baseline adjustment and use change-score or Oldham's models.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/ije/dyag245 / PMID:42974917

## 2. Long-term outcomes after PCI with drug-eluting stents versus CABG for left main coronary artery disease: IRIS-MAIN registry — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: The Lancet
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-03
- **Authors**: Wee S-B et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Compare long-term all-cause mortality after PCI versus isolated CABG in Asian patients with unprotected left main disease.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Target trial emulation, 46-hospital IRIS-MAIN registry, 5379 patients (3836 PCI, 1543 CABG), median follow-up 10 years.
  - **Estimand / target**: Observational analogue of per-protocol-style comparison: 10-year all-cause mortality, overlap-weighted hazard ratio and weighted Kaplan-Meier risk.
  - **Identification**: Conditional exchangeability given measured baseline covariates; positivity handled via overlap weighting.
  - **Estimator / model**: Propensity-score overlap weighting with weighted Kaplan-Meier and weighted Cox model.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: not reported in abstract.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: 10-year mortality 24.0% PCI versus 22.9% CABG; overlap-weighted HR 1.00 (95% CI 0.87–1.14).
- **Strengths**: Target trial framing, overlap weighting for positivity, and very long follow-up.
- **Limitations**: Measured confounding only; authors state causal effects cannot be established.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Overlap weighting within a target-trial framework suits comparisons where treatment overlap is limited.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S0140-6736(26)02104-5 / PMID:42976899

## 3. Efficacy and safety of statin discontinuation in patients 65 years and older according to frailty status: CPRD cohort — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: The Lancet Healthy Longevity
- **Pub date**: 2027-09-21 (as listed in PubMed; likely provisional)
- **Authors**: Seeley AE et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Estimate risks and benefits of statin discontinuation in older adults, stratified by frailty.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Retrospective CPRD GOLD–HES–ONS cohort; 65,727 discontinuers matched to 131,453 continuers.
  - **Estimand / target**: Effect of discontinuation on all-cause hospitalisation at 1 and 5 years: subdistribution HR and absolute risk difference.
  - **Identification**: Conditional exchangeability given matching and weighting covariates; discontinuers matched to continuers on index date.
  - **Estimator / model**: Inverse probability of treatment weighting with Fine-Gray models (death as competing risk), stratified by frailty.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: not reported in abstract.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: 1-year sHR 1.22 (1.20–1.25), ARD 37.6 per 1000; null in severe frailty (sHR 1.03, 0.94–1.12).
- **Strengths**: Reports absolute and relative effects with competing risks and effect heterogeneity by frailty.
- **Limitations**: Authors note residual confounding limits causal interpretation; no sensitivity analysis reported.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Pair subdistribution HRs with absolute risk differences when studying deprescribing in older adults.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.lanhl.2026.100920 / PMID:42971235

## 4. Sample Size and Power Calculation for Continuous Outcomes in Open-Cohort Stepped Wedge Designs With Multiple Interventions — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-11
- **Authors**: Feng S et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Provide a unified power framework for open-cohort stepped wedge designs with multiple interventions.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Analytical derivation plus simulation across concurrent, cross-over, supplementation and factorial multi-intervention designs.
  - **Estimand / target**: Intervention effects on continuous outcomes under cluster randomisation; variance of effect estimators for power.
  - **Identification**: Randomised stepped wedge; correct specification of within-cluster and within-individual correlation structures.
  - **Estimator / model**: Closed-form and generalised variance of linear mixed-model estimators; block-exchangeable and decay correlation.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulation checks of power accuracy; power versus correlation and retention parameters.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Simulations show the formulas give accurate power; participant retention and correlation parameters jointly drive power.
- **Strengths**: Covers open cohorts and several multi-intervention designs in one framework.
- **Limitations**: Continuous outcomes only; assumes specified correlation structures.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use when planning stepped wedge trials with turnover and more than one intervention.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70809 / PMID:42976872
