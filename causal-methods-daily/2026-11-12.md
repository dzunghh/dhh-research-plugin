## 1. Bisphosphonates vs Denosumab After Low-Trauma Fractures — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Network Open
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-02
- **Authors**: Lin M et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Compare refracture and all-cause mortality after initiating nitrogen-containing bisphosphonates versus denosumab following low-trauma fracture.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Sequential monthly target trial emulation, new-user active-comparator; NSW Australia linked administrative data; 15,099 adults ≥50y, 3-year follow-up.
  - **Estimand / target**: Intention-to-treat and per-protocol hazard ratios for 3-year mortality and refracture, by sex.
  - **Identification**: Conditional exchangeability given measured covariates; time-zero aligned with treatment initiation, treatment-naive eligibility.
  - **Estimator / model**: Inverse probability weighting with Cox models; Fine-Gray competing-risk analysis; per-protocol with censoring weights.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Competing-risk and per-protocol analyses; fracture-site subgroup (non-hip non-vertebral) analyses.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Mortality similar (women ITT HR 0.98, 0.87-1.10). Refracture higher with N-BP: women HR 1.14 (1.05-1.26), men 1.21 (1.03-1.42).
- **Strengths**: Population-wide data with explicit target trial framework and per-protocol estimates.
- **Limitations**: Unmeasured confounding by indication remains; no quantitative bias analysis reported.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Template for sequential-trial emulation of active-comparator drug comparisons using registry data.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.43512 / PMID:43013305

## 2. Estimation of mRNA-1283 vs BNT162b2 COVID-19 Vaccine Effectiveness in Older US Veterans — Score 4.5/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Network Open
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-02
- **Authors**: McConeghy KW et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Estimate relative effectiveness of mRNA-1283 versus BNT162b2 against medically attended COVID-19 and hospitalization in veterans ≥65.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Matched cohort emulating a target trial; VHA national data; 60,340 mRNA-1283 vs 321,845 BNT162b2 recipients, 150-day follow-up.
  - **Estimand / target**: Relative vaccine effectiveness and absolute risk difference in 150-day cumulative incidence.
  - **Identification**: Exact matching on county and vaccination week, then conditional exchangeability given demographic/clinical covariates.
  - **Estimator / model**: Matching plus inverse probability of treatment weighting; Bayesian estimation with credible intervals.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: not reported
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: rVE 35.9% (95% CrI 20.4-49.7) for illness; ARD −10.0 per 10,000. Hospitalization rVE 47.0% (5.4-74.8).
- **Strengths**: Large sample with calendar-time and geographic matching limiting temporal confounding.
- **Limitations**: Sponsor-affiliated authors; 94% male; few hospitalization events; residual confounding and testing-behaviour bias possible.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Combine design-stage matching on time/place with IPTW for head-to-head vaccine comparisons.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.44727 / PMID:43013304

## 3. Representativeness of participants in the Danish National Health Survey in relation to mental disorders and a range of other medical conditions and mortality — Score 4.5/6

- **Journal**: European Journal of Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-11
- **Authors**: Bødkergaard K et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Assess whether survey-weighted participants represent the Danish general population in morbidity and mortality.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Register-linked comparison of 183,194 survey participants versus 4.7 million residents aged ≥16, across 202 conditions.
  - **Estimand / target**: Period prevalence ratios (5 years pre-survey) and hazard ratios (5 years post-survey) of diagnoses and mortality.
  - **Identification**: Participant versus population contrast under original and updated survey weights; selection bias assessed against register truth.
  - **Estimator / model**: Survey-weight (inverse probability of participation) adjustment; Cox models for hazard ratios.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Updated weights incorporating mental disorders versus original weights.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Participants had lower mortality (HR 0.80, 0.79-0.82), especially with prior mental disorders (HR 0.62). Updated weights fixed prevalence (PPR 0.99) but not mortality.
- **Strengths**: Uses complete register data as benchmark to quantify residual selection bias after weighting.
- **Limitations**: Cannot identify the selection mechanism; applies to Danish survey context.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Weighting on observed covariates may not remove selection on health; benchmark survey weights against registers.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1007/s10654-026-01477-0 / PMID:43012935

## 4. Investigating the instability of variable selection in Lasso-penalized Cox proportional hazards models: a simulation study on low-dimensional real-world medical data — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: BMC Medical Research Methodology
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-24
- **Authors**: Dobies B et al.
- **Type**: simulation
- **Aims**: Quantify how cross-validation design affects variable-selection stability in Lasso Cox models.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Resampling/simulation on Berlin Initiative Study older adults (n=930; mortality n=882, cardiovascular composite n=638), 20 preselected predictors.
  - **Estimand / target**: Variable-selection consistency across random CV seeds; predictive C-index.
  - **Identification**: Not applicable (prognostic modelling, not causal).
  - **Estimator / model**: Lasso and elastic-net penalized Cox regression; varied CV folds, stratification, mixing parameter.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Repeated runs across random seeds; C-index comparison.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: More folds (10-15) gave more stable selection; elastic net no better than Lasso; mixing parameter below 0.6 increased variability.
- **Strengths**: Addresses a routine, often-unreported modelling choice using real clinical data.
- **Limitations**: Single cohort and low-dimensional setting; limited generalizability.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Report and tune CV fold number and seed sensitivity when using penalized Cox models.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1186/s12874-026-03019-4 / PMID:43009798

## 5. Initial data analysis of the national German transplantation registry with a focus on kidney transplantation — Score 3.5/6

- **Pub date**: 2026-10-15
- **Authors**: Klein L et al.
- **Type**: tutorial/guidance
- **Aims**: Perform a structured initial data analysis of German transplant registry data to guide future analyses.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Registry IDA of 14,954 first kidney-only deceased-donor recipients and 9,964 donors, 2006-2016, 25 tables.
  - **Estimand / target**: Descriptive: missingness patterns, consistency, event-time variable availability.
  - **Identification**: Not applicable.
  - **Estimator / model**: Decision-tree missingness pattern identification; influx/outflux analysis for imputation suitability.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Data consistency checks across 168 multi-sourced variables.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Missingness ranged from near-complete to >50%; event-time results depend strongly on variable choice.
- **Strengths**: Transparent, reproducible data-quality workflow before modelling.
- **Limitations**: Descriptive only; no downstream analysis demonstrating impact.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Run documented IDA, including missingness and influx/outflux checks, before registry-based survival or causal analyses.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1186/s12874-026-03014-9 / PMID:43009767
