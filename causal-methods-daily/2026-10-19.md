## 1. Valid Inference for Two Sample Summary Statistics Mendelian Randomization — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-10 (volume 45, issue 23-24)
- **Authors**: Wang K et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Derive valid summary-statistics estimators and variances for MR without assuming independence of GWAS effect estimates across instruments.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Theory plus extensive simulations; application to public GWAS summary statistics for body mass index and systolic blood pressure.
  - **Estimand / target**: Causal effect of exposure on outcome in two-sample MR with SNPs in linkage equilibrium.
  - **Identification**: Standard IV assumptions (relevance, independence, exclusion) with instrumental SNPs in linkage equilibrium; no independence assumed across marginal GWAS estimates.
  - **Estimator / model**: TSLS, two-sample TSLS and generalized method-of-moments from summary data; consistent alternative variance for IVW.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulation scenarios assessing Wald confidence interval coverage; real-data application.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: IVW is generally not equivalent to TSLS/TSTSLS; the conventional IVW standard error is inconsistent. Summary-statistics Wald intervals can be anti-conservative.
- **Strengths**: Corrects a widely repeated claim in the MR literature with derivations and simulations.
- **Limitations**: Requires instruments in linkage equilibrium; abstract reports no code availability.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Treat conventional IVW standard errors cautiously and consider the proposed consistent variance estimator in summary-data MR.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70784 / PMID:42893902

## 2. Evaluating Darbepoetin Treatment Strategies for Managing Anaemia in Haemodialysis Patients: Effects on All-Cause Mortality From a Target Trial Emulation Using Electronic Health Record Data — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Pharmacoepidemiology and Drug Safety
- **Pub date**: 2026-11 (volume 35, issue 11)
- **Authors**: Birnie K et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Estimate the effect of haemoglobin targets under protocolised darbepoetin dosing on mortality in haemodialysis patients.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Target trial emulation using UK Renal Registry EHR; 8628 haemodialysis patients, 10 centres, 2004-2016; 8-month follow-up.
  - **Estimand / target**: Per-protocol effect (hazard ratio) of sustained Hb target strategies on all-cause mortality.
  - **Identification**: Clone-censor-weight; conditional exchangeability given measured covariates, positivity; adherence to strategies drives censoring weights.
  - **Estimator / model**: Clone-censor-weight with inverse probability of censoring weights; hazard ratios for 105-125 and 125-145 versus 95-115 g/L.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Results compared with prior trial findings; low adherence to highest target noted as limiting precision.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: HR 0.92 (0.75-1.12) for 105-125 vs 95-115 g/L; HR 1.20 (0.94-1.54) for 125-145 vs 95-115 g/L.
- **Strengths**: Explicit protocol-based strategies emulated in routine data with clone-censor-weight handling of immortal time.
- **Limitations**: Low adherence to the highest target reduced precision; observational confounding possible.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Clone-censor-weight is a workable template for emulating dynamic treatment-target strategies in registry EHR data.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/pds.70507 / PMID:42893801

## 3. Loss of power by degree of heaping in covariables — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: BMC Medical Research Methodology
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-17
- **Authors**: Becher M et al.
- **Type**: simulation
- **Aims**: Quantify power loss from heaping (digit preference) in covariates.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulation study illustrated with a real dataset (not specified in abstract).
  - **Estimand / target**: Statistical power for covariate effects under varying degrees of heaping.
  - **Identification**: not reported
  - **Estimator / model**: Regression-based power comparison across heaping degrees (details not reported in abstract).
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Varying degree of heaping; real-data illustration.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Moderate heaping causes little power loss; heaping in broad categories causes major power loss.
- **Strengths**: Addresses a common but under-studied measurement problem.
- **Limitations**: Abstract gives few design details; focus is power rather than bias.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Avoid coarse categorical heaping when collecting covariates; moderate digit preference is likely tolerable.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1186/s12874-026-03043-4 / PMID:42892672

- **Pub date**: 2026-10-17
- **Authors**: Henderson HI et al.
- **Type**: review
- **Aims**: Characterise ICD-10 validation studies for eight common hospitalisation conditions and methodological factors limiting comparability.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Scoping review of PubMed and Embase 2005-2025; 116 primary publications via 11 systematic reviews and database search.
  - **Estimand / target**: Validity metrics (PPV, sensitivity, specificity) of ICD-10 case definitions.
  - **Identification**: not reported
  - **Estimator / model**: Descriptive summary of study characteristics, reference standards and performance metrics.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: not reported
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Over one-third reported only PPV; specificity generally high, sensitivity variable. Adding laboratory, procedure or medication data often improved performance.
- **Strengths**: Broad coverage across eight conditions highlighting design-driven incomparability.
- **Limitations**: Descriptive synthesis only; heterogeneity prevents pooled estimates.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Prefer validated algorithms reporting sensitivity and specificity, and consider linking lab or medication data to outcome definitions.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.jclinepi.2026.112570 / PMID:42892376
