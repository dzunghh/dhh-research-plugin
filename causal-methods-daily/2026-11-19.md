## 1. Loss to follow-up during tuberculosis treatment and 5-year mortality in Brazil: A target trial emulation — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: PLoS Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-18
- **Authors**: Lepka de Lima E et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Estimate excess 5-year mortality caused by loss to follow-up (LTFU) during tuberculosis treatment, and identify highest-risk groups.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Registry-linked cohort of 171,048 adults starting first TB treatment, São Paulo State 2013–2023; 12.2% LTFU.
  - **Estimand / target**: Standardized 5-year mortality difference: disengaging within 6 months versus remaining in care throughout.
  - **Identification**: Conditional exchangeability given listed covariates; clone-censor-weight handles immortal time; severity markers unmeasured.
  - **Estimator / model**: Clone-censor-weight target trial emulation (inverse probability of censoring weights), rolling landmark analysis for timing and cause-specific excess.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Rolling landmark analyses by disengagement timing; cause-specific mortality decomposition; residual confounding discussed, no quantitative bias analysis reported.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: LTFU raised standardized 5-year mortality by 2.49 percentage points (95% CI 1.94–3.17); 5.16 points (3.76–6.60) among people with HIV.
- **Strengths**: Handles immortal-time bias explicitly in a very large population-based cohort with cause-specific results.
- **Limitations**: No radiographic or bacillary-load severity data, so residual confounding cannot be excluded.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use clone-censor-weight emulation when exposure is defined by post-baseline behaviour such as disengagement from care.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1371/journal.pmed.1005303 / PMID:43050453

## 2. Using probabilistic bias analysis to adjust for selection bias in retrospective case-control studies — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: American Journal of Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-18
- **Authors**: Bashir H et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Adjust differential-participation selection bias by treating non-participant exposure prevalence as a missing-data problem.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulation study plus meta-analysis of passive smoking and breast cancer (retrospective case-control versus prospective cohort studies).
  - **Estimand / target**: Bias-adjusted summary odds ratio for exposure–disease association in retrospective case-control studies.
  - **Identification**: Participant exposure prevalence informs non-participant prevalence; defensible bounds on non-participant exposure prevalence assumed.
  - **Estimator / model**: Probabilistic bias analysis comparing uniform, triangular, trapezoidal and beta priors for non-participant prevalence; trapezoidal preferred.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulation across distributions; applied example benchmarked against prospective cohort summary estimate.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Summary OR moved from 1.23 (1.00–1.51) to 1.16 (0.99–1.35), nearer prospective RR 1.07 (0.92–1.24).
- **Strengths**: Gives a concrete, defensible prior choice and an empirical benchmark against prospective evidence.
- **Limitations**: Relies on subjective assumptions about non-participant exposure; code availability not reported.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Run trapezoidal-prior probabilistic bias analysis when case-control participation is differential, rather than ignoring selection.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/aje/kwag295 / PMID:43049044

## 3. Estimation of the length of receiver operating characteristic curve — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Statistical Methods in Medical Research
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-18
- **Authors**: Gang M et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Propose estimators of ROC curve length, a direction-invariant biomarker discrimination summary complementing AUC.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulations (proper and improper ROC settings) and two medical data set applications.
  - **Estimand / target**: ROC curve length, linked to distributional divergence and optimal discriminatory capacity.
  - **Identification**: Not a causal paper; Gaussian parametric model, plus nonparametric empirical survival probabilities.
  - **Estimator / model**: Piecewise linear estimator over a threshold grid; compared with kernel-density estimators using several bandwidth selectors.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Consistency and asymptotic normality derived; finite-sample simulation performance.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Length captures non-monotone biomarker structure where ROC crosses the diagonal and AUC may conceal information; numerical results not reported in abstract.
- **Strengths**: Addresses improper ROC curves with theory and nonparametric implementation.
- **Limitations**: Abstract reports no code and no quantitative performance figures.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Report ROC length alongside AUC when a biomarker may relate non-monotonically to disease.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1177/09622802261506997 / PMID:43050293

## 5. Similar time required to complete scoping and systematic reviews — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: Journal of Clinical Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-18
- **Authors**: Saywell I et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis (meta-research)
- **Aims**: Test whether scoping reviews are completed faster than systematic reviews.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Retrospective study of 236 JBI Evidence Synthesis reviews, 2018–2025, protocol-to-submission time.
  - **Estimand / target**: Association of review methodology with completion time, adjusted for authorship, funding and word count.
  - **Identification**: Covariate adjustment only; no causal framework; word count treated as mediator.
  - **Estimator / model**: Univariate and multivariable gamma GLMs with log link; mediation via review word count.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: not reported
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Median 1.64 years (scoping 1.57, systematic 1.73); methodology not significant after adjustment, student authorship and word count were.
- **Strengths**: Directly tests a common assumption with appropriate skewed-outcome modelling.
- **Limitations**: Single journal; observational with possible unmeasured confounding.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Choose review type by research question, not expected speed; budget time by anticipated report length.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.jclinepi.2026.112590 / PMID:43051498
