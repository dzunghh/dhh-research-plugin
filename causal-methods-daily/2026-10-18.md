## 2. A Path-Based Approach for Detecting and Assessing Inconsistency in Network Meta-Analysis — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-10
- **Authors**: Tahmasebi NR et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Detect and visualize inconsistency across all evidence paths in NMA without splitting direct versus indirect evidence.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Methods development; fictional and real-world network examples.
  - **Estimand / target**: Inconsistency between independent evidence paths for a treatment comparison.
  - **Identification**: Requires at least two independent evidence paths; consistency assumption assessed rather than assumed.
  - **Estimator / model**: Squared-difference inconsistency measure across paths; Netpath plot; extends frequentist graph-theoretical NMA.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Worked examples showing inconsistency masked when indirect sources are pooled.
- **Software**: netmeta R package
- **Key findings**: Detects path-level inconsistency hidden by pooled indirect evidence and works where direct evidence is absent.
- **Strengths**: Implemented in a widely used package; handles comparisons lacking direct evidence.
- **Limitations**: Validation limited to examples in the abstract; no simulation performance reported.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Run Netpath alongside node-splitting before trusting NMA rankings.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70752 / PMID:42881364

## 3. Locally Estimated Scatterplot Smoothing for Censored Survival Data — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-10
- **Authors**: van Klaveren D et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Extend LOESS to right-censored data for covariate–event-probability curves and calibration plots.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Complex coronary artery disease cohort example; simulations of 2000 samples of 1800 patients.
  - **Estimand / target**: Probability of event within a fixed time horizon (10-year mortality) as a function of a continuous covariate.
  - **Identification**: Censoring independent of event time given covariates (inverse-probability-of-censoring weighting).
  - **Estimator / model**: LOESS on IPC-weighted event indicators; Kaplan-Meier or Cox-spline censoring weights.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulations in four prognostic-validation scenarios; compared with grouped Kaplan-Meier.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Bias limited and coverage close to nominal; covariate-dependent weights helped when censoring varied by covariate.
- **Strengths**: Directly useful for calibration curves; authors include Harrell and Van Calster.
- **Limitations**: Relies on correctly specified censoring model; code availability not reported.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use covariate-dependent IPC weights for calibration plots whenever censoring differs across risk strata.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70783 / PMID:42888919

## 4. A Max 0-1 Weighted Log-Rank Test for Time-to-Event Data With Random Delayed Treatment Effect — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-10
- **Authors**: Liu J et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Provide a more powerful test for immunotherapy trials with random delayed treatment onset.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulations plus NSCLC trial data (OAK, POPLAR; NCT01903993, NCT02008227).
  - **Estimand / target**: Difference in survival distributions under delayed, random-onset treatment effect (hypothesis test).
  - **Identification**: Randomized trial; non-proportional hazards with treatment effect starting after unknown delay.
  - **Estimator / model**: Maximum over a set of 0-1 weighted log-rank statistics; permutation algorithm for inference.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Extensive simulation of empirical performance; theoretical properties established.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Authors report improved power over the standard log-rank test under delayed effects; numbers not reported in abstract.
- **Strengths**: Robust to unknown delay time without prespecifying one weight.
- **Limitations**: Test only; no effect-size estimand like restricted mean survival time.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Consider as a prespecified alternative when non-proportional hazards from delayed onset are expected.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70773 / PMID:42888494

## 5. Semantic Similarity-Informed Bayesian Borrowing for Quantitative Signal Detection of Adverse Events — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-10
- **Authors**: Haguinet F et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Improve pharmacovigilance signal detection by borrowing information across semantically similar MedDRA terms.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: FDA Adverse Event Reporting System with a time-stamped reference set.
  - **Estimand / target**: Information component (disproportionality) for drug–event pairs.
  - **Identification**: Not causal; disproportionality signal only, subject to reporting biases.
  - **Estimator / model**: Bayesian dynamic borrowing with robust meta-analytic predictive prior weighted by semantic similarity measures (IC SSM).
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Benchmarked against traditional IC and MedDRA HLGT borrowing; alerting quantile varied (2.5%, 1%).
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Detected true signals over five months earlier; sensitivity 57.0% versus 50.1%, precision 14.5% versus 16.1%.
- **Strengths**: Continuous similarity-based borrowing avoids rigid hierarchy limits; evaluated on a reference set.
- **Limitations**: Precision slightly lower than traditional IC at the standard threshold.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Useful for exploratory screening of sparse adverse-event signals, not confirmatory causal evidence.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70757 / PMID:42885177
