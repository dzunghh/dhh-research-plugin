## 1. A Doubly Robust Approach to Evaluating the Impact of Hospital Length of Stay on Post-Fracture Recovery in Older Adults With Cognitive Impairment — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-10
- **Authors**: Ren H et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Develop a doubly robust estimator for continuous exposures that also handles missing outcomes, applied to length of stay and recovery.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulations plus real-world data on older adults with Alzheimer's disease and related dementias after hip fracture; sample size not reported.
  - **Estimand / target**: Dose-response effect of continuous hospital length of stay on days at home (DAH).
  - **Identification**: Baseline confounding adjustment (conditional exchangeability, positivity), outcome missing at random.
  - **Estimator / model**: Doubly robust estimator for continuous exposure with conditional-density (generalized propensity) and missingness models.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Diagnostics for conditional density and missingness models; sensitivity analyses for assumption violations; extensive simulations.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Outperformed existing doubly robust continuous-exposure methods in simulations with outcome MAR. Stays beyond 20 days were associated with substantially fewer DAH.
- **Strengths**: Handles continuous exposure and missing outcome jointly, with practical diagnostics and sensitivity analyses.
- **Limitations**: Relies on MAR and no unmeasured confounding; code availability not reported in abstract.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use when estimating dose-response effects of continuous exposures with incomplete outcomes in observational health data.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70768 / PMID:42881137

## 2. Impact of COVID-19 Restrictions on Estimating Causal Effects: Fluoroquinolones and Tendon Rupture — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Pharmacoepidemiology and Drug Safety
- **Pub date**: 2026-11
- **Authors**: Wong AYS et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Assess whether pandemic healthcare disruption altered causal effect estimates in real-world data, using a replication case study.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Active-comparator cohort in CPRD Aurum, 2006–2023; 891,968 fluoroquinolone and 895,229 cephalosporin initiators.
  - **Estimand / target**: Hazard ratio for tendon rupture, fluoroquinolone versus cephalosporin, by pandemic period.
  - **Identification**: Confounding adjustment via propensity score weighting; four exposure/outcome ascertainment-period schemes.
  - **Estimator / model**: Propensity-score-weighted Cox regression with period-by-treatment interaction.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Comparison across pre-, during-, and post-pandemic windows; baseline characteristic and incidence drift checks.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Pre-pandemic PSW-HR 1.94 (1.46–2.59); all data 1.91 (1.46–2.51). Interaction HR 0.80 (0.35–1.84), low power.
- **Strengths**: Large cohort and explicit comparison of ascertainment periods addresses a timely threat to real-world evidence.
- **Limitations**: Few post-pandemic events, so power to detect temporal differences is limited.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Routinely examine temporal shifts in covariates and outcome incidence when RWD spans the pandemic.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/pds.70504 / PMID:42880886

## 3. Flipping death-to-survival event coding shifted pooled risk ratios and GRADE imprecision ratings in 2,071 Cochrane meta-analyses — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Journal of Clinical Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-15
- **Authors**: Tsujimoto Y et al.
- **Type**: simulation
- **Aims**: Quantify how recoding death versus survival as the event changes pooled risk ratios and GRADE imprecision.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Meta-epidemiological study of 2,071 Cochrane meta-analyses with death or survival outcomes.
  - **Estimand / target**: Ratio of risk ratios, original versus flipped coding; changes in significance and GRADE rating.
  - **Identification**: Not applicable (non-causal methodological comparison).
  - **Estimator / model**: Random-effects Mantel-Haenszel pooling of risk ratios and odds ratios on original and flipped 2x2 data.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Subgroups by sample size, control event rate, and single/double-zero studies; odds-ratio comparison.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Median ratio of risk ratios 1.17 (IQR 1.05–1.42); significance changed in 9.6%, GRADE imprecision in 58%.
- **Strengths**: Large empirical sample with clear practical consequence for systematic reviewers.
- **Limitations**: Uses reported 2x2 data only; quantifies discrepancies rather than proposing a new estimator.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Prefer odds ratios when primary studies code death and survival inconsistently, especially in sparse data.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.jclinepi.2026.112553 / PMID:42880678

## 4. A critical review of clinical prediction models in oncology: is this SEERious research? — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: Journal of Clinical Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-15
- **Authors**: Tsegaye B et al.
- **Type**: review
- **Aims**: Characterise SEER-based prediction model studies and assess the extent of formulaic research practices.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Systematic review of 436 SEER prediction model studies published January–August 2023.
  - **Estimand / target**: Prevalence of reporting, validation, and rationale practices; not a causal estimand.
  - **Identification**: Not applicable (descriptive meta-research).
  - **Estimator / model**: Structured data extraction; descriptive proportions of modelling approach, reporting guideline adherence, external validation.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Expanded search showed over 4,000 studies in ten years, output rising from 37 (2016) to over 700 (2023).
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Reporting guideline adherence was 13%; 87% did not discuss SEER suitability; 94% had China-affiliated first authors.
- **Strengths**: Quantifies research waste with a transparent, extensive extraction.
- **Limitations**: Sample limited to eight months of 2023 for detailed extraction.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Require a defined target population, intended use, and TRIPOD-style reporting for registry-based prediction models.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.jclinepi.2026.112566 / PMID:42880677

## 5. Reporting of Financial Relationships in Primary Studies Included in Systematic Reviews of Interventions for Common Diagnoses — Score 2/6

- **Journal**: Journal of Clinical Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-15
- **Authors**: Czajkowski M et al.
- **Type**: review
- **Aims**: Examine how completely systematic reviews report financial disclosures of included primary studies.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Cross-sectional methodological study of 300 RCTs in 17 low-risk-of-bias systematic reviews.
  - **Estimand / target**: Proportion of RCT financial relationships reported in systematic reviews.
  - **Identification**: Not applicable (descriptive meta-research).
  - **Estimator / model**: Descriptive comparison of extracted disclosures versus review reporting, stratified by review author COI status.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Stratification by systematic review author disclosure status.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: 69% of RCTs declared industry relationships versus 38% reported in reviews; no COI reported at all.
- **Strengths**: Direct trial-to-review comparison of disclosures.
- **Limitations**: Small sample of 17 reviews across six conditions.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Extract and report funding and COI of included trials when conducting systematic reviews.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.jclinepi.2026.112565 / PMID:42880676
