## 1. Dynamic Prediction in Mixture Cure Models: A Model-Based Landmarking Approach — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-10 (day not reported)
- **Authors**: Cipriani M et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Replace last-observation-carried-forward landmarking in mixture cure models with model-based summaries of longitudinal covariates for dynamic survival prediction.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulation across cure fractions, sample sizes and longitudinal structures; illustrated on renal transplant patients (N not reported).
  - **Estimand / target**: Dynamic (landmark-conditional) survival probability and cure probability given updated longitudinal history; not causal.
  - **Identification**: Prediction only; assumes correctly specified longitudinal mixed model and Cox cure model (two-stage, sequential).
  - **Estimator / model**: (Generalized) linear mixed models give individual-specific predicted summaries, entered as covariates in a Cox proportional hazards cure model.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulation including misspecification favouring LOCF; real-data illustration.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Model-based landmarking gave more refined predictions than LOCF, even when the model was misspecified in LOCF's favour.
- **Strengths**: Corrects measurement error and uses full longitudinal trajectory, with robustness check under misspecification.
- **Limitations**: Sequential two-stage fitting ignores uncertainty from stage one; no causal interpretation.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use when long-term survivors exist and biomarkers are sparsely or irregularly measured; prefer over LOCF.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70774 / PMID:42894317

## 2. Appraisal of Recent Intervention-Focused Umbrella Reviews Reveals Major Methodological Weaknesses: A Meta-Epidemiological Study — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Journal of Clinical Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-19
- **Authors**: Posadzki P et al.
- **Type**: review
- **Aims**: Evaluate methodological quality and PRIOR adherence of recent intervention-focused umbrella reviews.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Meta-epidemiological study; random sample of 100 umbrella reviews (Jan 2024–Jan 2026) from PubMed, Cochrane, Epistemonikos.
  - **Estimand / target**: Proportions of reviews meeting methodological domains (overlap, inherited bias, selective reporting, protocol registration).
  - **Identification**: Descriptive; not causal.
  - **Estimator / model**: Descriptive frequencies against PRIOR, Cochrane Handbook and JBI guidance.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: not reported
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Only 21% adhered to PRIOR; overlap considered in synthesis in 11%; 71% emphasised significant results; 19% had no protocol.
- **Strengths**: Random sample with multi-domain appraisal; senior methodologist author group.
- **Limitations**: Sample of 100 reviews; assessment tools only partially fit for umbrella reviews.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Pre-register, quantify primary-study overlap and handle superseded reviews when conducting umbrella reviews.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.jclinepi.2026.112569 / PMID:42896684

## 3. Identification of Risk-Related Prenatal Medication Exposure Profiles for Childhood Asthma Using the Bayesian Profile Regression: A Cohort Study Using the POMME Cohort — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: Pharmacoepidemiology and Drug Safety
- **Pub date**: 2026-11 (day not reported)
- **Authors**: Russo S et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Identify prenatal medication-exposure profiles associated with childhood asthma using a semi-supervised clustering approach.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: POMME population cohort, 18,851 children born 2010 and 2015 in Haute-Garonne, France; followed to 2023 via dispensing data.
  - **Estimand / target**: Profile-specific asthma risk relative to overall population risk (15.2%); associational signal detection.
  - **Identification**: No causal identification; exploratory, with residual confounding by indication acknowledged as plausible.
  - **Estimator / model**: Bayesian profile regression jointly clustering trimester-specific ATC-class exposures and asthma outcome (17 profiles).
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: not reported
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Six of 17 profiles had above-average asthma risk, marked by repeated exposure to acid-related, thyroid, antibiotic and asthma medications.
- **Strengths**: Handles high-dimensional exposure combinations and timing windows simultaneously.
- **Limitations**: Outcome is algorithm-defined from dispensing; exploratory, confounding by maternal conditions.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use as hypothesis-generating signal detection before targeted causal analyses such as target trial emulation.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/pds.70509 / PMID:42896866

## 4. Evaluating Prompting Strategies and Large Language Models in Systematic Literature Review Screening: Relevance and Task-Stage Classification — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: Research Synthesis Methods
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-19
- **Authors**: Han B et al.
- **Type**: simulation
- **Aims**: Quantify how prompting strategies interact with LLMs when automating systematic-review screening.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Benchmark of six LLMs under five prompt types on an SLR-automation corpus (size not reported).
  - **Estimand / target**: Accuracy, precision, recall and F1 for relevance and six Level-2 task classifications; plus cost per 1,000 abstracts.
  - **Identification**: Not applicable (diagnostic accuracy benchmark).
  - **Estimator / model**: Zero-shot, few-shot, chain-of-thought, CoT-few-shot and self-reflection prompts across GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, DeepSeek, Gemini, Claude Haiku, Llama.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Cost-performance analysis; authors note further validation needed beyond this corpus.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Zero-shot maximised recall; CoT-few-shot gave best precision/F1; self-reflection underperformed; GPT-4o-mini was competitive at low cost.
- **Strengths**: Systematic model-by-prompt grid with explicit cost analysis.
- **Limitations**: Single corpus; generalisability to other review topics unproven.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Consider staged screening: cheap high-recall zero-shot first, precision-oriented prompts for uncertain records.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1017/rsm.2026.10110 / PMID:42895556

## 5. Health risks of alcohol consumption in Aotearoa New Zealand: modelling to inform low-risk-drinking guidelines — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: International Journal of Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-14
- **Authors**: Mizdrak A et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Estimate alcohol-attributable mortality across 0–100 g/day intake to inform low-risk-drinking guidelines.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: New Zealand Alcohol Intervention Model, multi-state life table of a 12-year-old cohort, exposure and disease rates fixed at 2018 levels.
  - **Estimand / target**: Years of life lost at 1-in-100 and 1-in-1000 lifetime-risk thresholds, using 17.5 YLL as proxy for alcohol-related death.
  - **Identification**: Assumes causal relative-risk functions from WHO and GBD RR libraries apply to Aotearoa.
  - **Estimator / model**: Life-table microsimulation with exposure-dependent RR libraries; uncertainty intervals; ethnicity-specific results.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Alternative RR libraries (WHO vs GBD) and removal of one-star-evidence conditions.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: WHO library: 1-in-100 risk at 8.6 (males) and 9.2 (females) standard drinks/week; dropping one-star conditions cut this to 2.7 and 3.5.
- **Strengths**: Transparent sensitivity to RR library choice and evidence grade; ethnicity-specific estimates.
- **Limitations**: Depends on external RR libraries and steady-state 2018 rates; no model code reported.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Report threshold results under multiple RR sources, since guideline conclusions are highly sensitive to evidence-grade inclusion.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/ije/dyag191 / PMID:42893797
