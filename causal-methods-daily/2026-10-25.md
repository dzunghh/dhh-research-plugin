## 1. Which Small-Sample Correction Should be Used When Analyzing Stepped-Wedge Designs With Time-Varying Treatment Effects? — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-11 (issue 45(25-27); EDAT Oct 2026)
- **Authors**: Ouyang Y et al.
- **Type**: simulation
- **Aims**: Compare robust and bias-corrected sandwich variance estimators for exposure-time-indicator models in stepped-wedge cluster randomized trials.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulation studies, continuous and binary outcomes, correctly specified and misspecified random-effects structures, including one-cluster-per-sequence designs.
  - **Estimand / target**: Time-averaged treatment effect (TATE) and long-term effect (LTE) under time-varying treatment effects.
  - **Identification**: Randomization of cluster crossover timing; ETI models relax constant-effect assumption of immediate-treatment models.
  - **Estimator / model**: GLMM exposure-time-indicator models; classic sandwich, Kauermann-Carroll, Mancl-DeRouen, Morel-Bokossa-Neerchal (plus Fay-Graubard sub-study) variance corrections.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Coverage under misspecified random effects; t-distribution degrees-of-freedom choices (clusters minus two).
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Model-based SEs undercover under misspecification; MD-t best for continuous, MBN only reliable option for binary outcomes. LTE inference unstable throughout.
- **Strengths**: Systematic head-to-head comparison giving concrete, outcome-type-specific recommendations.
- **Limitations**: Simulation-based; LTE instability left unresolved and MD fragile with sparse designs.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use RVEs with small-sample correction in ETI SW-CRTs (MD-t for continuous, MBN for binary) and treat LTE estimates cautiously.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70782 / PMID:42910555

## 2. Assessing Multitreatment Effects for Discrete Observational Data — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Biometrical Journal
- **Pub date**: 2026-12 (issue 68(6); EDAT Oct 2026)
- **Authors**: Cugnata F et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Test for existence of treatment effects across multiple treatments in discrete observational data using a divergence-based statistic.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Theory, numerical simulations, and a clinical application on real-world observational data; sample details not reported.
  - **Estimand / target**: Existence and ordering of treatment-level effects across multiple treatments (stochastic dominance).
  - **Identification**: Observational comparison with Bayesian-network-based modelling of discrete variables; specific exchangeability conditions not reported.
  - **Estimator / model**: Divergence-measure test statistic with derived asymptotics; stochastic dominance tests for effect-ordering patterns.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulations of size and power; clinical application.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Simulations show good significance level and power; application indicates improved multitreatment evaluation. No numeric results reported in abstract.
- **Strengths**: Addresses multi-arm observational comparison with ordering-aware tests.
- **Limitations**: Restricted to discrete data; causal identification assumptions unclear from abstract.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Consider when comparing several categorical treatments in observational data and testing for ordered effects.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/bimj.70194 / PMID:42908970

## 3. Sleep health and uterine leiomyomata incidence and growth: a prospective ultrasound study — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: American Journal of Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-21
- **Authors**: Coleman CM et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Estimate association of self-reported sleep health with ultrasound-detected fibroid incidence and growth.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: SELF prospective cohort of Black women aged 23-35, Detroit; five visits 2010-2020; N=1,137, 351 incident cases.
  - **Estimand / target**: Hazard ratios for sleep duration, trouble sleeping and sleep quality on incident leiomyomata; growth outcomes.
  - **Identification**: Conditional exchangeability given time-varying confounders via inverse probability weighting.
  - **Estimator / model**: Cox proportional hazards with inverse probability weights for time-dependent confounding.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: not reported
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Short sleep HR 0.30 (0.17-0.55); trouble sleeping ≥15 days/month HR 1.96 (0.76-5.09). No association with growth.
- **Strengths**: Ultrasound-based outcome ascertainment at each visit avoids reliance on clinical diagnosis.
- **Limitations**: Self-reported sleep, imprecise estimates, and inconsistent direction suggesting residual bias.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Illustrates IPW for time-varying confounding in repeated-exposure cohorts; interpret counterintuitive protective estimates cautiously.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/aje/kwag259 / PMID:42905965

## 4. Choosing an Appropriate Outlier-Detection Method When Comparing Institutional Performances in Clinical Audits — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Biometrical Journal
- **Pub date**: 2026-12 (issue 68(6); EDAT Oct 2026)
- **Authors**: Sui A et al.
- **Type**: simulation
- **Aims**: Explain why Common Mean Model and random-effects logistic regression flag different institutions as outliers.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulation study across scenarios of healthcare-unit performance; applied clinical audit context.
  - **Estimand / target**: Identification of institutions deviating from expected performance (funnel-plot outlier classification).
  - **Identification**: not applicable; non-causal. Normality of between-unit effects drives method agreement.
  - **Estimator / model**: Common Mean Model versus random effects logistic regression.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Assumption-violation scenarios; QQ plots and Lin's concordance correlation coefficient as diagnostics.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Discrepancies are driven mainly by violation of each method's normality assumption.
- **Strengths**: Gives practical diagnostic recommendation for method choice.
- **Limitations**: Simulation-only; scope limited to binary audit outcomes.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Check normality assumptions with QQ plots and CCC before choosing CMM versus RELR for audit funnel plots.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/bimj.70196 / PMID:42908499

## 5. Partially Linear Additive Reversed Mean Model for Panel Count Data With Informative Terminal Event — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-11 (issue 45(25-27); EDAT Oct 2026)
- **Authors**: Feng A et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Model recurrent-event panel count data when an informative terminal event is present.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulations plus application to the Chinese Longitudinal Healthy Longevity Survey.
  - **Estimand / target**: Covariate effects on the reversed mean function of recurrent events, time-anchored at the terminal event.
  - **Identification**: Reversed time origin handles informative terminal event; semiparametric assumptions on link function.
  - **Estimator / model**: Partially linear additive reversed mean model; B-spline sieve; two-stage estimation with terminal-event distribution as nuisance.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Finite-sample simulations; asymptotic consistency, rate and normality established.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Estimator performs well in finite samples; numerical results not reported in abstract.
- **Strengths**: Flexible covariate effects with rigorous asymptotic theory.
- **Limitations**: Not causal; no code availability reported.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Useful for panel-observed recurrent outcomes (e.g. falls, hospitalizations) truncated by death.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70793 / PMID:42910583
