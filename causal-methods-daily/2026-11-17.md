## 1. A quantitative approach to evidence triangulation: development of a framework to address rigour and relevance — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: European Journal of Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-16
- **Authors**: Shapland CY et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Develop a framework that quantitatively combines evidence from different study designs into one causal effect estimate, rather than qualitatively checking agreement.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Framework paper with worked example: 3 RCTs, 1 Mendelian randomization study and 7 multivariable regression studies of beta-carotene and CHD/CVD.
  - **Estimand / target**: Effect defined by an explicit overall target causal question; each study-specific question is mapped to it (relevance). Illustrated as a risk ratio.
  - **Identification**: Design-specific assumptions (randomization, IV, no unmeasured confounding) are not assumed to hold; direction and magnitude of each study's bias are assessed (rigour).
  - **Estimator / model**: Bias-adjusted meta-analysis pooling RCT, MR and MVR estimates after relevance and rigour adjustments.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Comparison of unadjusted design-specific estimates with the triangulated estimate; repeated for CVD outcome with similar findings.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Unadjusted: RCTs RR 1.00 (0.98–1.01), MR RR 1.02 (0.98–1.07), MVR RR 0.83 (0.76–0.91). After the framework, designs agreed: RR 0.99 (0.82–1.21) for CHD.
- **Strengths**: Makes triangulation explicit and quantitative, forcing definition of the target question and of bias direction and magnitude per study.
- **Limitations**: Bias magnitudes and directions must be specified by the analyst; abstract reports a single illustrative example and no code.
- **Takeaway for practice**: When synthesising RCT, MR and observational evidence, define the target question first, then pool with explicit bias adjustment rather than vote-counting agreement.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1007/s10654-026-01484-1 / PMID:43037922

## 2. Non differential misclassification does not mean equal predictive values — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: European Journal of Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-16
- **Authors**: Fox MP et al.
- **Type**: tutorial/guidance
- **Aims**: Clarify how sensitivity/specificity relate to predictive values and what that implies for quantitative bias analysis of outcome misclassification.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Conceptual/didactic paper with a worked numerical example; no empirical cohort reported.
  - **Estimand / target**: Misclassification-adjusted exposure–outcome effect estimate.
  - **Identification**: Non-differential misclassification means equal sensitivity and specificity across exposure groups; PPV/NPV also depend on true outcome prevalence, so they differ by exposure stratum.
  - **Estimator / model**: Predictive-value-based quantitative bias analysis, contrasting a single unstratified predictive value against exposure-stratified predictive values.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Worked example comparing the two approaches; discussion of validation-study design limits, prevalence dependence and transportability of predictive values.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: A single unstratified predictive value gives biased adjusted estimates and can imply differential misclassification when none exists. Exposure-stratified predictive values give unbiased adjusted estimates.
- **Strengths**: Corrects a common, easily made QBA error with a transparent worked example.
- **Limitations**: Worked example only; obtaining exposure-stratified, transportable predictive values from validation studies remains practically difficult.
- **Takeaway for practice**: In QBA, estimate and apply PPV/NPV within exposure strata at minimum; never reuse one overall predictive value even under non-differential misclassification.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1007/s10654-026-01482-3 / PMID:43037921

## 3. Robust estimation of average treatment effects with binary outcomes subject to both missingness and misclassification — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Biometrics
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-07
- **Authors**: Weng Y et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Propose ATE estimators that remain valid when a binary outcome is both partly missing and misclassified.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Methodological paper with theoretical justification and numerical studies; data sources and sample sizes not reported in abstract.
  - **Estimand / target**: Average treatment effect (ATE) for a binary outcome.
  - **Identification**: Standard causal assumptions plus additional models for the missingness and misclassification processes; specifics not reported in abstract.
  - **Estimator / model**: A class of consistent estimators correcting for missingness and misclassification, robust to several model-misspecification scenarios.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Validity justified theoretically and numerically; scenario details not reported in abstract.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Naively applying existing ATE methods under outcome missingness and misclassification yields seriously biased results. Proposed estimators are consistent and robust to various misspecifications; numbers not reported in abstract.
- **Strengths**: Handles two outcome-data defects jointly, with robustness to misspecification of the extra nuisance models.
- **Limitations**: Restricted to binary outcomes; abstract gives no quantitative performance figures or software.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Consider when outcomes come from error-prone sources (e.g. records or self-report) with incomplete ascertainment and standard IPW/AIPW would be biased.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/biomtc/ujag176 / PMID:43037431

## 4. A new targeted-federated learning framework for estimating heterogeneity of treatment effects: a robust framework with applications in aging cohorts — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Biostatistics
- **Pub date**: 2026-01-20
- **Authors**: Zhao R et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Estimate treatment-effect heterogeneity for a target population by integrating multiple data sources without sharing individual-level data.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Methodological paper; extensive simulations plus application to nationwide Medicare-linked data from an aging cohort; N not reported in abstract.
  - **Estimand / target**: Effect modification in a target population, parameterised through a working structural model; supports distinct outcome types.
  - **Identification**: Standard causal assumptions plus transportability across sources, accounting for covariate distribution shift from the target population.
  - **Estimator / model**: Doubly robust targeted-federated estimator aggregating site-level information without sharing raw data.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Communication-efficient bootstrap-based selection algorithm detects non-transportable sources; simulations compare bias and efficiency against other methods.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Simulations show lower bias and efficiency gains relative to competing methods; numbers not reported in abstract.
- **Strengths**: Moves federated causal inference beyond average effects to effect modification, with double robustness and screening of non-transportable sites.
- **Limitations**: Relies on a working structural model for heterogeneity; abstract reports no quantitative results or software.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Useful for multi-site or distributed-network studies of effect modification where privacy rules prevent pooling individual-level data.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/biostatistics/kxag043 / PMID:43036002

## 5. Target trial emulation of effectiveness of COVID-19 mRNA vaccination during pregnancy in four European countries — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: Nature Communications
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-16
- **Authors**: Mercadé-Besora N et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Estimate real-world effectiveness of primary and first-booster mRNA COVID-19 vaccination during pregnancy against infection, hospitalisation and ICU admission.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Two emulated target trials using health data from the UK, Sweden, Spain and Norway; 62,061 (primary) and 41,096 (booster) pregnant women.
  - **Estimand / target**: Vaccine effectiveness of initiating primary vaccination versus unvaccinated, and first booster versus unboosted, against infection and hospitalisation.
  - **Identification**: Target trial emulation with weekly matching of vaccinated to comparable unexposed pregnant women; relies on conditional exchangeability given matching factors.
  - **Estimator / model**: Weekly matched cohort analysis per database, then meta-analysis across the four databases; specific outcome model not reported in abstract.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Not reported in abstract; heterogeneity across databases noted for infection outcomes.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Primary VE 26.2% (95% CI 16.2–35.1) against infection, 55.6% (44.1–64.8) against hospitalisation. Booster VE 33.6% (1.6–56.6) and 34.1% (0.6–56.4); ICU outcomes too rare.
- **Strengths**: Multinational design with explicit target trial specification and sequential weekly matching, which aligns time zero during pregnancy.
- **Limitations**: Wide booster confidence intervals, heterogeneous infection estimates across databases, and possible residual confounding from the observational design.
- **Takeaway for practice**: A template for multi-database target trial emulation with time-varying eligibility in pregnancy; pair it with negative-control outcomes to probe residual confounding.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41467-026-78469-x / PMID:43035513
