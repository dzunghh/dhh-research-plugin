## 1. Dynamic risk assessment by landmark modeling of the restricted residual mean survival time — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Lifetime Data Analysis
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-20
- **Authors**: Zhang Y et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Directly model restricted residual mean survival time at landmark dates, accommodating dependent censoring and non-proportional hazards.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulation studies plus application to end-stage liver disease natural history (no transplantation); cohort size not reported.
  - **Estimand / target**: Restricted residual mean survival time (RRMST) at each landmark time, conditional on time-varying covariate history.
  - **Identification**: Dependent censoring handled via covariate-dependent censoring model; no reliance on proportional hazards or independent censoring.
  - **Estimator / model**: Landmark RRMST regression estimating equations with censoring-weighting; asymptotic properties derived.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Extensive finite-sample simulations; real-data application to liver disease.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Estimators are asymptotically valid and perform well in finite samples; numeric results not reported in abstract.
- **Strengths**: Removes need for hazard model correctness at every landmark and gives interpretable area-under-curve metric.
- **Limitations**: Relies on correct specification of censoring model; code availability unclear.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use for dynamic prognosis when hazards are non-proportional or censoring is informative, as in transplant waitlist settings.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1007/s10985-026-09731-z / PMID:42900447

## 2. R-estimation with right-censored data — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Lifetime Data Analysis
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-20
- **Authors**: Satten GA et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Generalize rank-based R-estimators to linear models with right-censored outcomes.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Theoretical development; data application not reported.
  - **Estimand / target**: Regression coefficients of semiparametric accelerated failure time (linear) model.
  - **Identification**: Standard independent right-censoring; rank-based estimation avoids parametric residual distribution assumption.
  - **Estimator / model**: Wilcoxon and bounded nonlinear score R-estimators using self-consistent residual distribution and mid-cumulative distribution; Ritov/Tsiatis representations.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Asymptotics and variance estimation derived analytically; simulations not reported.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: R-estimator is exactly a member of Ritov and Tsiatis estimating-equation classes, yielding asymptotics and variance estimates directly.
- **Strengths**: Unifies rank estimation and weighted log-rank estimating equations for censored AFT models.
- **Limitations**: Theoretical focus; no reported software or empirical performance in abstract.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Consider as a robust, rank-based alternative for AFT modeling when outlier or residual-distribution misspecification is a concern.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1007/s10985-026-09733-x / PMID:42900297

## 3. Statistical modeling and inference for Jack's Alzheimer's disease biomarker cascade with practical and computational considerations — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Biostatistics
- **Pub date**: 2026-01-20
- **Authors**: Tang Z et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Provide an implementable statistical model for Jack's multi-biomarker trajectory hypothesis using sparse, noisy longitudinal data.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulations mimicking real data; applied to BIOCARD and ADNI longitudinal cohorts (imaging, CSF, cognitive biomarkers).
  - **Estimand / target**: Sigmoidal biomarker trajectories and relative timing across disease progression, with individual time shifts.
  - **Identification**: Latent disease-time variable; model-based assumptions on S-shaped curves and correlation structure; not causal.
  - **Estimator / model**: Multivariate sparse nonlinear latent variable model; penalized likelihood; Gaussian Quadrature Newton-Raphson algorithm; partial-trajectory handling.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Finite-sample simulations; two real cohorts.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Penalization stabilizes boundary estimates; method estimates trajectories across imaging, CSF and cognitive domains. Numeric results not reported.
- **Strengths**: Addresses practical computational problems (boundary estimates, incomplete trajectories) often ignored in disease-progression models.
- **Limitations**: Descriptive trajectory model, not causal; software availability not reported.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Useful template for disease-progression modeling with sparse biomarker data, with caution about identifiability.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/biostatistics/kxag038 / PMID:42901735

## 4. Forecasting risk-factor distributions by sex and age, 1990-2050: a parametric estimation framework using GBD summary exposure values, demonstrated for Australia — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: International Journal of Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-14
- **Authors**: Dhungel B et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Convert GBD summary exposure value forecasts into business-as-usual risk-factor distributions for use in preventive intervention modelling.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: GBD SEV forecasts for Australia by sex, age, year; compared with 2017/18 National Health Survey.
  - **Estimand / target**: Age- and sex-specific parametric exposure distributions for BMI, SBP, LDL cholesterol, fasting glucose to 2050.
  - **Identification**: Assumed coefficient of variation and parametric family (log-normal, gamma) consistent with relative-risk-weighted SEV definition.
  - **Estimator / model**: Parametric distribution fitting by inverting SEV formula given 99th global percentile and theoretical minimum risk exposure.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Survey comparison; sensitivity to 99th global percentile reported.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Glucose and LDL agreed in younger ages; SBP overestimated by ~3-10 mmHg and BMI by ~1 kg/m2 versus survey.
- **Strengths**: Extensible to any country and directly feeds comparative risk assessment and simulation models.
- **Limitations**: Overestimation versus survey and strong sensitivity to the global 99th percentile and CV assumption.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use to build baseline exposure inputs for modelling, but validate against national surveys first.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/ije/dyag223 / PMID:42900799
