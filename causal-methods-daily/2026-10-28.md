## 1. School Phone Bans and Adolescent Mental Health in Iceland — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Network Open
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-01
- **Authors**: Bonilla Aparicio E et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Test whether schoolwide mobile phone bans changed adolescent mental health and related social and behavioral outcomes.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Repeated cross-sectional Youth in Iceland surveys (2016, 2018, 2023), 70 schools, 23,938 adolescents aged 13–15; 22 schools adopted bans 2018–2023.
  - **Estimand / target**: Average effect of school ban on depressive symptoms, anxiety, hostility, well-being (DiD coefficient, ATT-type).
  - **Identification**: Parallel trends between ban and non-ban schools; no concurrent school-level shocks.
  - **Estimator / model**: Difference-in-differences regression across three survey waves, with gender-stratified analyses.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Alternative implementation timing and stricter phone-restriction definitions; results consistent.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Bans not associated with meaningful change; depressive symptoms β 0.65 (95% CI 0.01–1.30), anxiety β 0.21 (−0.08 to 0.50).
- **Strengths**: Nationwide multi-wave data with policy variation across many schools.
- **Limitations**: Only one pre-period contrast documented in abstract; school-level ban adoption not randomized; self-reported outcomes.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use staggered/ban-versus-control DiD with timing and intensity sensitivity checks when evaluating school policies with repeated cross-sections.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.40893 / PMID:42944682

## 2. Semaglutide and Panic Attacks: Active-Comparator Disproportionality Analysis, Temporal Assessment of Notoriety Bias and Evidence Triangulation in Post-Marketing Pharmacovigilance — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Pharmacoepidemiology and Drug Safety
- **Pub date**: 2026-11
- **Authors**: Melamed B et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Quantify how confounding by indication and notoriety bias shape a semaglutide–panic attack signal in FAERS.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: FAERS 2018Q1–2024Q4 reports for semaglutide (33,267), dulaglutide (54,282), empagliflozin (25,380); plus systematic review of 12 studies.
  - **Estimand / target**: Reporting odds ratio (ROR) for panic attack versus active comparators; not an incidence or causal effect.
  - **Identification**: Active-comparator restriction, adjustment for age, sex, indication; pre-warning period restriction to isolate notoriety bias.
  - **Estimator / model**: Logistic regression crude/adjusted RORs; drug-by-period interaction test; evidence triangulation with trials and cohorts.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Temporal restriction before July 2023; all-other-drugs reference set sensitivity analysis.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Crude ROR ~5.1 attenuated to aROR 2.8 (1.74–4.56); pre-warning aROR 1.38 (0.69–2.75); reports per 10,000 rose 6.7→42.0.
- **Strengths**: Explicit temporal notoriety-bias test combined with triangulation against randomized evidence.
- **Limitations**: Spontaneous reports cannot give incidence; residual confounding by indication remains.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Pair active-comparator disproportionality with pre/post-warning stratification before interpreting pharmacovigilance signals.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/pds.70517 / PMID:42946127

## 3. Bayesian multiple imputation for functional data — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Biometrics
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-07
- **Authors**: Jiang Z et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Provide proper multiple imputation for sparsely or irregularly observed functional data, replacing single-imputation FPCA reconstruction.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulation studies plus physical activity and infant gut microbiome case studies under severe missingness.
  - **Estimand / target**: Valid downstream inference (coverage) for analyses using imputed curves; reconstruction of missing trajectories.
  - **Identification**: Missingness handled via model-based posterior predictive imputation; MAR-type assumption implied, not stated in abstract.
  - **Estimator / model**: BAMIFun: Bayesian low-rank model with penalized splines, Gibbs sampler; multiway extension via Functional Tensor SVD.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulations compare coverage and imputation accuracy against existing methods.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Substantially improved coverage and more reliable downstream inference versus existing methods, with similar imputation accuracy.
- **Strengths**: Propagates imputation uncertainty and extends to multiway functional data.
- **Limitations**: Missingness mechanism assumptions and software availability not reported in abstract.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Prefer multiple over single imputation for wearable/functional data with heavy missingness, when downstream inference matters.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/biomtc/ujag182 / PMID:42937050

## 4. Comparing the Risk of Maternal Complications and Adverse Infant Outcomes Among Pregnancies With and Without COVID-19 in the Sentinel System — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Pharmacoepidemiology and Drug Safety
- **Pub date**: 2026-11
- **Authors**: Shinde MU et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Assess how timing and severity of COVID-19 in pregnancy affect maternal complications and infant outcomes.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Sentinel System claims cohort 2020–2023; 69,901 COVID-19 pregnancies matched 1:1 to unexposed; 22,865 linked mother-infant pairs.
  - **Estimand / target**: Risk of preterm delivery, cesarean section, low birth weight by trimester and severity of infection.
  - **Identification**: Conditional exchangeability given propensity score covariates; matching to pregnancies without COVID-19 during prior six months or pregnancy.
  - **Estimator / model**: 1:1 propensity score matching under the CONSIGN protocol, stratified by trimester and severity.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: not reported
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Severe first-trimester COVID-19 had higher preterm delivery; severe second-trimester had higher cesarean rate; no increased adverse infant outcomes.
- **Strengths**: Very large sample with timing and severity stratification.
- **Limitations**: Claims-based exposure ascertainment; changing treatment and prevention over time may affect trends.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Stratify exposure by gestational timing and severity rather than pooling when estimating infection effects in pregnancy.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/pds.70511 / PMID:42946541

## 5. Localizing support-set changepoints in high-dimensional linear models — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Biometrics
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-07
- **Authors**: Cui J et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Detect time points at which covariates switch between inactive and active states while coefficients vary continuously.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Theory, extensive simulations, and real Northern Taiwan 2015 air quality data.
  - **Estimand / target**: Locations of support-set changepoints in sparse time-varying regression coefficients.
  - **Identification**: Sparse high-dimensional linear model with continuously varying coefficients; minimax signal-to-noise condition.
  - **Estimator / model**: Proposed support-set changepoint localization estimator with nearly matching minimax lower and upper bounds on localization error.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Numerical studies showing superior performance versus existing methods.
- **Software**: R package "SCP"
- **Key findings**: Localization error attains near-minimax rate under essentially the weakest possible signal-to-noise condition.
- **Strengths**: Strong theory plus released R package.
- **Limitations**: Non-causal; relevance to epidemiologic practice indirect.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Consider when exposure–outcome associations may switch on/off over time in high-dimensional time series.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/biomtc/ujag180 / PMID:42937051
