## 1. Clinically Interpretable Time-To-Event Testing in Randomized Clinical Trials: Milestone Survival Combined With Restricted Mean Survival Time — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-10
- **Authors**: He S et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Propose combination tests of milestone survival and RMST differences that stay interpretable and powerful when proportional hazards fails.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Monte Carlo simulations (milestone-based and event-driven designs) plus re-analysis of three oncology RCTs.
  - **Estimand / target**: Between-group difference in milestone survival probability and in restricted mean survival time.
  - **Identification**: Randomization; prespecified milestone time; no proportional-hazards assumption required.
  - **Estimator / model**: Combination test of Kaplan-Meier milestone and RMST differences, compared against log-rank and MaxCombo.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Type I error and power across delayed, waning and crossing hazards, sample sizes and censoring rates.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Rejection rates near nominal under estimand-null; competitive to superior power in non-PH settings; detects benefit when HRs are near unity.
- **Strengths**: Aligns design, estimand and analysis with clinically meaningful absolute horizons.
- **Limitations**: Requires careful prespecification of the milestone; abstract reports no software.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Consider milestone+RMST combination tests as primary or complementary analysis when non-proportional hazards are anticipated.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70787 / PMID:42885609

## 2. Optimal Two-Phase Sampling Designs for Generalized Raking Estimators With Multiple Parameters of Interest — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-10
- **Authors**: Yang JB et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Derive optimal adaptive multiwave Phase 2 sampling designs for IPW and generalized raking estimators with multiple parameters.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulations plus example of people living with HIV at the Vanderbilt Comprehensive Care Clinic (EHR data).
  - **Estimand / target**: Multiple regression parameters under missingness, measurement error and misclassification in Phase 1 data.
  - **Identification**: Phase 2 subsample with validated measurements; known sampling probabilities; auxiliary Phase 1 variables for calibration.
  - **Estimator / model**: IPW and generalized raking; Neyman allocation per parameter and integer-valued A-optimal allocation.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Extensive simulation comparing allocation strategies against case-control sampling.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Per-parameter optimization beats case-control sampling; A-optimal integer allocation typically better; optimal GR designs differ substantially from IPW designs.
- **Strengths**: Practical allocation rules for validation sampling in error-prone EHR data.
- **Limitations**: Software availability not reported in abstract; single applied example.
- **Takeaway for practice**: When planning EHR validation substudies, optimize allocation specifically for raking, not IPW, and target multiple parameters jointly.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70786 / PMID:42888020

## 3. Rethinking time-lag bias in meta-analysis: Broader definitions and conceptual frameworks — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Research Synthesis Methods
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-16
- **Authors**: Xing X et al.
- **Type**: review
- **Aims**: Broaden time-lag bias to time-dependent evidence availability and illustrate varied dissemination mechanisms.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Conceptual framework with several real-world meta-analysis case studies.
  - **Estimand / target**: Pooled meta-analytic effect as a function of evidence-accrual time.
  - **Identification**: Not applicable; publication timing related to results treated as a selective dissemination mechanism.
  - **Estimator / model**: Cumulative/temporal meta-analysis diagnostics; specific estimators not reported.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Case studies showing exaggeration, attenuation or reversal of pooled estimates; sensitivity strategies advocated.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Time-lag bias includes inverse, non-monotone and window-localized patterns, not only early positive studies.
- **Strengths**: Unifies classical and atypical temporal patterns in one framework.
- **Limitations**: Conceptual; no new estimator or simulation reported in abstract.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Routinely inspect cumulative meta-analyses by publication time and run robustness checks for selective dissemination.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1017/rsm.2026.10121 / PMID:42885228

## 4. Prescription Intervals of Medications for Chronic Use: A Cohort Study — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: Pharmacoepidemiology and Drug Safety
- **Pub date**: 2026-11
- **Authors**: Muddiman R et al.
- **Type**: applied analysis
- **Aims**: Characterise prescription intervals of chronic drugs to inform gap definitions in deprescribing studies.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Irish primary-care cohort, 38,154 older patients, 2011-2020, 20 most common drug classes.
  - **Estimand / target**: Interval between prescriptions and its covariate associations; population interval distribution.
  - **Identification**: Descriptive; length bias from truncated data is the key concern.
  - **Estimator / model**: Multilevel model for intervals; parametric waiting time distribution (pWTD) as comparison.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Multilevel estimates compared with pWTD estimates across drug classes.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Each added prescription lengthened interval by 5.0 (NSAIDs) to 19.7 days (other antidepressants); pWTD agreed for most drugs.
- **Strengths**: Data-driven basis for discontinuation gaps across many drug classes.
- **Limitations**: Single-country older-adult cohort; large unexplained within-patient variation.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use pWTD rather than fixed gaps or truncated summaries to define treatment discontinuation in drug-utilisation studies.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/pds.70505 / PMID:42885382

## 5. Design and Analysis Strategies for Pooling in High Throughput Screening: Application to the Search for a New Anti-Microbial — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-10
- **Authors**: Smucker BJ et al.
- **Type**: simulation
- **Aims**: Compare pooling construction and analysis methods to guide pooled high-throughput screening for antimicrobials.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Pilot study and small screening campaign for new anti-bacterial compounds.
  - **Estimand / target**: Identification of active compounds from pooled wells.
  - **Identification**: Sparsity of active compounds in supersaturated pooled designs.
  - **Estimator / model**: Pooling constructions with regularized regression analysis.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Comparison of several construction and analysis methods in pilot and campaign data.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Pooling is more efficient than one-compound-per-well; successes and challenges described, no numbers in abstract.
- **Strengths**: Real screening campaign grounds method comparison.
- **Limitations**: Lab-screening focus; limited relevance to observational epidemiology.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Consider pooled designs with regularized regression analysis when screening many candidates with few expected actives.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70778 / PMID:42885925
