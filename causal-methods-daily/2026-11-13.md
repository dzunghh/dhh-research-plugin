## 1. A robust and accurate two-sample Mendelian randomization method with a large number of genetic variants — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Nature Communications
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-15
- **Authors**: Zhang L et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Develop MRZ, a two-sample MR estimator robust to uncorrelated and correlated horizontal pleiotropy when many genetic instruments are used.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulations across pleiotropy scenarios; applied two-sample MR of appendicular lean mass (ALM) and lipid traits using GWAS summary statistics; mouse randomized experiments for validation.
  - **Estimand / target**: Causal effect of exposure on outcome (per-SD effect of ALM on TC/LDL-C), plus pleiotropic parameters; bidirectional effects tested.
  - **Identification**: IV assumptions relaxed via mixture model classifying IVs as valid, uncorrelated pleiotropic, or correlated pleiotropic (InSIDE violation allowed).
  - **Estimator / model**: Mixture distribution on IV-outcome association z-scores; joint likelihood-based inference for causal and pleiotropy parameters.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulation benchmarks against existing MR methods; reverse-direction MR; experimental triangulation in mice.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: MRZ showed superior robustness/accuracy in simulations; found negative causal effects of ALM on TC and LDL-C without reverse causation, whereas competing methods suggested bidirectional effects.
- **Strengths**: Handles correlated pleiotropy with many instruments and triangulates MR findings with randomized animal experiments.
- **Limitations**: Abstract reports no numeric bias/coverage metrics or software availability; mixture-model assumptions may be misspecified.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Consider MRZ as a sensitivity estimator alongside IVW, MR-Egger and weighted median when many instruments raise correlated-pleiotropy concerns.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41467-026-78442-8 / PMID:43015287

## 2. Leveraging win time endpoint and combining moderators for personalized treatment recommendation in time-to-event clinical trials — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: BMC Medical Research Methodology
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-15
- **Authors**: Pan S et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Extend Kraemer's composite-moderator framework to time-to-event outcomes using expected win time against reference (EWTR) for personalized treatment recommendations.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulation study plus illustration using randomized trial data comparing two atrial fibrillation management strategies.
  - **Estimand / target**: Expected win time against reference (EWTR); heterogeneous treatment effect across composite moderator values.
  - **Identification**: Randomization ensures exchangeability; effect modification by baseline biomarkers/clinical variables combined into one composite moderator.
  - **Estimator / model**: Optimally weighted composite moderator (Kraemer approach) applied to EWTR; compared with single-moderator subgroup analyses.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulations varying quantitative vs qualitative modification, interaction-to-main-effect ratios, and moderator–endpoint correlations.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: With strong effect modification, the composite moderator matched or outperformed the best single moderator; advantage attenuated with weak interactions, correlated moderators, or null endpoints.
- **Strengths**: Combines a clinically interpretable time-based estimand with a pragmatic multi-moderator HTE approach.
- **Limitations**: Benefit depends on moderate-to-strong effect modification; restricted to randomized trial settings.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use composite moderators with EWTR in RCT secondary analyses when several candidate moderators exist and effect modification is plausibly strong.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1186/s12874-026-02960-8 / PMID:43015614

## 3. SIMEX-Trans: High-Dimensional Transfer Learning With Covariates Subject to Measurement Error — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-11
- **Authors**: Cai K et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Combine SIMEX measurement-error correction with high-dimensional transfer learning from informative auxiliary studies.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulations; GTEx brain-tissue analysis predicting hypothalamus JAM2 expression with limited target samples and heterogeneous auxiliary tissues.
  - **Estimand / target**: High-dimensional regression coefficients and prediction error in the target population.
  - **Identification**: Classical additive measurement error with known/estimable variance; auxiliary studies sufficiently similar to the target (known or data-selected).
  - **Estimator / model**: SIMEX-based Lasso- and SCAD-penalized transfer-learning estimators; SIMEX-Trans-Selector when informative sources are unknown.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Support recovery and asymptotic normality theory (SCAD, known sources); cross-validated prediction error; sensitivity analysis.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Outperformed competitors in estimation accuracy with reliable variable selection; SIMEX-Trans-Selector achieved the lowest cross-validated prediction error in GTEx.
- **Strengths**: Addresses measurement error and small target samples jointly with theoretical guarantees.
- **Limitations**: Focus is predictive/genomic rather than causal; relies on measurement-error variance specification.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Useful when error-prone high-dimensional covariates and small target cohorts coexist; borrow strength from auxiliary datasets cautiously.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70820 / PMID:43016007

## 4. Sample size estimation for pre- and post-intervention design with incomplete observations of count outcomes — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Statistical Methods in Medical Research
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-12
- **Authors**: Wang J et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Derive a GEE-based sample size formula for pre–post designs with count outcomes and incomplete paired observations.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Analytical derivation, simulation studies, and a clinical trial example.
  - **Estimand / target**: Pre–post change in mean count (rate ratio) under GEE marginal model.
  - **Identification**: Not applicable (design/power); accommodates varying missing patterns, missingness magnitude, and within-subject correlation.
  - **Estimator / model**: GEE for count outcomes with closed-form sample size formula; compared with crude inflation by complete-data proportion.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulations evaluating empirical power and type I error.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Proposed formula preserved nominal power and type I error across missingness scenarios; abstract gives no numeric comparison with the crude adjustment.
- **Strengths**: Uses partial pairs rather than discarding them, avoiding wasteful crude sample-size inflation.
- **Limitations**: Likely assumes missing completely at random; limited to two time points.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Replace crude "divide by completion rate" inflation with this GEE formula when planning pre–post count-outcome trials.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1177/09622802261506993 / PMID:43017909

## 5. Improving Multi-Trait Polygenic Risk Score Prediction of Drug Response Using Fine-Mapping and Ensemble Learning — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-11
- **Authors**: Zhai S et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Propose mtPRS-FIMEL, an ensemble multi-trait PRS integrating fine-mapped causal variants to predict drug response.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulations across genetic architectures; validation with pharmacogenomic GWAS from a randomized trial (LDL-C change on simvastatin) plus LDL/HDL/TG GWAS.
  - **Estimand / target**: Prediction accuracy and PRS–drug-response association; patient stratification performance.
  - **Identification**: Not applicable (prediction); fine-mapping used to prioritize likely causal variants.
  - **Estimator / model**: Ensemble weighting of mtPRS-PCA, elastic-net mtPRS-ML and single-trait PRS from GWAS summary statistics.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Extensive simulations; internal cross-validation in trial data.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: mtPRS-FIMEL outperformed existing mtPRS and stPRS in prediction accuracy, association strength and stratification in simulated settings; validated proof-of-concept for LDL-C response.
- **Strengths**: Robust ensemble avoids reliance on any single PRS method across architectures.
- **Limitations**: Only internal cross-validation; no external replication reported.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Consider ensemble fine-mapped mtPRS for pharmacogenomic stratification, but demand external validation before clinical use.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70826 / PMID:43016002
