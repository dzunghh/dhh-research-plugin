## 1. Hazard ratios and their causal interpretation: challenges and guidance — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: International Journal of Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-14
- **Authors**: Bartlett JW et al.
- **Type**: review
- **Aims**: Review critiques that hazard ratios lack causal interpretation, even in randomized trials, and give guidance on interpreting them.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Conceptual review of causal-interpretation critiques of Cox hazard ratios; no empirical data reported.
  - **Estimand / target**: Hazard ratio versus alternative time-to-event effect measures (e.g. risk difference, restricted mean survival time).
  - **Identification**: Depth-of-selection (frailty/conditioning on survivors) issue: hazards condition on survival, so the HR is not a clean causal contrast.
  - **Estimator / model**: Cox proportional hazards model; frailty-based reasoning.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: not reported
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Hazard ratios remain interpretable with care, but alternative measures may be preferable for describing treatment or exposure effects on time-to-event outcomes.
- **Strengths**: Authoritative, practical guidance on a ubiquitous but contested effect measure.
- **Limitations**: Abstract-level appraisal only; specific recommended alternatives and worked examples not visible.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Report absolute or survival-curve-based contrasts alongside HRs, and avoid strict causal claims from HRs alone.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/ije/dyag228 / PMID:42925542

## 2. Marginal Analysis of Clustered Interval-Censored Competing Risks Data With Informative Cluster Size — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-11
- **Authors**: Wang X et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Develop marginal regression for clustered, interval-censored competing-risks data when cluster size is informative.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulation studies plus National Alzheimer's Coordinating Center data on Lewy body dementia incidence.
  - **Estimand / target**: Marginal cumulative incidence function covariate effects (generalized odds-rate transformation models, incl. Fine-Gray, proportional odds).
  - **Identification**: Cluster-weighted estimation to handle informative cluster size; marginal modelling without a random-effects specification.
  - **Estimator / model**: Cluster-weighted sieve maximum pseudo-likelihood with B-spline approximation; asymptotic properties established.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Extensive finite-sample simulations.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Method yields valid inference where existing approaches do not apply; applied to Lewy body dementia risk factors.
- **Strengths**: Handles interval censoring, competing risks and informative cluster size simultaneously with theory.
- **Limitations**: Not causal; code availability and quantitative performance not reported in abstract.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Consider for multi-centre dementia or similar data with clustered, interval-censored competing events.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70792 / PMID:42922070

## 3. Bayesian Transfer Learning for Enhanced Estimation and Inference — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the American Statistical Association
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-24
- **Authors**: Lai D et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Enable multi-source Bayesian transfer learning with valid uncertainty quantification using pre-trained, summary-level source models.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulations and a high-dimensional genetic dataset application.
  - **Estimand / target**: Regression coefficients in high-dimensional linear regression for a target population.
  - **Identification**: Source-target alignment; adaptive weighting guards against negative transfer from heterogeneous sources.
  - **Estimator / model**: TRADER: guided horseshoe prior shrinking target parameters toward weighted average of source estimates; posterior contraction theory.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulations versus target-only and a frequentist competitor; multicollinearity application.
- **Software**: Reproducibility guide in supplementary materials; package not reported.
- **Key findings**: Inference no worse than target-only; comparable to frequentist method using only summary-level data, with computational advantages.
- **Strengths**: Needs only summary-level source data, supporting privacy-preserving borrowing.
- **Limitations**: Linear models only; not causal.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Useful for borrowing external-study estimates in small-sample, high-dimensional (e.g. genetic) analyses.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1080/01621459.2026.2685359 / PMID:42921457
