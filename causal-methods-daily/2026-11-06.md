## 1. A recipe for population-based doubly robust estimation via weighting, regression, calibration, and standardization — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: American Journal of Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-05
- **Authors**: Schnell PM et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Unify IPTW-GLM and AIPW doubly robust estimators as calibration of an outcome model within a weighted pseudosample representing a flexible target population.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Theoretical/methodological development; empirical data not reported in abstract.
  - **Estimand / target**: Causal effects in flexible target populations, including populations other than the study sample (e.g., exposure-dependent enrollment cohorts).
  - **Identification**: Randomized exposure in a weighted pseudosample; exchangeability and positivity conditional on covariates.
  - **Estimator / model**: IPTW-GLM with canonical link by MLE; AIPW viewed as outcome-model calibration to balanced pseudosample group means.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: not reported
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: IPTW-GLM with canonical link is doubly robust without explicit bias correction; framework extends double robustness to non-sample target populations.
- **Strengths**: Clarifies the connection among standardization, weighting and AIPW estimators.
- **Limitations**: Abstract reports no simulation or applied results.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use calibrated pseudosample estimators when cohort enrollment depends on exposure and target population differs from the sample.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/aje/kwag284 / PMID:42986185

## 2. What is overlap weighting, how has it evolved, and when to use it for causal inference? — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: International Journal of Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-14
- **Authors**: Lu H et al.
- **Type**: review
- **Aims**: Summarize recent overlap weighting developments and give guidance on when it suits causal inference.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Methodological review; no primary data.
  - **Estimand / target**: ATE in the overlap population (individuals at empirical equipoise).
  - **Identification**: Conditional exchangeability and overlap given measured covariates, propensity score model.
  - **Estimator / model**: Overlap weights proportional to probability of opposite treatment; exact mean balance under logistic propensity model; minimum asymptotic variance.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: not reported
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: OW gives exact mean covariate balance and minimal asymptotic variance; overlap population approximates, but is not equivalent to, a pragmatic trial population.
- **Strengths**: Concise practical guidance by leading OW developers.
- **Limitations**: Review, no new empirical evidence; estimand differs from ATE/ATT.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Prefer OW when treatment groups poorly overlap and the equipoise population is clinically meaningful; state the estimand.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/ije/dyag249 / PMID:42981494

## 3. Regression Adjusted Target Effects Using Auxiliary Strata Under Design-Based Inference — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-11
- **Authors**: Zhao Z et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Improve ATE efficiency in randomized settings with sparse target strata by borrowing from auxiliary strata.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulations; applications to a schooling experiment and the ACTG 175 randomized trial.
  - **Estimand / target**: Average treatment effect in sparse target strata.
  - **Identification**: Randomization-based (design-based); no outcome-model correctness or stochastic error assumptions.
  - **Estimator / model**: Covariate-adjusted design-based estimator using auxiliary strata, with Neyman-style conservative variance estimator.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulations across sparsity and correlation patterns versus conventional design-based and regression-adjusted estimators.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Lower variance and narrower confidence intervals than comparators; precision gains in both applications.
- **Strengths**: Model-free validity with large-sample theory and conservative variance.
- **Limitations**: Gains depend on auxiliary strata correlating with target; numeric results not reported in abstract.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Consider for trials with rare subgroups where subgroup effect estimates are imprecise.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70806 / PMID:42982420

- **Pub date**: 2026-11-05

- **Type**: applied causal analysis

  - **Estimand / target**: Randomized interventional analogues: pure natural direct effect (rPNDE), total natural indirect effect (rTNIE), proportion mediated.
  - **Identification**: Interventional mediation allowing exposure-induced mediator-outcome confounding; assumptions not detailed in abstract.
  - **Estimator / model**: Randomized interventional analogue mediation estimators; model details not reported.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: not reported
- **Software**: not reported

## 5. Development and validation of early warning scores predicting 24 h mortality addressing intercurrent medical interventions in Denmark — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: The Lancet Digital Health
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-05
- **Authors**: Katsiferis A et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Compare NEWS, simplified scores and XGBoost for 24 h mortality by net benefit while correcting intervention bias.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Multicentre retrospective cohort, 15 Danish hospitals, 2·08 million encounters (825,200 patients), 2018-2023.
  - **Estimand / target**: Predictive performance (net benefit, AUROC, calibration) for 24 h all-cause mortality, adjusted for intercurrent interventions.
  - **Identification**: Propensity score weighting to mitigate intervention bias; assumes measured confounding captured.
  - **Estimator / model**: NEWS, simplified NEWS, DEWS and XGB-EWS with sentence-transformer history embeddings; decision curve analysis.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Stratified analyses by initial NEWS risk group.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Overall gain XGB-EWS vs NEWS 1·9 per 10,000; in NEWS ≥7 group 78·1 per 10,000.
- **Strengths**: Very large sample, decision-curve utility focus, handles treatment-induced bias in prediction validation.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective; propensity weighting cannot remove unmeasured intervention confounding.
- **Takeaway for practice**: When validating risk scores retrospectively, account for interventions and report net benefit by risk stratum.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.landig.2026.101067 / PMID:42988715
