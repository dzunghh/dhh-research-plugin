## 1. Valid Data-Driven Subgroup Selection With Uniform Guarantees in Clinical Trials — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-10
- **Authors**: Müller MM et al.
- **Type**: simulation
- **Aims**: Show how two post-selection-valid methods can identify safe or high-benefit subgroups in trials while controlling Type I error.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulation study comparing methods for clinical-trial subgroup identification; real-data application not reported.
  - **Estimand / target**: Subgroups with treatment effect heterogeneity (high benefit or safe from adverse events), with uniform Type I error control.
  - **Identification**: Randomised-trial data; validity relies on regression model assumptions (GLM or monotone/isotonic structure) for post-selection inference.
  - **Estimator / model**: Generalised linear model-based and isotonic-regression-based subgroup selection with uniform (simultaneous) guarantees.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulations probing sensitivity of Type I error control to modelling assumptions.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Both methods control Type I error in the two tasks; their strengths and weaknesses depend on how well model assumptions hold.
- **Strengths**: Addresses the post-selection inference problem directly, with a thorough simulation comparison.
- **Limitations**: Abstract reports no code and no quantitative results.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use guarantee-bearing subgroup selection rather than ad hoc subgroup analyses when claiming heterogeneous effects in trials.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70745 / PMID:42870519

## 2. Evaluating the consequences of childhood adiposity on the human plasma proteome at four timepoints across the lifecourse — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Nature Communications
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-10
- **Authors**: Dickson P et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Estimate effects of childhood adiposity on circulating proteins across four life stages using genetic data.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Lifecourse Mendelian randomization of plasma proteome measured at four timepoints (childhood to midlife); sample sizes not reported.
  - **Estimand / target**: Effect of genetically predicted childhood adiposity on protein levels, independent of adulthood adiposity; mediation of asthma risk.
  - **Identification**: Genetic instruments (relevance, independence, exclusion restriction); colocalization to support shared causal variants.
  - **Estimator / model**: Lifecourse (multivariable) MR, mediation framework, colocalization posterior probabilities.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Colocalization (PP 88.6% childhood asthma vs 0.2% adult asthma).
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Childhood adiposity affected 8 proteins in childhood (e.g. TNFSF11) and 24 in midlife independent of adult adiposity.
- **Strengths**: Separates childhood from adult adiposity effects across the lifecourse.
- **Limitations**: Abstract gives no pleiotropy sensitivity analyses; instrument strength not reported.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Lifecourse MR with colocalization suits time-varying exposures where timing of effect matters.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41467-026-77097-9 / PMID:42864329

## 3. GLP-1 receptor agonist initiation and risk of psychiatric hospitalisation and ED presentation in adults with schizophrenia and type 2 diabetes in the USA — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Psychiatry
- **Pub date**: 2026-11
- **Authors**: Davoodian N et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Compare psychiatric hospitalisation and ED visits after GLP-1RA initiation versus DPP-4i, SGLT2i, or non-initiation.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Active-comparator and non-initiator EHR cohort (NeuroBlu), N=70,257, schizophrenia with type 2 diabetes, up to 2-year follow-up.
  - **Estimand / target**: Intention-to-treat weighted hazard ratio for psychiatric hospitalisation and ED presentation.
  - **Identification**: Conditional exchangeability given demographic, clinical, and medication covariates; time zero at first prescription (index date for non-initiators).
  - **Estimator / model**: Inverse probability weighting with weighted regression (HR).
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Residual confounding and selection bias acknowledged; no quantitative bias analysis reported.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: GLP-1RA vs non-initiation HR 0.76 (0.71–0.82) for hospitalisation; vs SGLT2i 0.83 (0.78–0.88).
- **Strengths**: Large cohort with active-comparator and non-user contrasts, explicit time zero.
- **Limitations**: Non-initiator time zero may introduce immortal-time-type bias; residual confounding.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Prefer active-comparator contrasts; treat non-user comparisons as more susceptible to bias.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S2215-0366(26)00276-2 / PMID:42874603

## 4. Racial discrimination and preterm birth subtype among a prospective cohort of nulliparous Black women in the United States, 2010-2013 — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: American Journal of Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-13
- **Authors**: Judge AS et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Test whether racial discrimination is associated differently with spontaneous versus indicated preterm birth.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Prospective clinic-based cohort of nulliparous self-identified Black women, 2010-2013; N not reported.
  - **Estimand / target**: Risk ratio of spontaneous and indicated PTB by discrimination exposure, via cumulative incidence.
  - **Identification**: Conditional exchangeability via inverse probability of exposure weights; post-stratification weights for late care initiators.
  - **Estimator / model**: IPW with cumulative incidence function, competing-risks framework, bootstrap standard errors.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Stratification by education and age for effect modification.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Discrimination in ≥3 situations: spontaneous PTB RR 1.66 (0.72–3.82); indicated PTB RR 0.37 (0.15–0.93).
- **Strengths**: Handles competing PTB subtypes explicitly with weighting and cumulative incidence.
- **Limitations**: Wide intervals; late prenatal care initiators underrepresented.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Model PTB subtypes as competing events rather than pooling outcomes.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/aje/kwag252 / PMID:42865083

## 5. Development and internal validation of the NOTICE assessment as a non-biological measure of prenatal nicotine, tobacco, and cannabis use and exposure — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: Journal of Clinical Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-14
- **Authors**: Burmeister EA et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Develop and validate a low-burden behavioural tool measuring prenatal tobacco, nicotine, and cannabis exposure.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Longitudinal cohort of Australian First Nations pregnant women with repeated assessments plus biomarkers (CO, cotinine, THC-COOH); N not reported.
  - **Estimand / target**: Discriminative validity (AUC) of weighted composite score against biochemical reference standards.
  - **Identification**: Not applicable; criterion validation against creatinine-corrected cotinine and THC-COOH thresholds.
  - **Estimator / model**: ROC analysis with BCa bootstrap CIs clustered by participant; generalised estimating equations.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Alternative biomarker thresholds; weekly versus daily scores; internal validation only.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: AUC 0.99 (0.98–1.00) for tobacco/nicotine and 0.92 (0.83–1.00) for cannabis exposure.
- **Strengths**: Cluster-aware bootstrap and multiple biomarker references.
- **Limitations**: Internal validation only; single population.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Validated behavioural scores can reduce exposure misclassification where biomarkers are impractical, easing measurement-error concerns.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.jclinepi.2026.112552 / PMID:42874889
