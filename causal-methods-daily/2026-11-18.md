## 1. Unraveling Time-Varying Causal Effects of Multiple Exposures: Integrating Functional Data Analysis With Multivariable Mendelian Randomization — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-11
- **Authors**: Fontana N et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Extend functional MR to jointly estimate time-varying causal effects of multiple correlated exposures, accounting for shared instruments and mediation.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulation study plus proof-of-concept one-sample MR in UK Biobank (SBP, BMI → coronary artery disease).
  - **Estimand / target**: Time-varying (life-course) direct causal effect functions of each exposure on outcome.
  - **Identification**: Genetic IV assumptions (relevance, independence, exclusion) in multivariable MR; handles overlapping instruments and mediating pathways.
  - **Estimator / model**: MV-FMR: functional PCA of exposure trajectories, cross-validated basis selection, multivariable IV estimation of functional effects.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulations across nonlinear effects, horizontal pleiotropy, mediation, sparse longitudinal data; joint vs separate estimation compared.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: MV-FMR recovered functional effect shapes across scenarios and outperformed univariable functional MR; numeric bias/coverage not reported in abstract.
- **Strengths**: Moves MR beyond constant lifelong effects to identify critical periods for correlated exposures.
- **Limitations**: Requires repeated exposure measures with genetic instruments; pleiotropy robustness and software availability unclear from abstract.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Consider for life-course MR questions where exposure timing matters (e.g., BP vs BMI by age), given dense longitudinal data.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70817 / PMID:43042614

## 2. Causal Mediation Analysis of Non-Mortality Outcomes With Follow-Up Truncated by Death: Survivor Natural Direct and Indirect Effects — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Biometrical Journal
- **Pub date**: 2026-12
- **Authors**: Tai AS et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Define and estimate mediation estimands when both mediator and non-mortality outcome are truncated by death.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Methodological development illustrated with National Emphysema Treatment Trial data.
  - **Estimand / target**: Survivor natural direct and indirect effects (principal-stratum-type NDE/NIE among always-survivors).
  - **Identification**: Exchangeability assumptions plus a pretreatment surrogate variable to identify survivor strata.
  - **Estimator / model**: Three estimation approaches, including data-adaptive G-computation.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: not reported
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Proposes new estimands and three estimators; abstract reports no numeric performance metrics.
- **Strengths**: Addresses a common, usually ignored problem combining truncation by death with mediation.
- **Limitations**: Relies on availability of a valid pretreatment surrogate; no simulation results reported in abstract.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use when mediating QoL/function outcomes in high-mortality trials instead of complete-case NDE/NIE, if a suitable surrogate exists.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/bimj.70199 / PMID:43042560

## 3. Estimating the duration of RT-PCR positivity for SARS-CoV-2 from doubly interval censored data with undetected infections — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Biometrics
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-07
- **Authors**: Blake J et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Estimate the full distribution of PCR-detectable infection duration in the general population for incidence estimation.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: UK Coronavirus Infection Survey, longitudinal general-population prevalence survey.
  - **Estimand / target**: Discrete-time distribution of duration of RT-PCR positivity.
  - **Identification**: Accounts for double interval censoring, undetected infections and false negatives via misclassification modelling.
  - **Estimator / model**: Bayesian nonparametric discrete-time survival model with informative prior from a complementary study.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulation study including robustness to model misspecification.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: First general-population estimate of full duration distribution; numeric results not given in abstract.
- **Strengths**: Jointly handles three realistic data imperfections common in surveillance surveys.
- **Limitations**: Depends on external prior information; transferability to other pathogens untested.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Relevant template for back-calculating incidence from prevalence surveys with imperfect tests and interval-censored episodes.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/biomtc/ujag162 / PMID:43043351

## 4. Including Quantitative Benefit-Risk Assessment in Seamless Phase 2/3 Designs With Dose Selection — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-11
- **Authors**: Ratta M et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Select doses at interim in seamless phase II/III trials using benefit-risk rather than efficacy alone.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Multi-arm two-stage seamless design; simulation-based evaluation.
  - **Estimand / target**: Treatment-vs-control efficacy at stage 2 for dose with best interim benefit-risk score.
  - **Identification**: Randomized comparison; strong familywise Type I error control via analytically derived decision boundary.
  - **Estimator / model**: Probabilistic multi-criteria decision analysis aggregating multiple efficacy and safety endpoints.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Extensive simulations of operating characteristics against existing designs; sample size guidance.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Design was particularly effective at stopping potentially harmful doses early while preserving Type I error control.
- **Strengths**: Formally integrates safety into interim selection with analytical error control.
- **Limitations**: MCDA weights are subjective; trial-design focus limits direct epi relevance.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Trial statisticians should consider MCDA-based selection where effective doses may differ materially in toxicity.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70790 / PMID:43039881

## 5. Smooth Penalized Marginal Intensity Models for Recurrent Event Data — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-11
- **Authors**: Coz E et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Provide a flexible marginal rate model for recurrent events capturing time dynamics and non-proportional covariate effects.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulation study with numerically computed true marginal intensity; real-data illustration.
  - **Estimand / target**: Marginal intensity (rate function) of recurrent events, with time-varying covariate effects.
  - **Identification**: No assumption on within-subject event correlation structure; estimating-equation inference.
  - **Estimator / model**: Natural cubic spline log-marginal-intensity model with quadratic penalty; robust sandwich variance vs bootstrap.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Coverage assessed with and without penalization; bootstrap compared.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Robust variance gave good coverage unpenalized but undercovered with penalization; bootstrap recommended then.
- **Strengths**: Flexible alternative to Andersen-Gill with robust variance, with practical variance guidance.
- **Limitations**: Marginal model cannot fully characterize the process or give dynamic individual predictions.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use for population-averaged recurrent-event rates with non-proportional effects; bootstrap CIs when penalizing.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70828 / PMID:43035987
