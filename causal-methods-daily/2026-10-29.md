## 1. Causal Inference with Missing Exposures and Missing Outcomes — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: American Journal of Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-28
- **Authors**: Landsiedel KE et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Extend hypothetical-intervention causal frameworks to missing exposures and missing baseline outcomes, defining Counterfactual Strata Effects.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Framework paper with real-data example: alcohol consumption and incident TB infection, rural Uganda; sample size not reported.
  - **Estimand / target**: Counterfactual Strata Effects: estimands conditioning on a counterfactual variable (e.g., at-risk population) under interventions setting exposure and preventing missingness.
  - **Identification**: Causal model per missingness setting; identification result assuming missingness mechanisms are accounted for by observed covariates (details not reported in abstract).
  - **Estimator / model**: TMLE with Super Learner for estimation and inference.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Not reported; practical consequences of the approach discussed.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Shows exposure-missingness extension is identifiable and estimable with TMLE; illustrated in Uganda cohort. Numeric results not reported in abstract.
- **Strengths**: Unifies missing exposure, outcome and at-risk-population selection within one counterfactual estimand framework.
- **Limitations**: Abstract gives no simulation evidence or software; identifying assumptions not detailed.
- **Takeaway for practice**: When exposure or baseline outcome data are missing, define estimands explicitly and use TMLE rather than complete-case analysis.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/aje/kwag281 / PMID:42950419

## 2. Two-Stage Estimation for Causal Inference Involving Semi-Continuous Exposures — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-11
- **Authors**: Wang X et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Propose a causal framework and two-stage estimator for exposures with a point mass at zero.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulation studies plus application to prenatal alcohol exposure and child cognition cohort; N not reported.
  - **Estimand / target**: Causal dose-response among exposed, and effect of exposure status at a reference dose.
  - **Identification**: Two-part propensity structure (exposure status; level among exposed) within a marginal structural model; conditional exchangeability assumed.
  - **Estimator / model**: IPW, propensity score regression adjustment and AIPW in second stage; consistency and asymptotic normality proven.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulations of finite-sample performance; limiting values characterized under propensity model misspecification.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Estimators are consistent and asymptotically normal; simulations support robustness. Numeric performance not reported in abstract.
- **Strengths**: Separates status and dose effects, with theory covering misspecification and flexible second-stage propensity methods.
- **Limitations**: Abstract does not report software or numeric simulation results.
- **Takeaway for practice**: For zero-inflated exposures (alcohol, smoking, drug dose), avoid forcing a single continuous-exposure model; estimate status and dose effects separately.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70800 / PMID:42951587

## 3. Can discrete-time analyses be trusted for stepped wedge trials with continuous recruitment? — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Statistical Methods in Medical Research
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-28
- **Authors**: Wang H et al.
- **Type**: simulation
- **Aims**: Assess discrete-time linear mixed models for stepped wedge cluster randomized trials with continuous recruitment.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulations with continuous-time exponential decay correlation; reanalysis of a completed SW-CRT.
  - **Estimand / target**: Intervention effect, constant or exposure-time-dependent, with or without random intervention effects across clusters.
  - **Identification**: Randomized rollout; model-robust inference under correlation-structure misspecification.
  - **Estimator / model**: Linear mixed models with exchangeable, nested exchangeable, exponential-decay working correlations; robust sandwich variance with Mancl-DeRouen correction.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Varied fixed period effects and recruitment patterns between control and intervention periods.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Discrete-time analysis gives negligible bias; Mancl-DeRouen variance achieves nominal coverage and type I error. Slight bias when recruitment differs by arm-period.
- **Strengths**: Addresses a prevalent design (95.1% of cross-sectional SW-CRTs recruit continuously) with several misspecification scenarios.
- **Limitations**: Continuous outcomes only; no software reported.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Discrete-time mixed models with Mancl-DeRouen sandwich variance are reasonable for SW-CRTs, but check recruitment balance across periods.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1177/09622802261502150 / PMID:42949527

## 4. Developing an Innovative Jackknife Estimator for Quantifying Between-Study Variance in Meta-Analysis of Proportions — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-11
- **Authors**: Mohsin M et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Improve DerSimonian-Laird between-study variance estimation for meta-analysis of single-arm proportions via jackknife.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Binomial simulations plus three real-life examples; small numbers of studies emphasized.
  - **Estimand / target**: Between-study variance and pooled proportion (mean event probability).
  - **Identification**: Not applicable (meta-analytic random-effects model; no causal identification).
  - **Estimator / model**: DL jackknife (DLJ) and transformed DL jackknife (TDLJ) variance estimators.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Compared bias and coverage against DL, Paule-Mandel, REML and Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: DLJ/TDLJ better quantify between-study variance, especially with few studies, high heterogeneity and high event probability. Numbers not reported.
- **Strengths**: Targets known DL under-coverage with head-to-head comparison against standard estimators.
- **Limitations**: Limited to single-arm proportions; no software reported.
- **Takeaway for practice**: For proportion meta-analyses with few heterogeneous studies, consider jackknife-corrected variance rather than plain DL.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70802 / PMID:42949273

## 5. Effectiveness of the mRNA-1345 Vaccine Against Respiratory Syncytial Virus in US Veterans — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Network Open
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-01
- **Authors**: Bui DP et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Estimate mRNA-1345 RSV vaccine effectiveness over one season using target trial emulation.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Veterans Health Administration cohort, age ≥50; four nested monthly sequential trials; 74,926 vaccinated matched to 286,425 controls.
  - **Estimand / target**: Vaccine effectiveness = 100 × (1 − risk ratio) for vaccination versus none, up to 24 weeks.
  - **Identification**: Matching (up to 4:1) and weighting on baseline covariates; conditional exchangeability assumed.
  - **Estimator / model**: Matched, weighted risk ratios; details of weighting not reported in abstract.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Active-comparator analysis: same-day influenza plus mRNA-1345 versus influenza alone.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: VE 60.3% (95% CI 49.0-70.4) against infection; 81.8% (58.1-99.9) against hospitalization. Active comparator: 67.1% (48.9-82.6).
- **Strengths**: Sequential nested trials plus active-comparator check against health-care-seeking bias.
- **Limitations**: Observational, 91.3% male; wide intervals for hospitalization; residual confounding possible.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Pair target trial emulation with an active-comparator sensitivity analysis when vaccinated and unvaccinated differ in care-seeking.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.40981 / PMID:42949743
