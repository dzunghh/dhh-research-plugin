## 1. Early best available antibiotic therapy for multidrug-resistant gram-negative bloodstream infections: a target trial emulation — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: EClinicalMedicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-29
- **Authors**: Mezzadri L et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Estimate the effect of early guideline-recommended best available therapy (BAT) on 14-day mortality in MDR gram-negative bloodstream infections.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Target trial emulation in prospective ACORN-HAI cohort, 19 Asian countries, 1412 adults with monomicrobial BSI (3GCRE, CRE, CRA).
  - **Estimand / target**: 14-day all-cause mortality risk difference, early BAT (<24 h) versus no early BAT; effect modification by severity.
  - **Identification**: Conditional exchangeability given measured baseline confounders; positivity and consistency assumed; treatment-strategy time zero aligned at blood culture.
  - **Estimator / model**: Inverse probability weighting; RERI on additive scale for interaction with disease severity, stratified by pathogen group.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Pathogen-stratified analyses; further sensitivity analyses not reported in abstract.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Early BAT lowered mortality (18.5% vs 25.6%; RD -7.1%, 95% CI -11.3 to -3.0). Benefit seen in 3GCRE, uncertain in CR-GNB.
- **Strengths**: Explicit target-trial framework in a multi-country, low/middle-income-heavy AMR setting.
- **Limitations**: Observational; unmeasured confounding (e.g., severity, source control) and immortal-time handling not detailed in abstract.
- **Takeaway for practice**: A template for emulating antibiotic-timing trials with IPW; report effect modification on the additive scale.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.eclinm.2026.104302 / PMID:42997807

## 2. Discrepancies in reporting of study design and analysis methods between protocols and reports of interrupted time series (ITS) studies: a methodological study — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: BMC Medical Research Methodology
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-10
- **Authors**: Nguyen PY et al.
- **Type**: review
- **Aims**: Quantify discrepancies between ITS protocols and their corresponding results reports.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Meta-research; 120 ITS protocols found across 22 databases, 44 with matched reports, compared on 20 design/analysis items.
  - **Estimand / target**: Proportion of items discrepant or potentially important discrepant; justification rates.
  - **Identification**: not applicable (descriptive meta-research); ITS quasi-experimental design is the subject.
  - **Estimator / model**: Descriptive proportions of discrepancies for autocorrelation, seasonality, segment length, effect measures and ITS model choice.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: not reported
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Autocorrelation (77%) and seasonality (82%) handling often unreported; important discrepancies in eligibility (43%) and segment length (45%).
- **Strengths**: First systematic protocol-versus-report comparison for ITS, a common quasi-experimental causal design.
- **Limitations**: Only 37% of protocols had a published report, limiting generalisability.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Pre-specify autocorrelation, seasonality, and effect measure in ITS protocols and justify any deviations.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1186/s12874-026-02959-1 / PMID:42989139

## 3. Distribution of combined p-values for baseline variables in a consecutive sample of randomized controlled trials — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: BMC Medical Research Methodology
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-06
- **Authors**: Klang R et al.
- **Type**: simulation
- **Aims**: Assess whether Carlisle-method combined baseline p-values in RCTs follow the expected uniform distribution.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Meta-research of 568 consecutive RCTs from PubMed search (Feb 2022).
  - **Estimand / target**: Proportion of trials with combined p ≤0.05 or ≥0.95 versus 10% expected under valid randomisation.
  - **Identification**: Valid randomisation implies baseline-variable p-values are uniform; deviation signals randomisation or reporting problems.
  - **Estimator / model**: Carlisle's method per trial; two-sample Anderson-Darling tests comparing subgroups (trial size, number of baseline variables).
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Re-analysis adjusting for SE/SD reporting errors; scenarios assuming correlated baseline variables.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: 26.2% of RCTs had extreme combined p-values versus 10% expected; 20.6% remained after SE/SD correction.
- **Strengths**: Consecutive sample across fields with sensitivity analyses for reporting errors and correlation.
- **Limitations**: Method assumes independent baseline variables; extreme values are not proof of misconduct.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use Carlisle screening cautiously as a trial-integrity flag, not a verdict, in evidence syntheses.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1186/s12874-026-03082-x / PMID:42995542

## 5. Improving rare event prediction in imbalanced clinical data using cost-sensitive logistic regression — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: BMC Medical Research Methodology
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-10
- **Authors**: Zhu J et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Develop cost-sensitive logistic regression (CS-LR) to improve minority-class detection while keeping odds-ratio interpretability.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulations (4 covariate scenarios, 1–30% prevalence, N=500/2000) plus liver fibrosis and post-ESD bleeding datasets.
  - **Estimand / target**: Prediction performance for rare outcomes: recall, F1, G-mean, AUC (not a causal estimand).
  - **Identification**: not applicable (prediction).
  - **Estimator / model**: Logistic loss with class-specific misclassification costs; compared with standard logistic regression, cost-sensitive SVM, and AdaBoost.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulation across imbalance ratios and sample sizes; external validation on two clinical datasets.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Higher recall and G-mean at N=500 and prevalence <5%; AUC broadly comparable to standard logistic regression.
- **Strengths**: Retains interpretable odds ratios while improving rare-event sensitivity.
- **Limitations**: Cost choice is subjective; calibration and decision-curve performance not reported in abstract.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Consider when missing rare events is costly, but check calibration since cost weighting distorts predicted risks.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1186/s12874-026-02970-6 / PMID:42989158
