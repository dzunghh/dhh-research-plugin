## 1. Informing sample size and power calculations for clone-censor-weight analyses: a Monte Carlo simulation study of the variance inflation factor relative to the underlying target trial — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Journal of Clinical Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-30
- **Authors**: Austin PC et al.
- **Type**: simulation
- **Aims**: Quantify the variance inflation factor of clone-censor-weighting analyses versus the underlying target RCT to support a priori power calculations.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Monte Carlo simulation, 270 scenarios varying sample size, treatment prevalence, early-treatment threshold, covariate-treatment odds ratios; 10 baseline covariates.
  - **Estimand / target**: Risk difference and relative risk at a time horizon, emulating a target trial with treatment initiated during follow-up.
  - **Identification**: Conditional exchangeability given baseline covariates; positivity; inverse probability of censoring weights from clone-censor-weight design.
  - **Estimator / model**: Clone-censor-weighting with IPC weights; VIF = variance of CCW estimator divided by variance of the target RCT.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Secondary simulations varying number of baseline covariates; VIF examined across confounding strength.
- **Software**: R code in a public GitHub repository
- **Key findings**: With low-to-moderate confounding, CCW was more precise than the RCT; VIF rose with more covariates and depended on prevalence, sample size and horizon.
- **Strengths**: Fills a practical design gap with transparent, reusable code and broad scenario grid.
- **Limitations**: Simulation-based; VIFs apply only within the tested data-generating settings.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use the published VIF estimates to size CCW target-trial emulations rather than assuming RCT-equivalent power.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.jclinepi.2026.112576 / PMID:42962903

## 2. Scale-Transformed Power Priors for External Data Integration in Longitudinal Studies — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-11
- **Authors**: Sheikh MT et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Correct power-prior borrowing in longitudinal studies where external data differ in effective Fisher information scale.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Linear mixed-effects framework; simulations plus a prostate cancer clinical trial application.
  - **Estimand / target**: Fixed-effect parameters of the current longitudinal study, with improved bias and precision.
  - **Identification**: Historical parameters rescaled by Fisher information before borrowing; assumes comparability after scaling, with an additive adjustment term.
  - **Estimator / model**: Bayesian scale-transformed power prior, conditional Fisher information accounting for random effects; partial borrowing of fixed effects only.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Theoretical bias conditions for conventional power priors; simulation studies guiding when scaling helps.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Theory shows information-scale differences bias conventional power priors; simulations show improved performance. Numeric results not reported in abstract.
- **Strengths**: Theoretical grounding plus simulation guidance on when the transformation is beneficial.
- **Limitations**: Developed for linear mixed models; code availability not stated.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Consider rescaling before borrowing historical trial data when follow-up, variability or correlation structure differ.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70808 / PMID:42963109

## 3. JMbdirect: joint modeling with semi-parametric link functions for bidirectional feedback in longitudinal-survival data — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: BMC Medical Research Methodology
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-01
- **Authors**: Bhattacharjee A et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Relax linearity and one-way biomarker-to-event assumptions in joint longitudinal-survival models.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulations (four design factors, several sample sizes) and the primary biliary cirrhosis cohort.
  - **Estimand / target**: Dynamic event-risk prediction (AUC at 5 years, integrated Brier score) and feedback parameters.
  - **Identification**: Association structures via penalized splines or neural additive components; post-event level shifts and slope changes model event-to-biomarker feedback.
  - **Estimator / model**: Penalized ML with Laplace approximation, or Bayesian HMC; supports competing risks.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulation across association form, feedback magnitude, single versus competing events; cross-validated discrimination in PBC.
- **Software**: JMbdirect R package and public dashboard
- **Key findings**: Competing-risks scenario: AUC@5 0.85 versus 0.72 for linear model, coverage near nominal versus 88%. PBC: bilirubin threshold ~3 mg/dL, AUC@5 0.78 versus 0.71.
- **Strengths**: Open-source software with explicit feedback modelling and calibration assessment.
- **Limitations**: Prediction-focused; feedback is not given a causal interpretation.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Try when events plausibly change biomarker trajectories or risk relationships look threshold-like.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1186/s12874-026-02955-5 / PMID:42958492

## 4. Statistical Pitfalls in Large Language Model Evaluation: A Practical Guide to Study Design and Analysis — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Journal of Clinical Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-30
- **Authors**: Vahedi H et al.
- **Type**: tutorial/guidance
- **Aims**: Show why paired question-level LLM evaluations should not be analysed as independent samples.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Conceptual guidance for designs where several sources answer the same fixed question set.
  - **Estimand / target**: Paired differences in accuracy, completeness or readability scores between sources.
  - **Identification**: Question treated as the pairing unit, accounting for shared difficulty and ambiguity.
  - **Estimator / model**: Friedman or repeated-measures ANOVA, paired Wilcoxon or t-tests with multiplicity correction; mixed-effects models with question random effect.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Argues independent tests distort type I error and power; no empirical validation reported.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Independent-sample tests (Mann-Whitney, Kruskal-Wallis, unpaired t) on paired data lose power and distort nominal error. No numbers reported.
- **Strengths**: Clear, actionable fix for a widespread design-analysis mismatch.
- **Limitations**: Conceptual only; no simulation or reanalysis shown in abstract.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use paired or mixed-effects analyses with question as random effect when benchmarking LLMs.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.jclinepi.2026.112581 / PMID:42962902

## 5. Dimension reduction for characterizing sexual dimorphism in biomechanics of the temporomandibular joint — Score 4/6

- **Pub date**: 2026-10-07
- **Authors**: Park SH et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Identify sex-specific skull-muscle attachment associations by conditional cross-covariance reduction.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: 21 cadaver heads (11 female, 10 male); craniofacial morphology and masticatory muscle attachments.
  - **Estimand / target**: Sex-conditional cross-covariance between two sets of variables.
  - **Identification**: Not causal; association conditional on a binary variable (sex).
  - **Estimator / model**: Conditional cross-covariance reduction model with sparse SVD; sequential permutation for choosing sparsity.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Permutation-based variable selection; simulation results not reported in abstract.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Highlights the most sex-distinctive skull-muscle relationships; quantitative results not reported in abstract.
- **Strengths**: Permutation-based sparsity selection gives a data-driven way to choose the number of variables.
- **Limitations**: Very small sample (n=21); non-causal; software not stated.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Relevant for multimodal, group-conditional association problems, not for effect estimation.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/biomtc/ujag181 / PMID:42961296
