## 1. Laundered Survival: When Target Trial Emulation Doesn't Emulate — GLP-1 RAs and Cancer Outcomes — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Journal of Clinical Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-20
- **Authors**: Brophy JM et al.
- **Type**: simulation
- **Aims**: Show that TTE invoked without eligibility–assignment–time-zero (EAT0) alignment produces spurious protective GLP-1 RA–cancer associations.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Monte Carlo simulation, 500 replications, n=5,000 each, reproducing two published GLP-1 RA cohort designs under the null.
  - **Estimand / target**: Hazard ratio for GLP-1 RA vs comparator on cancer outcomes; true HR=1.0.
  - **Identification**: Time-zero alignment of eligibility, treatment assignment and follow-up start; post-baseline exposure definition (≥2 prescriptions) violates it.
  - **Estimator / model**: Cox-type HR comparing flawed exposure definition against EAT0-aligned analysis; varying interval between first and second prescription.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Varied prescription interval and follow-up length; compared simulated HRs against published estimates.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Flawed designs yielded HRs 0.72–0.39 (long follow-up) and 0.85–0.60 (short), mimicking published results; EAT0 alignment recovered HR≈1.0.
- **Strengths**: Directly reproduces published designs, quantifying immortal time bias magnitude.
- **Limitations**: Simulation reflects assumed data-generating mechanism; no re-analysis of the original cohorts.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Check that treatment is assigned at time zero; "target trial" labels do not prevent immortal time bias.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.jclinepi.2026.112595 / PMID:43064262

## 2. Evaluating the Strength of Instrumental Variables in the Evaluation of Time-Varying Treatments — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Biometrical Journal
- **Pub date**: 2026-12 (issue date; electronic entry within window)
- **Authors**: Tompsett D et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Propose a robust F-statistic for instrument strength with time-varying instruments and treatments.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulation study plus applied example of second-line oral anti-diabetic treatments in UK type 2 diabetes patients.
  - **Estimand / target**: Instrument strength (first-stage association) for time-varying treatment effects; prescribing preference instruments.
  - **Identification**: IV relevance assessed under autocorrelation and heteroskedasticity; standard F assumes homoscedastic, serially uncorrelated data.
  - **Estimator / model**: Heteroskedasticity- and autocorrelation-robust F-statistic in two-stage least squares setting.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulations compare robust F against standard F-statistics.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Robust F-statistic better reflects instrument strength than standard versions under serial correlation; numerical results not reported in abstract.
- **Strengths**: Addresses a practical gap for longitudinal IV analyses with a real-world example.
- **Limitations**: Abstract gives no quantitative performance results or code.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use a robust F-statistic when screening time-varying prescribing-preference instruments, not the conventional F.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/bimj.70200 / PMID:43064525

## 3. A unified approach to generalized entropy calibration with missing data — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Biometrics
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-07
- **Authors**: Kwon Y et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Build a single set of doubly robust calibration weights under MAR via generalized entropy maximization.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Theory, simulations, and NHANES application.
  - **Estimand / target**: Population mean under missing at random.
  - **Identification**: MAR; correct specification of either outcome regression or response propensity model.
  - **Estimator / model**: Entropy calibration weights with OR calibration, IPW debiasing and Neyman-orthogonality constraints; influence function invariant to which model is correct.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulations under misspecification versus AIPW, empirical likelihood, exponential tilting and other calibration estimators.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Improved stability and efficiency versus AIPW and competitors, especially under misspecification; variance minimized within entropy class when OR correct.
- **Strengths**: Doubly robust inference without model selection or variance switching.
- **Limitations**: Relies on MAR; no code reported.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Consider calibration-weight estimators over AIPW when nuisance models may be misspecified.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/biomtc/ujag192 / PMID:43062474

## 4. Applying Target Trial Emulation in Vaccine Research: A Scoping Review — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Network Open
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-02
- **Authors**: Dai H et al.
- **Type**: review
- **Aims**: Assess methodological rigor, reporting and regulatory alignment of target trial emulation in vaccine studies.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Scoping review of PubMed, Embase, Web of Science (2021–Aug 2025); 79 studies, mostly COVID-19.
  - **Estimand / target**: Not applicable; assessed adherence to TTE core components (eligibility, strategies, time zero, contrasts, analysis plan).
  - **Identification**: Two-reviewer extraction of TTE protocol elements, time-zero definition, bias handling.
  - **Estimator / model**: Descriptive synthesis.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Independent dual screening and extraction.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: 31.6% documented protocols; 77.2% defined time zero; 32.9% used negative controls; only 2.5% used E-values.
- **Strengths**: Maps reporting gaps against regulatory RWE guidance.
- **Limitations**: Descriptive; reporting quality not equated with validity.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Pre-register TTE protocols, validate operational definitions, and add negative controls and quantitative bias analysis.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.43921 / PMID:43062831

## 5. Mortality From Winter Storm Uri and Texas Power Outages Among Medicare Patients — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Network Open
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-02
- **Authors**: Berlin NL et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Estimate mortality and hospitalization effects of Winter Storm Uri and power outages in Texas Medicare beneficiaries.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Quasi-experimental cohort; 100% Texas Medicare sample 2019–2023; county-week data, 2021 vs 2022 control year.
  - **Estimand / target**: Excess weekly deaths and hospitalizations per 10,000 beneficiaries during storm week and 4 weeks.
  - **Identification**: Difference-in-differences with same counties in following year; parallel-trend-type assumption on seasonal patterns.
  - **Estimator / model**: DiD regression; subgroup analyses by power outage quartile and low income.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Stratification by outage quartile (dose-response); other checks not reported.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Storm-week excess mortality 2.94 (95% CI 2.12–3.76) per 10,000; no excess in lowest-outage counties (0.66, −2.16 to 3.48).
- **Strengths**: Complete Medicare population with dose-response by outage exposure.
- **Limitations**: Control is a different calendar year; county-level exposure.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use same-unit prior/following-year DiD for disaster impact with exposure-gradient checks to support causal reading.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.45376 / PMID:43062828
