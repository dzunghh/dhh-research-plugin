## 1. Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drug Versus Acetaminophen Initiation at Influenza Diagnosis and 14-Day Neuropsychiatric Diagnoses in Adults: A Target Trial Emulation Using Japanese Claims Data — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Pharmacoepidemiology and Drug Safety
- **Pub date**: 2026-11 (EDAT 2026-10)
- **Authors**: Kutsuna S et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Estimate 14-day neuropsychiatric diagnosis risk after NSAID versus acetaminophen initiation at influenza diagnosis in adults.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Active-comparator new-user target trial emulation; JMDC claims 2018-2025; 1,125,391 adults aged 18-64; protocol registered (UMIN000062444).
  - **Estimand / target**: Observational analogue of the intention-to-treat effect: 14-day risk ratio and risk difference in the overlap population.
  - **Identification**: Conditional exchangeability given covariates; time zero aligned with diagnosis and same-day prescription; new-user design; residual confounding by presenting symptoms acknowledged.
  - **Estimator / model**: Propensity-score overlap weighting (ATO), weighted 14-day risks.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Alternative outcome definitions and sensitivity analyses; results consistent.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Overlap-weighted risk 79.6 vs 71.2 per 100,000; RR 1.12 (0.92-1.36), RD 8.4 (-7.2 to 24.0) per 100,000.
- **Strengths**: Registered protocol, very large sample, clear time-zero and active comparator.
- **Limitations**: Claims-based outcome and symptom confounding; diclofenac and mefenamic acid too rare to evaluate.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Overlap weighting with explicit time zero suits rare-outcome claims studies; report the confidence interval's upper bound alongside a null result.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/pds.70513 / PMID:42952435

## 2. Bivariate Copula Models With Event-Free Cure Effect for Survival Extrapolations and Individual-Level Correlation Analysis — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-11 (EDAT 2026-10)
- **Authors**: Sharpe DJ et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Jointly model OS and PFS/RFS with a single event-free cure fraction for more cautious OS extrapolation.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Applied to two nivolumab trials: previously treated advanced esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (OS-PFS) and resected melanoma (OS-RFS).
  - **Estimand / target**: Extrapolated OS distribution, cure fraction, and patient-level OS-PFS/RFS correlation.
  - **Identification**: Copula dependence between marginal survival times; cure applies only to patients event-free; marginals chosen beforehand.
  - **Estimator / model**: Bivariate mixture cure models with five candidate copulas; model averaging; conditional OS given intermediate event times.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Comparison with univariate cure models; copula sensitivity of correlation coefficients.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Bivariate extrapolations were more conservative than univariate; correlation estimates were copula-sensitive, mitigated by model averaging.
- **Strengths**: Practical copula selection framework tied to clinically interpretable conditional OS distributions.
- **Limitations**: Only two case studies; no reported simulation of operating characteristics in the abstract.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Useful for HTA extrapolation with immature OS when cure after progression or recurrence is implausible.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70763 / PMID:42952322

## 3. Online Updating Mixture Cure Models for Large-Scale Survival Data Streams — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-11 (EDAT 2026-10)
- **Authors**: Jin L et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Enable mixture cure model estimation and variable selection on streaming survival data without storing historical records.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulations plus SEER breast cancer application.
  - **Estimand / target**: Cure-fraction (incidence) and latency model parameters, updated batchwise.
  - **Identification**: Standard mixture cure assumptions; historical information aggregated into summary statistics.
  - **Estimator / model**: Renewable penalized EM algorithm with a tuning-free strategy; asymptotic properties established.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulations for accuracy, prediction and computational scalability.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Reported accurate estimation, good predictive performance and scalability; numeric results not in abstract.
- **Strengths**: Privacy-friendly and memory-light with theoretical guarantees.
- **Limitations**: Cure-model identifiability with short follow-up not addressed in abstract; code availability unclear.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Consider for large registry streams with long-term survivors when raw data cannot be pooled.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70795 / PMID:42947606

## 4. Best Practice Methods for Living Evidence Synthesis in Health Care: An International Modified Delphi Survey — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: Journal of Clinical Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-29
- **Authors**: Golob MM et al.
- **Type**: tutorial/guidance
- **Aims**: Establish consensus best-practice statements for conducting, publishing and implementing living evidence syntheses.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Three-round modified Delphi, 29 experts, January-April 2025; 22 final statements.
  - **Estimand / target**: Consensus (≥80% agreement, ≥85% response per round) on 22 statements.
  - **Identification**: not applicable (consensus method).
  - **Estimator / model**: Five-point Likert ratings with qualitative feedback driving revisions; extends PRISMA-LSR.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Iterative rounds; response rates 90-93%.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: 19 of 22 statements reached consensus: 12 conduct, 2 reporting, 4 publishing, 1 implementation/appraisal.
- **Strengths**: First international consensus on living evidence synthesis with global organisational representation.
- **Limitations**: Small expert panel; no consensus on three statements; appraisal tools still lacking.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use as a checklist alongside PRISMA-LSR when setting up version control and update units for living reviews.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.jclinepi.2026.112574 / PMID:42956975

## 5. Projected obstetric risks in an aging maternal population in Taiwan: a Bayesian age-period-cohort analysis — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: International Journal of Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-14
- **Authors**: Chuang H-M et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis (descriptive projection; not causal)
- **Aims**: Project maternal and neonatal outcome prevalence and burden to 2050 under population ageing.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Linked National Health Insurance and Birth Certificate data, all Taiwan singleton pregnancies 2004-2021.
  - **Estimand / target**: Age-, period- and cohort-specific prevalence and projected absolute burden through 2050.
  - **Identification**: Descriptive; projection assumes continuation of period and cohort trends; one-step GDM screening change after 2010 flagged.
  - **Estimator / model**: Bayesian age-period-cohort (BAPC) models with credible intervals.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: GDM estimate caveated for screening change; no formal validation reported.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Projected 2050 GHTN 26.4% (95% CrI 5.2-67.4) and GDM 60.9% (28.9-85.0).
- **Strengths**: Population-wide linked data with many outcomes and transparent uncertainty.
- **Limitations**: Very wide credible intervals; extrapolation sensitive to diagnostic and screening changes.
- **Takeaway for practice**: BAPC suits burden projection, but treat long-horizon intervals and policy-driven coding changes cautiously.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/ije/dyag230 / PMID:42945353
