## 1. Assessing the Impact of Intercurrent Events on Power and Sample Size for Estimands With Time-to-Event Endpoints — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-11 (day not reported)
- **Authors**: Bratton DJ et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Derive closed-form power/sample-size formulae for ICH E9(R1) estimands with time-to-event endpoints that account for intercurrent events (IEs).
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Analytic derivation plus simulation; illustrative case study in nasal polyposis trial design.
  - **Estimand / target**: Treatment-policy, hypothetical, composite and combined-strategy estimands for time-to-event outcomes (hazard-based treatment effect).
  - **Identification**: Constant hazards; IE risk independent of other IEs and primary endpoint; IE effect on endpoint determined by handling strategy.
  - **Estimator / model**: Exponential/constant-hazard power formulae; assurance calculations.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulation checking accuracy under dependent event times, non-constant hazards and variable follow-up.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Formulae give rapid, accurate power versus simulation under stated assumptions; sample size is sensitive to IE rates and post-IE outcome behaviour.
- **Strengths**: Fast, transparent alternative to heavy simulation that ties trial sizing directly to the chosen estimand.
- **Limitations**: Relies on constant hazards and independence of IEs from outcome risk, often implausible in practice.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use when planning trials with explicit estimands to check whether IE rates and strategy choice leave the study under- or overpowered.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70804 / PMID:43058056

## 2. A Bayesian Approach for Fitting Semi-Markov Mixture Models of Cancer Latency to Individual-Level Data — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-11 (day not reported)
- **Authors**: Morsomme RN et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Develop an efficient Bayesian sampler to fit semi-Markov mixture natural-history models to individual-level screening and diagnosis histories.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulation plus application to US Breast Cancer Surveillance Consortium mammography screening cohort; N not reported.
  - **Estimand / target**: Natural-history parameters (preclinical sojourn, indolence fraction) and screening-related overdiagnosis rate.
  - **Identification**: Semi-Markov multi-state structure with mixture of progressive/indolent latent tumours; screening sensitivity modelled.
  - **Estimator / model**: Fully Bayesian data-augmented MCMC jointly sampling parameters and latent onset times.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulation showing efficient posterior exploration and parameter recovery.
- **Software**: R package baclava
- **Key findings**: Sampler efficiently explores the joint posterior; applied model yields uncertainty-quantified overdiagnosis estimates for mammography (numbers not in abstract).
- **Strengths**: Rigorous uncertainty quantification for non-Markovian models with released R implementation.
- **Limitations**: Overdiagnosis estimates hinge on untestable latent-structure assumptions; abstract reports no numeric estimates.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Useful for screening evaluation and overdiagnosis estimation from individual-level cohort data via baclava.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70844 / PMID:43068885

## 3. Spatial cluster randomized trials - sampling design with spillover effects & spatial dependence — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: BMC Medical Research Methodology
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-26
- **Authors**: Walther A et al.
- **Type**: simulation
- **Aims**: Compare treatment-allocation designs for cluster randomized trials when spillover (interference) and spatial dependence are present.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulation over spatial grids of varying size, ranges of spillover magnitude and spatial autocorrelation.
  - **Estimand / target**: Direct intervention effect in presence of spillover to neighbouring clusters.
  - **Identification**: Randomization; spillover modelled via neighbour-treatment indicators (partial interference by spatial adjacency).
  - **Estimator / model**: Spatial autoregressive (SAR) model; simple random sampling vs block stratified allocation.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: MSE across all treatment combinations; worst-case allocation risk.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: SRS gives lower average MSE in some scenarios; BSS gives near-zero variation in MSE across allocations; both biased when treatment and spillover indicators are collinear.
- **Strengths**: Explicitly addresses interference, a frequently ignored violation of SUTVA in cluster trials.
- **Limitations**: Grid-based simulation only; SAR model-dependent; no real-trial application.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Consider block stratified allocation when spillover into control clusters is expected, to bound worst-case estimation error.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1186/s12874-026-02968-0 / PMID:43058491

## 4. Robust Bayesian Variable Selection and Inference Under Misspecified Nonparametric Mixed Models in Longitudinal Studies — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-11 (day not reported)
- **Authors**: Fan K et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Provide robust variable selection and valid inference for high-dimensional nonlinear longitudinal gene–environment interactions under model misspecification.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulations plus Childhood Asthma Management Program (CAMP) subset with high-dimensional SNPs.
  - **Estimand / target**: Main and nonlinear time-varying interaction effects in a mixed model.
  - **Identification**: not applicable (associational); robustness to outliers, heavy tails and misspecified interaction forms.
  - **Estimator / model**: Sparse robust nonparametric Bayesian mixed model; Gibbs sampler with spike-and-slab priors.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulation comparing identification, estimation and credible-interval coverage with alternatives.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Superior selection and estimation versus alternatives; credible intervals achieved nominal finite-sample coverage under misspecification.
- **Strengths**: Combines exact sparsity with robust, calibrated uncertainty in longitudinal settings.
- **Limitations**: Non-causal; computationally heavy MCMC; no software reported.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Consider for longitudinal omics interaction screening where outliers and misspecification threaten standard mixed-model inference.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70830 / PMID:43046303

## 5. Impact of prompt engineering and workflow modifications on the performance of a Delphi-based automated abstract screening system in a psychiatric systematic review — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: BMC Medical Research Methodology
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-27
- **Authors**: Tolend M et al.
- **Type**: simulation
- **Aims**: Evaluate how prompt wording and workflow settings affect recall and efficiency of a five-LLM Delphi-ensemble abstract screener.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Methodological experiment on a published autism-biomarker systematic review; 4,745 abstracts, 1,655 human-judged eligible.
  - **Estimand / target**: Recall and work saved over sampling at 95% recall (WSS@95%).
  - **Identification**: not applicable; human screening as reference standard.
  - **Estimator / model**: Delphi ensemble of five open-weight LLMs; 12 experiments varying prompts, temperature, embeddings, few-shot sampler, safety net.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Comparison across prompt variants and workflow parameter settings.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Recall ranged 59–99% and WSS@95% 13–42%; simplified prompt balanced recall 98% with WSS@95% 41–42%.
- **Strengths**: Locally deployed open-weight models support privacy and reproducibility; systematic ablation of design choices.
- **Limitations**: Single review topic; generalisability to other domains untested.
- **Takeaway for practice**: If using LLM screening for evidence synthesis, favour simplified eligibility prompts and validate recall against a human-screened subset.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1186/s12874-026-02981-3 / PMID:43058497
