## 1. Prognostic Accuracy of Biomarkers for Cure Status Prediction Under Diverse Censoring Patterns — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-11 (day not reported)
- **Authors**: Liu X et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Estimate how well biomarkers discriminate cured from uncured individuals when cure status is only partially observed because of interval censoring.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Methods development with simulation studies and an application to the ADNI study; sample sizes not reported in abstract.
  - **Estimand / target**: Sensitivity, specificity, positive and negative predictive values of a biomarker for latent cure status (non-causal, prognostic accuracy parameters).
  - **Identification**: Mixture cure model structure separating incidence (cure probability) from latency (event timing among uncured) under interval censoring; further assumptions not reported in abstract.
  - **Estimator / model**: Semiparametric mixture cure models: logistic or flexible single-index incidence component, semiparametric transformation model for latency.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulation studies in finite samples; comparison against conventional survival-analysis approaches; real-data illustration in ADNI.
- **Software**: "User-friendly estimation tool" mentioned; package name and repository not reported in abstract.
- **Key findings**: Proposed estimators showed satisfactory finite-sample performance and greater flexibility than conventional survival approaches; numerical bias/coverage not reported in abstract.
- **Strengths**: Extends cure-status accuracy measures to interval censoring and relaxes the logistic incidence assumption via single-index models.
- **Limitations**: Relies on correct mixture cure specification and an identifiable cured fraction; quantitative performance and software details are absent from the abstract.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Consider when evaluating biomarkers in periodically assessed cohorts (e.g. dementia conversion) where a plateau suggests a never-event subgroup.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70815 / PMID:43035349

## 2. AI as gatekeeper: a cross-sectional study of AI-generated peer review of landmark oncological studies — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: BMC Medical Research Methodology
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-18
- **Authors**: Erdat EC et al.
- **Type**: applied meta-research (cross-sectional evaluation)
- **Aims**: Compare the peer-review behaviour of three large language models on anonymised landmark oncology trials.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Cross-sectional study; 50 blinded landmark oncology trials from ASCO, ESMO and NCCN guidelines; three LLMs given one standardized prompt.
  - **Estimand / target**: Between-model differences in verdicts, trial awareness, journal-suitability assessment and concerns across five domains; non-causal descriptive contrasts.
  - **Identification**: Not applicable (descriptive comparison); manuscripts anonymised; two investigators independently coded reviews.
  - **Estimator / model**: Chi-square tests with Bonferroni correction; logistic regression for predictors of critical verdicts.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Dual independent coding; no human-reviewer comparator or repeated-run variability assessment reported in abstract.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Verdicts differed across models (p < 0.001); trial awareness ranged 14%–54%. Statistical concerns alone predicted critical verdicts (OR 8.57; 95% CI 1.28–57.4).
- **Strengths**: Standardized prompt, blinded manuscripts and a defined trial sample make the between-model comparison transparent; open access.
- **Limitations**: Already-accepted landmark trials give no ground truth for review quality, and the wide CI reflects only 50 manuscripts.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Treat LLM review output as model-dependent; do not substitute it for statistical review without a standardized framework and human oversight.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1186/s12874-026-02978-y / PMID:43021090
