## 1. Long-term effect estimation when combining clinical trial and observational follow-up datasets — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the American Statistical Association
- **Pub date**: 2026 (epub 2025-08-27)
- **Authors**: Cheng G et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Estimate long-term treatment effects when trial participants are incompletely linked to observational follow-up data, framing incomplete linkage as missing data.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Trial linked to Medicare claims follow-up; simulations plus SWOG prostate cancer trial application; N not reported.
  - **Estimand / target**: Long-term treatment effect defined through Cox model hazard ratio.
  - **Identification**: Non-linked-as-censored needs correct Cox model and conditional independence; conditional linking at random (CLAR) assumption for weighting approaches.
  - **Estimator / model**: NLAC; inverse probability of linkage weighting; augmented IPW; extension to time-dependent covariates.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulation studies confirm validity; real-data application to SWOG study.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Simulations confirm validity of the proposed estimators; IPW/AIPW give robustness to Cox misspecification. Numeric results not reported in abstract.
- **Strengths**: Formalizes incomplete trial–registry linkage as missing data with a doubly robust-style option.
- **Limitations**: Relies on the untestable CLAR assumption and a Cox-based estimand.
- **Takeaway for practice**: When extending trial follow-up via claims linkage, model linkage probability rather than treating unlinked participants as censored.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1080/01621459.2025.2526703 / PMID:43075269

## 2. Two-sample survival probability curves: a graphical method for survival data beyond right censoring — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: BMC Medical Research Methodology
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-26
- **Authors**: Castro-Pearson S et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Extend two-sample survival probability curves to interval-censored, doubly censored and truncated data for treatment comparison.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Monte-Carlo simulations plus well-known survival datasets with interval, double and truncated censoring.
  - **Estimand / target**: Treatment comparison via survival probability curves in [0,1]×[0,1] space, avoiding hazard ratio.
  - **Identification**: Non-informative censoring/truncation assumed; not reported in detail.
  - **Estimator / model**: One-sample NPMLEs (Kaplan-Meier analogues) combined into two-sample curves.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Monte-Carlo performance evaluation; model-fit adequacy assessed graphically.
- **Software**: R package developed (name not reported)
- **Key findings**: Method works across several censoring/truncation mechanisms in examples. Quantitative simulation results not reported in abstract.
- **Strengths**: Avoids discarding information by forcing right-censoring; R package provided.
- **Limitations**: Descriptive graphical tool; not a causal estimator with confounding control.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use for interval-censored or truncated trial data where the hazard ratio is hard to interpret.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1186/s12874-026-02986-y / PMID:43073665

## 4. Propensity score estimation: assessing machine learning methods for an observational prison cohort — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: BMC Medical Research Methodology
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-21
- **Authors**: Altermatt A et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Compare ML algorithms against logistic regression for propensity score covariate balance in a small real-world cohort.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Prison-recruited Australian cohort, N=328 men; emotional support vs ED presentation within 100 days post-release.
  - **Estimand / target**: Effect of emotional support in prison on ED presentation (estimand type not reported).
  - **Identification**: Measured confounding balanced via propensity weighting.
  - **Estimator / model**: PS weighting with logistic regression, gradient boosting, random forest, single-hidden-layer neural network.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Balance assessed by average standardised absolute mean difference (ASAM).
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: ASAM fell from 17.7 unweighted to 10.6 (neural net), 13.7 (RF), 14.3 (logistic), 17.1 (boosting).
- **Strengths**: Real-data comparison in a small-sample setting where most ML-PS evidence is simulated.
- **Limitations**: Single dataset; balance metric only, no outcome-effect estimate or bias/variance assessment.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Compare several PS estimators on balance, but do not infer superiority from one small cohort.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1186/s12874-026-02974-2 / PMID:43073767

- **Pub date**: 2026-11-20
- **Authors**: Hagar L et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Determine sample sizes robust to multiple data-generating mechanisms with far fewer simulations.
- **Methodology**:

  - **Estimand / target**: Power across sample-size space for M-estimator-based tests.
  - **Identification**: not reported (design-stage method)
  - **Estimator / model**: Model p-values as a function of n; simulate at only two sample sizes per data-generating mechanism.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Sensitivity of sample size to data-generating assumptions across mechanisms.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Power curves obtained from two simulated sample sizes per mechanism. Quantitative savings not reported.
- **Strengths**: Cuts simulation burden for robust-analysis (M-estimator) designs.
- **Limitations**: Relies on theoretical p-value approximation; validity beyond M-estimators unclear.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use for power planning when analysis uses robust or estimating-equation methods and the data-generating process is uncertain.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1177/09622802261509367 / PMID:43062319
