## 1. Efficient estimation of average causal effect with mismeasured exposures — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Statistical Methods in Medical Research
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-10
- **Authors**: Sui Z et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Estimate the average causal effect when exposure is misclassified, using a limited validation subsample and no model for the misclassification mechanism.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Two-phase design (misclassified main sample plus validated subsample); simulations; Global Enteric Multicenter Study case-control application (ELISA vs TaqMan Array Card).
  - **Estimand / target**: Average causal effect of true exposure status.
  - **Identification**: Validated subsample gives true exposure; no parametric assumptions about the mismeasurement mechanism. Other assumptions not reported in abstract.
  - **Estimator / model**: Efficient semiparametric-style estimator combining main and validation samples, with large-sample properties for inference.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Extensive simulation scenarios versus validation-only estimator.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: 39%-53% variance reduction versus validation-only estimator in simulations. Application revealed substantial adjustment of pathogen-specific diarrhea associations distorted by diagnostic error.
- **Strengths**: Avoids modeling misclassification, a common practical barrier.
- **Limitations**: Requires a validation subsample with correctly ascertained exposure; code availability not reported.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Consider when a gold-standard test exists on a subset and diagnostic misclassification threatens causal estimates.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1177/09622802261506990 / PMID:43006699

## 3. Orthogonal Factorial Designs for Trials of Therapist-Delivered Interventions: Randomizing Intervention-Therapist Combinations to Patients — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-11
- **Authors**: Walwyn REA et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Propose crossed therapist-intervention factorial designs separating therapist effects from intervention effects in randomized trials.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Randomized therapist-intervention combinations; small simulation study; psychotherapy-type trials.
  - **Estimand / target**: Intervention effect, between-therapist variance, and between-therapist variance in intervention effect.
  - **Identification**: Randomization of combinations to patients; allows therapist learning and centre effects.
  - **Estimator / model**: Classical design-of-experiments orthogonal designs analysed by ANOVA and regression (fixed intervention, random therapist).
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Small simulation of effect and standard-error estimation.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Simulations explored estimation of intervention effect, its SE and variance components; numeric results not reported in abstract.
- **Strengths**: Principled handling of treatment-related clustering via DoE theory.
- **Limitations**: Simulation is small; requires every therapist to deliver every intervention.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Consider crossed designs when therapist variation is itself a scientific target.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70813 / PMID:43006738

## 4. A comprehensive simulation study evaluating the predictive performance of Cox proportional hazards model and machine learning methods for time-to-event data — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: BMC Medical Research Methodology
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-10
- **Authors**: Otieno JA et al.
- **Type**: simulation
- **Aims**: Compare Cox with machine-learning survival models for prediction under varied data-generating mechanisms.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulated datasets varying sample size, censoring, noise variables and misspecification, plus a real stroke dataset.
  - **Estimand / target**: Predictive performance (C-index, Integrated Brier Score).
  - **Identification**: Not applicable (prediction).
  - **Estimator / model**: Cox PH, random survival forests, XGBoost, DeepSurv.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Scenarios across misspecification, censoring and noise; real-data comparison.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Tree-based models outperformed Cox and DeepSurv under misspecification and many noise variables; Cox did well with large N, few noise variables, correct specification.
- **Strengths**: Systematic, fair comparison on equal-sized datasets.
- **Limitations**: Prediction-focused; no causal or calibration emphasis reported.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Choose survival models by data structure; Cox remains competitive when well specified.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1186/s12874-026-03050-5 / PMID:43004245
