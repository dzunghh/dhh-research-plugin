## 1. Use of Sparse Internal Validation Data — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-26
- **Authors**: Lash TL et al.
- **Type**: simulation
- **Aims**: Determine which strategies improve quantitative bias adjustment informed by small internal validation sub-studies.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulated small cohorts with nested balanced validation sub-study; twelve strategy combinations compared against misclassified cohorts.
  - **Estimand / target**: Bias-adjusted exposure–outcome association under misclassification; evaluated by bias, coverage and RMSE.
  - **Identification**: Validation sub-study supplies predictive values (bias parameters) for misclassification correction; assumes validation sample is representative of the cohort.
  - **Estimator / model**: Probabilistic bias analysis with beta-distributed predictive values, optional weak prior, and multiple imputation of gold-standard measurement.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulation comparing 12 combinations: prior vs none, three vs four predictive values, imputing only non-sampled members.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: All 12 strategies reduced bias and improved coverage versus misclassified estimates, but raised RMSE and widened intervals. Priors sometimes helped; other restrictions added complexity without benefit.
- **Strengths**: Directly practical guidance for a common situation (sparse validation data) using transparent simulation.
- **Limitations**: Simulation-only, small cohort scenarios, balanced validation design; code availability not reported.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use the simple approach (all four predictive values, imputing everyone), consider a weak prior, and expect wider intervals.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1097/EDE.0000000000002057 / PMID:42929177

## 2. A weighted casebase framework for outcome-dependent two-phase sampling designs — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: BMC Medical Research Methodology
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-29
- **Authors**: Kwan K et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Extend casebase hazard regression with phase-two inclusion weights for smooth hazards and absolute risk under two-phase sampling.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulations versus weighted Cox; illustration using frequency-matched biomarker substudy in the Ontario Health Study.
  - **Estimand / target**: Log-hazard coefficients and smooth absolute-risk curves.
  - **Identification**: Known phase-two sampling probabilities enable inverse-probability weighting; standard regularity conditions for consistency and asymptotic normality.
  - **Estimator / model**: Casebase logistic regression on sampled person-moments with offset and IPW; participant-level M-estimator with dfbeta jackknife/sandwich variance.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulation scenarios incl. skewed covariates and fixed phase-two sizes; discrimination and weighted Brier score compared with Cox.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Coefficient performance similar to weighted Cox; small bias, near-nominal coverage for binary covariates. Both unstable with skewed covariates and large weights.
- **Strengths**: GLM-based route to absolute risk with efficient variance estimator and formal asymptotics.
- **Limitations**: Finite-sample instability with skewed covariates and large weights; complements rather than replaces Cox.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Consider when you need smooth absolute risk from case-cohort or two-phase biomarker studies.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1186/s12874-026-02957-3 / PMID:42928138

## 3. Stroke and Bleeding Risk in Long-Term Care Residents With Atrial Fibrillation Who Discontinue Anticoagulation — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Network Open
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-01
- **Authors**: Kapoor A et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Estimate effect of anticoagulation discontinuation versus continuation on stroke and bleeding in LTC residents with AF.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Target trial emulation in US Medicare claims for LTC residents entering April–December 2020; N=15,426.
  - **Estimand / target**: Per-protocol effect of discontinue versus continue strategies on stroke and bleeding (sHR) and death (cause-specific HR).
  - **Identification**: Clone-censor-weight; assumes no unmeasured confounding of adherence to strategy and positivity.
  - **Estimator / model**: Cloning with inverse probability of censoring weights; Fine-Gray subdistribution hazards with competing mortality.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Limitations acknowledged qualitatively; no quantitative bias analysis reported in abstract.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Stroke sHR 1.07 (0.93–1.23); bleeding sHR 1.02 (0.92–1.13); mortality HR 1.27 (1.23–1.31) for discontinuers.
- **Strengths**: Explicit target trial framework with clone-censor-weighting avoids immortal time bias.
- **Limitations**: Very high mortality, unmeasured confounding and claims-based outcome ascertainment.
- **Takeaway for practice**: A template for emulating discontinuation (deprescribing) trials with CCW and competing risks.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.40964 / PMID:42931771
