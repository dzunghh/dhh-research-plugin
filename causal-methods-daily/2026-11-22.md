## 1. Interpretability of estimands based on mean function regression models for recurrent and terminal events — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Statistical Methods in Medical Research
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-20
- **Authors**: Bühler A et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Clarify what treatment-effect estimands the Ghosh-Lin and Mao-Lin mean-function models actually target under different underlying intensity processes.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Theoretical limiting-value analysis under intensity-based models; illustrated on a palliative trial in bone-metastatic breast cancer.
  - **Estimand / target**: Cumulative mean number of recurrent events (Ghosh-Lin); composite recurrent-terminal event outcome (Mao-Lin); multiplicative treatment effects.
  - **Identification**: Randomized treatment; interpretation depends on the intensity model and terminal-event (death) dependence.
  - **Estimator / model**: Ghosh-Lin and Mao-Lin mean function regression with multiplicative covariate effects; limiting values derived under several intensity models.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Comparison of limiting values across intensity-model specifications; trial application.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Estimand interpretation varies with the true intensity structure and terminal-event dependence; the paper gives recommendations on which model to use.
- **Strengths**: Directly addresses estimand clarity (ICH E9(R1)-style) for recurrent events with competing death.
- **Limitations**: Abstract reports no numerical results; software and code availability not stated.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Pick Ghosh-Lin versus Mao-Lin by the clinical estimand wanted, not convention, and check intensity-model assumptions.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1177/09622802261507853 / PMID:43061925

## 2. Graphical Approaches for Multiple Comparison Procedures Using Mixed Types of Intersection Tests — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Biometrical Journal
- **Pub date**: 2026-12-01
- **Authors**: Xi D et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Unify graphical multiple-testing procedures that mix weighted Bonferroni, Simes, Hochberg and parametric intersection tests in one closed procedure.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Theoretical framework with proof; illustrated on a clinical trial example.
  - **Estimand / target**: Strong familywise error rate control across hypothesis groups.
  - **Identification**: Each group-level test valid; no assumption on between-group dependence; closure principle.
  - **Estimator / model**: Hypotheses partitioned into groups, each tested by its own intersection test at a group-specific level, combined by closure.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Proof of dominance: at least as powerful as weighted-Bonferroni-only graphical procedure; trial illustration.
- **Software**: R package graphicalMCP
- **Key findings**: Strong FWER control proven; power at least that of the Bonferroni-only procedure.
- **Strengths**: Open implementation and a general proof without dependence assumptions.
- **Limitations**: Power gains not quantified in the abstract.
- **Takeaway for practice**: For multi-endpoint confirmatory trials, use mixed-test graphical procedures via graphicalMCP when some hypotheses justify Simes or parametric tests.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/bimj.70205 / PMID:43064500

## 3. The fundamental problem of risk prediction for individuals: health AI, uncertainty, and personalized medicine — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Journal of Clinical Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-20
- **Authors**: Barreñada L et al.
- **Type**: simulation
- **Aims**: Show that individual-level risk estimates carry large estimation, model and applicability uncertainty even when population performance is good.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Real and synthetic ovarian cancer diagnosis data; 59,400 models trained; fixed test set of 100 patients.
  - **Estimand / target**: Variability of individual predicted probability of ovarian cancer across modeling and data choices.
  - **Identification**: Not applicable (prediction); varies training sample size, modeling choices, and measurement/population settings.
  - **Estimator / model**: Repeated model fitting across estimation, model and applicability uncertainty scenarios.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Large-scale model multiverse; variation by training sample size.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Model and applicability uncertainty dominated and stayed large as training size grew; estimation uncertainty shrank considerably.
- **Strengths**: Very large model multiverse exposing hidden instability.
- **Limitations**: Single clinical example; test set of 100 patients.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Report individual-level prediction instability, not only population metrics, and treat algorithm outputs as inputs to clinician judgment.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.jclinepi.2026.112596 / PMID:43064263

## 4. Planning adaptive trials supporting conditional and permanent registration using the same primary endpoint — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Statistical Methods in Medical Research
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-20
- **Authors**: Kluge L et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Design two-stage adaptive trials whose first stage supports conditional registration and whose combined data support permanent registration on one endpoint.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Two-stage confirmatory adaptive designs; regulatory context EMA conditional authorization and German DiGA fast-track.
  - **Estimand / target**: Overall probability of conditional and permanent registration; conditional power.
  - **Identification**: Randomized trial; type I error control by adaptive design theory.
  - **Estimator / model**: Interim sample-size recalculation targeting conditional power, with minimum and maximum sample sizes.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Analytical comparison of minimum, maximum and expected sample sizes against separate-study designs.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Adaptive designs usually perform much better than separate studies for conditional and permanent registration.
- **Strengths**: Links regulatory requirements to concrete design and power considerations.
- **Limitations**: Specific to the shared-endpoint fast-track scenario; code not reported.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Consider a single adaptive trial with sample-size recalculation instead of two separate studies for fast-track programs.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1177/09622802261508998 / PMID:43061938

## 5. A Flexible Test for Comparing Two Hazard Rate Functions With Arbitrary Differences — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-11
- **Authors**: Zhang X et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Provide an omnibus test sensitive to proportional, crossing and delayed-effect hazard differences.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulation studies of survival data under several hazard-difference patterns.
  - **Estimand / target**: Equality of two hazard rate functions under arbitrary difference patterns.
  - **Identification**: Not applicable (testing); no proportional hazards assumption required.
  - **Estimator / model**: Combination (Cauchy-based) of log-rank, weighted log-rank and a Fleming-Harrington test.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Numerical studies across proportional, crossing and time-lag scenarios.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Numerical studies show the combined test works well across the considered difference patterns; no numbers in abstract.
- **Strengths**: Handles crossing hazards and treatment lag without choosing a single weight in advance.
- **Limitations**: Simulation-only evidence in abstract; no software stated.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use a combination test when non-proportional hazards are plausible and the pattern cannot be prespecified.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70831 / PMID:43059197
