## 1. Loneliness and cognitive function in later life: a population-based counterfactual analysis — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Int J Epidemiol
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-18
- **Authors**: Sharma S et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Estimate population-level effect of preventing loneliness on cognitive decline, and the share mediated by depression.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Health and Retirement Study 1996–2018, n=34,774, adults aged 50–80, repeated biennial waves.
  - **Estimand / target**: Population-level effect of a hypothetical intervention preventing loneliness on cognitive score; total effect and proportion mediated via depression.
  - **Identification**: Sequential conditional exchangeability with time-varying confounding, positivity, correct model specification; depression as mediator.
  - **Estimator / model**: Parametric g-formula with Monte Carlo simulation of interventions; formal mediation decomposition; gender-stratified.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: not reported in abstract.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Prevention slows decline by 0.075 points (95% CI 0.063–0.089); 17.3% of the effect mediated via depression.
- **Strengths**: Handles time-varying confounding with a g-method and quantifies mediation.
- **Limitations**: Strong parametric/no-unmeasured-confounding assumptions; sensitivity analyses not reported in abstract.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use the parametric g-formula for hypothetical-intervention estimands with time-varying confounding in longitudinal cohorts.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/ije/dyag212 / PMID:42839716

## 2. Exposure misclassification of self-reported vegetarian diet and its impact on health outcomes — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Int J Epidemiol
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-18
- **Authors**: Ghoreishi N et al.
- **Type**: tutorial/guidance
- **Aims**: Show how exposure misclassification biases direction, size and uncertainty of diet–health associations using quantitative bias analysis.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: NHANES 2007–10, 11,180 participants; self-reported diet versus 24-hour recalls as reference.
  - **Estimand / target**: Bias-adjusted exposure–outcome association for four health outcomes under non-differential and differential misclassification.
  - **Identification**: Bias parameters (sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV) from validation; assumed misclassification structure.
  - **Estimator / model**: Quantitative bias analysis with sensitivity/specificity and PPV/NPV parameterisations; compared with conventional analysis.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Non-differential versus differential scenarios; sensitivity 32% (95% CI 26–38), specificity 98%.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: 68% of self-reported vegetarians reported eating meat/fish; misclassification was differential. PPV/NPV avoided negative adjusted cell counts.
- **Strengths**: Worked example showing PPV/NPV parameterisation avoids impossible cell counts.
- **Limitations**: Conclusions depend heavily on assumed bias-model structure.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Prefer PPV/NPV bias parameters when sensitivity/specificity adjustment yields negative cells; report differential scenarios.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/ije/dyag168 / PMID:42837416

## 3. Early Mitral Valve Surgery and Survival in Asymptomatic Severe Primary Mitral Regurgitation — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Netw Open
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-01
- **Authors**: Ahn J et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Estimate survival effect of early mitral valve surgery versus watchful waiting using target trial emulation.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Multicentre valve registry, 3 Korean tertiary hospitals 2000–2022, n=802 (344 early surgery).
  - **Estimand / target**: Per-protocol effect of surgery within 6 months versus waiting; restricted mean survival time difference (primary) and HR.
  - **Identification**: Clone-censor-weight with 6-month grace period; exchangeability given covariates; positivity.
  - **Estimator / model**: Clone-censor-weight with inverse probability of censoring weights; RMST differences and weighted Cox HR.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Subgroups (no LV decompensation, degenerative MR); further sensitivity not reported.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: 5-year RMST gain 66 days (95% CI 19–129); mortality HR 0.69 (0.41–0.94).
- **Strengths**: RMST as primary measure avoids proportional-hazards reliance and handles immortal time via cloning.
- **Limitations**: Small sample, few deaths (27 early surgery), observational confounding possible.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use clone-censor-weight with grace periods to emulate "treat within X months" strategies.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.37061 / PMID:42832200

## 4. Upfront or deferred radiotherapy combined with chemoimmunotherapy for inoperable oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma: a target trial emulation in China — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: EClinicalMedicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-22
- **Authors**: Liu H et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Compare survival under upfront versus deferred radiotherapy with first-line chemoimmunotherapy.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Multicentre retrospective cohort, eight Chinese hospitals 2018–2023, n=1,242; protocol registered (ChiCTR2500111871).
  - **Estimand / target**: Per-protocol absolute 3-year OS/PFS rate differences and cumulative risk ratios; adjusted HR.
  - **Identification**: Time zero at treatment start, 42-day grace period; baseline exchangeability; immortal time handled by cloning.
  - **Estimator / model**: Stabilised IPTW with clone-censor-weight; Kaplan–Meier based rate differences and risk ratios.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Alternative sensitivity and subgroup analyses; time-varying covariates not adjusted (acknowledged).
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: 3-year OS 41.9% vs 57.5% (RD −15.6%, 95% CI −26.3 to −5.6) for upfront versus deferred RT.
- **Strengths**: Explicit time-zero, grace period and immortal-time handling with registered protocol.
- **Limitations**: No adjustment for time-varying covariates; retrospective confounding by indication.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Pair clone-censor-weight with time-varying weights when treatment timing depends on evolving patient status.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.eclinm.2026.104236 / PMID:42857596

## 5. Continuous Glucose Monitoring and Long-Term Glycemic and Kidney Outcomes — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Netw Open
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-01
- **Authors**: Karter AJ et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Estimate long-term glycemic and kidney effects of sustained real-time CGM in high-risk type 2 diabetes.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Kaiser Permanente cohort 2015–2023; 2,771 rtCGM initiators vs 22,877 temporally matched CGM-naive controls sampled monthly from risk sets.
  - **Estimand / target**: Per-protocol cumulative difference in HbA1c, UACR and eGFR at 36 months.
  - **Identification**: Sequential-trial emulation with baseline at initiation; conditional exchangeability; positivity; informative censoring addressed by weights.
  - **Estimator / model**: Inverse probability-weighted marginal structural models with censoring at protocol deviation.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: not reported in abstract.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: HbA1c 7.83% vs 8.58% at 36 months; no eGFR difference. Heavy censoring: 44.3% of rtCGM arm.
- **Strengths**: Large cohort, per-protocol weighting for adherence and treatment switching.
- **Limitations**: Substantial artificial censoring raises positivity and weight-model concerns; sensitivity not reported.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Check weight distributions and positivity when per-protocol censoring exceeds ~40% in sequential trial emulations.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.37698 / PMID:42842246
