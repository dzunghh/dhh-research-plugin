## 1. Beyond fixed restriction time: adaptive restricted mean survival time methods in clinical trials — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Biometrika
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-22
- **Authors**: Sun J et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Data-driven selection of the RMST restriction time, with valid inference accounting for selection.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Theory, extensive simulations across survival scenarios, and a phase III pancreatic cancer trial with transient treatment effect.
  - **Estimand / target**: Difference in restricted mean survival time at an adaptively chosen restriction time.
  - **Identification**: Randomised comparison with right censoring; non-proportional hazards allowed; post-selection inference handles nonregularity under the null.
  - **Estimator / model**: Nonparametric RMST maximising an effect-size/precision criterion; convex-hull and penalised estimators; oracle-equivalence on a discrete grid.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulations for power and type I error versus fixed-time RMST and log-rank test.
- **Software**: R package AdaRMST
- **Key findings**: Higher power than fixed-time RMST and log-rank, with approximately nominal type I error. Found clinically meaningful benefit missed by standard methods in the pancreatic trial.
- **Strengths**: Rigorous post-selection theory plus released software.
- **Limitations**: Right-censored randomised-trial setting; no confounding adjustment for observational data in the abstract.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use when the treatment-effect timing is unknown and hazards are non-proportional, instead of arbitrarily picking a restriction time.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/biomet/asag056 / PMID:43101825

## 2. Heritable confounding of an exposure and outcome in Mendelian randomization studies — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: European Journal of Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-27
- **Authors**: Sanderson E et al.
- **Type**: simulation
- **Aims**: Show that small-effect genetic instruments are more likely to proxy heritable confounders, biasing MR estimates.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulations plus applied example of C-reactive protein on type 2 diabetes using GWAS summary data.
  - **Estimand / target**: Causal effect of exposure on outcome (e.g. CRP on type 2 diabetes).
  - **Identification**: MR instrument validity; violation of exclusion restriction when variants associate with heritable confounders.
  - **Estimator / model**: Common MR estimators compared; mitigation by multivariable MR and Steiger filtering.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Bias evaluated across instrument effect-size thresholds in simulation and applied analysis.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Bias runs in the direction of the confounded observational association, often larger, and is shared across common MR methods. Multivariable MR or Steiger filtering reduces it.
- **Strengths**: Explains why larger GWAS may worsen MR validity, with practical mitigations.
- **Limitations**: Abstract reports no quantitative bias magnitudes or code availability.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Consider heritable confounders when using weak, many-variant instruments; run multivariable MR or Steiger filtering as sensitivity analyses.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1007/s10654-026-01476-1 / PMID:43102763

## 3. Methods to estimate when to stop a treatment and to compare treatment durations using observational data: a methodological scoping review — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: BMC Medical Research Methodology
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-05
- **Authors**: Hao P et al.
- **Type**: review
- **Aims**: Map methods and design biases in observational studies comparing treatment durations or stopping versus continuing.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Scoping review of Medline, Embase, CINAHL, Google Scholar 2005-2023; 71 studies from 13,980 records.
  - **Estimand / target**: Effect of stopping versus continuing treatment, or of treatment duration strategies.
  - **Identification**: Appraised against target trial emulation elements: eligibility, treatment assignment, follow-up alignment.
  - **Estimator / model**: Not applicable; descriptive synthesis of design biases and methods.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Duplicate independent extraction by two reviewers.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: 89% of 56 duration studies had at least one design bias; 86% ignored time-varying confounding. 25% had immortal time.
- **Strengths**: Systematic, target-trial-based audit of a common but error-prone design.
- **Limitations**: Descriptive; does not compare estimator performance.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Align time zero, avoid post-baseline eligibility, and use g-methods for time-varying confounding in duration questions.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1186/s12874-026-02997-9 / PMID:43099349

## 4. The Complexity of Defining Estimands in Cohort Stepped Wedge Cluster Randomized Trials With Endogenous Covariates: A Call for Awareness — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: Biometrical Journal
- **Pub date**: 2026-12
- **Authors**: Basten J et al.
- **Type**: tutorial/guidance
- **Aims**: Clarify estimand definition in cohort SW-CRTs when covariates are affected by prior intervention.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Conceptual analysis of cross-sectional, closed-cohort and open-cohort stepped wedge designs; no empirical data reported.
  - **Estimand / target**: Total intervention effect, direct plus covariate-mediated, depending on baseline characteristics and exposure duration.
  - **Identification**: Randomised crossover; endogenous covariates act as mediators; secular and individual time effects.
  - **Estimator / model**: not reported
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: not reported
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: The total effect is time-dependent even without learning or waning, so target population and duration of exposure need explicit definition.
- **Strengths**: Applies the estimand framework to a design where it is often neglected.
- **Limitations**: Conceptual; no simulation or worked analysis in the abstract.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Pre-specify the target population and exposure duration of the estimand, and avoid adjusting for post-intervention covariates.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/bimj.70203 / PMID:43102162

## 5. Joint Modeling of Recurrent and Terminal Events With Discretely-Distributed Non-Parametric Frailty: Application on Re-Hospitalizations and Death in Heart Failure Patients — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: Biometrical Journal
- **Pub date**: 2026-12
- **Authors**: Masci C et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Joint frailty model with bivariate discrete non-parametric frailties identifying latent patient subgroups.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulation study plus Lombardia heart failure cohort on ACE inhibitors; N not reported.
  - **Estimand / target**: Recurrent hospitalisation and death hazards with frailty-defined latent subgroups.
  - **Identification**: Shared latent frailty links recurrent and terminal processes; finite unknown number of mass points.
  - **Estimator / model**: Joint frailty model (JMDF) fitted by expectation-conditional maximisation, estimating number of subgroups.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulations under varied random-effects distributions.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Method recovers latent subgroups and serves as unsupervised classifier; quantitative performance not reported in abstract.
- **Strengths**: Clinically interpretable discrete frailty classes for recurrent and terminal events.
- **Limitations**: Descriptive, non-causal; code not reported.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Consider when recurrent events are informatively truncated by death and patient subgroups are of interest.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/bimj.70206 / PMID:43102280
