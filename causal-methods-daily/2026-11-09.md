## 1. Dynamic borrowing via covariate-adjusted self-adapting mixture priors in clinical trials — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Statistical Methods in Medical Research
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-04
- **Authors**: Park Y et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Propose covariate-dependent dynamic borrowing of historical control data, with a propensity-score-integrated implementation to correct covariate mismatch.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Bayesian methodology for trials borrowing historical controls; simulation details not reported in abstract.
  - **Estimand / target**: Treatment effect in current trial using external controls; exact estimand not reported.
  - **Identification**: Historical controls reweighted by propensity score to match current control covariates; compatibility assessed conditional on covariates.
  - **Estimator / model**: CASAM empirical-Bayes self-adapting mixture prior, with PS-reweighted historical controls.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Automatic down-weighting under residual heterogeneity; validation specifics not reported.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Abstract reports no quantitative results; claims adaptive borrowing combining covariate alignment with outcome-compatibility assessment.
- **Strengths**: Combines propensity-score alignment with outcome-model compatibility, addressing patient-level covariate differences that single-weight priors ignore.
- **Limitations**: No numerical performance or code reported in abstract.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Consider for external-control trials where historical and current covariate distributions differ.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1177/09622802261505243 / PMID:42981113

## 2. Robust transfer learning for accelerated failure time model — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Statistical Methods in Medical Research
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-04
- **Authors**: Wang C et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Borrow information from an external source cohort in AFT survival models, robust to heterogeneity and outliers.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulations plus lung adenocarcinoma application with SEER registry as external source.
  - **Estimand / target**: AFT regression coefficients in the target cohort.
  - **Identification**: Target-source coefficient differences are penalized; covariate distributions need not match.
  - **Estimator / model**: RTrans-AFT: least-absolute-deviation AFT with adaptive lasso penalty on source-target discrepancy; individual or summary-level source data.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Consistency and asymptotic normality proved; simulations show variance reduction.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Simulations show substantially improved estimation accuracy and efficiency; no numbers in abstract.
- **Strengths**: Handles summary-level source data and outliers with theoretical guarantees.
- **Limitations**: Prediction/association focus; no causal interpretation and no code reported.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Useful for small survival cohorts when a heterogeneous registry like SEER is available.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1177/09622802261502156 / PMID:42981114

## 3. Efficacy and safety of intrathecal chemotherapy in leptomeningeal disease: a global retrospective multicenter analysis and review of the literature — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: EClinicalMedicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-26
- **Authors**: Msherghi A et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Compare survival and safety of intrathecal plus systemic chemotherapy versus systemic alone in leptomeningeal disease.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Retrospective TriNetX EHR cohort, 106 organizations; 292 ITC versus 2311 systemic-only; 247 per arm after matching.
  - **Estimand / target**: Hazard ratio for all-cause mortality at 30/90/180 days and 1 year.
  - **Identification**: Conditional exchangeability given age, sex, cancer type, treatments, baseline labs; no target-trial framing reported.
  - **Estimator / model**: Multivariable Cox regression and propensity score matching with univariate survival analysis.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Subanalyses by drug; literature review of trials; no residual-confounding analysis reported.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: 30-day mortality HR 0.45 (0.26-0.76); 1-year post-PSM HR 1.34 (1.01-1.78); more meningitis and hydrocephalus.
- **Strengths**: Large multinational EHR data with safety outcomes and trial literature context.
- **Limitations**: Time-varying HRs suggest immortal-time or confounding by indication; unmeasured confounding unaddressed.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Illustrates how PSM and Cox on EHR networks give conflicting time-window effects; apply target-trial design instead.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.eclinm.2026.104274 / PMID:42990651

## 4. An experimental evaluation of invitation modes for promoting engagement of reluctant cohort participants in a remote evaluation of pain experiences — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: Journal of Clinical Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-07
- **Authors**: Pires C et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Test second-line recruitment strategies for non-responders and their effect on participation and sample composition.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Randomized experiment nested in Generation XXI birth cohort (Portugal); 5756 families invited, non-responders randomized to three strategies.
  - **Estimand / target**: Participation proportion at 2 weeks and 2 months; benchmark association estimates.
  - **Identification**: Randomization of non-responders to invitation mode.
  - **Estimator / model**: Kaplan-Meier time-to-participation; logistic regression comparing benchmark associations across strategies.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Compared sample composition and associations across strategies versus email responders.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Overall response 20.4%; phone yielded 20.6% among non-responders; associations remained consistent despite composition differences.
- **Strengths**: Randomized evidence on retention and selection bias.
- **Limitations**: Low yield and a single cohort limit generalizability.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Email-only follow-up is insufficient; phone contact reduces selection bias modestly at higher cost.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.jclinepi.2026.112587 / PMID:42998741
