## 1. When negative control exposures fail to detect bias — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: American Journal of Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-02
- **Authors**: Nevo D et al.
- **Type**: simulation
- **Aims**: Characterise analytically and by simulation when negative control exposure (NCE) analyses fail to detect residual confounding.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Analytical derivations plus simulation scenarios varying exposure–NCE association sources; no empirical cohort.
  - **Estimand / target**: Large-sample NCE regression coefficient and power of bias detection, relative to true confounding bias in the exposure effect.
  - **Identification**: Linear structural models with unmeasured confounder; NCE assumed to have no effect on outcome; shared exposure–NCE common causes varied.
  - **Estimator / model**: Linear regression of outcome on exposure and NCE; power evaluated across confounder-exposure strength and common-cause adjustment.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Simulation across scenarios, including adjustment for exposure–NCE common causes affecting outcome only through exposure.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Power does not always rise with bias; stronger confounder-exposure links can raise bias while shrinking NCE coefficient. Extra common causes can attenuate or flip the NCE sign.
- **Strengths**: Clarifies a widely used falsification tool with analytic results, not just simulation.
- **Limitations**: Linear-model setting; generalisability to nonlinear or time-varying settings not shown in abstract.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Do not read a null NCE result as proof of no confounding; examine why exposure and NCE are associated.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/aje/kwag282 / PMID:42970832

## 4. Decompression With or Without Fusion Across Subgroups With Degenerative Lumbar Spondylolisthesis: A Target Trial Emulation — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Annals of Internal Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-03
- **Authors**: Hansford HJ et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Estimate comparative effectiveness of decompression with versus without fusion across prespecified patient subgroups.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Target trial emulation in Norwegian Registry for Spine Surgery; N=2252, including 236 from NORDSTEN-DS randomized trial.
  - **Estimand / target**: Subgroup-specific mean difference in 12-month Oswestry Disability Index and reoperation risk, fusion versus decompression alone.
  - **Identification**: Baseline conditional exchangeability given measured covariates; trial participants included, with subgroup effect modification assessed.
  - **Estimator / model**: Linear regression with inverse probability weighting; subgroups by sex, age, smoking, BMI, back pain, ASA grade.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Possible residual confounding acknowledged; trial data embedded in cohort; further validation not reported.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: 12-month ODI mean difference 2.2 (95% CI -1.6 to 6.1); reoperation did not differ. No differential subgroup effects.
- **Strengths**: Registry emulation incorporating randomized trial participants, with Dahabreh as coauthor.
- **Limitations**: Residual confounding possible; subgroup analyses exploratory and underpowered.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use target trial framing with IPW for subgroup effects, benchmarking against embedded trial data.
- **ID**: DOI:10.7326/ANNALS-25-05260 / PMID:42970804

## 5. Prevention of mpox by JYNNEOS vaccination: a case-control study comparing test-negative and alternative-disease control populations — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: American Journal of Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-02
- **Authors**: Saadeh K et al.
- **Type**: applied causal analysis
- **Aims**: Compare vaccine effectiveness (VE) estimates using test-negative versus gonorrhea control groups under risk-based vaccination.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Multi-arm case-control, California, 2022–2024; 198 mpox cases, 208 MPXV-negative controls, 155 gonorrhea controls; telephone interviews.
  - **Estimand / target**: VE (1 minus odds ratio) of 2 pre-exposure JYNNEOS doses against mpox.
  - **Identification**: Control selection and adjustment for behavioral risk factors to address differential infection risk and testing propensity.
  - **Estimator / model**: Adjusted odds ratio via regression, with and without behavioral adjustment.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Contrast of two control populations and of adjusted versus unadjusted estimates.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Adjusted VE 89.9% (69.6–96.7) versus test-negative and 92.0% (70.6–97.8) versus gonorrhea controls. Unadjusted estimates diverged.
- **Strengths**: Collects behavioral data typically missing from surveillance, enabling direct bias comparison of control designs.
- **Limitations**: Small sample, wide CIs; telephone-reported behavior subject to recall bias.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Alternative-disease controls may reduce bias in risk-based vaccine VE studies, but collect risk-behavior data.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/aje/kwag257 / PMID:42970831
