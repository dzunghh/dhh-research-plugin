## 1. A marginal structural proportional rates model for recurrent event data — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Statistical Methods in Medical Research
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-23
- **Authors**: Zee J et al.
- **Type**: new method
- **Aims**: Estimate causal effects of a time-varying treatment on recurrent events under time-dependent confounding.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulations plus observational CureGN cohort of glomerular disease patients; steroid use and recurrent infection-related hospitalizations.
  - **Estimand / target**: Marginal rate ratio of recurrent events for binary time-varying treatment.
  - **Identification**: Sequential conditional exchangeability given time-varying confounders (e.g., proteinuria), positivity, consistency; weights handle treatment-confounder feedback.
  - **Estimator / model**: Marginal structural proportional rates model (MSPRM) with inverse-probability-of-treatment weights; derived estimation and inference procedures.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Finite-sample simulation series; other sensitivity analyses not reported.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Simulations show good finite-sample properties; applied example estimates steroid effect on infection-related hospitalization rate (numbers not in abstract).
- **Strengths**: Fills a gap for causal analysis of recurrent events, avoiding efficiency loss of time-to-first-event analyses.
- **Limitations**: Binary treatment only; abstract reports no code and no unmeasured-confounding sensitivity analysis.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Use when time-varying treatment and confounders affect recurrent outcomes such as hospitalizations, instead of first-event MSM.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1177/09622802261509024 / PMID:43070384

## 2. What is the key bias that the test-negative design is intended to remove? — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-19
- **Authors**: Pearce N et al.
- **Type**: tutorial/guidance
- **Aims**: Clarify which bias the test-negative design addresses, with a corresponding DAG.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Theoretical/conceptual DAG analysis; no empirical data.
  - **Estimand / target**: Exposure (e.g., vaccine) association with disease among tested population.
  - **Identification**: Restricting to tested subpopulation controls for determinants of testing, including health-seeking behaviour.
  - **Estimator / model**: Case-control comparisons within tested subpopulation versus general-population controls; no new estimator.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Discusses imperfect testing and residual confounding as additional, non-unique biases.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: The unique bias is outcome misclassification from incomplete testing; restriction to the tested removes it under either full-population or sampled-control designs.
- **Strengths**: Resolves conceptual disagreement and gives clear DAG-based rationale.
- **Limitations**: Conceptual only; assumes outcome is defined by natural testing process.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Frame TND validity as selection by testing, and still address confounding and test imperfection separately.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1097/EDE.0000000000002065 / PMID:43070611

## 3. Accounting for Uncertainty in the Standardized Mean Difference — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Pharmacoepidemiology and Drug Safety
- **Pub date**: 2026-12-01
- **Authors**: Diepstra K et al.
- **Type**: simulation
- **Aims**: Evaluate 95% CIs for SMDs and covariate-selection strategies based on SMD precision.
- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Simulation, 1000 replicates; exposure, outcome, five exposure-independent covariates; n=500, 2000, 10,000.
  - **Estimand / target**: Risk difference (crude, and adjusted for covariates selected by SMD rules).
  - **Identification**: Covariates independent of exposure by construction; imbalance arises by chance only.
  - **Estimator / model**: Crude RD versus RD adjusted by |SMD|>0.1 (RD1) or SMD CI excluding 0 (RD2); linked to IPTW balance checking.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: Three sample-size scenarios; RMSE comparison.
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: Imbalance proportion with |SMD|>0.1 was 25.5–29.5% at n=500, 0% at n=10,000. RMSE at n=500: crude 0.045, RD2 0.041, RD1 0.034.
- **Strengths**: Directly addresses a ubiquitous but sample-size-sensitive balance diagnostic.
- **Limitations**: Only covariates independent of exposure were simulated; confounded settings untested.
- **Takeaway for practice**: In small samples, keep the |SMD|>0.1 rule for adjustment; interpret apparent imbalance cautiously.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/pds.70537 / PMID:43070070

- **Takeaway for practice**: Use to plan phase II platforms where design choices drive expected patient burden.

- **Pub date**: 2026-11-21
- **Authors**: Belini MLS et al.
- **Type**: new method

- **Methodology**:
  - **Design & data**: Real breast cancer survival dataset (details not reported); methodological development.
  - **Estimand / target**: Cure probability and susceptible-population survival/cumulative hazard.
  - **Identification**: Non-causal; mixture cure assumption of a cured fraction with separate incidence and latency.
  - **Estimator / model**: Cumulative hazard transformation with monotone spline, RW2-penalized nonparametric baseline; Metropolis-within-Gibbs MCMC.
  - **Sensitivity / validation**: not reported
- **Software**: not reported
- **Key findings**: not reported in abstract.
- **Strengths**: Unifies PH, proportional-odds and related structures with cure fraction.
- **Limitations**: Abstract gives no simulation results, comparisons or code.
- **Takeaway for practice**: Consider when long-term survivors exist and proportional hazards are doubtful, after checking identifiability.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1177/09622802261509363 / PMID:43066590
