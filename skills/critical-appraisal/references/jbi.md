# JBI critical appraisal checklists

Items are paraphrased. Source: JBI Critical Appraisal Tools, https://jbi.global/critical-appraisal-tools (Moola et al. 2020 for aetiology and risk; Munn et al. 2015 for prevalence; Barker et al. 2023 for the revised RCT tool; Barker et al. 2024 for quasi-experimental; for the other tools see the citation printed on each checklist).

Answer each item Yes / No / Unclear / Not applicable. JBI sets no cut-off score; a review team may pre-specify one.

Read only the section for the design in hand.

## Contents
Cohort · Case-control · Analytical cross-sectional · Prevalence · Case series · Case report · Quasi-experimental · RCT · Diagnostic test accuracy · Economic evaluation · Qualitative · Systematic review · Text and opinion

## Cohort (11)
1. Exposed and unexposed groups similar and drawn from the same population
2. Exposure measured the same way to assign both groups
3. Exposure measured validly and reliably
4. Confounders identified
5. Strategies to deal with confounders stated
6. Participants free of the outcome at the start (or at exposure)
7. Outcomes measured validly and reliably
8. Follow-up time reported and long enough for outcomes to occur
9. Follow-up complete; if not, reasons for loss described and explored
10. Strategies used to address incomplete follow-up
11. Appropriate statistical analysis

Watch for: differential loss (9–10); adjustment for mediators or colliders (5); immortal time and time-zero misalignment – not an item, so check that exposure classification uses no post-baseline information and report it under main threat.

## Case-control (10)
1. Groups comparable apart from disease status
2. Cases and controls matched appropriately
3. Same criteria used to identify cases and controls
4. Exposure measured in a standard, valid and reliable way
5. Exposure measured the same way for cases and controls
6. Confounders identified
7. Strategies to deal with confounders stated
8. Outcomes assessed in a standard, valid and reliable way for both groups
9. Exposure period long enough to be meaningful
10. Appropriate statistical analysis

Watch for: control source (items 1, 3 – do controls represent the population that produced the cases?), recall bias (5), over-matching (2).

## Analytical cross-sectional (8)
1. Inclusion criteria clearly defined
2. Subjects and setting described in detail
3. Exposure measured validly and reliably
4. Objective, standard criteria used to measure the condition
5. Confounders identified
6. Strategies to deal with confounders stated
7. Outcomes measured validly and reliably
8. Appropriate statistical analysis

Watch for: reverse causation (not an item – state it under main threat), prevalent-case bias.

## Prevalence (9)
1. Sample frame appropriate for the target population
2. Participants sampled appropriately
3. Sample size adequate
4. Subjects and setting described in detail
5. Analysis covered enough of the identified sample
6. Valid methods used to identify the condition
7. Condition measured in a standard, reliable way for everyone
8. Appropriate statistical analysis
9. Response rate adequate, or low response handled appropriately

## Case series (10)
1. Clear inclusion criteria
2. Condition measured in a standard, reliable way for all
3. Valid methods used to identify the condition for all
4. Consecutive inclusion
5. Complete inclusion
6. Demographics clearly reported
7. Clinical information clearly reported
8. Outcomes or follow-up results clearly reported
9. Presenting site or clinic demographics clearly reported
10. Appropriate statistical analysis

## Case report (8)
1. Patient demographics clearly described
2. History clearly described and given as a timeline
3. Presenting clinical condition clearly described
4. Diagnostic tests or assessment methods and results clearly described
5. Intervention or treatment clearly described
6. Post-intervention condition clearly described
7. Adverse or unanticipated events identified and described
8. Takeaway lessons provided

## Quasi-experimental (9)
1. Clear which variable is cause and which is effect (temporal order)
2. A control group present
3. Participants in the comparisons similar
4. Groups received similar care apart from the intervention
5. Outcome measured several times, before and after the intervention
6. Outcomes measured the same way across groups
7. Outcomes measured reliably
8. Follow-up complete; if not, differences between groups described and analysed
9. Appropriate statistical analysis

Watch for: secular trend and regression to the mean when item 2 or 5 fails.

## RCT – revised tool (13)
1. True randomisation used
2. Allocation concealed
3. Groups similar at baseline
4. Participants blind to assignment
5. Those delivering treatment blind to assignment
6. Groups treated identically apart from the intervention
7. Outcome assessors blind to assignment
8. Outcomes measured the same way for all groups
9. Outcomes measured reliably
10. Follow-up complete; if not, differences between groups described and analysed
11. Participants analysed in the groups to which they were randomised
12. Appropriate statistical analysis
13. Trial design appropriate, and deviations from a standard parallel design (cluster, crossover) accounted for

In the revised tool items 7–9 are judged per outcome, 10–12 per result (each reported estimate) and 13 per study. For a trial inside a formal SR/MA use RoB 2 via `srma`.

## Diagnostic test accuracy (10)
1. Consecutive or random sample enrolled
2. Case-control design avoided
3. Inappropriate exclusions avoided
4. Index test read without knowledge of the reference standard
5. Any threshold pre-specified
6. Reference standard likely to classify the target condition correctly
7. Reference standard read without knowledge of the index test
8. Appropriate interval between index test and reference standard
9. All patients received the same reference standard
10. All patients included in the analysis

## Economic evaluation (11)
1. Well-defined question, including perspective
2. Comprehensive description of alternatives
3. All important and relevant costs and outcomes identified for each alternative
4. Clinical effectiveness established
5. Costs and outcomes measured accurately
6. Costs and outcomes valued credibly
7. Costs and outcomes adjusted for differential timing (discounting)
8. Incremental analysis of costs and consequences
9. Sensitivity analyses on uncertainty in costs or consequences
10. Results cover all issues of concern to users
11. Results generalisable to the setting of interest

## Qualitative (10)
1. Stated philosophical perspective congruent with the methodology
2. Methodology congruent with the research question
3. Methodology congruent with data-collection methods
4. Methodology congruent with representation and analysis of data
5. Methodology congruent with interpretation of results
6. Statement locating the researcher culturally or theoretically
7. Influence of researcher on the research, and the reverse, addressed
8. Participants and their voices adequately represented
9. Research ethical by current criteria, with evidence of ethical approval
10. Conclusions flow from the analysis or interpretation of the data

## Systematic review (11) – quick screen; AMSTAR 2 is the fuller tool
1. Review question clearly and explicitly stated
2. Inclusion criteria appropriate for the question
3. Search strategy appropriate
4. Sources searched adequate
5. Appraisal criteria appropriate
6. Appraisal done by two or more reviewers independently
7. Methods used to minimise extraction errors
8. Methods used to combine studies appropriate
9. Likelihood of publication bias assessed
10. Policy or practice recommendations supported by the reported data
11. Directives for new research appropriate

## Text and opinion
**Narrative (6):** credible source · relationship between text and context explained · events in logical sequence · reader reaches similar conclusions · conclusions flow from the account · the account is a narrative.

**Expert opinion (6):** source clearly identified · source has standing in the field · interests of the relevant population are the focus · argument logically defended · extant literature referenced · any incongruence with the literature defended.

**Policy or consensus guideline (7):** developers and affiliations identified · developers have standing · appropriate stakeholders involved and conclusions represent intended users · competing-interest biases acknowledged and handled · evidence gathering and summarising described · incongruence with the literature defended · methods for developing recommendations described.
