## 1. Ensartinib in Resected ALK-Positive Non-Small-Cell Lung Cancer — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: N Engl J Med
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-09
- **Authors**: Yue D et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate adjuvant ensartinib (2nd-gen ALK inhibitor) after resection and chemotherapy in stage IB–IIIB ALK+ NSCLC.
- **Method**: Phase 3 double-blind RCT; 274 patients randomized 1:1 to ensartinib 225 mg daily vs placebo × 24 months.
- **Key findings**: 24-month DFS 86.4% vs 53.5% in stage II–IIIB (HR 0.20, 95% CI 0.11–0.38, P<0.001); overall DFS 87.3% vs 57.2% (HR 0.20, 95% CI 0.10–0.37, P<0.001).
- **Strengths**: Prespecified primary endpoint, multicenter, large effect size with precise confidence intervals.
- **Limitations**: OS data immature; grade 3+ AEs higher with ensartinib (35.8% vs 18.2%), mainly rash.
- **Conclusion**: Ensartinib significantly improves DFS in resected ALK+ NSCLC, supporting its adjuvant use.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1056/NEJMoa2518990 / PMID:42418775

## 2. Chromatin Landscape and Epigenetic Heterogeneity of Acute Myeloid Leukaemia — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Nature
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-08
- **Authors**: Ochi Y et al.
- **Aims**: Define epigenomically distinct AML subgroups with prognostic and therapeutic relevance using large-scale chromatin profiling.
- **Method**: ATAC-seq in 1,563 AML patients (eCHROMA cohort); integrated multiomics including mutations, transcriptome, DNA methylation, histone marks, and single-cell ATAC-seq.
- **Key findings**: 16 ATAC subgroups with distinct driver mutations, differentiation states, and drug sensitivities; ATAC classification independently predicts prognosis beyond genomic classification.
- **Strengths**: Largest published ATAC-seq cancer cohort; validated in independent cohorts; single-cell confirmation.
- **Limitations**: Predominantly adult AML; generalizability to pediatric AML or other myeloid malignancies uncertain.
- **Conclusion**: Epigenomic subtyping adds independent prognostic value beyond genomics and identifies actionable drug sensitivities in AML.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41586-026-10703-4 / PMID:42420449

## 3. Global Breast Cancer Survival Estimates in 2017–2021 to Advance the WHO Global Breast Cancer Initiative — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Nat Med
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-08
- **Authors**: Girardi F et al.
- **Aims**: Estimate population-based 5-year net breast cancer survival across all 194 WHO Member States for 2017–2021 to benchmark progress toward 2040 targets.
- **Method**: Bayesian statistical modelling of population-based registry data; age-standardized 5-year net survival stratified by WHO region.
- **Key findings**: Median 5-year net survival ranged from 39.1% (Africa, 95% UI 34.1–44.7%) to 88.5% (Americas, 95% UI 86.7–90.1%); persistent large between-country disparities.
- **Strengths**: Global scope (194 countries), WHO-sponsored standardised methodology, uncertainty intervals reported.
- **Limitations**: Survival modelled for many LMICs with sparse registry data; uncertainty intervals wide in low-income settings.
- **Conclusion**: Profound global inequities in breast cancer survival demand sustained investment in diagnosis and treatment in low- and middle-income countries.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41591-026-04531-2 / PMID:42420542

## 4. Aneuploidy Selects for the Acquisition of Driver Genes in Breast Cancer — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Nature
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-08
- **Authors**: Al-Zahrani KN et al.
- **Aims**: Identify cancer driver genes embedded within common chromosome-arm copy-number alterations in basal-like breast cancer via systematic in vivo functional screening.
- **Method**: CRISPR-KOALA bidirectional screen of 3,752 genes on 10 most frequent CNA arms in immunocompetent Trp53-mutant basal-like breast cancer mouse models.
- **Key findings**: 90 cancer driver genes identified across MAPK, HIPPO, and WNT pathways; PLGRKT (chr9p) identified as potent oncogene linked to stress-resistant mitochondria and ROS detoxification.
- **Strengths**: Large-scale in vivo bidirectional functional screen in immunocompetent models; novel mechanistic insight into aneuploidy-driven oncogenesis.
- **Limitations**: Mouse-model findings require human validation; limited extrapolation beyond basal-like breast cancer subtype.
- **Conclusion**: Arm-level copy-number alterations functionally select specific driver genes that promote heterogeneous oncogenic processes in basal-like breast cancer.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41586-026-10752-9 / PMID:42420452

## 5. Consolidative Thoracic Radiotherapy With Atezolizumab Maintenance in Extensive-Stage Small Cell Lung Cancer: The Phase 2 TREASURE Randomized Clinical Trial — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-09
- **Authors**: Bozorgmehr F et al.
- **Aims**: Determine whether consolidative thoracic radiotherapy (TRT) combined with atezolizumab maintenance improves OS versus atezolizumab alone in ES-SCLC after chemoimmunotherapy.
- **Method**: Phase 2 multicenter open-label RCT (Germany/Austria); 68 patients randomized 1:1; TRT 30 Gy/10 fr + atezolizumab vs atezolizumab alone; stopped early for safety.
- **Key findings**: Median OS 6.7 vs 13.4 months in TRT vs control (HR 1.55, 95% CI 0.90–2.69); SAEs 61.3% vs 18.2% (P<0.001); fatal AEs 19.4% vs 3.0% (P=0.04).
- **Strengths**: RCT with prespecified endpoint; mechanistic explanation (TRT-induced lymphopenia facilitating infections); important safety signal.
- **Limitations**: Small N=68, early termination limits statistical power; open-label design.
- **Conclusion**: TRT combined with immunotherapy increases toxicity without survival benefit in unselected ES-SCLC; biomarker-driven patient selection required.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamaoncol.2026.2330 / PMID:42424047

## Appraisal notes

This week's papers span global cancer epidemiology (WHO breast cancer survival), epigenomic population-scale subtyping (AML eCHROMA cohort), functional cancer genomics (CRISPR-KOALA breast cancer screen), and clinical trial outcomes (adjuvant ensartinib, TRT-immunotherapy in SCLC). A key methodological theme is the convergence of large-cohort biomarker studies with prognostic epidemiological objectives—the AML chromatin analysis in 1,563 patients demonstrates that molecular profiling at population scale can yield independent prognostic signals beyond genomic classification alone. The WHO global breast cancer survival estimates quantify a persistent 50-percentage-point survival gap between low- and high-income regions, providing an essential benchmark for health equity monitoring. The TREASURE negative trial reinforces the need for biomarker-guided patient selection before combining immunotherapy and radiotherapy.
