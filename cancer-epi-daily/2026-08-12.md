## 1. Health Communication and Stepped Reminders Interventions for Lung Cancer Screening: A Randomized Clinical Trial — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Internal Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-10
- **Authors**: Wernli KJ et al.
- **Aims**: To test two multilevel, patient-centered interventions to improve adherence to annual lung cancer screening (LCS).
- **Method**: Pragmatic 2×2 factorial RCT at Kaiser Permanente Washington; 1,837 patients; arms: usual care, health communication, Stepped Reminders, or both.
- **Key findings**: Stepped Reminders raised adherence by 27.7 percentage points (75.5% vs 47.4%; RR 1.59, 95% CI 1.47–1.72; P<.001). Health communication alone slightly reduced adherence (−4.7 pp; P=.04).
- **Strengths**: Large pragmatic RCT with factorial design cleanly isolating two intervention mechanisms.
- **Limitations**: Single integrated health system; predominantly White cohort (88.7%), limiting generalizability.
- **Conclusion**: PCP-directed pended orders plus patient outreach reminders substantially improve annual LCS adherence.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamainternmed.2026.3666 / PMID:42574003

## 2. Racial/Ethnic Disparities in Risk of Second Primary Malignancies among Diffuse Large B-Cell Lymphoma Survivors — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiology, Biomarkers & Prevention
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-07
- **Authors**: Gole Advani P et al.
- **Aims**: To comprehensively examine second primary malignancy (SPM) risk after DLBCL by race/ethnicity.
- **Method**: Population-based cohort; 68,358 ≥12-month DLBCL survivors from 17 SEER registries (2000–2022); standardized incidence ratios (SIRs) by race/ethnicity and cancer site.
- **Key findings**: Overall SPM risk was 1.2-fold elevated (95% CI 1.16–1.22); API and Hispanic patients had highest overall risk; anal cancer SIRs were 9.29 (Black) and 8.55 (Hispanic).
- **Strengths**: Largest race-stratified DLBCL survivor cohort to date using population-representative SEER data.
- **Limitations**: SEER lacks treatment details; unmeasured confounding by therapy type may drive racial risk differences.
- **Conclusion**: Minority DLBCL survivors bear substantially higher SPM burdens, warranting targeted surveillance strategies.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-0672 / PMID:42565782

## 3. Osimertinib With or Without Chemotherapy in Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer With EGFR and Concurrent TP53 Mutations — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: JAMA
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-10
- **Authors**: Zhou T et al.
- **Aims**: To compare first-line osimertinib plus chemotherapy vs osimertinib monotherapy in EGFR-mutated NSCLC with concurrent TP53 mutations.
- **Method**: Multicenter open-label phase 3 RCT across 17 sites in China; 294 patients; 1:1 randomisation; primary endpoint: investigator-assessed progression-free survival.
- **Key findings**: Median PFS 34.0 vs 15.6 months (HR 0.44, 95% CI 0.32–0.60; P<.001); benefit consistent across subgroups, including brain metastases; OS data immature.
- **Strengths**: First prospective RCT specifically selecting on TP53 co-mutation; clinically meaningful and statistically robust PFS difference.
- **Limitations**: Single-country population; OS maturity only 30.6%; open-label design with investigator-assessed primary endpoint.
- **Conclusion**: Osimertinib plus chemotherapy significantly extends PFS in biomarker-selected EGFR+TP53-comutant NSCLC.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jama.2026.10599 / PMID:42574006

## 4. The Tumour Microenvironment Influences Long-Term Tamoxifen Benefit in Postmenopausal ER+/HER2- Breast Cancer Patients — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: BMC Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-05
- **Authors**: Camargo-Romera P et al.
- **Aims**: To determine whether tumour microenvironment (TME) composition predicts tamoxifen benefit in ER+/HER2- postmenopausal breast cancer.
- **Method**: Secondary analysis of the randomised STO-3 trial (513 patients); bulk RNA transcriptome deconvolution (ConsensusTME, 18 cell types); multivariable Cox models adjusted for clinical covariates.
- **Key findings**: Low immune abundance predicted improved DRFI on tamoxifen (aHR 0.17, 95% CI 0.08–0.40); low/intermediate fibroblast abundance also predicted benefit (aHR 0.50 and 0.36).
- **Strengths**: Leverages a well-powered RCT with randomised control arm; systematic multi-compartment TME profiling.
- **Limitations**: Single historical trial (1970s–80s enrollment); contemporary adjuvant therapy landscape differs substantially.
- **Conclusion**: Both immune and stromal TME compartments modulate tamoxifen efficacy, informing precision endocrine therapy selection.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1186/s12916-026-05091-5 / PMID:42557562

## 5. Exploring PDLIM2 as a Prognostic Biomarker and Therapeutic Target in Lung Adenocarcinoma — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: British Journal of Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-08
- **Authors**: Ashouri K et al.
- **Aims**: To evaluate PDLIM2 expression as a prognostic marker and assess PDLIM2-based gene therapy in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD).
- **Method**: Retrospective cohort of 15,765 LUAD patients with NGS and IHC; multivariate survival analysis; preclinical syngeneic mouse models with intravenous nano-complexed plasmid DNA.
- **Key findings**: High PDLIM2 associated with improved OS (24.1 vs 18.1 months; multivariate HR 0.88, 95% CI 0.83–0.93); nanoPDLIM2 gene therapy prolonged OS vs chemoimmunotherapy alone in mouse models (P=0.0001).
- **Strengths**: Very large retrospective cohort provides well-powered prognostic estimates; dual validation across clinical data and two mouse models.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective design precludes causal inference; predictive (vs prognostic) role requires prospective RCT validation.
- **Conclusion**: PDLIM2 is a robust independent prognostic biomarker in LUAD, with early preclinical support for gene therapy augmentation.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41416-026-03570-3 / PMID:42570967

## Appraisal notes

This week's picks highlight a pragmatic-RCT push in cancer-epi translation: the lung cancer screening trial (JAMA Intern Med) demonstrates that system-level reminder infrastructure outperforms patient education alone, a finding with direct policy implications for national LCS roll-out. The JAMA osimertinib phase 3 trial exemplifies an emerging biomarker-stratification paradigm in NSCLC—enrolling solely on TP53 co-mutation to isolate a subpopulation that derives exceptional PFS benefit from combination therapy. Across all five papers, the use of large population registries (SEER, Caris NGS) alongside RCT-anchored secondary analyses reflects a maturing cancer-epi toolkit that pairs causal inference from trials with the generalisability of registry-scale data.
