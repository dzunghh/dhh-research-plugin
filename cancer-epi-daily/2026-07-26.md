## 1. Cautionary Note for Plasmode Simulation Studies in Causal Inference — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-01
- **Authors**: Shaw PA et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate properties of competing plasmode simulation frameworks for causal-inference studies using EHR data.
- **Method**: Comparative simulation study with theoretical analysis, model-generated data, and real EHR and RCT datasets.
- **Key findings**: One widely-used plasmode framework systematically inflates estimator bias; this artifact persists at large sample sizes and in RCT data.
- **Strengths**: Combines formal theoretical result with empirical demonstrations in both real EHR and RCT data, enhancing credibility.
- **Limitations**: Analysis centres on point-treatment estimands; generalisability to time-varying or survival settings requires further study.
- **Conclusion**: A specific plasmode framework preserving good theoretical properties should be adopted to fairly benchmark causal estimators.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70676 / PMID:42487285

## 2. Accelerated Aging and Subsequent Neoplasms in Childhood Cancer Survivors — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiology, Biomarkers & Prevention
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-20
- **Authors**: Berkman AM et al.
- **Aims**: Determine whether accelerated-aging measures predict subsequent malignant neoplasm (SMN) risk in childhood cancer survivors.
- **Method**: Prospective cohort (Childhood Cancer Survivor Study); three aging indices (physiologic frailty, CIRS-G, DAI); piecewise exponential models.
- **Key findings**: Accelerated aging not consistently associated with SMN risk; higher DAI associated with benign meningioma only (not SMN or NMSC).
- **Strengths**: Large, well-characterised cohort with multiple independent aging measures and comprehensive confounder adjustment.
- **Limitations**: Observational design; aging indices may not fully capture biological aging specific to cancer-treatment survivors.
- **Conclusion**: Treatment exposures remain the dominant determinants of SMN risk, reinforcing risk-adapted therapy and long-term surveillance.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-0609 / PMID:42474278

## 3. Cancer Cases Attributable to Excess Body Fat in South Korea — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: International Journal of Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-06-24
- **Authors**: Lee D et al.
- **Aims**: Estimate population-attributable fractions (PAF) of excess body fat for cancer in Korea, contrasting BMI vs waist circumference (WC).
- **Method**: PAF analysis using Korean NHIS data (4.4 M adults screened 2012; 242,243 cancer cases to 2020) and 2022 national cancer registry.
- **Key findings**: BMI-based PAF 5.5–6.9%; WC-based PAF 6.7–9.3%; WC identified 22,487 vs 16,534 avoidable cases compared to BMI in 2022.
- **Strengths**: Very large national cohort; sensitivity analysis in never-smokers clarifies smoking confounding of obesity-cancer associations.
- **Limitations**: Cross-sectional obesity prevalence used; BMI and WC are proxies that may misclassify adiposity in Asian adults.
- **Conclusion**: Abdominal obesity (WC) substantially exceeds BMI in attributable cancer burden; policies should adopt WC alongside BMI.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/ije/dyag119 / PMID:42475506

## 4. Low-Rank Variational Bayes for Multi-Source Heterogeneous Quantile Regression — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-01
- **Authors**: Li H et al.
- **Aims**: Develop a scalable Bayesian framework for high-dimensional quantile regression across heterogeneous multi-source cancer datasets.
- **Method**: Mean-field variational Bayes with Laplace approximation and novel low-rank correction; spike-and-slab priors; applied to TCGA data (4 cancer cohorts).
- **Key findings**: Low-rank correction markedly improves posterior approximation accuracy; method outperforms alternatives in simulation and identifies cancer-relevant genomic features.
- **Strengths**: R package (LRQVB) publicly available on CRAN; validated across simulation and four real cancer genomics cohorts.
- **Limitations**: Assumes linear quantile model; theoretical guarantees in ultrahigh-dimensional regimes warrant further investigation.
- **Conclusion**: Novel low-rank variational correction enables robust, scalable inference for multi-source cancer genomics data.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70678 / PMID:42493463

## 5. Traffic-Related Air Pollution and Epithelial Ovarian Tumour Risk — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiology, Biomarkers & Prevention
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-20
- **Authors**: Viau L et al.
- **Aims**: Examine long-term ambient NO2 exposure in relation to invasive vs borderline epithelial ovarian tumour risk.
- **Method**: Population-based case-control study, Greater Montreal 2011–2016 (473 cases, 887 controls); land-use regression NO2; polytomous logistic regression.
- **Key findings**: Borderline tumour OR 1.36 (95% CI 0.99–1.87) per IQR of NO2; invasive cancer OR 0.98 (95% CI 0.79–1.21).
- **Strengths**: First study to separately analyse borderline vs invasive ovarian tumours by air-pollution exposure; dual LUR model validation.
- **Limitations**: Single metropolitan area; postal-code-level exposure may misclassify individuals; moderate sample size limits precision.
- **Conclusion**: Air pollution may elevate borderline ovarian tumour risk, but no evidence of association with invasive ovarian cancer.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-0435 / PMID:42474266
