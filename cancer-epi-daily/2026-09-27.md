## 1. Performance of a multi-cancer early detection test in the randomized controlled NHS-Galleri trial — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Nature Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-22
- **Authors**: Neal RD et al.
- **Aims**: Report prespecified secondary performance endpoints of a multi-cancer early detection (MCED) blood test across three annual NHS-Galleri RCT screening rounds.
- **Method**: RCT (N=142,250, aged 50–77); positive MCED results referred to NHS diagnostic workup; three annual rounds evaluated.
- **Key findings**: Cancer detection rates 0.41–0.60%; PPV 45.8–58.0%; episode sensitivity 26.7–37.2% all cancers, 47.6–63.4% for 12 prespecified types; CSO accuracy 91.1–93.6%.
- **Strengths**: Largest population RCT of MCED screening; multi-round longitudinal data; all endpoints prespecified.
- **Limitations**: Descriptive secondary analyses only; primary endpoint (stage III/IV reduction) not met.
- **Conclusion**: MCED screening achieves high specificity and moderate sensitivity, providing key population benchmarks for future cancer screening programmes.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41591-026-04652-8 / PMID:42773209

## 2. Disparities in Cancer Incidence by Sex and Associated Lifestyle Factors and Health Conditions — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-24
- **Authors**: Li ZY et al.
- **Aims**: Quantify how much of the male-female cancer incidence gap is attributable to modifiable lifestyle factors and health conditions across Chinese and UK populations.
- **Method**: Prospective cohort study; 589,766 participants (Shanghai Men's/Women's Health Studies + UK Biobank); attributable rate difference analysis for 11 risk factors across 34 cancer types.
- **Key findings**: Modifiable factors explained 84.4% of sex disparity in China (smoking 51.8%) vs. 26.2% in the UK (obesity 7.7%, smoking 6.3%).
- **Strengths**: Large multi-population prospective cohorts; rigorous attributable fraction methodology; 34 cancer endpoints.
- **Limitations**: Single baseline risk factor measurement; residual confounding possible; differential follow-up completeness.
- **Conclusion**: Lifestyle factors explain most sex cancer gaps in China but far less in the UK, indicating population-tailored prevention strategies are needed.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamaoncol.2026.3747 / PMID:42783354

## 3. Trastuzumab plus nivolumab with gemcitabine and cisplatin as first-line therapy for HER2-positive unresectable biliary tract cancer: a phase 1b/2 trial — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Nature Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-22
- **Authors**: Lee CK et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate a first-line HER2-targeted quadruplet regimen (trastuzumab + nivolumab + gemcitabine + cisplatin) in HER2-positive advanced biliary tract cancer.
- **Method**: Phase 1b/2 open-label multi-institutional trial (HERBOT; N=40); primary endpoint: objective response rate.
- **Key findings**: ORR 55% (95% CI 38.5–70.7%); disease control rate 95%; median PFS 10.6 months; median OS not reached at 17.0 months.
- **Strengths**: Prospective; prespecified primary endpoint; AI-powered whole-slide HER2 analysis linked to clinical benefit.
- **Limitations**: Small sample (N=40); single-arm; no comparator; predominantly Korean cohort.
- **Conclusion**: Upfront HER2-targeted intensification shows promising activity in HER2-positive biliary tract cancer, supporting phase 3 investigation.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41591-026-04679-x / PMID:42773210

## 4. Chemotherapy and Surgical Outcomes for Ovarian Metastases From Colorectal Cancer: A Narrative Review — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-24
- **Authors**: Weil JBE et al.
- **Aims**: Examine evidence for chemotherapy resistance and surgical resection for ovarian metastases in metastatic colorectal cancer.
- **Method**: Narrative review of 24 retrospective studies comprising 2,705 patients with ovarian metastases from colorectal cancer.
- **Key findings**: Ovarian metastases showed lower response rates and discordant growth vs. extraovarian lesions; complete resection with negative margins associated with improved survival.
- **Strengths**: Clinically meaningful synthesis of a therapeutically challenging entity; spans chemotherapy and surgical evidence.
- **Limitations**: Narrative review only; no meta-analytic pooling; retrospective source studies with selection bias.
- **Conclusion**: The ovary acts as a relative chemotherapy sanctuary; complete surgical resection alongside systemic therapy is optimal management.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamaoncol.2026.3634 / PMID:42783246
