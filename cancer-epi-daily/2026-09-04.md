## 1. Long-Read Haplotype Phasing Resolves Allelic Configuration as a Missing Layer of Precision Oncology — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Discovery
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-01
- **Authors**: Vo JN et al.
- **Aims**: Determine whether long-read nanopore sequencing can resolve cis/trans allelic configuration of co-occurring cancer variants missed by short-read sequencing.
- **Method**: Prospective molecular study of 768 patients (prostate/breast/ovarian cancers) plus 4,496 MiOncoSeq samples; nanopore long-read sequencing with methylation detection and structural variant characterisation.
- **Key findings**: Long-read phasing resolved 69.6% (32/46) of cryptic genomic-instability cases; 78.7% of multi-hit gene pairs exceeded the 500 bp short-read phasing limit.
- **Strengths**: Two large independent cohorts; head-to-head comparison of short- vs. long-read phasing; direct therapeutic implications demonstrated.
- **Limitations**: Single-institution sequencing pipeline; clinical actionability of resolved cis compound alleles not yet prospectively validated.
- **Conclusion**: Haplotype phasing addresses a systematic gap in cancer variant interpretation and warrants routine integration into precision oncology workflows.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/2159-8290.CD-26-0839 / PMID:42678928

## 2. Cancer Rarity and Mortality: Evidence from a Nationwide Cohort Study — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-01
- **Authors**: Kønig SM et al.
- **Aims**: Quantify excess mortality in rare versus common cancers and determine how much is explained by tumour histology and stage at diagnosis.
- **Method**: Nationwide population-based cohort, all adults with first primary solid malignancy in Denmark 2004–2023 (RARECAREnet Tier 2 classification); Cox regression adjusted for age, sex, calendar period; further adjustment for histology and stage.
- **Key findings**: Rare cancer patients had 74% higher all-cause mortality (HR 1.74, 95% CI 1.72–1.76); after adjustment for histology and stage, HR fell to 0.96 (95% CI 0.95–0.98).
- **Strengths**: Complete nationwide registry, 20-year period, rigorous multi-step confounder adjustment.
- **Limitations**: Observational design; residual confounding from treatment access and provider experience not fully captured.
- **Conclusion**: Histology and stage at diagnosis largely explain the survival disadvantage in rare cancers; earlier diagnosis is the priority intervention.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag308 / PMID:42679382

## 3. Matched Targeted Therapy Use After Broad Genomic Profiling in Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-01
- **Authors**: Wang X et al.
- **Aims**: Assess guideline-concordant targeted therapy use and underuse/overuse patterns after actionable genomic profiling in advanced NSCLC.
- **Method**: Merged nationwide US patient-level database cohort (n=6,620 aNSCLC patients, 2017–2023); categorised by regulatory approval and NCCN concordance; multivariable Poisson regression.
- **Key findings**: 89.6% received on-label first-line targeted therapy; off-label underuse was 72.3%; 8.6% received guideline-discordant off-label options (overuse).
- **Strengths**: Two large nationwide US databases merged; stratified by actionability level; clear clinical relevance for guideline adherence.
- **Limitations**: US-specific; treatment intent and performance status not captured; genomic profiling selection bias possible.
- **Conclusion**: Substantial underuse of off-label options and smoker-related clinical profiles predict lower targeted therapy receipt despite actionable alterations.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag310 / PMID:42679379

## 4. Evaluating the Incidence and Predictors of Chronic Opioid Use in Early Survivorship for Patients with Diffuse Large B-Cell Lymphoma — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-01
- **Authors**: Ravindran M et al.
- **Aims**: Quantify new persistent opioid use (NPOU) and identify clinical predictors among DLBCL survivors following first-line chemoimmunotherapy.
- **Method**: Population-based cohort study, Ontario Canada (n=4,988 DLBCL patients, 2014–2021); multivariable modified Poisson regression for relative risk of NPOU.
- **Key findings**: 55.0% exposed to opioids during treatment; 10.4% of exposed patients developed NPOU vs. 2.4% unexposed (RR 3.79, 95% CI 2.85–5.06).
- **Strengths**: Complete population-based administrative data; defined NPOU using two consecutive 6-month windows; multiple clinical predictors examined.
- **Limitations**: Single-province data; opioid dose information limited; no pain severity or functional status data.
- **Conclusion**: Opioid exposure during DLBCL treatment strongly predicts NPOU; relapsed disease, mental health diagnoses, and high-dose exposure identify highest-risk patients.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag305 / PMID:42679364

## 5. Human Papillomavirus Infection and Risk of Cardiovascular Disease: A Population-Based Matched Cohort Study with Sibling Analysis — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: PLoS Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-01
- **Authors**: Deng Y et al.
- **Aims**: Investigate whether HPV infection is independently associated with incident cardiovascular disease and CVD-related mortality.
- **Method**: Swedish national register matched cohort (n=497,445 HPV-infected women vs. 5× age-matched controls); flexible parametric models; sibling analysis (n=143,787 with HPV vs. 174,637 siblings).
- **Key findings**: HPV infection associated with incident CVD (adjusted HR 1.07, 95% CI 1.05–1.08) and CVD mortality (HR 1.25, 95% CI 1.16–1.34); highest risk in first year post-infection.
- **Strengths**: Near-complete national registers; sibling design controls familial confounding; time-varying analysis captures temporal dynamics.
- **Limitations**: Observational; HPV misclassification possible; causal inference not supported; restricted to women.
- **Conclusion**: HPV infection associates with mildly elevated CVD risk, especially in the first year; absolute differences are small but population-attributable fraction may be relevant given HPV prevalence.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1371/journal.pmed.1005222 / PMID:42678931

## Appraisal notes

This week's digest highlights two dominant themes: precision oncology methodology and real-world cancer outcomes research. The long-read haplotype phasing study (Cancer Discovery) represents a methodological advance with direct clinical impact, while three JNCI cohort studies demonstrate the strength of large administrative database epidemiology for quantifying treatment gaps and survivorship risks. The sibling-analysis design in the HPV-CVD PLoS Medicine paper exemplifies rigorous quasi-experimental epidemiology increasingly applied to infectious disease-cancer intersections.
