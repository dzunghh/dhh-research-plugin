## 1. Processed Meat, Red Meat, and Stomach Cancer Risk: 20-Cohort Pooled Analysis — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Int J Epidemiol
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-14
- **Authors**: Peng Y et al.
- **Aims**: Determine whether processed meat, unprocessed red meat, poultry, seafood, and eggs associate with stomach cancer risk across diverse populations.
- **Method**: Pooled individual-level Cox regression in 20 prospective cohorts (1,992,289 participants; 8,561 gastric adenocarcinomas); random-effects meta-analysis of harmonised diet histories.
- **Key findings**: Processed meat showed modest association (HR 1.15 per ≥45 vs <3 g/day) only among current smokers (P-interaction = .01) and in Australian/European but not Asian cohorts (P-interaction = .02). Egg intake showed positive association; no effect for unprocessed red meat, poultry, or seafood.
- **Strengths**: Largest pooled stomach cancer diet analysis to date; harmonised exposure data; rigorous interaction testing.
- **Limitations**: Residual confounding by smoking dose; single dietary assessment at baseline; differential loss to follow-up across cohorts.
- **Conclusion**: Processed meat modestly increases stomach cancer risk in smokers; findings differ by geography, warranting region-specific dietary guidance.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/ije/dyag224 / PMID:42888021

## 3. Waiting Times and Determinants of Delay for Head and Neck Cancer in England — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Br J Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-16
- **Authors**: Jashek-Ahmed F et al.
- **Aims**: Quantify treatment waiting times and identify patient, tumour, and health-service factors associated with delay in head and neck cancer (HNC) in England.
- **Method**: Population cohort study using linked national datasets (Cancer Registry, Cancer Waiting Times, Hospital Episodes Statistics, Radiotherapy/Chemotherapy); multivariable logistic regression in 30,506 patients.
- **Key findings**: 43% experienced >62-day delay; 20% of chemoradiation patients had >90-day delays. Non-urgent referral was the strongest predictor (aOR 4.07, 95% CI 3.54–4.68); deprivation and non-specialist initial assessment also contributed.
- **Strengths**: Largest English HNC waiting-time study using linked national registries; real-world population coverage; treatment-modality-specific analysis.
- **Limitations**: Waiting-time thresholds defined administratively; missing data on patient comorbidity and patient choice.
- **Conclusion**: Treatment delays are worsening in HNC, particularly for chemoradiation patients; optimising referral pathways is the highest-priority intervention.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41416-026-03630-8 / PMID:42888204

## 4. Molecular Heterogeneity and Clinical Impact of Genomic Driver Alterations in Prostate Cancer — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Nat Commun
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-16
- **Authors**: Pellegrina D et al.
- **Aims**: Comprehensively catalogue recurrent somatic and germline driver alterations in primary prostate cancer and link them to clinical outcomes.
- **Method**: Integrative whole-genome sequencing (WGS) analysis in 961 primary prostate tumours from the Pan Prostate Cancer Group; structural variant, copy number, and point mutation cataloguing; unsupervised oncotype clustering.
- **Key findings**: At least one recurrent alteration identified in 97% of tumours; four oncotypes defined with distinct metastasis-free survival; drivers enriched in African-ancestry tumours identified; 25% of tumours carry genome-informed therapeutic targets.
- **Strengths**: Largest WGS prostate cancer cohort to date; integrative multi-alteration approach; inclusion of ancestry-stratified analyses.
- **Limitations**: Primary tumours only; limited follow-up for clinical endpoints; generalisability across health systems uncertain.
- **Conclusion**: Prostate cancer harbours extensive driver heterogeneity with oncotype-specific prognosis and ancestry-enriched alterations offering precision medicine targets.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41467-026-77900-7 / PMID:42888190

## 5. Radiotherapy + Serplulimab + Chemotherapy as First-Line Treatment for Metastatic Pancreatic Adenocarcinoma: Phase II Trial — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Clin Cancer Res
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-16
- **Authors**: Cheng K et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate radiotherapy combined with serplulimab (anti-PD-1) and gemcitabine/nab-paclitaxel (ICSBR regimen) as first-line therapy for metastatic PDAC.
- **Method**: Multicenter, single-arm, phase II trial (ChiCTR2300073237); n=46; primary endpoint 6-month PFS rate; comprehensive biomarker analysis including ctDNA and tumour microenvironment profiling.
- **Key findings**: 6-month PFS rate 78.3% (95% CI 67.2–91.1%), exceeding the 60% target; median OS 14.0 months; ORR 73.9%; ctDNA negativity and tumour-infiltrating T-cell proportions correlated with survival.
- **Strengths**: Exceeded primary endpoint with promising OS in a notoriously poor-prognosis cancer; integrated biomarker analysis identifies potential precision selection markers.
- **Limitations**: Single-arm design without randomised control; small sample (n=46); predominantly Chinese population limits generalisability.
- **Conclusion**: ICSBR regimen shows promising first-line efficacy in mPDAC; ctDNA and TME biomarkers warrant validation in a randomised trial.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1078-0432.ccr-26-1824 / PMID:42886001
