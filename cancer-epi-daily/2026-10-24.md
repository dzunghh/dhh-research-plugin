## 1. Foundation AI Models for Health Recognition by Facial Photographs (FAHR-Face) — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Digital Health
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-19 (EDAT; article ahead of print)
- **Authors**: Haugg F et al.
- **Aims**: Develop a foundation AI model that estimates biological age and predicts cancer survival from facial photographs.
- **Method**: Foundation model trained on >40 million facial photographs; fine-tuned for age estimation and survival prediction in 34,363 cancer patients; validated with Cox regression in two independent cohorts.
- **Key findings**: FAHR-FaceAge outperformed benchmark models (MAE 5.1 years). Highest-risk quartile by FAHR-FaceSurvival had >3× mortality (aHR 3.23, p<0.0001).
- **Strengths**: Large training dataset, independent validation, generalisability across age, sex, race, and cancer subgroups.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective validation only; facial photographs affected by cosmetic interventions or lighting may reduce accuracy.
- **Conclusion**: Non-invasive facial biomarkers derived from a foundation model provide complementary, scalable cancer prognostic information.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.landig.2026.101056 / PMID:42897078

## 2. Body Roundness Index and Cancer Risk in Postmenopausal Women — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiology, Biomarkers & Prevention
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-19
- **Authors**: Peila R et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate whether Body Roundness Index (BRI), capturing central adiposity, predicts site-specific cancer risk independently of BMI in postmenopausal women.
- **Method**: Prospective Cox regression in 144,865 WHI participants (median follow-up 17.9 years); 33,892 incident cancers; baseline and repeated BRI measures.
- **Key findings**: Higher BRI associated with 11 cancer sites. BMI adjustment attenuated associations for endometrial/breast/kidney but strengthened liver, lung, and esophageal cancer associations.
- **Strengths**: Very large sample, long follow-up, repeated measures, multiple sensitivity analyses.
- **Limitations**: Observational; BRI measured at limited time points; findings limited to postmenopausal women.
- **Conclusion**: BRI captures central adiposity-related cancer risk beyond BMI across multiple cancer sites.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-0667 / PMID:42895472

## 3. Germline Copy Number Variants and Lung Cancer Susceptibility — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiology, Biomarkers & Prevention
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-19
- **Authors**: Xiao F et al.
- **Aims**: Identify germline copy number variants (CNVs) contributing to lung cancer susceptibility beyond known SNP loci.
- **Method**: Genome-wide CNV analysis in TRICL consortium (19,342 cases, 15,917 controls); two CNV callers, polygenic risk scores, siRNA functional validation in lung fibroblasts.
- **Key findings**: Four CNV regions (1p36.22, 2q31.2, 6p21.32, 19q13.32) significantly associated with lung cancer risk. CNV-PRS from 4 genes replicated across independent datasets.
- **Strengths**: Large multi-consortium sample, dual analytical strategies, functional validation, independent replication.
- **Limitations**: Mostly European ancestry; CNV calling from array data has known limitations.
- **Conclusion**: Germline CNVs contribute to lung cancer susceptibility via mechanisms related to genomic instability.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-0120 / PMID:42895475

## 4. Lifetime Ovulatory Years and DNA Repair Variants on Ovarian Cancer Risk — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiology, Biomarkers & Prevention
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-21
- **Authors**: Garofalo DC et al.
- **Aims**: Test whether DNA repair capacity and lifetime ovulatory years (LOY) interact to elevate epithelial ovarian cancer (EOC) risk.
- **Method**: UK Biobank cohort (251,476 women, 847 incident EOC cases); gene-environment interaction analysis using a novel DNA repair capacity score from 163 genes.
- **Key findings**: LOY independently raised EOC hazard (HR 1.15 per 5-year increase). Pathogenic DNA repair variants also raised risk (HR 1.27). Combined HR 2.00; no significant synergistic interaction.
- **Strengths**: Large population cohort, genome-wide panel of DNA repair genes, novel LOY metric.
- **Limitations**: Single-population study; pathogenic variant classification relies on existing databases.
- **Conclusion**: LOY and DNA repair variants are independent, non-synergistic risk factors for ovarian cancer.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-0353 / PMID:42905104

## 5. Gut Microbiome-Related Plasma Metabolites and Colorectal Cancer Risk — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiology, Biomarkers & Prevention
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-19
- **Authors**: Malcomson FC et al.
- **Aims**: Determine whether pre-diagnostic circulating microbiome-related metabolites are associated with colorectal cancer (CRC) risk.
- **Method**: Nested case-control in Colon Cancer Family Registry (312 cases, 427 controls); 70 metabolites by targeted LC-MS/MS; conditional logistic regression; median follow-up 7 years.
- **Key findings**: Primary bile acids cholate and chenodeoxycholate positively associated with CRC (OR ~1.60 for Q4 vs Q1). Short-chain fatty acid associations did not survive multiple comparison correction.
- **Strengths**: Prospective design, pre-diagnostic blood samples, targeted metabolomics platform, stratified analyses by Lynch syndrome.
- **Limitations**: Single cohort enriched for familial CRC; small sample limits power for correction.
- **Conclusion**: Circulating bile acids may link gut microbiome composition to colorectal carcinogenesis.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-0718 / PMID:42895480
