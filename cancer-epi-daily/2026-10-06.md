## 1. Smoking Cessation Among Underserved Patients Referred for Lung Cancer Screening — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: JAMA
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-05
- **Authors**: Hart JL et al.
- **Aims**: Compare four smoking cessation strategies in underserved patients referred for lung cancer screening.
- **Method**: 4-arm RCT across 4 US health systems (n=3,220); primary endpoint biochemically confirmed sustained tobacco abstinence at 6 months.
- **Key findings**: Financial incentives achieved 8.8% abstinence vs 4.3% usual care (difference 4.6%, 95%CI 2.1–7.0%, P<0.001). Free pharmacotherapy alone did not outperform usual care.
- **Strengths**: Large multi-site RCT with biochemical confirmation, focused on historically underserved populations.
- **Limitations**: 6-month follow-up only; overall abstinence rates remained low (4–9%) across all arms.
- **Conclusion**: Financial incentives are the most effective strategy for smoking cessation among underserved lung cancer screening patients.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jama.2026.17192 / PMID:42832220

## 2. Hysterectomy-Corrected Incidence of Cervical Cancer Among Women With HIV in the US, 2001–2019 — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: J Natl Cancer Inst
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-03
- **Authors**: Tsegaye AT et al.
- **Aims**: Estimate hysterectomy-corrected cervical cancer incidence among US women with HIV to reduce surveillance bias.
- **Method**: Registry cohort study; HIV/AIDS Cancer Match linking 14 HIV and cancer registries; hysterectomy prevalence from BRFSS; Poisson regression for temporal trends.
- **Key findings**: Hysterectomy correction increased the age-standardized incidence rate by 23% overall (47.4 to 58.4/100,000); greatest increase in Black women (+28%).
- **Strengths**: Large national registry linkage, race/ethnicity-stratified correction, temporal trend analysis over 19 years.
- **Limitations**: Hysterectomy prevalence from BRFSS self-report may introduce misclassification; incomplete HIV data in some registries.
- **Conclusion**: Uncorrected cervical cancer rates substantially underestimate true burden among US women with HIV, especially Black and older women.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag352 / PMID:42830453

## 3. Clinical Practice Guideline for Systemic Antibacterial and Antifungal Prophylaxis in Pediatric Cancer and HCT Recipients: 2026 Update — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: J Clin Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-02
- **Authors**: Morgan JE et al.
- **Aims**: Update and harmonize evidence-based CPG for antibacterial and antifungal prophylaxis in pediatric cancer and HCT patients.
- **Method**: Systematic review of 195 RCTs (13 new since 2019); GRADE approach; international multidisciplinary panel.
- **Key findings**: Strong recommendation maintained for levofloxacin prophylaxis; new conditional recommendation for no prophylaxis when levofloxacin is contraindicated.
- **Strengths**: Comprehensive harmonized framework across antibacterial and antifungal CPGs; 195 trials reviewed with formal GRADE.
- **Limitations**: Several recommendations extrapolated from adult data due to limited pediatric-specific trial evidence.
- **Conclusion**: Harmonized 2026 guidelines consolidate prophylaxis strategies for pediatric cancer patients with actionable new recommendations.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1200/JCO-26-00720 / PMID:42826360

## 4. Late Effects After Novel Therapies for Childhood Cancer: Anticipating the Future Landscape — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: J Natl Cancer Inst
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-06
- **Authors**: Berkman AM et al.
- **Aims**: Synthesize emerging evidence on long-term toxicities from novel cancer therapies (immunotherapy, targeted agents, ADCs) in pediatric survivors.
- **Method**: Narrative review of FDA-approved novel pediatric cancer therapies; integrates acute toxicity data and available long-term follow-up including adult extrapolations.
- **Key findings**: Novel therapies introduce persistent toxicities across cardiovascular, neurologic, endocrine, and immune systems; subsequent malignancies documented; long-term profiles remain poorly characterised.
- **Strengths**: Timely and comprehensive; highlights critical survivorship research gap as 5-year survival exceeds 85%.
- **Limitations**: Primarily relies on adult population data; systematic long-term paediatric follow-up is largely absent.
- **Conclusion**: Extending existing survivorship cohorts to include novel-therapy–exposed children is urgently needed to define late effects and guide surveillance.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag371 / PMID:42836651

## 5. Rethinking Treatment Intensification in Poorly Chemosensitive Ovarian Cancer: A Review — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-01
- **Authors**: You B et al.
- **Aims**: Examine rationale and evidence for treatment intensification (chemosensitization) in poorly chemosensitive advanced high-grade serous ovarian cancer.
- **Method**: Review and synthesis of post-hoc analyses from six phase 3 RCTs (ICON-7, GOG-0218, ICON-8, ICON-8B, VELIA, IMagyn050); proposes response-adapted framework using KELIM biomarker.
- **Key findings**: Weekly dose-dense paclitaxel improved survival in poorly chemosensitive subset (ICON-8); bevacizumab showed greater benefit in high-risk/poor chemosensitivity subgroups.
- **Strengths**: Covers all major first-line trials; introduces KELIM and pathological chemotherapy response score as actionable predictors endorsed by ESMO/ASCO.
- **Limitations**: Evidence base relies on post-hoc subgroup analyses; prospective validation ongoing (SALVOVAR, RegeNovar).
- **Conclusion**: A response-adapted first-line strategy using chemosensitivity biomarkers enables selective intensification in patients most likely to benefit.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamaoncol.2026.3875 / PMID:42821287

## Appraisal notes

This week's digest is anchored by two high-rigour epidemiological studies: an RCT demonstrating financial incentives outperform pharmacotherapy for smoking cessation in underserved lung cancer screening populations, and a methodologically important registry study showing that failure to correct for hysterectomy prevalence meaningfully underestimates cervical cancer burden in HIV-positive women, with the largest bias among Black women. Together they highlight the dual importance of equity-focused trial design and surveillance methodology correction in cancer epidemiology. The remaining three papers extend into survivorship and clinical guideline synthesis, reflecting a trend toward long-term outcomes research as survival rates for childhood and gynaecological cancers continue to improve.
