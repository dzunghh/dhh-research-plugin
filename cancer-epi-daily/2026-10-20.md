## 1. Prediagnostic Proteomic Biomarkers for Colon and Rectal Cancer in 3 Prospective Cohorts — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: J Natl Cancer Inst
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-19
- **Authors**: Zhang X et al.
- **Aims**: Identify circulating prediagnostic protein biomarkers for colorectal cancer with site-specific and cross-cohort validation.
- **Method**: Nested case-control in 3 prospective cohorts (n=851 incident CRC cases); 1,801 plasma proteins via Olink panels; FDR-controlled.
- **Key findings**: 12 proteins significantly differed (FDR<0.1); colon and rectal cancers had distinct proteomic profiles (P=0.005); HDGF partially mediated BMI–rectal cancer risk (25%, FDR=0.044).
- **Strengths**: Three independent cohorts provided cross-cohort consistency checks (I²<0.01 for key biomarkers).
- **Limitations**: Modest effect sizes; mechanistic validation still required.
- **Conclusion**: Site-specific prediagnostic plasma proteomics reveals distinct colon vs. rectal cancer pathways, motivating targeted biomarker discovery.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag217 / PMID:42895287

## 2. Claims-Based Frailty, Dual Eligibility, and Unmet Home-Health Needs in Older Breast Cancer Survivors — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: J Natl Cancer Inst
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-19
- **Authors**: Qin X et al.
- **Aims**: Quantify frailty-related unmet home-health needs among older breast cancer survivors using linked Medicare administrative data.
- **Method**: SEER-Medicare-OASIS linkage (n=347,953 survivor-years); claims-based deficit-accumulation frailty index calibrated by elastic-net regression.
- **Key findings**: 59.6% of high-need survivor-years had no Medicare home-health use; frailty-HH association weaker in always-dual (+0.41 pp/0.01 DAFI) vs never-dual (+1.21 pp) survivors.
- **Strengths**: Population-based scale; frailty proxy validated against OASIS functional assessment (AUC=0.715).
- **Limitations**: Claims-based frailty likely underestimates true functional burden.
- **Conclusion**: Medicare home-health leaves substantial frailty-related needs unaddressed in older breast cancer survivors, with marked insurance-related disparities.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag327 / PMID:42893805

## 3. Camrelizumab Plus Docetaxel and Carboplatin as Neoadjuvant Therapy for Stage II–III TNBC: HELEN-Trio 011 RCT — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Ann Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-16
- **Authors**: Jiao D et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate anthracycline-free chemoimmunotherapy (camrelizumab + docetaxel-carboplatin) as neoadjuvant treatment for stage II–III TNBC.
- **Method**: Multicenter phase 3 RCT (14 hospitals, China); n=369 randomized 2:1; primary endpoint pCR (ypT0/Tis ypN0) in mITT population.
- **Key findings**: pCR 57.5% (camrelizumab arm) vs 45.4% (chemotherapy alone); absolute difference 12.2% (95% CI 1.4–22.9; one-sided P=0.014). Grade 3–4 adverse events 24.9% vs 21.8%.
- **Strengths**: Phase 3 multicenter RCT with a clinically meaningful anthracycline-free design and manageable toxicity.
- **Limitations**: Single-country (China), open-label; long-term survival data not yet mature.
- **Conclusion**: Adding camrelizumab to docetaxel-carboplatin significantly increases pCR in TNBC, supporting an anthracycline-free neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy strategy.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.annonc.2026.10.102 / PMID:42887596

## 4. Lifestyle and Genetic Risk Factors for Postmenopausal Breast Cancer in the Multiethnic Cohort — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: J Natl Cancer Inst
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-15
- **Authors**: Zhao C et al.
- **Aims**: Assess whether a healthy lifestyle index score reduces postmenopausal breast cancer risk regardless of polygenic risk score (PRS) in a diverse cohort.
- **Method**: Prospective cohort (n=22,725 postmenopausal women, 5 racial/ethnic groups); multivariable Cox models; 313-variant breast cancer PRS.
- **Key findings**: High vs low HLIS: HR=0.67 (95% CI 0.57–0.79); no multiplicative HLIS×PRS interaction (P=0.48); consistent across both PRS strata.
- **Strengths**: Large multiethnic cohort with validated PRS; five racial/ethnic groups represented.
- **Limitations**: Self-reported lifestyle measures; associations most evident in White and Japanese American women.
- **Conclusion**: Healthy lifestyle reduces postmenopausal breast cancer risk independent of genetic predisposition across diverse populations.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag394 / PMID:42879705

## 5. Colonoscopy Screening Outcomes in Lynch Syndrome: Differential Colorectal Cancer Paths in MLH1 vs MSH2 Carriers — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: J Natl Cancer Inst
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-19
- **Authors**: Briollais L et al.
- **Aims**: Model differential CRC development pathways under colonoscopy surveillance in Lynch syndrome by MMR gene type.
- **Method**: Registry-based study (Familial GI Cancer Registry, Toronto); novel joint model integrating colonoscopy visits, adenoma counts, and CRC occurrence.
- **Key findings**: Increased colonoscopy and adenoma detection reduced CRC risk in MSH2 but not MLH1 carriers; 10-year CRC risk higher in MLH1 vs MSH2 (males: 7.9% vs 4.0%; females: 4.2% vs 1.6%).
- **Strengths**: Novel joint modeling accounts for interdependencies among surveillance events; gene-specific insight for Lynch syndrome management.
- **Limitations**: Single-center registry; limited sample size for reliable subgroup estimates.
- **Conclusion**: MLH1 and MSH2 carriers follow distinct carcinogenic pathways, suggesting gene-tailored colonoscopy surveillance protocols may be warranted.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag392 / PMID:42895288
