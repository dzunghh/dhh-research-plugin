## 1. Sex Differences in Mortality Among Patients With Hepatocellular Carcinoma — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Network Open
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-01
- **Authors**: Yan T et al.
- **Aims**: To assess the association between sex and overall, liver-related, and non-liver-related mortality in patients with HCC across 17 international centres.
- **Method**: Retrospective cohort (8,594 HCC patients, 2000–2023, 5 countries) with inverse probability of treatment weighting to balance baseline characteristics.
- **Key findings**: Male sex independently associated with 14% higher overall mortality (aHR 1.14, 95% CI 1.05–1.23); highest excess risk in alcohol-associated liver disease (aHR 1.90) and Asian patients.
- **Strengths**: Large multiethnic multinational sample with robust IPTW adjustment and subgroup analyses by aetiology.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective design; unmeasured confounders (lifestyle, surveillance intensity) cannot be excluded.
- **Conclusion**: Sex-based heterogeneity in HCC prognosis varies by ethnicity and liver disease aetiology, supporting sex-stratified management approaches.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.37313 / PMID:42826017

## 2. Facilitated Cascade Genetic Testing for Relatives of Individuals With Pathogenic Variants: A Randomized Controlled Trial — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Journal of Clinical Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-29
- **Authors**: Wilke RN et al.
- **Aims**: To compare facilitated cascade genetic testing (CGT) with standard care in first-degree relatives of BRCA pathogenic-variant carriers.
- **Method**: Cluster-RCT stratified by cancer history and family size; 151 probands, 286 first-degree relatives; 6-month primary endpoint of testing completion.
- **Key findings**: Testing uptake 73.2% (intervention) vs 50.7% (control) at 6 months (P<.001); 46% of relatives found to carry the familial variant.
- **Strengths**: Randomised design with pragmatic eligibility; free testing offered to all arms minimising access confounding.
- **Limitations**: Single cancer type (BRCA variants); generalisability to other hereditary syndromes unclear.
- **Conclusion**: Navigation-supported facilitated CGT more than doubles timely uptake and has substantial cascade public-health impact.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1200/JCO-26-00735 / PMID:42809807

## 3. Urine Cell-Free RNA for Bladder Cancer Detection and Treatment Response Prediction — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Nature Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-02
- **Authors**: Liu KJ et al.
- **Aims**: To develop and validate a urine cell-free RNA liquid biopsy method (uRARE-seq) for non-invasive bladder cancer detection and treatment response classification.
- **Method**: Diagnostic validation study across 683 urine samples from cancer patients and controls; ROC analysis and biomarker modelling for BCG vs chemotherapy response.
- **Key findings**: 95% sensitivity at 90% specificity for localised bladder cancer detection; AUC 0.93 for predicting BCG vs chemotherapy response in 114 pretreatment samples.
- **Strengths**: Large multi-cohort validation; outperforms urine tumour DNA and is unaffected by field-effect mutations.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective sample collection; prospective clinical utility studies not yet completed.
- **Conclusion**: Urine cfRNA profiling is a promising non-invasive tool for bladder cancer detection and treatment selection, warranting prospective trials.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41591-026-04673-3 / PMID:42827132

## 4. Intravenous Hyaluronidase-Expressing Oncolytic Adenovirus With Chemotherapy in Metastatic Pancreatic Cancer: A Randomized Phase 2b Trial — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Nature Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-30
- **Authors**: Garcia-Carbonero R et al.
- **Aims**: To evaluate VCN-01 (hyaluronidase-expressing oncolytic adenovirus) plus gemcitabine/nab-paclitaxel (GnP) versus GnP alone as first-line therapy in metastatic pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma.
- **Method**: Randomised phase 2b trial (n=101 evaluable); primary endpoint overall survival in intent-to-treat and full analysis set populations.
- **Key findings**: FAS median OS 10.8 vs 8.6 months (HR 0.57, P=.055); 18-month survival 31.1% vs 8.5%; PFS significantly improved (HR 0.55, P=.011).
- **Strengths**: Randomised design; sustained second-dose viral replication confirmed despite neutralising antibodies; long follow-up survival data.
- **Limitations**: Primary OS endpoint narrowly missed significance in intent-to-treat population; small sample limits subgroup inference.
- **Conclusion**: VCN-01 + GnP warrants phase 3 evaluation as first-line therapy for metastatic PDAC, particularly given durable survival benefit at 15–18 months.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41591-026-04705-y / PMID:42816596

## 5. Sentinel Lymph Node Biopsy After Neoadjuvant Chemotherapy in Patients Presenting With Locally Advanced Breast Cancer — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Journal of Clinical Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-29
- **Authors**: Barrio AV et al.
- **Aims**: To prospectively determine the false-negative rate (FNR) of sentinel lymph node biopsy (SLNB) after neoadjuvant chemotherapy in locally advanced breast cancer.
- **Method**: Prospective cohort (n=145, cT4/cN2-3 patients at MSK and Miami Cancer Institute); dual-tracer SLNB with completion axillary dissection; pre-specified adequacy criteria.
- **Key findings**: SLN identification rate 74%; FNR 2.1% (95% CI 0.1–12.7%) in adequately mapped patients; nodal pCR rate 46%.
- **Strengths**: Prospective multi-institutional design in a historically excluded LABC population; rigorous FNR calculation methodology.
- **Limitations**: Low mapping rate (74%) limits applicability; wide CI around FNR estimate due to modest residual-disease events.
- **Conclusion**: SLNB appears feasible with acceptable FNR after NAC in LABC, potentially sparing axillary dissection in node-downstaged patients.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1200/JCO-26-01114 / PMID:42809799

## Appraisal notes

This week's digest reflects two dominant themes: precision cancer epidemiology and liquid biopsy diagnostics. The HCC sex-disparity cohort and the BRCA cascade-testing RCT both exemplify rigorous real-world epi design—large-scale IPTW and a cluster-RCT respectively—that directly informs clinical and public-health policy. The urine cfRNA paper advances non-invasive cancer detection with state-of-the-art sensitivity/specificity, while the oncolytic adenovirus trial and SLNB cohort highlight the ongoing push to refine treatment selection and surgical staging in hard-to-treat cancers. Methodologically, IPTW and target-trial emulation continue to appear in lieu of RCTs wherever randomisation is impractical, reinforcing the growing centrality of causal inference frameworks in cancer epidemiology.
