## 1. Second Cancer Risk in Female Survivors of Hodgkin Lymphoma: A National Cohort Analysis Across Treatment Eras — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: J Clin Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-12
- **Authors**: Macklin-Doherty A et al.
- **Aims**: Quantify subsequent primary malignancy (SPM) risks in 7,428 female HL survivors treated over 60 years.
- **Method**: Population-based national cohort (England/Wales 1954–2010); national cancer registry linkage; standardised incidence ratios and multivariable models.
- **Key findings**: Overall SIR 3.3 (95% CI 3.1–3.5); solid cancer risks fell 25% from 1954–1989 to 2000–2010 eras; cumulative SPM incidence 36% at 50 years.
- **Strengths**: Large registry-linked national cohort spanning 60 years enables temporal trend analysis across treatment eras.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective; detailed treatment records may be incomplete for the earliest eras.
- **Conclusion**: Radiotherapy reduction drives substantially lower SPM risk in modern HL survivors, but elevated risks persist >50 years.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1200/JCO-26-00418 / PMID:43019503

## 2. Normalized Membrane Ratio of TROP2 as a Predictor of Clinical Outcomes With Datopotamab Deruxtecan in Non-Small-Cell Lung Cancer — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Nat Med
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-11
- **Authors**: Garassino MC et al.
- **Aims**: Develop and validate a computational TROP2 biomarker predicting response to Dato-DXd ADC in advanced NSCLC.
- **Method**: Supervised computational pathology quantitative continuous scoring; developed in TROPION-PanTumor01 (n=115), validated in TROPION-Lung01 (n=352).
- **Key findings**: TROP2 NMR-positive patients had longer PFS with Dato-DXd (7.2 vs 4.0 months; HR 0.40, 95% CI 0.25–0.64); interaction p=0.0098.
- **Strengths**: Validated in independent RCT with pre-specified interaction testing; directly clinically actionable.
- **Limitations**: Validated for single ADC only; NMR cut-point requires further external validation beyond trial setting.
- **Conclusion**: TROP2 NMR is a validated predictive biomarker for Dato-DXd efficacy in nonsquamous NSCLC.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41591-026-04716-9 / PMID:43015400

## 3. Prospective and Genetic Analyses Implicate Lower Immunosurveillance in the Aetiology of Prostate Cancer — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Nat Commun
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-10
- **Authors**: Parsaeian M et al.
- **Aims**: Identify circulating immune proteins prospectively and causally linked to prostate cancer risk.
- **Method**: Proteomics (368 immune proteins) in EPIC cohort (1,434 cases + matched controls) with UK Biobank meta-analysis; Mendelian randomisation for causal inference.
- **Key findings**: Higher NK/T-cell surveillance proteins (IL15, FLT3LG) associated with lower risk; PARP1, SPINT2, NME3 genetically implicated in increased risk.
- **Strengths**: Prospective pre-diagnostic samples with multi-cohort meta-analysis and MR for causal validation.
- **Limitations**: Multiple testing thresholds not met in EPIC alone; replication in non-European populations needed.
- **Conclusion**: Immune surveillance pathways causally influence prostate cancer, highlighting prevention and therapeutic targets.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41467-026-77378-3 / PMID:43009480

## 4. Environmental Tobacco Smoke and Gastric Cancer: Evidence from the Stomach Cancer Pooling (StoP) Project and a Comprehensive Meta-analysis — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiol Biomarkers Prev
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-12
- **Authors**: Wang Y et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate the association between secondhand smoke (ETS) exposure and gastric cancer risk.
- **Method**: Pooled case-control analysis (5 StoP studies; 1,560 cases, 2,497 controls); comprehensive meta-analysis (14 published studies); random-effects models.
- **Key findings**: H. pylori-adjusted meta-analysis aOR=1.41 (95% CI 1.21–1.64); overall pooled aOR 1.25 (95% CI 0.98–1.59; I²=69.9%).
- **Strengths**: Largest pooled ETS–GC analysis to date with H. pylori confounder adjustment in meta-analytic subgroup.
- **Limitations**: High heterogeneity (I²=69.9%); retrospective ETS exposure measurement susceptible to recall bias.
- **Conclusion**: ETS is a probable preventable gastric cancer risk factor, supporting targeted public health policies.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-0404 / PMID:43018473

## 5. Toxicity, Health-Related Quality of Life and Sexuality After Molecular Profile-Based Adjuvant Treatment in High-Intermediate Risk Endometrial Cancer: PORTEC-4a — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Br J Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-11
- **Authors**: Van den Heerik ASVM et al.
- **Aims**: Report toxicity, HRQoL, and sexual health from the PORTEC-4a molecular-guided adjuvant RCT in endometrial cancer.
- **Method**: Pre-specified secondary RCT analysis; n=564 randomised; 58-month follow-up; EORTC QLQ-C30/EN24 patient-reported outcomes.
- **Key findings**: Molecular arm had fewer grade 1–2 AEs at treatment completion (28.3% vs 45.2%, p<0.01); HRQoL preserved vs normative data; reduced sexual inactivity largely non-disease-driven at 3 years.
- **Strengths**: Large multi-centre RCT with long follow-up, validated PROMs, and normative data benchmarking.
- **Limitations**: Sexual outcomes subject to self-report bias; compliance not randomised.
- **Conclusion**: Molecular-guided adjuvant therapy reduces treatment toxicity while preserving HRQoL in endometrial cancer.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41416-026-03642-4 / PMID:43015360
