## 1. Alcohol amount, drinking patterns, and gastric cancer risk: a nationwide study using targeted maximum likelihood estimation — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: European Journal of Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-22
- **Authors**: Kim CH et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate dose-response associations of alcohol amount and drinking patterns with gastric cancer (GC) risk using causal inference methods.
- **Method**: Nationwide retrospective cohort of 6,007,469 Korean adults aged 50–69, 10-year follow-up; TMLE with Super Learner and DAG-guided confounder selection.
- **Key findings**: GC risk increased progressively from light to heavy drinking vs. non-drinkers (heavy: 5-year RR 1.46, 95% CI 1.41–1.51). Even light drinking showed modest significant risk increase (5-year RR 1.08, 95% CI 1.05–1.11).
- **Strengths**: Massive national cohort with repeated alcohol measurements; rigorous causal inference framework (TMLE + Super Learner) reduces confounding bias.
- **Limitations**: Self-reported alcohol intake; light-drinking association attenuated in sensitivity analyses with expanded adjustment set.
- **Conclusion**: Alcohol is associated with GC risk in a dose-response manner; frequent drinking carries higher risk than binge-type patterns at equivalent intake.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1007/s10654-026-01452-9 / PMID:42631910

## 2. Ultrasensitive circulating tumor DNA analysis improves detection of molecular residual disease in locally advanced esophageal cancer — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Clinical Cancer Research
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-21
- **Authors**: Rhoades J et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate whether ultrasensitive ctDNA testing improves molecular residual disease (MRD) detection in esophageal cancer post-curative-intent therapy.
- **Method**: Prospective biomarker study; 331 plasma samples from 36 patients; two MAESTRO-based ultrasensitive tumor-informed assays (MAESTRO-Pool vs. Oncodetect V2) compared.
- **Key findings**: Oncodetect V2 detected ctDNA in 64% of recurrence patients at first postoperative sample; 59% of ctDNA-positive samples were below first-generation assay limits; 100% of patients with recurrence eventually tested positive.
- **Strengths**: Head-to-head comparison of two state-of-the-art ultrasensitive assays with strong PPV/NPV for recurrence; tracks up to 5,000 mutations per patient.
- **Limitations**: Small sample (n=36); single-institution; requires tumor-informed approach limiting broad applicability.
- **Conclusion**: Ultrasensitive ctDNA testing detects part-per-million residual disease and should be standard in esophageal cancer MRD monitoring.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-25-4852 / PMID:42627756

## 3. A deep learning-derived risk score model from digital pathology to forecast nivolumab outcomes in gastric carcinoma — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: British Journal of Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-21
- **Authors**: Hong Y et al.
- **Aims**: Develop a deep learning model to predict nivolumab response in gastric cancer (GC) using PD-L1 immunohistochemistry whole-slide images.
- **Method**: Cell-detection network trained on 1,927 patches from 88 WSIs; validated in 147 patients on nivolumab plus chemotherapy; computational positive cell ratio (cPCR) combined with tumour-stroma ratio via stepwise selection.
- **Key findings**: cPCR AUC (0.601) was non-inferior to PD-L1 CPS (0.586) but superior by net reclassification (p=0.027); risk-score model combining cPCR + tumour-stroma ratio improved immunotherapy benefit prediction.
- **Strengths**: Independent validation cohort; head-to-head comparison with standard PD-L1 CPS; objective computational approach.
- **Limitations**: Single-institution Korean cohort; limited to PD-L1 28-8 antibody; deep learning model generalisability unclear.
- **Conclusion**: AI-derived digital pathology risk scores outperform manual PD-L1 scoring and could guide immunotherapy selection in GC.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41416-026-03590-z / PMID:42629440

## 4. Dual-oxygen pancreatic cancer organoids recapitulate basal-classical heterogeneity validated by spatial transcriptomics — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: British Journal of Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-22
- **Authors**: Kumano K et al.
- **Aims**: Determine whether oxygen tension during organoid culture affects retention of basal-like vs. classical PDAC subtype heterogeneity.
- **Method**: Spatially resolved single-cell transcriptomics on 12 primary PDAC tumours; paired organoids grown under normoxia (20% O₂) vs. hypoxia (1% O₂); morphology, subtype scores, and drug sensitivity compared.
- **Key findings**: Hypoxic organoids retained basal-like traits and showed reduced chemotherapy sensitivity; normoxic organoids skewed classical. Basal-like phenotype was stable after oxygen switching.
- **Strengths**: Spatial transcriptomics validation directly links in vivo hypoxic niches to in vitro organoid phenotype; functional drug sensitivity data included.
- **Limitations**: Small tumour cohort (n=12); single institution; causal relationship between hypoxia and basal-like stability needs further mechanistic investigation.
- **Conclusion**: Hypoxic culture conditions are necessary to preserve tumour heterogeneity in PDAC organoid models, improving translational fidelity.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41416-026-03577-w / PMID:42632864

## 5. Elacestrant in Combination with Everolimus for Estrogen Receptor-Positive, HER2-Negative Previously Treated Advanced Breast Cancer: Results from ELEVATE — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Clinical Cancer Research
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-24
- **Authors**: Rugo HS et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate safety and efficacy of elacestrant (oral SERD) plus everolimus (mTOR inhibitor) in ER+/HER2- advanced breast cancer after CDK4/6i progression.
- **Method**: Phase 1b/2 umbrella trial (ELEVATE); n=50 in phase 2; 100% prior CDK4/6i, 50% prior fulvestrant; primary endpoint PFS; exploratory ESR1/PIK3CA biomarkers.
- **Key findings**: Median PFS 8.3 months (95% CI 4.0–10.2) overall; consistent across ESR1-mutant (8.3 mo), ESR1-wild-type (9.0 mo), PIK3CA-mutant (8.3 mo); manageable toxicity (grade ≥3 in 6%).
- **Strengths**: Biomarker-stratified all-oral regimen; clinically meaningful PFS across genomic subgroups; includes primary endocrine-resistance patients.
- **Limitations**: Phase 1b/2 without randomised control; small subgroup sizes limit biomarker conclusions.
- **Conclusion**: Elacestrant plus everolimus shows promising all-oral activity irrespective of ESR1/PIK3CA mutation, warranting phase 3 evaluation.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-26-1816 / PMID:42635598
