## 1. Racial and Ethnic Disparities in US Colorectal Cancer Mortality by County, Sex, and Age — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-20
- **Authors**: Strassle PD et al.
- **Aims**: Quantify variation in colorectal cancer (CRC) mortality across US counties by race/ethnicity, sex, and age group from 2000 to 2019.
- **Method**: Population-based cross-sectional study using validated small-area estimation models corrected for race/ethnicity misclassification on death certificates.
- **Key findings**: Most counties saw declines in CRC mortality among adults ≥45 years (male: 82.5 → 54.4/100,000; female: 58.9 → 39.7/100,000). American Indian/Alaska Native males had extreme county-level variation (13.6–213.5/100,000 in 2019). Rates among adults <45 years were stable or increased.
- **Strengths**: Large population-based data (306 million/year), race/ethnicity misclassification correction, county-level granularity across 2000–2019.
- **Limitations**: Cross-sectional design limits causal inference; race/ethnicity self-reported on death certificates.
- **Conclusion**: Major CRC mortality gains at the national level mask persistent extreme disparities in specific counties and racial/ethnic groups, demanding targeted interventions.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamaoncol.2026.3194 / PMID:42623048

## 2. Adiposity and Risk of Breast Cancer in 280,000 Chinese Women: A Prospective Cohort Study — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: International Journal of Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-18
- **Authors**: Kartsonaki C et al.
- **Aims**: Examine associations between multiple adiposity measures and breast cancer risk by menopausal status and receptor subtype in Chinese women.
- **Method**: Prospective cohort (China Kadoorie Biobank, n=284,538), Cox regression, 10-year follow-up, 2,379 incident breast cancer cases.
- **Key findings**: Higher BMI increased breast cancer risk in both postmenopausal (HR 1.35, 95% CI 1.24–1.47) and premenopausal (HR 1.13, 95% CI 1.03–1.24) women. Among postmenopausal women, adiposity associated with ER+ but not ER− breast cancer.
- **Strengths**: Very large prospective cohort with measured adiposity, menopausal-status and receptor-subtype stratification, multiple adiposity metrics.
- **Limitations**: Predominantly Han Chinese; menopausal status based on self-report; residual confounding possible.
- **Conclusion**: General and central adiposity elevate breast cancer risk in Chinese women regardless of menopausal status, with ER+ subtype specificity in postmenopausal women.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/ije/dyag155 / PMID:42618884

## 3. Association of Food Insecurity with Reduced US Cancer Screening Rates — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiology, Biomarkers & Prevention
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-19
- **Authors**: Shah M et al.
- **Aims**: Determine whether lower food security status is associated with lower completion of colorectal, breast, cervical, and prostate cancer screening.
- **Method**: Retrospective analysis of National Health Interview Survey 2018–2023; multivariable logistic regression; n=20,518–59,849 per cancer cohort.
- **Key findings**: Marginal food insecurity associated with lower screening across all four cancers (aOR 0.63–0.88); low/very low food security showed similar or larger deficits (aOR 0.60–0.88). Associations significant after multivariable adjustment.
- **Strengths**: Large nationally representative sample across six survey years, four cancer screening types, sensitivity analyses with age-stratified eligibility definitions.
- **Limitations**: Cross-sectional (self-reported screening and food security); causal inference not established; complete-case analysis may introduce bias.
- **Conclusion**: Food insecurity is independently associated with reduced cancer screening; addressing food insecurity may reduce screening inequities.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-0024 / PMID:42615991

## 4. Risk-Adapted Intervals for Colorectal Cancer Screening Using Fecal Immunochemical Tests — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: BMC Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-18
- **Authors**: Heisser T et al.
- **Aims**: Assess whether quantitative fecal hemoglobin (Hb) concentration among FIT-negative participants can define risk-adapted screening intervals.
- **Method**: Modelling study using COSIMO validated simulation tool; colonoscopy-confirmed neoplasia prevalences from 6,661 FIT-negative screening participants (BLITZ cohort, Germany).
- **Key findings**: High-Hb FIT-negative patients exceeded the 2-year cancer detection rate of low-Hb patients within just 1 year of follow-up. Expected CRC detection in the low-Hb group: 0.17% at 1 year vs. 0.62% at 5 years.
- **Strengths**: Validated simulation model, real-world colonoscopy data, clinically actionable stratification using routinely available Hb concentration.
- **Limitations**: Modelling study without prospective validation; single-country cohort may limit generalizability.
- **Conclusion**: Fecal Hb concentration in FIT-negative participants enables risk-adapted intervals, potentially optimising resource use in colorectal cancer screening programs.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1186/s12916-026-05138-7 / PMID:42613607

## 5. Federal and Industry Sponsorship in US Cancer Clinical Trials — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-20
- **Authors**: Unger JM et al.
- **Aims**: Characterise differences between federally and industry-sponsored US cancer clinical trial portfolios from 2008 to 2024.
- **Method**: Comparative cross-sectional study of 11,681 interventional cancer trials from ClinicalTrials.gov; χ² tests and odds ratios.
- **Key findings**: Industry more likely to run single-agent drug trials (77.4% vs 48.6%). Federal trials more likely prevention (4.7% vs 1.1%, OR 4.43), deescalation (3.1% vs 0.4%, OR 8.38), rare cancers (17.5% vs 12.1%), and paediatric populations (16.1% vs 5.3%).
- **Strengths**: Comprehensive registry-based sample of 16 years of trial data; standardised classification of sponsor type and trial characteristics.
- **Limitations**: ClinicalTrials.gov registration completeness varies; sponsor classification may oversimplify mixed-funding trials.
- **Conclusion**: Federal and industry cancer trial portfolios are empirically complementary; federal funding uniquely drives prevention, deescalation, rare cancer, and paediatric research.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamaoncol.2026.3026 / PMID:42623089

## Appraisal notes

This week's digest highlights a strong intersection of cancer health equity and screening methodology. Two independently derived analyses — county-level CRC mortality disparities (JAMA Oncol) and food insecurity barriers to multi-cancer screening (Cancer Epidemiol Biomarkers Prev) — converge on structural socioeconomic drivers as primary determinants of cancer inequity, suggesting that clinical screening improvements alone are insufficient. Methodologically, the shift toward simulation-based personalised screening intervals (FIT Hb stratification, BMC Med) and validated small-area estimation (JAMA Oncol) reflects growing epi capacity to operationalise risk-adapted cancer control beyond RCT evidence.
