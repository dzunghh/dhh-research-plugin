## 1. Liquid Biopsy for Early Detection of Pancreatic Ductal Adenocarcinoma — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Nature Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-16
- **Authors**: Xu C et al.
- **Aims**: Develop and validate a blood-based miRNA composite score (PANXEON) for early-stage PDAC detection.
- **Method**: International multicenter prospective observational biomarker study (n=1,785) across 4 countries; 10-miRNA signature + CA19-9 composite.
- **Key findings**: miRNA signature AUC 88.6% in testing cohort; sensitivity 86.8% for stage I–II PDAC combined with CA19-9; FPR 3.2% in low-risk controls.
- **Strengths**: Large prospective validation with longitudinal correlation to neoadjuvant treatment response and recurrence.
- **Limitations**: High-risk control FPR 15.6% may limit utility in surveillance populations; requires large-scale prospective validation.
- **Conclusion**: PANXEON demonstrates potential as a clinically actionable blood test complementing existing PDAC early-detection strategies.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41591-026-04625-x / PMID:42749887

## 2. Per- and Polyfluoroalkyl Substances Levels and Liver Cancer Risk: A Nested Case-Control Analysis — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-17
- **Authors**: Watling CZ et al.
- **Aims**: Assess nine lesser-studied PFAS compounds and liver cancer risk in prospective cohorts.
- **Method**: Nested case-control study within 12 US prospective cohorts (853 cases, 853 matched controls); multivariable conditional logistic regression.
- **Key findings**: PFHpA positively associated with liver cancer (OR 1.78, 95% CI 1.26–2.52); associations stronger in males (OR 3.72) and hepatocellular carcinoma (OR 4.53).
- **Strengths**: Multi-cohort design with pre-diagnostic serum; subgroup analyses by sex, cancer subtype, and time to diagnosis.
- **Limitations**: Single blood draw per participant; no correction for multiple comparisons across 9 PFAS compounds.
- **Conclusion**: Short-chain PFAS, particularly PFHpA, may elevate liver cancer risk, warranting further prospective research given ongoing consumer-product use.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag325 / PMID:42752878

## 3. Blood Test Trend vs Single Threshold Abnormality for Cancer Discrimination in Unexpected Weight Loss — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: PLoS Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-17
- **Authors**: Nicholson BD et al.
- **Aims**: Assess whether historical blood test trends improve cancer detection over single-threshold abnormality in primary care patients with unexpected weight loss.
- **Method**: Retrospective cohort study (CPRD; n=275,205 UWL patients, 5.0% cancer); joint Cox models for 1–10-year trends across 26 tests; AUC comparison.
- **Key findings**: Age/sex-adjusted trends outperformed unadjusted abnormalities in 34 test-cancer combinations; highest AUC 0.82.
- **Strengths**: Large national primary care dataset; systematic evaluation of 26 blood tests across multiple cancer sites.
- **Limitations**: Observational design with residual confounding; trend analysis restricted to patients with ≥2 tests in window.
- **Conclusion**: Incorporating age/sex-adjusted longitudinal blood test trends into triage algorithms improves cancer detection in primary care.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1371/journal.pmed.1004956 / PMID:42752402

## 4. Sex-Specific Biological Aging Clocks Across Organs and Omics — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Nature Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-16
- **Authors**: not reported et al.
- **Aims**: Develop sex-stratified biological aging clocks across 15 organ systems and characterise their genetic and molecular determinants.
- **Method**: 38 sex-specific clocks (multi-omic, multi-cohort); Mendelian randomization for organ-aging genetic liability; longitudinal survival and cognitive analyses.
- **Key findings**: Sex-specific clocks predicted systemic disease and all-cause mortality in sex- and organ-dependent patterns; female and male clocks showed marked divergence in genetic architecture.
- **Strengths**: Multi-omics, multi-cohort design with MR for causal inference on aging-disease relationships including cancer risk.
- **Limitations**: Cross-sectional training reduces longitudinal causal inference; sex-stratified training halves effective sample size per stratum.
- **Conclusion**: Sex-stratified aging models uncover distinct organ-level biological age signatures obscured by pooled approaches, with implications for cancer risk stratification.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41591-026-04662-6 / PMID:42749890

## 5. Accelerometer-Derived Real-World Sleep Stages and Risk of Incident Diseases: UK Biobank Phenome-Wide Analysis — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: PLoS Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-17
- **Authors**: Luo J et al.
- **Aims**: Map associations between objectively measured sleep patterns (stages, duration, fragmentation) and incident disease across 1,049 phenotypes.
- **Method**: UK Biobank cohort (n=95,559); wrist-accelerometer-derived sleep staging; Cox proportional hazard phenome-wide analysis; median 8.9-year follow-up.
- **Key findings**: Sleep patterns significantly associated with 156 disease incidences; higher REM sleep linked to lower risk of 83 conditions; 6–8 h sleep most protective.
- **Strengths**: Large population cohort with objective accelerometer measures; phenome-wide design covering 1,049 outcomes including cancers.
- **Limitations**: Observational; residual confounding and selection bias in who was measured; accelerometer staging approximates polysomnography.
- **Conclusion**: Objectively measured REM sleep and 6–8 h total duration associate with broadly lower disease incidence, including cancer-related phenotypes.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1371/journal.pmed.1005213 / PMID:42752434
