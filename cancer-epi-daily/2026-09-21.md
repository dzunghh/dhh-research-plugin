## 1. Menopausal Hormone Therapy and Primary Liver Cancer: Long-Term Follow-Up of the Women's Health Initiative Randomized Trials — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: British Journal of Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-16
- **Authors**: Pichardo MS et al.
- **Aims**: To evaluate long-term effects of menopausal hormone therapy on primary liver cancer incidence and mortality in postmenopausal women.
- **Method**: Long-term follow-up (~24 years) of two WHI randomized placebo-controlled trials (n=27,347); Cox regression for HR estimates.
- **Key findings**: No overall effect of CEE-alone or CEE+MPA on liver cancer incidence or mortality. CEE+MPA associated with increased liver cancer among prior oral contraceptive users (HR 4.30, 95% CI 1.46–12.72).
- **Strengths**: Longest follow-up of any RCT on this question; large, well-characterised postmenopausal cohort with 74 incident liver cancers.
- **Limitations**: Small event numbers limit subgroup precision; women enrolled 1993–1998, limiting generalisability to contemporary HRT formulations.
- **Conclusion**: MHT has no overall liver cancer effect, but prior OC users receiving combined HRT may warrant closer monitoring.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41416-026-03621-9 / PMID:42749856

## 2. Antidepressant and Antipsychotic Prescriptions Among 21,605 English Survivors of Childhood Cancer: A Population-Based Cohort — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-15
- **Authors**: Heymer EJ et al.
- **Aims**: To quantify antidepressant and antipsychotic prescribing rates in childhood cancer survivors versus the general population.
- **Method**: Population-based cohort using English national prescription data (April 2015–December 2021); SIRs and AERs per 100 person-years.
- **Key findings**: 31% of survivors received antidepressants (expected 21%; SIR=1.5). Female survivors had highest excess (AER=2.7%); bone cancer survivors had highest antidepressant SIR (2.0); CNS tumour survivors had highest antipsychotic SIR (2.4).
- **Strengths**: Largest study to date using comprehensive national prescription linkage; long observation window (6+ years); cancer-type stratification.
- **Limitations**: Prescriptions are a proxy for mental illness diagnosis; data limited to England and may not generalise internationally.
- **Conclusion**: Childhood cancer survivors have substantially elevated antidepressant use, supporting systematic mental health assessment in survivorship care.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag323 / PMID:42745477

## 3. Effectiveness of an eHealth Intervention to Improve Quality of Life in Long-Term Breast Cancer Survivors: A 9-Month Randomized Controlled Trial — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-16
- **Authors**: Pimentel-Parra GA et al.
- **Aims**: To evaluate the CUMACA-M eHealth intervention's effect on quality of life in long-term breast cancer survivors over 9 months.
- **Method**: Parallel-group RCT (n=201; ≥5 years post-diagnosis); mixed linear models; intention-to-treat analysis with >90% retention.
- **Key findings**: QoL declined in control group (−0.26 over 9 months) but remained stable in intervention group; adjusted difference 0.24 (95% CI 0.03–0.44, P=0.024, Cohen d=0.32).
- **Strengths**: Registered RCT with high retention and rigorous ITT analysis using validated QoL instrument.
- **Limitations**: Single-country trial (Spain); modest effect size; no blinding possible in behavioural intervention.
- **Conclusion**: A digital eHealth programme can prevent QoL deterioration in long-term breast cancer survivors, offering a scalable survivorship care tool.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag328 / PMID:42747290

## 4. Expected Late-Stage Reduction Under Extended Screening in the First Multicancer Screening Trial — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiology, Biomarkers & Prevention
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-16
- **Authors**: Gogebakan KC et al.
- **Aims**: To model whether continued annual multi-cancer screening would produce greater late-stage incidence reductions than the three-round trial reported.
- **Method**: Multi-cancer natural history model calibrated to observed late-stage reductions from the trial; projections over 10 annual screening rounds across 15 best-fit model variants.
- **Key findings**: Calibrated models reproduced round-1 excess and increasing reductions thereafter; late-stage reductions plateau at 13–17% by round 10 with cumulative 9–13% by round 10.
- **Strengths**: Formally calibrated to trial-observed data; uncertainty quantified across 15 model variants; directly addresses a policy-relevant gap.
- **Limitations**: Projections depend on model assumptions about preclinical detectable period; sensitivity estimates are lower than clinically diagnosed cancers.
- **Conclusion**: Extended multi-cancer screening could achieve meaningful late-stage reductions, but sustained multi-year programmes are required to realise the benefit.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-0942 / PMID:42748045
