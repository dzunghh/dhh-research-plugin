## 1. Follicular Lymphoma Transformation is Characterized by Cytokine-associated Remodeling of Stromal and Macrophage Compartments — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Discovery
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-04
- **Authors**: Haradhvala NJ et al.
- **Aims**: To dissect molecular and cellular mechanisms of follicular lymphoma transformation to aggressive large cell lymphoma.
- **Method**: Multimodal study (scRNA-seq, bulk RNA-seq, spatial transcriptomics, proteomics) of 95 FL and transformed FL samples, validated in independent FL-tFL pairs.
- **Key findings**: GPNMB+ macrophages and fibroblasts expand upon transformation; loss of follicular organising cells alters cytokine distribution impacting T cell infiltration and macrophage differentiation.
- **Strengths**: Comprehensive multi-modal design with single-cell spatial resolution and independent cohort validation.
- **Limitations**: 95-sample size limits statistical power; predominantly retrospective tissue collection.
- **Conclusion**: Stromal and macrophage remodeling are key hallmarks of FL transformation with diagnostic and therapeutic implications.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/2159-8290.CD-25-1104 / PMID:42550967

## 2. Artificial intelligence empowers full-stack histopathological diagnosis and prognosis of renal cell tumor: a multi-center study with external validation — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: BMC Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-04
- **Authors**: Xiong Y et al.
- **Aims**: To develop a comprehensive AI framework for kidney cancer detection, subtype classification, nuclear grading, and survival prediction from whole-slide images.
- **Method**: Deep learning using Prov-GigaPath foundation model + multi-instance learning; 11,135 WSIs from 7,033 patients across four centers and two public cohorts.
- **Key findings**: Subtype classification AUC 0.956–0.998 across multi-center validation; AI risk score outperformed WHO/ISUP grading in prognostic stratification (p<0.001).
- **Strengths**: Large multi-center dataset with external validation; covers full diagnostic and prognostic stack.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective design; prospective workflow studies needed to confirm clinical utility.
- **Conclusion**: A validated full-stack AI framework demonstrates clinical decision-support potential across all major renal tumor diagnostic tasks.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1186/s12916-026-05110-5 / PMID:42547870

## 3. The joint association of clonal hematopoiesis of indeterminate potential and biological aging with cancer risk among 332,911 individuals — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-04
- **Authors**: Wang L et al.
- **Aims**: To examine the joint effect of CHIP and biological aging (phenotypic age acceleration) on risk of 25 cancers in a large prospective cohort.
- **Method**: UK Biobank prospective cohort (N=332,911); Cox proportional hazard models with additive and multiplicative interaction testing across 25 cancer types.
- **Key findings**: CHIP carriers who were biologically older had HR 6.56 (95% CI 5.67–7.59) for hematologic neoplasms; 40% excess risk attributable to additive CHIP–aging interaction.
- **Strengths**: Very large population, comprehensive cancer outcomes, rigorous interaction analysis on additive and multiplicative scales.
- **Limitations**: UK Biobank predominantly European ancestry; CHIP status assessed at baseline only.
- **Conclusion**: CHIP and accelerated biological aging synergistically amplify cancer risk, supporting their combined use in risk stratification.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag268 / PMID:42550479

## 4. Electroacupuncture for acute gastrointestinal symptoms during chemotherapy for colorectal cancer: a randomized clinical trial — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-04
- **Authors**: Chen Z et al.
- **Aims**: To evaluate electroacupuncture versus sham acupuncture and usual care for acute GI symptom burden during colorectal cancer chemotherapy.
- **Method**: Multicenter (6 hospitals, China), triple-arm, patient- and assessor-blinded RCT (N=230); 3-day treatment; primary endpoint GSRS total score change.
- **Key findings**: Electroacupuncture reduced GSRS vs sham (MD -1.54, 95% CI -2.35 to -0.74) but did not meet MCID of 2.15 points; odds of remaining rescue-free were 2.86× higher vs sham.
- **Strengths**: Blinded, sham-controlled RCT with pre-specified MCID threshold; multicenter design.
- **Limitations**: Statistically significant but below clinical importance threshold; conducted exclusively in China.
- **Conclusion**: Electroacupuncture significantly reduces acute GI symptoms statistically but falls below the minimum clinically important difference.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag265 / PMID:42550476

## 5. Cancer incidence and the risk for multiple primary cancers in neurofibromatosis type 1 — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-04
- **Authors**: Kallionpää RA et al.
- **Aims**: To analyze cancer incidence and multiple primary cancer risk in a national cohort of individuals with neurofibromatosis type 1.
- **Method**: Registry-based cohort study; Finnish NF1 cohort (N=1,811) linked to Finnish Cancer Registry; 30,612 person-years (1987–2020); standardized incidence ratios.
- **Key findings**: Overall SIR 4.92 (95% CI 4.38–5.52); breast cancer lifetime risk in women with NF1 was 20.9%; prior cancer did not alter risk for second primary (P=0.377).
- **Strengths**: National cancer registry linkage minimising selection bias; 33-year follow-up period.
- **Limitations**: Single-country study; NF1 genotype heterogeneity not examined.
- **Conclusion**: NF1 confers approximately fivefold elevated lifetime cancer risk with breast cancer risk elevated at all ages, confirming NF1 as a moderate-penetrance predisposition gene.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag269 / PMID:42549890
