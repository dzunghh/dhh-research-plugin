## 1. A multimodal foundation model for molecular and morphologic data in oncology — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Nature Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-20
- **Authors**: Vaidya A et al.
- **Aims**: Develop THREADS, a whole-slide foundation model aligning tissue morphology with genomic/transcriptomic profiles for generalizable oncology tasks.
- **Method**: Self-supervised pretraining on 47,000 H&E sections with genomic alignment; evaluated across 107 oncology tasks.
- **Key findings**: THREADS outperformed leading whole-slide foundation models on cancer subtyping, grading, TME characterization, treatment response, and survival. Strong transfer across external cohorts with high label efficiency.
- **Strengths**: Unprecedented scale (107 tasks, 47k slides); molecularly guided pretraining is a principled strategy for generalizable representations.
- **Limitations**: Requires paired genomic/transcriptomic data for training; external cohort diversity not fully reported.
- **Conclusion**: Molecularly guided pretraining enables robust, general-purpose whole-slide representations for precision oncology.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s43018-026-01252-w / PMID:43064800

## 2. Endocrine, nutritional and metabolic diseases drive cancer risk through subtype-specific and shared proteomic mediators — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Nature Communications
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-20
- **Authors**: Wu J et al.
- **Aims**: Examine how 25 endocrine, nutritional, and metabolic diseases (ENMs) influence cancer incidence, multi-primary cancers, and mortality.
- **Method**: Multi-state model in a prospective cohort of 405,023 participants; proteomic mediation analysis; deep-learning risk prediction.
- **Key findings**: 8 ENMs define 4 cancer subtypes (iron deficiency, T2D, lipid disorders, osteoporosis dominated); RNASET2 is the major shared proteomic mediator; deep-learning AUCs 0.80–0.90 for cancer incidence.
- **Strengths**: Very large cohort, novel multi-state framework linking metabolic subtypes to cancer trajectories with proteomic mechanistic layer.
- **Limitations**: Prospective observational design limits causal inference; protein measurements may not capture dynamic changes.
- **Conclusion**: ENM subtypes and proteomic mediators provide mechanistic insights into metabolism-associated carcinogenesis and enable personalized risk prediction.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41467-026-78791-4 / PMID:43064703

## 3. Low-dose aspirin use and the risk of synchronous metastatic colorectal cancer: results from a population-based study — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: British Journal of Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-21
- **Authors**: Shahrivar M et al.
- **Aims**: Investigate whether low-dose aspirin use is associated with the presence of distant metastases at colorectal cancer (CRC) diagnosis.
- **Method**: Population-based study using CRCBaSe Sweden (n=81,269 CRC patients); adjusted logistic regression for odds of synchronous metastases.
- **Key findings**: Current aspirin users had lower odds of synchronous metastases (OR_adj 0.86, 95% CI 0.82–0.90); effect was strongest in right-sided colon cancer (OR_adj 0.79, 95% CI 0.74–0.86).
- **Strengths**: Very large national registry cohort; site-specific subgroup analyses; duration-dependent exposure modelling.
- **Limitations**: Observational design with potential confounding by indication; aspirin adherence is self-reported.
- **Conclusion**: Low-dose aspirin use is associated with reduced odds of synchronous distant metastases at CRC diagnosis, particularly in right-sided colon cancer.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41416-026-03663-z / PMID:43067625

## 4. Spatial profiling of primary CRC and synchronous lung and liver metastases identifies a liver-specific stromal niche associated with immune exclusion — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Clinical Cancer Research
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-23
- **Authors**: Basu S et al.
- **Aims**: Determine how organ-specific microenvironments shape immune exclusion in microsatellite-stable (MSS) CRC metastases.
- **Method**: Spatial transcriptomics and multiplex immunofluorescence on synchronous liver/lung metastases and matched primary CRC; survival analysis in TCGA-COAD/READ.
- **Key findings**: Liver metastases showed dense fibrotic stroma enriched with activated hepatic stellate cells and MRC1+/SPP1+ macrophages; high aHSC signature correlated with worse progression-free survival in TCGA.
- **Strengths**: Synchronous comparison of liver vs. lung within same patient; spatial resolution of stromal-immune interactions; TCGA validation.
- **Limitations**: Small spatial cohort; MSS-only focus limits generalizability to MSI-high tumors.
- **Conclusion**: A liver-specific stromal-myeloid niche driven by aHSCs and CXCL12-CXCR4 signaling underpins immune exclusion and poor checkpoint therapy response.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-26-0498 / PMID:43071532

## 5. Impact of opioids on chemotherapy-induced gastrointestinal toxicity: Complementary retrospective and real-world cohorts — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiology, Biomarkers & Prevention
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-23
- **Authors**: Jennings O et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate whether prior opioid use exacerbates irinotecan chemotherapy-induced gastrointestinal toxicity.
- **Method**: Retrospective cohort (n=331 GI malignancy patients) plus large TriNetX real-world database (CRC/pancreatic cancer); adjusted logistic regression for dose changes and GI symptoms.
- **Key findings**: Opioid use associated with dose reduction (OR 2.14, 95% CI 1.21–3.77) and dose delay (OR 2.74, 95% CI 1.32–5.69); real-world cohort confirmed higher GI symptom risk.
- **Strengths**: Two complementary cohorts corroborate findings; large real-world database adds external validity.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective design with residual confounding; TriNetX lacks granular dosing data.
- **Conclusion**: Prior opioid use meaningfully increases irinotecan intolerance risk, warranting routine pharmacoepidemiology surveillance in cancer treatment planning.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-0816 / PMID:43070907

## Appraisal notes

This week's picks reflect two emerging methodological themes: (1) the integration of large-scale omic data with deep learning for cancer risk prediction and clinical decision support (THREADS foundation model, ENM-proteomics cohort), where molecularly guided pretraining and proteomic mediation analysis are becoming standard tools; (2) pharmacoepidemiology studies leveraging national registries and real-world databases to quantify drug effects on cancer outcomes (aspirin-CRC metastases, opioid-chemo toxicity), with increasingly granular exposure modeling. The spatial transcriptomics study underscores a methodological push toward microenvironment characterization that complements population-level epi inference.
