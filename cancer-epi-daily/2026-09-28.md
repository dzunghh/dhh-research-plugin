## 1. Alcohol consumption and colorectal cancer risk in prediabetes and diabetes — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-24
- **Authors**: Kim TS et al.
- **Aims**: Examine dose-response relationship between alcohol consumption and CRC risk stratified by glycemic status.
- **Method**: Nationwide Korean cohort (4,475,492 adults, 2012–2022); Cox regression with synergy index for alcohol–glycemia interaction.
- **Key findings**: Even light-to-moderate drinking increased CRC risk in IFG (HR 1.23) and diabetes (HR 1.48); steepest dose-response gradient observed in diabetics.
- **Strengths**: Largest study of alcohol-glycemia-CRC interaction; 51,592 incident CRC cases; 10-year follow-up.
- **Limitations**: Self-reported alcohol intake; Korean population may limit global generalizability.
- **Conclusion**: Strict alcohol restriction recommended to reduce CRC risk, especially in individuals with impaired glycemia or diabetes.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag340 / PMID:42786582

## 2. Evorpacept plus trastuzumab, ramucirumab and paclitaxel in HER2-positive gastric cancer — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Nature Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-24
- **Authors**: Shitara K et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate CD47-blocking agent evorpacept added to standard HER2-positive advanced gastric/GEJ cancer second-line therapy.
- **Method**: Open-label, randomized phase 2 trial (n=127); primary endpoint investigator-assessed ORR vs historical 30% benchmark.
- **Key findings**: ORR 40.3% vs 26.6% (evo+TRP vs TRP) in ITT population; 54.8% vs 23.1% in fresh-biopsy HER2+ subgroup.
- **Strengths**: Novel CD47-SIRPα innate immune strategy; biomarker-enriched subgroup shows clinically meaningful response increase.
- **Limitations**: Open-label design; did not meet pre-specified one-sided α=0.025 primary criterion in ITT population.
- **Conclusion**: Evorpacept demonstrated encouraging efficacy and manageable safety in HER2+ advanced gastric cancer; phase 3 evaluation warranted.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41591-026-04700-3 / PMID:42786305

## 3. Physical activity and molecular residual disease in stage III colon cancer — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-24
- **Authors**: Zhang GQ et al.
- **Aims**: Determine whether postoperative physical activity outcomes differ by ctDNA residual disease status in stage III colon cancer.
- **Method**: Post-hoc analysis of CALGB/SWOG 80702 phase III RCT (n=763); ctDNA by Signatera assay; physical activity in validated MET-h/week.
- **Key findings**: Moderate and high physical activity associated with improved DFS in ctDNA-negative patients (HR 0.58 and 0.49 respectively); PA+celecoxib improved DFS regardless of ctDNA status.
- **Strengths**: First study integrating ctDNA status with physical activity in colon cancer; clinically validated ctDNA assay.
- **Limitations**: Post-hoc subgroup analysis; small ctDNA-positive subcohort; interaction test not statistically significant.
- **Conclusion**: Physical activity is prognostic for disease-free survival primarily in ctDNA-negative stage III colon cancer patients.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag342 / PMID:42786576

## 4. Early palliative care for African American and rural-dwelling advanced cancer family caregivers: long-term outcomes — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-24
- **Authors**: Odom JN et al.
- **Aims**: Examine 36- and 48-week outcomes of the ENABLE Cornerstone lay-coach-led palliative care intervention for underserved cancer caregivers.
- **Method**: Multi-site, single-blind RCT (n=222 caregivers, 165 patients); baseline-constrained linear mixed-effects models with random effects.
- **Key findings**: Intervention reduced caregiver demand burden at 36 weeks (Cohen's d=−0.41); patients showed lower depression at 36 weeks and better physical QoL at 48 weeks.
- **Strengths**: Addresses cancer care equity gap; rigorous long-term follow-up; pre-registered trial with validated patient-reported outcomes.
- **Limitations**: Single-blind design; modest effect sizes; findings specific to African American and rural populations.
- **Conclusion**: Caregiver-focused early palliative care yields significant long-term benefits for both caregivers and advanced cancer patients from underserved populations.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag339 / PMID:42786668
