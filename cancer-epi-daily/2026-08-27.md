## 1. Five-Year Outcomes of Perioperative Pembrolizumab for Early-Stage Non-Small-Cell Lung Cancer (KEYNOTE-671) — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Ann Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-21
- **Authors**: Wakelee H et al.
- **Aims**: Report 5-year efficacy and safety of perioperative pembrolizumab plus neoadjuvant chemotherapy vs chemotherapy alone in resectable early-stage NSCLC.
- **Method**: Phase III randomised controlled trial (n=797); dual primary endpoints of event-free survival (EFS) and overall survival (OS) per RECIST v1.1.
- **Key findings**: 5-year EFS 49.9% vs 26.5% (HR 0.58, 95% CI 0.48–0.69); 5-year OS 64.6% vs 53.6% (HR 0.74, 95% CI 0.59–0.92).
- **Strengths**: Long-term follow-up (median 60.4 months) of a large, well-powered global Phase III trial confirming durable benefit.
- **Limitations**: Open-label design; crossover not reported; survival curves may mature further in subsequent years.
- **Conclusion**: Perioperative pembrolizumab doubles 5-year EFS and improves OS, supporting its use as standard of care for resectable early-stage NSCLC.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.annonc.2026.08.005 / PMID:42628840

## 2. Pharmacogenetic Variants in the Molecular Characterization Initiative: A Report from the Children's Oncology Group — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: J Natl Cancer Inst
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-24
- **Authors**: Haidar CE et al.
- **Aims**: Characterise the frequency and distribution of actionable pharmacogenomic variants in a large paediatric cancer cohort.
- **Method**: Cross-sectional analysis of germline exome sequencing in 3,177 patients via PharmCAT for 13 CPIC-guided pharmacogenes; iAdmix for ancestry estimation.
- **Key findings**: 93% of patients had at least one actionable pharmacogenomic phenotype; no significant ancestry-based differences in actionable variant frequency (p=0.07).
- **Strengths**: Large, diverse cohort (51% European, 21% Admixed American, 9% African) with standardised, validated PGx methods.
- **Limitations**: Single-institution exome sequencing pipeline; limited to 13 pharmacogenes; clinical implementation outcomes not assessed.
- **Conclusion**: Actionable pharmacogenomic variants are near-universal in paediatric oncology, underscoring the importance of routine germline PGx testing.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag286 / PMID:42636256

## 3. Evaluating Longitudinal Toxicity and Adverse Events in Cetuximab-Treated Metastatic Colorectal Cancer: A Pooled Analysis of 1,302 Patients (ARCAD Database) — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: J Natl Cancer Inst
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-24
- **Authors**: Yin J et al.
- **Aims**: Apply a longitudinal toxicity framework using adverse event load (AEL) to compare toxicity profiles of chemotherapy ± cetuximab in mCRC.
- **Method**: Pooled analysis of two randomised first-line trials (n=1,302); outcomes included grade ≥3 AE rates, time-to-maximum grade, early onset, and AEL (normalised cumulative toxicity burden).
- **Key findings**: Cetuximab arm had markedly higher rash AEL (0.257 vs 0.069, adjusted mean difference 0.22) and grade ≥3 rash (21% vs 0.5%; aOR 49.89); AEL distinguished persistent burden from isolated peak events.
- **Strengths**: Novel epi-methodology capturing within-grade chronicity; large pooled RCT dataset with adjusted analyses.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective pooling of two trials; AEL metric not yet validated externally; limited AE types examined.
- **Conclusion**: The AEL framework detects persistent toxicity burden missed by traditional worst-grade reporting, advancing comparative tolerability assessment in oncology trials.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag287 / PMID:42636265

## 4. Atezolizumab with or without Tiragolumab in Unresectable Esophageal Squamous Cell Carcinoma Following Definitive Chemoradiotherapy (SKYSCRAPER-07) — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Ann Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-21
- **Authors**: Xu RH et al.
- **Aims**: Investigate efficacy and safety of atezolizumab ± tiragolumab versus placebo post-definitive chemoradiotherapy in unresectable ESCC.
- **Method**: Global, randomised, placebo-controlled Phase III trial (n=760, 166 centres, 28 countries); hierarchical primary endpoints of INV-PFS and OS.
- **Key findings**: Combo arm did not meet primary PFS endpoint (HR 0.82, p=0.095); atezolizumab monotherapy significantly improved OS vs placebo (HR 0.69, 95% CI 0.52–0.91).
- **Strengths**: Large, well-powered multi-national RCT with robust hierarchical testing strategy and >25-month median follow-up.
- **Limitations**: Primary endpoint not met for the combination; anti-TIGIT addition provided no additional benefit over PD-L1 monotherapy.
- **Conclusion**: Atezolizumab monotherapy improves OS post-dCRT in unresectable ESCC; adding tiragolumab confers no additional benefit.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.annonc.2026.07.413 / PMID:42628839

## 5. Improving Dietary Data Quality in Nutrition Studies: ML-Assisted Framework for Outlier Detection in 24-Hour Recalls — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Am J Epidemiol
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-24
- **Authors**: Massara P et al.
- **Aims**: Develop and validate an open, explainable machine learning pipeline to detect implausible values in 24-hour dietary recalls for nutritional epidemiology.
- **Method**: ML-assisted framework integrating NCI percentile-based thresholds and IQR-based longitudinal flagging; validated in 126 children (MiGrowD) and 15,216 Canadian adults (CCHS-Nutrition).
- **Key findings**: Framework achieved >77% sensitivity, 97% specificity, and 88% precision in longitudinal data, outperforming static methods across 7 nutrients.
- **Strengths**: Open pipeline, AI-ready outputs, dual-cohort validation including a nationally representative sample; strong reproducibility.
- **Limitations**: Validated in Canadian populations only; thresholds rely on NCI reference ranges; child-specific performance may not generalise to other settings.
- **Conclusion**: This explainable ML framework substantially improves dietary data quality and reproducibility, enabling scalable nutritional cancer-epi analyses.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/aje/kwag204 / PMID:42635107
