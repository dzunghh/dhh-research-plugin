## 1. Thymic Radiation Is Associated with Worse Outcomes in Patients with NSCLC — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Ann Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-12 (online first)
- **Authors**: Prudente V et al.
- **Aims**: Determine whether incidental thymic irradiation during thoracic radiotherapy adversely affects oncologic outcomes in NSCLC.
- **Method**: Multicohort observational analysis (n=1,107); phase III RTOG-0617 trial plus two independent real-world cohorts; deep-learning thymic radiographic quantification; multivariable Cox regression.
- **Key findings**: Each 1 Gy increase in mean thymic dose associated with 1.57–4.21% increased distant metastasis risk (aHR 1.29–1.95 across all three cohorts; all p<0.03). Effect strongest in patients with preserved baseline thymic health.
- **Strengths**: Three independent cohorts including a phase III RCT validate the finding; novel deep-learning organ-at-risk quantification.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective multicohort design; thymic function assessed radiographically, not by direct immunological assay.
- **Conclusion**: Thymus should be treated as an organ at risk in RT planning for NSCLC to preserve immune function and reduce metastatic spread.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.annonc.2026.07.001 / PMID:42641684

## 2. CATALINA: AI-Based TIL Quantification in TNBC — Independent Validation — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-09
- **Authors**: Dixon-Douglas JR et al.
- **Aims**: Validate AI-derived TIL scores against long-term clinical outcomes in early triple-negative breast cancer.
- **Method**: Independent external validation study; 1,759 patients pooled from 7 prospective RCTs; two locked AI pipelines evaluated; multivariable Cox regression with clinicopathological adjustment.
- **Key findings**: Both pathologist sTIL (HR 0.73 for iDFS, q<0.0001) and AI cTIL (HR 0.80, q<0.0001) were independently prognostic. Modest AI-pathologist correlation (r=0.375–0.473); AI did not add prognostic value beyond pathologist sTIL.
- **Strengths**: Large multi-trial pooled dataset; independent deployment of locked AI models ensures unbiased validation.
- **Limitations**: Modest concordance between AI and pathologist scores; AI pipelines did not outperform manual scoring.
- **Conclusion**: AI TIL tools are prognostic in TNBC but do not yet surpass experienced pathologist scoring in large external validation.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00339-6 / PMID:42636839

## 3. APHINITY: Manual, Digital, and AI TIL Scoring in HER2+ Early Breast Cancer — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-09
- **Authors**: Lara González LE et al.
- **Aims**: Compare TIL quantification methods and assess predictive value for pertuzumab benefit in HER2-positive early breast cancer.
- **Method**: Secondary analysis of phase 3 APHINITY RCT (n=4,805); 4,262 H&E images; 5 TIL approaches; median follow-up 74 months; Cox models with treatment-by-biomarker interaction.
- **Key findings**: Higher TILs associated with improved iDFS across all measurement methods (HR 0.41–0.93). AI reclassified 11.6% of node-positive tumours from immune-low to immune-high with clinically meaningful outcome differences; pertuzumab benefit largest in TIL-high patients (6-year absolute improvement 12.1 pp).
- **Strengths**: Large phase 3 RCT with 74-month follow-up; comprehensive head-to-head TIL method comparison.
- **Limitations**: Single-trial context; AI reclassification subgroup analysis is post-hoc.
- **Conclusion**: AI-based immune hotspot scoring provides additive prognostic and predictive information beyond manual TIL assessment in HER2+ early breast cancer.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00247-0 / PMID:42636838

## 4. HARMONi: Ivonescimab Plus Chemotherapy in EGFR-Mutated NSCLC After TKI Failure — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-09
- **Authors**: Le X et al.
- **Aims**: Assess ivonescimab (bispecific anti-PD-1/VEGF) plus chemotherapy versus placebo plus chemotherapy in advanced EGFR-mutated NSCLC after third-generation TKI failure.
- **Method**: Phase 3 double-blind RCT (n=438); 114 global centres; stratified by brain metastasis status and geographic region; dual primary endpoints (PFS + OS by blinded IRRC).
- **Key findings**: Median PFS 6.8 vs 4.4 months (HR 0.52; p<0.0001). Median OS 16.8 vs 14.0 months (HR 0.79; 95% CI 0.62–1.01; interim not significant).
- **Strengths**: Global phase 3 RCT; diverse patient population across Asia, Europe, North America.
- **Limitations**: OS not significant at interim; 70% Asian patients limits generalisability; follow-up ongoing.
- **Conclusion**: Ivonescimab plus chemotherapy significantly improves PFS in EGFR-mutated NSCLC post-TKI, a clinically underserved population.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00282-2 / PMID:42636833

## 5. GETUG AFU 18: Dose-Escalated Radiotherapy in High-Risk Prostate Cancer With Long-Term ADT — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-09
- **Authors**: Hennequin C et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate effect of 80 Gy versus 70 Gy radiotherapy on progression-free survival in high-risk prostate cancer treated with long-term ADT.
- **Method**: Multicentre open-label phase 3 RCT (n=505); 25 French centres; 9.5-year median follow-up; minimisation randomisation stratified by centre and lymph node dissection.
- **Key findings**: 10-year PFS 83.6% vs 72.2% (stratified HR 0.56; p<0.0001). Acute grade ≥3 toxicity similar (24% vs 25%).
- **Strengths**: Long-term follow-up (9.5 years); multicenter phase 3; significant 10-year PFS benefit with manageable toxicity.
- **Limitations**: Single-country (France); open-label design; ethnicity data not collected.
- **Conclusion**: Dose escalation to 80 Gy significantly improves long-term PFS without increased acute toxicity in high-risk prostate cancer with long-term ADT.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00277-9 / PMID:42636837

## Appraisal notes

This week's digest is dominated by oncology biomarker methodology: two Lancet Oncology studies (CATALINA and APHINITY) rigorously evaluate AI versus pathologist TIL quantification in breast cancer, finding consistent prognostication but AI not yet surpassing manual scoring — a key finding for the field's push toward digital pathology. The thymic radiation study stands out as a novel cancer-epi contribution, re-framing the thymus as an immunologically critical organ at risk in thoracic RT, with consistent multicohort evidence. Taken together, the methodological theme is validation rigour — independent external validation and secondary RCT analyses with long follow-up dominate over first-in-human or single-arm designs.
