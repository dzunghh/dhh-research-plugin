## 1. Effect of Screening with Multicancer Early-Detection Test on Late-Stage Cancer Diagnosis — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: N Engl J Med
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-22
- **Authors**: Sasieni P et al.
- **Aims**: To determine whether adding a blood-based multicancer early-detection (MCED) test to usual care reduces late-stage cancer incidence across 12 prespecified cancer types.
- **Method**: Randomized controlled trial (1:1) in England; n=142,250 participants aged 50–77 over three annual screening rounds.
- **Key findings**: No significant difference in stage III/IV cancer incidence (IRR 1.03; 95% CI 0.92–1.14; P=0.63). Key secondary end point IRR for stage IV was 0.86 (95% CI 0.74–1.00); <1% adverse events.
- **Strengths**: Largest MCED screening RCT to date; robust randomization; blinded outcome ascertainment; 12-month minimum follow-up per participant.
- **Limitations**: Three screening rounds may be insufficient follow-up to detect stage-shift benefit; lead-time bias not yet fully addressable.
- **Conclusion**: MCED screening did not significantly reduce late-stage cancer incidence after three rounds; longer follow-up is needed to evaluate evolving stage IV trends.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1056/NEJMoa2505723 / PMID:42776074

## 2. Large-scale esophageal cancer screening through noncontrast computed tomography and artificial intelligence — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Nat Med
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-22
- **Authors**: Zhou J et al.
- **Aims**: To develop and validate an AI model (EAGLE) that detects esophageal cancer and precancerous lesions from routine noncontrast CT scans.
- **Method**: Deep-learning model trained on 6,813 patients at two centers; validated across 12 centers in three countries involving 80,612 patients in opportunistic and population-based screening settings.
- **Key findings**: External multicenter test (n=11,466): 98.5% specificity, 90.0% sensitivity for cancer, 52.5% for precancerous lesions. Real-world calibration reduced false positives by 72.7% while preserving sensitivity.
- **Strengths**: Exceptional scale of external validation across three countries; real-world prospective cohort included; LDCT arm supports integration with lung cancer screening programs.
- **Limitations**: Sensitivity for precancerous lesions (52.5%) remains suboptimal; generalisation beyond East Asian populations not yet established.
- **Conclusion**: EAGLE is a scalable, high-specificity AI tool for opportunistic esophageal cancer screening from existing CT infrastructure.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41591-026-04656-4 / PMID:42773211

## 3. Clinical Uses of Common Genetic Variants Associated with Common Diseases — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: N Engl J Med
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-24
- **Authors**: Hunter DJ et al.
- **Aims**: To review why polygenic risk scores (PRS) from genome-wide association studies have had limited clinical impact, and to assess their future utility for cancer prevention and treatment.
- **Method**: Narrative review synthesising 25 years of GWAS data, PRS development, and pharmacogenomics evidence across common adult diseases including cancer.
- **Key findings**: Thousands of common variants underpin polygenic cancer risk; PRS predict risk but clinical translation remains slow due to calibration, ancestry diversity, and implementation barriers. Pharmacogenomics offers more immediate clinical utility.
- **Strengths**: Authored by leading epidemiologists (Hunter, Inouye); broad synthesis in a high-impact venue; directly addresses cancer risk stratification.
- **Limitations**: Review methodology; GWAS findings predominantly from European-ancestry populations limits generalisability.
- **Conclusion**: Inexpensive whole-genome sequencing is likely to accelerate clinical uptake of PRS and pharmacogenomics, but equitable implementation remains a critical challenge.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1056/NEJMra2413201 / PMID:42777243

## 4. Five-Year Follow-Up of the Phase III ASCOT Trial of Adjuvant S-1 After Resection for Biliary Tract Cancer — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: J Clin Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-23
- **Authors**: Ikeda M et al.
- **Aims**: To report the prespecified 5-year follow-up of the ASCOT phase III trial evaluating adjuvant S-1 versus observation after curative-intent resection for biliary tract cancer.
- **Method**: Multicenter open-label randomized trial, Japan; n=440 patients with resected biliary tract cancer; observation vs. S-1 (oral fluoropyrimidine, 4 cycles).
- **Key findings**: 5-year OS 64% (S-1) vs 52% (observation), HR 0.72 (95% CI 0.55–0.95); median OS 8.2 vs 5.9 years. Relapse-free survival HR 0.76 (stratified).
- **Strengths**: Prespecified long-term analysis; sustained survival benefit across subgroups; data from a high-quality national cooperative group.
- **Limitations**: Japan-only population limits generalisability; open-label design; no standard-of-care comparator arm with gemcitabine-based regimens.
- **Conclusion**: Adjuvant S-1 confers durable 5-year OS benefit after resection for biliary tract cancer, confirming its role as standard adjuvant treatment.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1200/JCO-26-00017 / PMID:42777180

## 5. The liver niche accelerates, while the lung niche restrains secondary spread of breast cancer — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Discov
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-22
- **Authors**: Sznurkowska MK et al.
- **Aims**: To characterise organ-specific microenvironmental differences that drive differential metastasis-to-metastasis dissemination in breast cancer.
- **Method**: Multi-modal study: patient data and rapid autopsy tissues, mouse models with barcode-mediated lineage tracing, niche-labelling technology, and single-cell transcriptomic analysis.
- **Key findings**: Liver niche promotes secondary seeding via BMP2-producing endothelial cells; lung niche restricts dissemination. Targeting the BMP2 axis suppressed secondary spread from liver metastases.
- **Strengths**: Convergent evidence from human tissues and mechanistic mouse models; novel barcode-tracing methodology; therapeutic target identified and validated.
- **Limitations**: Pre-clinical BMP2 targeting data not yet in clinical trials; patient cohort size for single-cell analysis not reported.
- **Conclusion**: Organ-specific niches differentially regulate metastatic behaviour; BMP2 signalling in the liver niche represents a tractable therapeutic target to limit secondary cancer spread.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/2159-8290.CD-25-1652 / PMID:42770697

## Appraisal notes

This week's digest is dominated by two landmark cancer screening papers: the NHS-Galleri RCT (NEJM) delivering the first large-scale RCT evidence on MCED blood tests — finding no significant stage-shift benefit after three rounds — and the EAGLE AI model (Nature Medicine) demonstrating that routine noncontrast CT can serve as a scalable esophageal cancer screening platform. Together they highlight the growing methodological sophistication in early-detection research, including adaptive use of existing healthcare infrastructure for opportunistic screening. The Hunter & Inouye polygenic risk score review (NEJM) signals maturing consensus on when genomic risk stratification will realistically enter clinical oncology practice, with equity in implementation emerging as the central unresolved challenge.
