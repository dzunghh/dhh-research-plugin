## 1. Nitrate and Nitrite Intake and Risk of Colorectal Cancer: A Population-Based Cohort Study — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: J Natl Cancer Inst
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-05
- **Authors**: Rodrigues A et al.
- **Aims**: To assess whether dietary nitrate/nitrite intake is associated with colorectal cancer risk in a large Swedish population cohort.
- **Method**: Population-based cohort study (n=82,009); Cox proportional hazards models; dietary data linked to food/water database updated at three time points (1997, 2009, 2019).
- **Key findings**: Nitrite intake associated with dose-dependent higher CRC risk in men (HR 1.23, 95% CI 1.06–1.43); distal colon cancer showed strongest effect (HR 1.50, 95% CI 1.13–1.98). No association for nitrate or in women.
- **Strengths**: Very large cohort with 24-year follow-up, registry-ascertained outcomes, repeated dietary measurements capturing exposure change over time.
- **Limitations**: Sex-specific finding requires replication; N-nitroso compound pathways not directly measured; residual confounding possible.
- **Conclusion**: Dietary nitrite intake is associated with higher CRC risk in men, particularly for distal colon cancer, suggesting sex-specific susceptibility.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag271 / PMID:42554802

## 2. Examining the Role of Extrachromosomal DNA in Lung Cancer — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: J Natl Cancer Inst
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-05
- **Authors**: Khandekar A et al.
- **Aims**: To characterise the prevalence, oncogene targets, and prognostic significance of extrachromosomal DNA (ecDNA) across lung cancer subtypes including never-smokers.
- **Method**: Whole-genome sequencing of >1,200 lung cancer samples; ecDNA detection algorithms; overall survival analysis comparing ecDNA vs. other focal amplifications.
- **Key findings**: ecDNA detected in 18.9% of patients; tumours with ecDNA had worse overall survival; MDM2 identified as a recurrently amplified oncogene via ecDNA, particularly in never-smokers.
- **Strengths**: Large multi-institutional NCI WGS cohort; rigorous sequencing; novel survival comparison of ecDNA vs. non-ecDNA focal amplifications.
- **Limitations**: Short communication format limits methodological detail; survival confounders not fully specified in abstract.
- **Conclusion**: ecDNA is prevalent in lung cancer and confers worse prognosis, implicating genomic instability as a key driver especially in never-smoker subsets.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag273 / PMID:42554978

## 3. Multi-Omics Integration Unravels Four Molecular Subgroups of Corticotroph Pituitary Neuroendocrine Tumours — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Nat Commun
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-04
- **Authors**: Dottermusch M et al.
- **Aims**: To establish a molecular classification of corticotroph pituitary neuroendocrine tumours (PitNETs) with distinct clinicopathological features.
- **Method**: Multi-omics integration (epigenome, transcriptome, proteome) of 270 corticotroph PitNETs (111 internal + 159 external validation); clustering and integrated prognostic modelling.
- **Key findings**: Four robust molecular subgroups identified, broadly distinguishable by SSTR1/GATA3/SSTR5 immunohistochemistry; integrated stratification model shows significant prognostic utility across both cohorts.
- **Strengths**: Large multi-institutional cohort, three-platform multi-omics, external validation, freely available epigenomic classifier.
- **Limitations**: Prospective clinical validation still required; rare tumour type may limit immediate generalisability.
- **Conclusion**: Multi-omics reveals four clinically meaningful corticotroph PitNET subgroups with prognostic value, supported by a practical IHC-based proxy and open-access classifier.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41467-026-76292-y / PMID:42552318

## 4. Comparison of NICE Criteria with the BOADICEA Multifactorial Risk Model to Guide Breast Cancer Risk Assessment in Women Under Age 50 — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Br J Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-04
- **Authors**: Frost R et al.
- **Aims**: To compare performance of NICE family-history criteria versus BOADICEA multifactorial risk model for identifying women under 50 who develop breast cancer within 10 years.
- **Method**: Comparative analysis of 1,258 women from the Generations Study (392 BC cases), weighted to UK population age/family-history distribution; sensitivity, specificity, and referral-rate estimation.
- **Key findings**: NICE criteria identified only 4.4% of BC cases within referral criteria (1.4% of women); BOADICEA identified up to 35% of cases but referred 26.5% of women.
- **Strengths**: Population-representative weighting, validated against incident registry-confirmed cancer, directly policy-relevant comparison.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective design; BOADICEA requires full polygenic/lifestyle data not routinely collected in primary care; modest sample size limits subgroup analyses.
- **Conclusion**: NICE family-history criteria miss the vast majority of BCs in younger women; multifactorial models could substantially improve early identification.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41416-026-03547-2 / PMID:42547567

## 5. Identification of MicroRNAs as Biomarkers for Triage of Cervical Intraepithelial Neoplasia in HR-HPV-Positive Women — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Br J Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-04
- **Authors**: Mejia Guarnizo LV et al.
- **Aims**: To identify miRNA biomarkers in routine cervical scrapes that distinguish high-grade CIN from low-grade lesions in HR-HPV-positive women.
- **Method**: Small RNA-seq in HR-HPV-positive cervical scrapes stratified by CIN grade; bioinformatics prioritisation; RT-qPCR validation of top candidates in independent samples.
- **Key findings**: Three-miRNA signature (hsa-miR-501-3p, hsa-miR-1271-5p, hsa-miR-9-5p) achieved AUC 0.88–0.94 for high-grade lesion detection; individual hsa-miR-501-3p AUC 0.88.
- **Strengths**: Dual discovery-validation design; clinically accessible sample type; LMIC-setting Colombian cohort adds geographic diversity.
- **Limitations**: Single-centre small validation set; generalisability to non-Colombian populations uncertain; analytical sensitivity of RT-qPCR in routine screening unclear.
- **Conclusion**: A three-miRNA signature in cervical scrapes from HR-HPV-positive women shows high diagnostic accuracy for high-grade CIN triage.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41416-026-03521-y / PMID:42552363

## Appraisal notes

This week's digest spans two strong methodological threads: large registry-linked dietary cohort epidemiology (nitrite-CRC in 82,000 Swedes with 24-year follow-up) and NCI-scale cancer genomic epidemiology (ecDNA in >1,200 WGS lung cancers with survival data). Two companion papers from Cambridge present a compelling policy-relevant challenge to the UK's family-history-only breast cancer referral framework, making a data-driven case for BOADICEA-style multifactorial models in primary care for women under 50. The multi-omics PitNET classification and Colombian miRNA cervical triage study round out the week with advances in molecular stratification and biomarker-based screening from underrepresented settings.
