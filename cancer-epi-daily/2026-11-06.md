## 1. Impact of psychosocial interventions on cancer survival: an updated meta-analysis — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-30
- **Authors**: McCarvell V et al.
- **Aims**: Determine whether psychosocial interventions improve overall survival (OS) in cancer patients beyond quality of life.
- **Method**: Systematic review and meta-analysis of 24 RCTs from MEDLINE, EMBASE, Cochrane; random effects model; Kaplan-Meier curve extraction.
- **Key findings**: Psychosocial interventions associated with 13% lower mortality (SHR=0.87, 95% CI 0.77–0.99); curative-intent subgroup showed SHR=0.75 (95% CI 0.64–0.87).
- **Strengths**: Search updated through December 2025; 4,795 patients across 24 RCTs; prespecified subgroup analyses by intent and duration.
- **Limitations**: Moderate heterogeneity (I²=45%); small patient numbers in some cancer-site subgroups.
- **Conclusion**: Psychosocial interventions may improve OS in cancer patients, especially in curative-intent and longer-duration programmes.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag414 / PMID:42968372

## 2. Micro[nano]plastic particles and cancer: challenges and opportunities for epidemiology studies — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-02
- **Authors**: Mahabir S et al.
- **Aims**: Review methodological challenges and opportunities for epidemiological research linking MNP exposure to cancer risk.
- **Method**: Narrative review synthesising in-vitro, in-vivo, occupational, and emerging biomarker-detection studies on MNPs and carcinogenesis.
- **Key findings**: MNPs detected in human cancer tissues across lung, colon, breast, and prostate; in-vitro and animal data implicate multiple carcinogenic pathways.
- **Strengths**: NCI-led synthesis; highlights novel biomarker advances; identifies specific measurement standardisation gaps needed to enable population studies.
- **Limitations**: No original epidemiological data; no validated exposure-assessment methods for human studies yet available.
- **Conclusion**: MNPs are an emerging environmental carcinogen; advances in detection technology make large-scale cancer-epi studies feasible and urgently needed.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag409 / PMID:42969394

## 3. Opium Consumption and Lung Cancer: From Self-reported Use to Exposure Biomarkers — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiology, Biomarkers & Prevention
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-02
- **Authors**: Leng S et al.
- **Aims**: Assess whether urinary metabolites can characterise complex opium-related carcinogen exposures linked to lung cancer risk.
- **Method**: Editorial commentary on a nested case-control study of 53 matched lung cancer pairs within the prospective Golestan Cohort Study; 35 urinary metabolites measured.
- **Key findings**: Higher morphine-3-glucuronide, morphine-6-glucuronide, and two VOC mercapturic acid metabolites were associated with lung cancer in opium users.
- **Strengths**: Novel molecular-epi approach integrating multiple exposure biomarker classes from a large prospective cohort in a high-exposure population.
- **Limitations**: Small nested case-control (53 matched pairs); confined to opium users; generalisability uncertain.
- **Conclusion**: Urinary biomarkers can capture complex carcinogen profiles from opium use and may advance mechanism-informed cancer prevention strategies.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-1173 / PMID:42968657
