## 1. Evaluating associations of advanced stage at diagnosis of squamous cell anal cancer by sex among adults with and without HIV in the US — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiol Biomarkers Prev
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-03
- **Authors**: Islam JY et al.
- **Aims**: Identify factors associated with advanced-stage SCCA at diagnosis, stratified by sex and HIV status.
- **Method**: Retrospective analysis of US National Cancer Database (2004–2020, n=36,389); hierarchical multivariable Poisson regression estimating adjusted prevalence ratios.
- **Key findings**: 42% diagnosed at stage 3–4; women without HIV had higher advanced-stage odds than men (aPR 1.20, 95% CI 1.05–1.37). Being uninsured strongly associated with advanced stage (aPR 1.31, 95% CI 1.23–1.39).
- **Strengths**: Large national registry, stratified sex analysis, multilevel covariates including insurance and geography.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective; HIV ascertainment may be incomplete in registry data.
- **Conclusion**: Sex, race, insurance, and socioeconomic factors—not HIV status—drive advanced-stage SCCA disparities.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-0622 / PMID:42975869

## 2. A randomized clinical trial comparing visual inspection with acetic acid (VIA) to Pocket Colposcopy for the triage of HPV+ women living with HIV in Kisumu, Kenya — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Br J Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-03
- **Authors**: Santos MO et al.
- **Aims**: Compare Pocket Colposcope-assisted VIA to naked-eye VIA for triage of HPV-positive women living with HIV in a resource-limited setting.
- **Method**: Multi-clinic RCT (n=6 clinics, Kisumu, Kenya); biopsy-confirmed CIN2/3 as outcome; diagnostic accuracy metrics computed.
- **Key findings**: CIN2/3 prevalence 15.4%; Pocket colposcope showed higher PPV (37.5% vs 20.6%) and detected more treatable disease. Sensitivity/specificity/NPV were comparable.
- **Strengths**: RCT design, pathology-confirmed outcomes, real-world resource-limited setting.
- **Limitations**: Non-significant PPV difference; small sample per clinic; short recruitment window (8 months).
- **Conclusion**: Pocket colposcope is a feasible, lower-cost device that reduces overtreatment and supports remote diagnosis in LMIC cervical cancer programs.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41416-026-03609-5 / PMID:42977752

## 3. Modeling neoadjuvant treatment plus additional therapy effects on US population breast cancer mortality — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: J Natl Cancer Inst
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-04
- **Authors**: Huang X et al.
- **Aims**: Quantify projected US breast cancer mortality reduction from NAPAT (neoadjuvant + response-guided adjuvant therapy) vs no NAPAT through 2030.
- **Method**: Six CISNET simulation models applied to US female birth-cohorts; inputs from clinical trials, national registries, and observational studies; molecular subtype-specific projections.
- **Key findings**: NAPAT projected to reduce breast cancer mortality to 23.0/100,000 by 2030, a 10.5% reduction overall; largest effect in triple-negative (30.7%) and ER-/HER2+ (27.9%) subtypes.
- **Strengths**: Consensus multi-model approach (6 CISNET models), subtype-specific projections, national population scope.
- **Limitations**: Assumes 2018 treatment/screening trends continue; models may not capture future therapeutic innovations.
- **Conclusion**: NAPAT could meaningfully reduce US breast cancer mortality even without further therapeutic advances.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag413 / PMID:42979992

## 4. Electronic health record embedded frailty index and outcomes among older adults receiving cancer chemotherapy — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: J Natl Cancer Inst
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-03
- **Authors**: Klepin HD et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate whether an automated EHR-based frailty index (eFI) predicts hospitalization and survival in older adults (≥65) receiving chemotherapy.
- **Method**: Retrospective cohort (n=509); automated cumulative deficit eFI; Cox regression for mortality; Kaplan-Meier OS estimates.
- **Key findings**: 6-month hospitalization ranged from 24% (fit) to 62% (frail); median OS 38 vs 17 months. Frail HR for mortality 2.0 (95% CI 1.3–3.1) vs fit.
- **Strengths**: Automated EHR derivation enables scalability; three cancer types; objective frailty measurement.
- **Limitations**: Single-institution, retrospective; eFI non-calculable in 15%, potentially missing the most vulnerable.
- **Conclusion**: EHR-derived frailty index is a scalable prognostic tool for risk stratification prior to chemotherapy in older adults.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag406 / PMID:42974945

## 5. Translating absolute risk of breast cancer into screening frequency: a framework to guide practice — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: J Natl Cancer Inst
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-03
- **Authors**: Jiang S et al.
- **Aims**: Develop a risk-model-agnostic framework mapping absolute breast cancer risk cut points to standardized screening intervals.
- **Method**: Framework development and evaluation against ongoing precision mammography screening trial designs; screening intervals derived from absolute-risk thresholds in US women.
- **Key findings**: Framework yields annual screening for 2–3% risk, biennial for 1–2%; ongoing trials showed wide inconsistency in interval-to-risk mapping.
- **Strengths**: Addresses critical gap in standardizing personalized screening; model-agnostic design enables broad applicability.
- **Limitations**: Framework paper without primary clinical outcomes data; clinical validation in prospective trials still needed.
- **Conclusion**: A standardized risk-to-interval framework could improve consistency in precision mammography screening trial design and clinical implementation.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag411 / PMID:42974942
