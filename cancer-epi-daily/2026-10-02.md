## 1. Giredestrant plus Everolimus in Advanced Breast Cancer — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: N Engl J Med
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-01
- **Authors**: Mayer EL et al.
- **Aims**: To assess giredestrant plus everolimus vs standard endocrine therapy plus everolimus in ER+/HER2− advanced breast cancer post-CDK4/6 inhibitor.
- **Method**: Phase 3 open-label RCT (n=373); primary endpoint investigator-assessed progression-free survival in ESR1-mutated subgroup then overall population.
- **Key findings**: Median PFS 10.0 vs 5.5 months (ESR1-mutated, HR 0.38, 95% CI 0.27–0.54, P<0.001); overall population 8.8 vs 5.5 months (HR 0.56, 95% CI 0.44–0.71, P<0.001).
- **Strengths**: Large multicentre Phase 3 RCT with pre-specified ESR1-mutation stratification and clinically meaningful PFS improvement.
- **Limitations**: Open-label design; OS data immature; benefit driven primarily by ESR1-mutated subgroup.
- **Conclusion**: All-oral giredestrant-everolimus significantly improves PFS over standard endocrine-everolimus in previously CDK4/6-inhibitor-treated ER+/HER2− advanced breast cancer.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1056/NEJMoa2602457 / PMID:42814929

## 2. Medical Debt and Advanced Cancer Stage at Diagnosis — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Netw Open
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-01
- **Authors**: Xing J et al.
- **Aims**: To examine whether county-level medical debt burden is associated with late-stage cancer incidence across the US.
- **Method**: Cross-sectional ecological study linking 2016 county-level medical debt data to age-adjusted late-stage cancer incidence rates (2017–2021) across 2958 US counties; multivariable models adjusted for rurality, SVI, and physician density.
- **Key findings**: Each 10-percentage-point increase in county medical debt associated with 5.15 extra late-stage lung cancer cases/100 000 person-years (95% CI 4.62–5.68) and significant increases across 6 other cancer types.
- **Strengths**: Large population-based ecological dataset covering nearly all US counties with multi-cancer scope and robust adjustment for social determinants.
- **Limitations**: Ecological design precludes individual-level causal inference; medical debt measured in 2016, preceding outcome window.
- **Conclusion**: Higher county-level medical debt burden is associated with higher late-stage cancer incidence, implicating financial barriers as a structural driver of delayed diagnosis.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.37508 / PMID:42821259

## 3. Immunologic Consequences of Genomic Instabilities — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Discov
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-01
- **Authors**: Alouani E et al.
- **Aims**: To review how mutational instability vs chromosomal instability differentially shape tumor immunogenicity and immunotherapy response.
- **Method**: Narrative review integrating mutational instability (substitutions/indels, neoantigen generation) and chromosomal instability (aneuploidy, innate immune dysregulation) frameworks.
- **Key findings**: Mutational instability generates neoantigens and sensitises tumours to checkpoint blockade; chromosomal instability drives chronic innate immune signalling that paradoxically suppresses antitumour immunity via myeloid and T-cell dysfunction.
- **Strengths**: Integrates two previously siloed genomic instability concepts into a unified immunological framework with clear therapeutic implications.
- **Limitations**: Review without systematic search protocol; mechanistic evidence is largely preclinical.
- **Conclusion**: Opposing immune effects of mutational and chromosomal instability should be jointly considered when designing personalised immunotherapeutic strategies.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/2159-8290.CD-25-2063 / PMID:42817715

## 4. When Neutrophils Do Not Die: Senescence as a New Axis of Tumor Immunosuppression — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Discov
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-01
- **Authors**: Droin L et al.
- **Aims**: To highlight new findings on how glucose deprivation in colorectal cancer liver metastases induces an immunosuppressive senescent neutrophil population.
- **Method**: Commentary on a primary scRNA-seq study characterising BHLHE40-driven tumour-associated neutrophil (TAN1) subpopulation in colorectal cancer liver metastases.
- **Key findings**: Glucose-deprived metastatic microenvironment upregulates BHLHE40 in neutrophils; TAN1 senescent neutrophils promote angiogenesis and exclude CD8+ T cells, supporting tumour growth.
- **Strengths**: Identifies a metabolically regulated neutrophil senescence axis as a druggable immunosuppressive mechanism in metastatic disease.
- **Limitations**: Commentary on a single study; functional contributions of TAN1 require in vivo therapeutic validation.
- **Conclusion**: Senescent tumour-associated neutrophils represent a newly characterised immunosuppressive mechanism in colorectal cancer liver metastases driven by metabolic reprogramming.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/2159-8290.CD-26-1529 / PMID:42817716

## 5. Activity over Abundance: ADAMTSL4 Unlocks Latent TGFβ1 in Cancer Cachexia — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Discov
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-01
- **Authors**: Liu Y et al.
- **Aims**: To highlight a new tumour-secreted mechanism driving cancer cachexia via ADAMTSL4-mediated activation of latent TGFβ1 in peripheral tissues.
- **Method**: Commentary on primary research identifying ADAMTSL4 as a tumour-secreted protein that locally activates latent TGFβ1 and drives tissue wasting in cachexia.
- **Key findings**: ADAMTSL4 promotes post-secretory activation of latent TGFβ1, inducing tissue wasting; this post-secretory control mechanism represents a potential therapeutic target distinct from total TGFβ1 blockade.
- **Strengths**: Identifies post-secretory ligand activation as a novel and potentially tumour-specific therapeutic vulnerability in cancer cachexia.
- **Limitations**: Commentary on single-group findings; clinical relevance of ADAMTSL4 targeting in human cachexia not yet established.
- **Conclusion**: Tumour-secreted ADAMTSL4 drives cachexia through local latent TGFβ1 activation, revealing a new mechanistic and therapeutic axis in cancer wasting.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/2159-8290.CD-26-1661 / PMID:42817717
