## 1. Frequency and prognostic outcomes of emergency diagnosis in 13 non-neoplastic conditions in England — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: PLoS Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-25
- **Authors**: Whitfield E et al.
- **Aims**: To document the frequency of emergency diagnosis across 13 non-cancer conditions and examine its association with mortality and hospitalisation.
- **Method**: Population-based cohort using linked CPRD, Hospital Episode Statistics, and ONS death records (n=1,701,154) in England, 1999–2019.
- **Key findings**: Emergency diagnosis occurred in ≥20% of patients for 9/13 conditions; associated with substantially higher 1-year mortality (e.g., aOR 7.53, 95% CI 5.64–10.1, for women with coeliac disease).
- **Strengths**: Exceptionally large sample; two CPRD validation datasets; cancer sites used as internal validity benchmark.
- **Limitations**: Assumes accurate EHR diagnosis recording; residual confounding possible.
- **Conclusion**: Emergency diagnosis is common and prognostically important across diverse non-neoplastic conditions, extending cancer-diagnosis-pathway insights to broader medicine.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1371/journal.pmed.1005182 / PMID:42640879

## 2. Epigenetic aging of colorectal mucosa in cancer development — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-25
- **Authors**: Hardikar S et al.
- **Aims**: To examine epigenetic aging across colorectal tissue types (normal, adjacent mucosa, tumors) and lifestyle factor associations.
- **Method**: Cross-tissue DNA methylation study applying 9 epigenetic clock models to 96 normal, 245 adjacent, and 208 tumor colorectal tissue samples.
- **Key findings**: Horvath model best estimated chronological age in normal mucosa (r>0.70); age acceleration was significant in adjacent mucosa (6 models) and highly significant in tumors (all models).
- **Strengths**: Multiple validated clock models applied simultaneously; tissue-matched design across cancer development stages.
- **Limitations**: Cross-sectional design; substantial heterogeneity between epigenetic models limits unified interpretation.
- **Conclusion**: Lifestyle factors (alcohol, smoking, physical activity) shape tissue-specific epigenetic aging in colorectal carcinogenesis; model choice critically affects results.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag297 / PMID:42640567

## 3. Randomized trial of adjuvant 5-FU-platinum vs paclitaxel-platinum for high-risk penile cancer — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-24
- **Authors**: Noronha V et al.
- **Aims**: To compare 5-fluorouracil-platinum with paclitaxel-platinum as adjuvant chemotherapy in high-risk node-positive penile squamous cell carcinoma.
- **Method**: Open-label randomized controlled trial (n=49) at Indian tertiary center, 2017–2024; primary endpoint disease-free survival (DFS).
- **Key findings**: No significant DFS difference (HR 1.16, P=0.686); paclitaxel-platinum had substantially fewer grade ≥3 toxicities (40.9% vs 77.3%, P=0.014).
- **Strengths**: First randomized trial in this rare malignancy; long median follow-up of 69 months.
- **Limitations**: Small sample (n=49) due to early termination; underpowered for efficacy conclusions.
- **Conclusion**: Paclitaxel-platinum is better tolerated than 5-FU-platinum in high-risk penile cancer and warrants evaluation in larger multicenter studies.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag288 / PMID:42636270

## 4. EMB-01, an Anti-EGFR/c-MET Bispecific Antibody, in Patients with Heavily Pretreated Metastatic Colorectal Cancer — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Clinical Cancer Research
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-24
- **Authors**: Xu T et al.
- **Aims**: To evaluate safety and antitumor activity of EMB-01 (EGFR×c-MET bispecific antibody) in heavily pretreated metastatic colorectal cancer.
- **Method**: Phase Ib/II open-label multicenter study (n=52); EMB-01 1600 mg IV weekly; endpoints safety (Ib) and antitumor activity (II).
- **Key findings**: Confirmed ORR 12.5% overall; 20.7% in RAS/RAF wild-type left-sided mCRC naïve to late-line therapies; median PFS 19.0 weeks.
- **Strengths**: Biomarker-stratified design identifies a clinically predictive subgroup; multinational enrollment.
- **Limitations**: Single-arm Phase II design; high grade ≥3 TRAE rate (63.5%); small subgroup for efficacy.
- **Conclusion**: EMB-01 shows manageable safety and promising activity in a selected mCRC subgroup, supporting further clinical development.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-26-2128 / PMID:42635595

## 5. Challenges in the use of the ESTRO-EORTC oligometastatic disease classification system in ovarian cancer — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: The Lancet Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-09
- **Authors**: Macchia G et al.
- **Aims**: To identify barriers to applying the ESTRO-EORTC oligometastatic disease classification in ovarian cancer and propose a multidisciplinary research agenda.
- **Method**: Expert methodological review and critique of existing disease classification framework, with a consensus-building proposal.
- **Key findings**: Standard oligometastatic criteria fail in ovarian cancer due to diffuse peritoneal spread and difficulty enumerating lesions; ovarian-specific rules are urgently needed.
- **Strengths**: Published in Lancet Oncology; engages major European oncology societies for structured consensus.
- **Limitations**: Expert opinion without prospective empirical validation; proposed adaptations untested.
- **Conclusion**: A joint ESTRO-EORTC-ESGO initiative is needed to define ovarian cancer-specific oligometastatic classification rules and harmonize clinical trial eligibility.
