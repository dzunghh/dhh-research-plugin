## 1. Induction and consolidation atezolizumab with SBRT versus radiation alone in high-risk early-stage NSCLC (SWOG/NRG S1914) — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Lancet
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-06
- **Authors**: Daly ME et al.
- **Aims**: To test whether adding neoadjuvant, concurrent, and adjuvant atezolizumab to SBRT improves overall survival in medically inoperable early-stage NSCLC.
- **Method**: Multicentre, open-label, phase 3 RCT; 417 patients randomised 1:1 at 146 US institutions; primary endpoint overall survival.
- **Key findings**: No overall survival benefit from atezolizumab (HR 1.04, 95% CI 0.69–1.58); estimated 2-year OS was 82% in both groups. Grade ≥3 adverse events higher with atezolizumab (12% vs 3%).
- **Strengths**: First fully reported phase 3 cooperative-group trial of immunotherapy in inoperable early-stage NSCLC; rigorous RCT design.
- **Limitations**: Closed early for futility at first interim analysis; relatively short median follow-up of 24.8 months.
- **Conclusion**: Atezolizumab added to SBRT did not improve survival and increased toxicity in high-risk early-stage NSCLC.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S0140-6736(26)01655-7 / PMID:42702214

## 2. Pembrolizumab + olaparib maintenance versus chemotherapy for advanced BRCA non-mutated epithelial ovarian cancer (KEYLYNK-001) — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-07
- **Authors**: Vergote I et al.
- **Aims**: To evaluate pembrolizumab + chemotherapy followed by pembrolizumab–olaparib maintenance in newly diagnosed BRCA non-mutated stage III–IV ovarian cancer.
- **Method**: Randomised double-blind placebo-controlled phase 3 trial; 1,367 patients at 224 centres in 22 countries; primary endpoint progression-free survival.
- **Key findings**: Pembrolizumab–olaparib significantly improved PFS versus control in CPS ≥10 (HR 0.63, p<0.0001) and ITT (HR 0.68, p<0.0001) populations; benefit maintained at 49.6-month final analysis (HR 0.71 ITT).
- **Strengths**: Large international RCT with long follow-up (∼50 months); extends PARP-inhibitor benefit to BRCA non-mutated population.
- **Limitations**: Higher grade ≥3 adverse events with pembrolizumab–olaparib (66%); no overall survival data yet.
- **Conclusion**: Pembrolizumab plus olaparib maintenance improves PFS in BRCA non-mutated advanced ovarian cancer, expanding eligibility for combination maintenance therapy.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00223-8 / PMID:42705254

## 3. Association between blood haemoglobin concentration and mortality by age and sex — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Ann Intern Med
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-08
- **Authors**: Ahmad M et al.
- **Aims**: To evaluate associations between baseline haemoglobin concentration and long-term all-cause, cardiovascular, and cancer mortality stratified by age and sex.
- **Method**: Prospective cohort study; 502,188 UK Biobank participants; median follow-up 13.6 years; outcomes analysed by sex and age (<60 vs ≥60 years).
- **Key findings**: U-shaped mortality association; lowest risk 1–3 g/dL above WHO threshold; those >2 g/dL below threshold had 10-year SCI of 12.5% vs 4.5% (ARD +8.1 pp); cancer mortality showed similar low-end pattern.
- **Strengths**: Very large population-based cohort with near-complete haemoglobin data (95.2%); robust adjustment for confounders.
- **Limitations**: Single baseline measurement; predominantly White UK population limits generalisability.
- **Conclusion**: Mortality is lowest at haemoglobin 1–3 g/dL above WHO thresholds, questioning the clinical sufficiency of current anemia definitions including for cancer outcomes.
- **ID**: DOI:10.7326/ANNALS-26-00057 / PMID:42704899

## 4. Cancer incidence trends among Asian American, Native Hawaiian, and Pacific Islander populations in the United States, 2000–2022 — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: J Natl Cancer Inst
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-07
- **Authors**: Noone AM et al.
- **Aims**: To characterise contemporary cancer incidence rates and trends in disaggregated AANHPI ethnic groups using a new nationwide SEER database.
- **Method**: Population-based analysis; 21 SEER registries, 2000–2022; average annual percentage change (AAPC) calculated for 11 AANHPI subgroups.
- **Key findings**: Breast and uterine cancer incidence increasing in most groups (AAPC up to 5.29% for breast); CRC declining in most groups; prostate trends generally increasing (AAPC 2.1–5.0%) with notable decreases among Laotians (−11.8%).
- **Strengths**: First nationwide SEER resource with disaggregated AANHPI data; 22-year longitudinal coverage with 11 subgroups.
- **Limitations**: Small numbers in some ethnic-cancer subgroups may limit precision; registry coverage varies by region.
- **Conclusion**: Ethnically distinct cancer incidence patterns within AANHPI groups underscore the need for disaggregated surveillance to guide equitable cancer control.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag314 / PMID:42704676

## 5. PD-L1 expression and survival in unresectable/recurrent gastric cancer treated by 1st-line treatment without immune checkpoint inhibitors (JCOG1013A1) — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Br J Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-04
- **Authors**: Hirano H et al.
- **Aims**: To evaluate the prognostic significance of PD-L1 expression in patients receiving first-line chemotherapy without ICI for unresectable/recurrent gastric cancer.
- **Method**: Ancillary analysis of phase III JCOG1013 RCT; n=540; PD-L1 assessed by Dako 28-8 pharmDx (CPS and TPS); multivariable Cox regression.
- **Key findings**: TPS ≥1% was an independent favourable prognostic factor for OS (HR 0.78, p=0.028) and PFS (HR 0.76, p=0.012); CPS ≥5 was not significantly associated with either.
- **Strengths**: Pre-specified biomarker analysis anchored in a phase III RCT; large biomarker-evaluable population.
- **Limitations**: Ancillary retrospective analysis; Japanese population limits generalisability; 27% of enrolled patients had no evaluable specimens.
- **Conclusion**: TPS ≥1%, but not CPS ≥5, independently predicts better survival in gastric cancer patients treated with chemotherapy alone, informing biomarker selection for this setting.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41416-026-03607-7 / PMID:42697937

## Appraisal notes

This week's digest is dominated by large RCTs and registry-based epidemiology. Two landmark phase 3 trials — one negative (SBRT + atezolizumab in NSCLC) and one positive (pembrolizumab + olaparib maintenance in ovarian cancer) — illustrate the importance of BRCA/biomarker stratification in determining immunotherapy benefit. The UK Biobank haemoglobin cohort study and the SEER-based AANHPI cancer trends analysis represent high-quality population epidemiology, with the latter highlighting the value of ethnic disaggregation in surveillance data to uncover heterogeneous cancer trends previously hidden by aggregate reporting.
