## 1. Complete blood count-derived inflammation indices in colorectal cancer: head-to-head case-control and prognostic analyses with population reference intervals and external replication — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: British Journal of Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-24
- **Authors**: Choe EK et al.
- **Aims**: Head-to-head evaluation of 5 CBC-derived inflammatory indices (NLR, PLR, MLR, SIRI, SII) for CRC diagnosis and prognosis.
- **Method**: Retrospective case-control study; 2305 CRC patients vs 78,281 controls; propensity score matching, Cox regression, restricted cubic splines; external replication in 1031 CRC patients; NHANES cross-population validation.
- **Key findings**: All 5 indices independently associated with survival; MLR most informative in Stage I disease and replicated externally. Reference interval overlap with healthy populations limits screening utility.
- **Strengths**: Large sample (2305 cases + 78,281 controls), external replication cohort, population reference intervals derived with NHANES cross-validation.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective design; substantial index-overlap with healthy populations reduces screening utility of derived thresholds.
- **Conclusion**: CBC-derived indices, particularly MLR, provide reproducible prognostic value in early-stage CRC but should not serve as standalone screening thresholds.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41416-026-03647-z / PMID:42926227

## 2. Extracorporeal photopheresis for steroid-refractory immune-related adverse events from immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy: a multi-centre phase Ib/II clinical trial — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Nature Communications
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-23
- **Authors**: Riemer R et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate safety and efficacy of extracorporeal photopheresis (ECP) for steroid-refractory irAEs in cancer patients on immune checkpoint inhibitors.
- **Method**: Prospective single-arm non-randomized multicenter phase Ib/II trial; N=30; primary endpoint <50% with ECP-related severe adverse events; mechanistic biomarker substudy (T-cell cytokines, intestinal gene expression).
- **Key findings**: No ECP-related SAEs observed; overall irAE response rate 96.3% (CI 81.0–99.9%); 12-month overall survival 84.3% (CI 63.4–93.8%).
- **Strengths**: Prospective multicenter design with pre-specified endpoints; mechanistic data linking ECP to reduced inflammatory gene expression in intestinal tissue.
- **Limitations**: Small N=30, single-arm design, no randomized comparator; colitis (n=13) and hepatitis (n=15) dominate sample.
- **Conclusion**: ECP is a safe and clinically active option for SR-irAEs, warranting investigation in randomized trials.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41467-026-78897-9 / PMID:42919851

## 3. Underrepresentation of women in U.S. occupational cohort mortality studies — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: American Journal of Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-26
- **Authors**: Johnson CY et al.
- **Aims**: Examine contemporary U.S. occupational cohort mortality studies for sex representation and how sex is used in analyses.
- **Method**: PubMed systematic search (2000–2022) of U.S. occupational cohort mortality studies with cancer/cardiovascular endpoints; N=176 cohort analyses; descriptive trend analysis by publication era.
- **Key findings**: 57% included both sexes, 42% included men only; representation of women rose from 48% (2000–2004) to 69% (2015–2022); sex most commonly used for confounding adjustment (69%) or exclusion (26%).
- **Strengths**: 22-year systematic review quantifying sex-representation gaps and temporal trends across a large sample of occupational epi studies.
- **Limitations**: US-focused; relies on published reports without access to raw data; does not assess quality of sex-stratified analyses.
- **Conclusion**: Women remain poorly represented in U.S. occupational cohort mortality research, though the gap has narrowed over two decades.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/aje/kwag273 / PMID:42929209
