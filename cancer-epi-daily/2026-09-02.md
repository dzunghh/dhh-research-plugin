## 1. Atezolizumab in early triple-negative breast cancer (NSABP B-59/GeparDouze) — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Nature Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-01
- **Authors**: Loibl S et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate whether adding atezolizumab to neoadjuvant chemotherapy improves event-free survival (EFS) in stage II–III TNBC.
- **Method**: Phase 3 double-blind RCT (n=1550); sequential taxane–carboplatin–anthracycline ± atezolizumab; primary endpoint EFS.
- **Key findings**: Atezolizumab did not significantly improve EFS (HR 0.80, 95% CI 0.62–1.03; 4-year difference 3.3%). Benefit suggested in clinically node-positive patients (p=0.039); basal-like immune-activated subtype may respond.
- **Strengths**: Large multinational double-blind Phase 3 design with pre-specified subgroup and mRNA subtype analyses.
- **Limitations**: Primary endpoint missed; exploratory subtype analysis underpowered for definitive conclusions.
- **Conclusion**: Routine addition of atezolizumab to TNBC neoadjuvant chemo not supported; biomarker-selected populations warrant further study.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41591-026-04565-6 / PMID:42680950

## 2. Tumor immune microenvironment remodeling predicts response to checkpoint inhibitor therapy — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Cell
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-01
- **Authors**: Lin Z et al.
- **Aims**: Map longitudinal tumor immune microenvironment (TIME) remodeling under immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) therapy and derive predictive signatures.
- **Method**: Pan-cancer scRNA-seq atlas of 441 samples from 241 patients across 10 cancer types; 77 immune/stromal subtypes; validated 59-gene signature in 1383 tumors from 19 cohorts.
- **Key findings**: ~40% of tumors shift TIME subtype during ICI therapy; transitions toward inflamed/B cell-enriched states track improved response; myeloid-dominant persistence indicates resistance.
- **Strengths**: Large pan-cancer longitudinal atlas with robust 19-cohort external validation.
- **Limitations**: Biopsies were heterogeneous in timing and site; causal direction of TIME transitions not fully established.
- **Conclusion**: Immunotype transitions are more predictive of ICI outcome than baseline TIME state, supporting dynamic monitoring strategies.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.ccell.2026.08.007 / PMID:42679812

## 3. Integrated clinicogenomic analysis of metastatic tropisms in advanced colorectal cancer — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Cell
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-01
- **Authors**: Manca P et al.
- **Aims**: Characterise genetic drivers and organ-specific metastatic patterns across a large consecutively sequenced CRC cohort.
- **Method**: Integrated clinical–genomic analysis of >7000 consecutively sequenced CRC samples; clonal evolutionary analysis; site-specific metastatic risk modelling.
- **Key findings**: RAS pathway activation increased risk of spread to all metastatic sites; WNT pathway activation was protective; brain and adrenal metastases occur as late events in microsatellite-stable CRC.
- **Strengths**: Population-scale consecutive cohort; integrates clonal evolution with organ-tropism data.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective clinical data; residual confounding from treatment heterogeneity possible.
- **Conclusion**: Primary tumor location and genomics together predict organ-specific metastasis risk in CRC, informing surveillance and trial design.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.ccell.2026.08.004 / PMID:42679811

## 4. SlideChat — multimodal generative AI for whole-slide computational pathology across 31 cancer types — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Nature Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-01
- **Authors**: Chen Y et al.
- **Aims**: Develop and evaluate a multimodal generative AI assistant capable of interpreting gigapixel whole-slide images (WSIs) across cancer types.
- **Method**: LLM integrated with patch-level and slide-level pathology encoders; trained on 274,233 instruction samples; evaluated on 8836 closed-ended questions, 3149 WSI reports from 5 cohorts, 31 cancer types.
- **Key findings**: SlideChat outperformed leading baselines by 19.1% in closed-ended accuracy and 7.7% in report METEOR score; received highest expert ratings on open-ended QA.
- **Strengths**: Large diverse training set spanning 31 cancer types; multiple evaluation benchmarks with human expert rating.
- **Limitations**: AI tool validated on benchmarks; prospective clinical deployment and real-world generalisability not yet assessed.
- **Conclusion**: SlideChat demonstrates state-of-the-art performance for whole-slide pathology interpretation, with potential to support diagnostic workflows.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s43018-026-01220-4 / PMID:42680939

## 5. On-treatment core needle biopsy identifies early responders for axillary surgery de-escalation in breast cancer — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: British Journal of Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-27
- **Authors**: Xie Y et al.
- **Aims**: Assess whether on-treatment core needle biopsy (CNB) after 2–4 NACT cycles predicts axillary pathologic complete response (pCR) and guides de-escalation.
- **Method**: Prospective cohort of 1693 breast cancer patients receiving on-treatment CNB during NACT (2013–2021); pCR and 5-year survival as endpoints.
- **Key findings**: Of 500 early responders (no residual disease on CNB), 83.4% achieved axillary pCR; 98% of cN0 early responders were node-negative; combining post-treatment MRI raised pCR rate to 89.6% in cN+ patients.
- **Strengths**: Large prospective cohort with long follow-up and combination MRI analysis.
- **Limitations**: Single-centre design limits external generalisability; applicability across NACT regimens unclear.
- **Conclusion**: On-treatment CNB reliably identifies early responders likely to achieve axillary pCR, supporting de-escalation trial design.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41416-026-03583-y / PMID:42661023

## Appraisal notes

This week's digest is dominated by high-quality clinical and molecular epidemiology from top journals. Two Cancer Cell studies showcase the growing power of large-scale integrated genomic–clinical databases for cancer epidemiology: pan-cancer longitudinal scRNA-seq atlases and population-level consecutive sequencing cohorts are replacing smaller case series as the standard for tumour biology insights. The Nat Med Phase 3 trial (NSABP B-59) exemplifies precision oncology challenges—improving average trial results requires better baseline biomarker stratification, underscoring an epi-methodology shift toward biomarker-adaptive trial designs rather than all-comers RCTs.
