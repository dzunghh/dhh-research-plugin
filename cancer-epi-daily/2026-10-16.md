## 1. Ponatinib-Blinatumomab or Imatinib-Chemotherapy for Adult Ph-Positive ALL — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: N Engl J Med
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-15
- **Authors**: Chiaretti S et al.
- **Aims**: To compare chemotherapy-free ponatinib+blinatumomab vs standard imatinib+chemotherapy as frontline Ph+ ALL treatment.
- **Method**: Phase 3 randomized controlled trial (2:1 allocation), 236 adults, primary endpoint event-free survival, median 23.7-month follow-up.
- **Key findings**: 24-month EFS 86% vs 67% (P<0.001); only 19% in intervention arm required transplant after first complete response.
- **Strengths**: Well-powered multicenter phase 3 RCT with crossover option, independently assessed MRD endpoints.
- **Limitations**: Relatively short follow-up (23.7 months); long-term OS benefit not yet established.
- **Conclusion**: Chemotherapy-free targeted immunotherapy is superior to imatinib+chemotherapy for frontline Ph+ ALL.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1056/NEJMoa2606208 / PMID:42874102

## 2. Trends in Incidence Rates of Uterine Corpus Cancer by Birth Cohort in 24 Countries, 1998–2017 — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: J Natl Cancer Inst
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-14
- **Authors**: Kelly KP et al.
- **Aims**: To characterize birth cohort effects on uterine corpus cancer incidence trends globally.
- **Method**: Age-period-cohort analysis using Cancer Incidence in Five Continents Plus, NORDCAN, and NAACCR data; cohort rate ratios estimated across 14 birth cohort periods.
- **Key findings**: Rates increased in 13/24 countries; UK women born ~1983 had 3.45-fold higher rates vs 1953 cohort (95% CI 2.67–4.46); steepest rises age 30–34.
- **Strengths**: 24-country comparative scope with rigorous APC modelling; multiple high-quality cancer registry sources.
- **Limitations**: Ecological design cannot disentangle specific generational exposures; limited data for low-income countries.
- **Conclusion**: Birth cohort effects drive rising uterine cancer rates in most high-incidence countries, implicating early-life exposures.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag364 / PMID:42872484

## 3. DNA-Based Biomarkers as Predictors of Carboplatin Response in Neoadjuvant Breast Cancer — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Clin Cancer Res
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-14
- **Authors**: Pladsen AV et al.
- **Aims**: To identify genomic biomarkers predicting carboplatin benefit in neoadjuvant treatment of TNBC and HR+ breast cancer.
- **Method**: Prospective randomized neoadjuvant trial (n=187); tumor whole-exome sequencing for CAAI, LOH fraction, HRD; independent validation cohort (n=67).
- **Key findings**: Low genomic LOH fraction predicted improved EFS with carboplatin in TNBC (P=0.007); HRD predicted pCR with carboplatin in HR+BC (5/8 vs 4/47, P=0.002).
- **Strengths**: Prospective randomized design; independent cohort validation; multi-biomarker analysis with mechanistic rationale.
- **Limitations**: Moderate sample size; HR+BC subgroup analyses underpowered.
- **Conclusion**: Distinct DNA-based biomarker profiles—LOH for TNBC, HRD for HR+BC—can guide carboplatin selection in neoadjuvant therapy.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-26-2212 / PMID:42870529

## 4. Artificial Intelligence-Enabled Clinical Phenotyping of Human Brown Fat — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Nature
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-14
- **Authors**: Gómez-Banoy N et al.
- **Aims**: To systematically define the clinical impact of brown adipose tissue (BAT) using big data and AI in a cancer centre cohort.
- **Method**: Retrospective big-data analysis of 101,243 patients at MSK; ML-based semi-automatic BAT quantification in ~8,700 patients; drug repurposing proof-of-concept.
- **Key findings**: BAT presence associated with lower prevalence of cardiovascular and psychiatric diseases; higher axillary BAT activity linked to healthier cardiometabolic profiles; losartan increased BAT activity.
- **Strengths**: Unprecedented scale (>100k patients); unbiased AI-driven phenotyping; actionable drug repurposing finding.
- **Limitations**: Single cancer centre, retrospective design; causal direction of BAT–disease associations unconfirmed.
- **Conclusion**: AI and big-data methods reveal new clinical phenotypes linked to BAT and support drug repurposing strategies for metabolic disease prevention.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41586-026-11162-7 / PMID:42875491

## 5. Second- and Third-Line Outcomes After Selective RET Inhibitor Failure in RET Fusion-Positive Metastatic Lung Cancer — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Clin Cancer Res
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-14
- **Authors**: Miliziano D et al.
- **Aims**: To describe real-world second- and third-line treatment outcomes after first-line selective RET inhibitor progression in RET+ NSCLC.
- **Method**: Retrospective real-world registry study (RET-MAP, 52 international centers); 207 first-line SRI patients; 65 received subsequent therapy; median 53-month follow-up.
- **Key findings**: Second-line platinum-pemetrexed ± IO: rwORR 41%, median rwPFS 6.1 months, OS 13.7 months; chemo ± IO outcomes similar; third-line outcomes poor (no responses, mPFS 1.5 months).
- **Strengths**: Largest international registry for this rare molecular subgroup; long follow-up; multi-line outcomes reported.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective registry; heterogeneous second-line regimens; small subgroups for some analyses.
- **Conclusion**: Post-SRI platinum-pemetrexed achieves modest but real benefit; third-line outcomes highlight urgent need for new strategies in RET+ NSCLC.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-26-2942 / PMID:42873584
