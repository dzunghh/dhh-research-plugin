## 1. Global Proposal for the TNM Staging System for Poorly Differentiated Thyroid Cancer — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-06
- **Authors**: Shi X et al.
- **Aims**: Develop a PDTC-specific TNM staging system to replace the differentiated-thyroid-cancer schema (TNM-8) now used by default.
- **Method**: International cohort study (91 centers, 14 countries, n=1010) with multivariable Cox models, AIC selection across 16 738 TN combinations; external validation in SEER (n=170).
- **Key findings**: Proposed system achieved higher C-statistic than TNM-8 (0.74 vs 0.71). Level VII LNM was reclassified from N1a to N1c; stage IV prevalence rose from 18.5% to 32.4%.
- **Strengths**: Largest international PDTC cohort to date, blinded external validation, rigorous model selection.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective; SEER validation confidence interval crosses null for the incremental C-statistic difference.
- **Conclusion**: PDTC-specific staging improves prognostic stratification and supports a stand-alone AJCC staging category.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamaoncol.2026.4705 / PMID:42994809

## 2. Origins and Heterogeneity of Colorectal Cancer — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Nature Reviews Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-06
- **Authors**: Sphyris N et al.
- **Aims**: Explain how crypt-axis position of the cell-of-origin shapes distinct CRC carcinogenesis pathways and molecular heterogeneity.
- **Method**: Comprehensive narrative review integrating stem-cell biology, clonal dynamics, morphogenetic gradient data, and molecular subtype evidence.
- **Key findings**: Cell-of-origin position on the vertical crypt axis predetermines oncogenic mechanisms; phenotypic (not only genotypic) heterogeneity drives therapeutic resistance and metastasis.
- **Strengths**: Integrates emerging plasticity mechanisms with clinicopathological outcomes across all major CRC subtypes.
- **Limitations**: Narrative review; causal inferences await prospective lineage-tracing validation in humans.
- **Conclusion**: Crypt topology and cell plasticity together explain the heterogeneous molecular landscape of CRC and have direct therapeutic implications.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41568-026-00988-y / PMID:42995214

## 3. [¹⁸F]FET PET Enables Histopathology-Validated Detection of Non-Contrast-Enhancing Glioblastoma — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Nature Communications
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-06
- **Authors**: Stürzl R et al.
- **Aims**: Determine whether [¹⁸F]FET amino acid PET outperforms CE-MRI for detecting IDH-wildtype glioblastoma including metabolically active non-enhancing regions.
- **Method**: Histopathology-validated cohort; 472 tissue samples from 61 patients; sensitivity and PPV compared between FET PET and CE-MRI.
- **Key findings**: FET PET sensitivity 91.2% vs CE-MRI 52.6%; PPV in non-enhancing tissue 98.3%; findings consistent across newly diagnosed and recurrent disease.
- **Strengths**: Histopathology validation at tissue-sample level is methodologically rigorous; results replicated across two surgical cohorts.
- **Limitations**: Single-centre; n=61 patients; no randomized comparison or survival endpoints.
- **Conclusion**: FET PET substantially improves tumor visualization over MRI and may guide more accurate biopsy, resection, and radiotherapy planning.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41467-026-78980-1 / PMID:42995172

## 4. Kinetics of Re-differentiation with Short-Course Dabrafenib and Trametinib in Radioiodine-Refractory Thyroid Cancer — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Clinical Cancer Research
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-09
- **Authors**: Yang SP et al.
- **Aims**: Assess whether short-course BRAF/MEK inhibition can rapidly restore iodine uptake and provide clinical benefit in radioiodine-refractory thyroid cancer.
- **Method**: Multicentre phase II trial; I-124 PET/CT dosimetry at baseline and week 2 (±week 4) in 14 patients with BRAFV600E or RAS-mutant metastatic disease.
- **Key findings**: 64% (9/14) met dosimetry criteria; 67% restored iodine uptake within 2 weeks; 75% objective response rate among radioiodine-treated patients; median PFS 10.8 months.
- **Strengths**: Prospective, multi-centre; individualized dosimetry-guided approach; biomarker exploratory analysis (FOXE1, GLIS3).
- **Limitations**: Small n=14; uncontrolled; RAS vs BRAFV600E subgroup analysis underpowered.
- **Conclusion**: Rapid re-differentiation achievable in 2 weeks supports individualized rather than empiric 4-6 week BRAF/MEK inhibition protocols.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-26-2415 / PMID:43002139

## 5. SKP2-Mediated CDK4 Selective Degradation Contributes to SCP-7e-Induced Cell Death of Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: British Journal of Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-07
- **Authors**: Yan Q et al.
- **Aims**: Identify a selective CDK4 inhibitor/degrader with improved specificity over dual CDK4/6 inhibitors to overcome haematological toxicity and resistance.
- **Method**: Target fishing with biotinylated probe, DARTS, CETSA, SPR, LC-MS/MS, site-directed mutagenesis, covalent docking; in vivo NSCLC xenograft models.
- **Key findings**: SCP-7e covalently binds Cys78 (outside ATP pocket) on CDK4, blocks CDK4-cyclin D1 interaction, and induces SKP2-mediated CDK4 degradation with no effect on CDK6.
- **Strengths**: Multi-method target validation; identifies a novel druggable allosteric site; in vivo activity confirmed.
- **Limitations**: Preclinical only; no patient-derived or clinical translation data yet.
- **Conclusion**: Cys78 is a novel druggable site on CDK4; SCP-7e represents a selective CDK4 degrader advancing next-generation NSCLC therapies.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41416-026-03653-1 / PMID:42995200
