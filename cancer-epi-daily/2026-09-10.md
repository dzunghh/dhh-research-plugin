## 1. Measurement of Treatment Adequacy Influences Racial Disparities Estimates in Endometrial Cancer Care — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiology, Biomarkers & Prevention
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-09
- **Authors**: Felix AS et al.
- **Aims**: To evaluate how treatment measurement method and data source affect racial disparities estimates in guideline-concordant endometrial cancer treatment.
- **Method**: Comparative analysis using SEER-17, NCDB, and SEER-Medicare; logistic regression with adequacy-based vs. receipt-based guideline-concordant treatment definitions.
- **Key findings**: Adequacy-based definitions consistently showed larger Black–White disparities than receipt-based definitions (e.g., NCDB OR=0.75 vs. higher in receipt-only). All three data sources showed Black patients had lower odds of guideline-concordant care.
- **Strengths**: Three independent large databases with complementary population coverage; dual measurement frameworks tested systematically.
- **Limitations**: SEER-17 and NCDB may incompletely capture treatment adequacy details; residual confounding possible.
- **Conclusion**: Measurement of treatment adequacy is critical; receipt-based definitions underestimate racial disparities in endometrial cancer care.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-0638 / PMID:42714943

## 2. Recurrent Mechanisms of Biallelic Epigenetic Inactivation Reveal New Putative Tumour Suppressor Genes in Prostate Cancer — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Nature Communications
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-09
- **Authors**: Kiriy D et al.
- **Aims**: To identify tumour suppressor genes inactivated through epigenetic silencing of one allele combined with genomic loss of the other in prostate cancer.
- **Method**: Epi2Hit integrative computational method applied to 2,021 prostate cancers combining whole-genome sequencing, epigenomic profiling, and gene expression data.
- **Key findings**: ZFHX3 biallelic inactivation found at recurrence level comparable to TP53; ZFHX3 inactivation upregulates oncogenes including MYC and correlates with shorter time to metastasis.
- **Strengths**: Large cohort (n=2,021); integrative multi-omic approach revealing a previously under-recognised inactivation mechanism.
- **Limitations**: Computational method requires matched WGS + epigenomic + expression data; functional validation mainly in prostate cancer.
- **Conclusion**: Epigenetic silencing as a second hit is enriched near essential genes; Epi2Hit expands the catalogue of cancer-relevant tumour suppressors beyond homozygous deletion.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41467-026-72182-5 / PMID:42716948

## 3. A Guide to Understanding Tumour Evolution Through the Lens of Population Genetics — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Nature Reviews Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-09
- **Authors**: Caravagna G et al.
- **Aims**: To provide a methodological framework using population genetics to infer evolutionary dynamics from cancer genome sequencing data.
- **Method**: Review synthesising models of mutation, selection, and drift applied to allele frequencies in cancer sequencing data; covers epigenetic inheritance, plasticity, and ecological tumour interactions.
- **Key findings**: Population genetics transforms allele frequencies into quantitative clonal fitness estimates and evolutionary timings; models can predict relapse trajectories and treatment resistance emergence.
- **Strengths**: Comprehensive methodological synthesis; bridges quantitative evolutionary theory and clinical cancer genomics; discusses assumptions and limitations.
- **Limitations**: Review does not provide new empirical data; model assumptions often violated in heterogeneous tumour samples.
- **Conclusion**: Population genetics offers a principled mathematical foundation for moving cancer genomics from descriptive mutation cataloguing to predictive evolutionary inference.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41568-026-00973-5 / PMID:42717056

## 4. Changes in Alcohol Use Among Adults with Cancer Participating in the ECOG-ACRIN EAQ171CD Tobacco Treatment Trial — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiology, Biomarkers & Prevention
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-09
- **Authors**: Walter AW et al.
- **Aims**: To examine changes in alcohol use disorder risk among cancer patients receiving a virtual tobacco cessation intervention vs. enhanced usual care.
- **Method**: Post-hoc analysis of an NCORP RCT (n=305); multivariable logistic regression assessing AUDIT-C risk category shifts at 3 and 6 months.
- **Key findings**: Both arms achieved reductions in alcohol risk; no significant difference between VST and enhanced usual care at 3 months (adjusted OR=0.56, p=0.125) or 6 months.
- **Strengths**: Addresses clinically important co-use of tobacco and alcohol in cancer patients; uses validated AUDIT-C measure.
- **Limitations**: Post-hoc analysis of RCT powered for tobacco cessation, not alcohol; small n; predominantly White sample.
- **Conclusion**: Cancer patients with tobacco–alcohol co-use can reduce alcohol risk during structured cessation support; integrated co-use strategies warrant further prospective study.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-0589 / PMID:42714936

## 5. Adaptation and Usability of a Lifestyle Cancer Prevention Screener for US Populations: A Mixed Methods Study — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiology, Biomarkers & Prevention
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-09
- **Authors**: Goldbaum AA et al.
- **Aims**: To culturally adapt and assess usability of a WCRF/AICR Cancer Prevention Recommendations screener originally developed in Spain for use in US adults.
- **Method**: Iterative mixed-methods design; two rounds of online surveys (n=153, n=151) and cognitive interviews; 17-item screener covering anthropometrics, physical activity, diet, and breastfeeding.
- **Key findings**: >90% of participants rated screener as easy to understand and acceptable for clinical or research use across both rounds; dietary recall items required iterative refinement.
- **Strengths**: Systematic iterative adaptation process; both quantitative and qualitative usability assessment; NCI-led development.
- **Limitations**: Convenience sample without demographic diversity reporting; validation against biomarkers or cancer outcomes not yet conducted.
- **Conclusion**: Adapted WCRF/AICR screener is feasible and acceptable for US adults; further psychometric and predictive validation needed.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-0495 / PMID:42714964
