## 1. Functional role of skull lymphoid structures in CNS immunosurveillance — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Nature
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-19
- **Authors**: Park JH et al.
- **Aims**: Identify whether skull bone marrow lymphoid structures mediate antigen-specific adaptive immune surveillance of the CNS.
- **Method**: Mouse models with anatomical, flow cytometric, and functional assays; germinal-centre-like skull BM structures characterised; brain tumour challenge experiments.
- **Key findings**: Skull BM harbours follicular-helper-like T cells activating B cells via CD40L/IL-21/IFNγ; these cells mount anti-tumour responses in brain cancer models.
- **Strengths**: Novel anatomical discovery with clear mechanistic pathway and direct cancer relevance.
- **Limitations**: All experiments in mice; human translational data absent.
- **Conclusion**: Skull bone marrow is an active CNS immunosurveillance hub with implications for brain cancer immunity.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41586-026-10951-4 / PMID:42618784

## 2. Pathologic Complete Response Rate and Predictors of Response to Taxane, Trastuzumab, and Pertuzumab in HER2-Positive Breast Cancer — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: J Clin Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-20
- **Authors**: Tung N et al.
- **Aims**: Report pCR rates and identify clinical/genomic predictors in HER2+ breast cancer treated with neoadjuvant THP.
- **Method**: Single-arm trial EA1181/CompassHER2 (NCT04266249), n=2,175; logistic regression including HER2DX pCR score.
- **Key findings**: Overall pCR 43.8% (HER2+/ER− 63.7%, HER2+/ER+ 32.4%); high HER2DX score independently predicted pCR after adjustment.
- **Strengths**: Large multicentre ECOG-ACRIN cohort with comprehensive biomarker analysis.
- **Limitations**: Primary RFS endpoint not yet mature; HER2DX assessed in only 569 patients.
- **Conclusion**: ER status, HER2 IHC 3+, weekly paclitaxel, and high HER2DX score independently predict pCR to neoadjuvant THP.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1200/JCO-25-02255 / PMID:42623567

## 3. Myeloproliferative Neoplasms — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: N Engl J Med
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-20
- **Authors**: Plo I et al.
- **Aims**: Review pathobiology, clonal evolution, and emerging therapies for classic myeloproliferative neoplasms (ET, PV, PMF).
- **Method**: Narrative review covering JAK2/CALR/MPL mutations, epigenetic co-mutations, inflammation-driven clonal dominance, and targeted therapy trials.
- **Key findings**: Driver mutations arise decades before diagnosis; selective mutant-CALR and JAK2 V617F immunotherapy may achieve clonal eradication.
- **Strengths**: Authoritative NEJM review synthesising current and emerging therapeutic paradigms.
- **Limitations**: Review design; no new primary data generated.
- **Conclusion**: Selective immunotherapy targeting mutant CALR/JAK2 represents a breakthrough opportunity for durable disease modification.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1056/NEJMra2507867 / PMID:42617115

## 4. Biological aging and the hidden architecture of early-onset cancer risk — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Cell
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-18
- **Authors**: Abbasi A et al.
- **Aims**: Synthesise findings on biological age gaps as integrative markers of early-onset lung, colorectal, and uterine cancer risk across birth cohorts.
- **Method**: Commentary contextualising a Nature Medicine cohort study; proposes combining aging clocks with mutational signatures.
- **Key findings**: Larger biological-age gaps are consistently associated with early-onset cancers; positions accelerated biological aging as physiological dysregulation marker.
- **Strengths**: Directly relevant to cancer epidemiology; bridges aging biology and early-onset cancer surveillance.
- **Limitations**: Commentary design; no new primary data; clinical translation of aging clocks not yet established.
- **Conclusion**: Integrating aging clocks with mutational signatures could distinguish tumour-promoting states from tumour-initiating exposures.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.ccell.2026.07.018 / PMID:42612635

## 5. Tumor cells recruit macrophage bodyguards to outlast chemotherapy — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Cell
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-20
- **Authors**: Wang Y et al.
- **Aims**: Describe spatially organised tumour-macrophage niches mediating cisplatin resistance in bladder cancer.
- **Method**: Preview of Carvalho et al.; EMT-like CD44+ cancer cells paired with SPP1+/PARP14+ macrophages; PARP14 inhibition experiments.
- **Key findings**: PARP14 inhibition flips protective tumour-macrophage niches to inflammatory, cisplatin-sensitising states in bladder cancer.
- **Strengths**: Identifies a novel actionable target (PARP14) for overcoming chemoresistance.
- **Limitations**: Preview commentary; primary data in mouse/in vitro models only.
- **Conclusion**: Spatial tumour-macrophage niches driven by PARP14 are a targetable mechanism of chemoresistance in bladder cancer.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.ccell.2026.07.015 / PMID:42624109
