## 1. SLFN11 Enhances Cisplatin Sensitivity in Pediatric Cancer via Stress-Response and Survival Pathway Modulation — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: British Journal of Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-02
- **Authors**: Jabeen A et al.
- **Aims**: To characterise SLFN11 as a predictive biomarker of chemotherapy response in paediatric solid tumours.
- **Method**: Bioinformatics analysis of TARGET/ICGC datasets + CRISPR-dCas9 SLFN11 activation in Wilms tumour, osteosarcoma, and medulloblastoma cell lines.
- **Key findings**: SLFN11 was epigenetically silenced via promoter methylation; reactivation sensitised all three cell lines to cisplatin and talazoparib. Survival associations were tumour-type-specific.
- **Strengths**: Combines population-level dataset analysis with functional epigenetic validation across three cancer types.
- **Limitations**: In vitro cell line data; clinical validation in prospective paediatric cohorts lacking.
- **Conclusion**: SLFN11 is a context-dependent biomarker and therapeutic target for overcoming chemoresistance in paediatric solid tumours.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41416-026-03571-2 / PMID:42686854

## 2. Elevated Triglycerides Promote NSCLC Metastasis via Lipid β-Oxidation and RhoA-Driven Cytoskeletal Remodelling — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: British Journal of Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-03
- **Authors**: Dai Y et al.
- **Aims**: To elucidate mechanisms by which elevated serum triglycerides (TG) worsen NSCLC prognosis and promote metastasis.
- **Method**: Retrospective cohort (n=77 NSCLC patients) stratified by serum TG; hypertriglyceridemia mouse model; ECM-constrained invasion assays with CPT1A/RhoA inhibition.
- **Key findings**: Hypertriglyceridemia was associated with shorter OS (log-rank p=0.009), higher lymph node metastasis (84.4% vs 37.9%), and distant metastasis (31.3% vs 3.4%). TG-driven invasion was suppressed by CPT1A/RhoA silencing.
- **Strengths**: Combines clinical epidemiologic data with mechanistic in vivo and in vitro validation of the TG–β-oxidation–RhoA axis.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective design with small n=77; single-centre, limiting generalisability.
- **Conclusion**: Targeting lipid catabolism and RhoA cytoskeletal signalling may be a therapeutic strategy for hypertriglyceridemia-associated NSCLC.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41416-026-03586-9 / PMID:42686855

## 3. Radiomics as Spatial Context for Biomarker-Guided Treatment Decision-Making in Head and Neck Cancer — Score 1/6

- **Journal**: British Journal of Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-03
- **Authors**: Kono T et al.
- **Aims**: To position radiomics as a spatial contextualiser for biopsy-derived biomarkers in HNSCC treatment decisions.
- **Method**: Narrative review synthesising evidence linking radiomic features to hypoxia, necrosis, stromal architecture, and immune exclusion in HNSCC.
- **Key findings**: Radiomics can contextualise PD-L1 expression, immune infiltration, and HPV status by capturing tumour spatial heterogeneity. Evidence is largely retrospective.
- **Strengths**: Provides a structured clinical decision framework mapping radiomic features to specific HNSCC treatment decision points.
- **Limitations**: All cited evidence retrospective; no primary data; clinical validation of proposed framework lacking.
- **Conclusion**: Radiomics offers complementary spatial information to improve interpretation of biopsy-derived biomarkers in HNSCC clinical workflows.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41416-026-03600-0 / PMID:42693214
