## 1. Dietary Fat Intake and Mortality Among Patients With Nonmetastatic Prostate Cancer — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Network Open
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-01
- **Authors**: Zhang Y et al.
- **Aims**: To assess whether postdiagnosis dietary fat type is associated with long-term survival in nonmetastatic prostate cancer survivors.
- **Method**: Prospective cohort (Health Professionals Follow-Up Study; n=4,884); validated food frequency questionnaires every 4 years; multivariable Cox regression; median 12.8-year follow-up.
- **Key findings**: Higher saturated fat intake associated with greater all-cause mortality (HR 1.24, 95% CI 1.05–1.47). Replacing 10% of animal fat calories with plant-based fats reduced all-cause mortality (HR 0.84, 95% CI 0.76–0.93).
- **Strengths**: Large cohort with repeated validated dietary assessments and very long follow-up; multiple fat subtypes examined simultaneously.
- **Limitations**: Observational design with self-reported diet; sample restricted to male health professionals, limiting generalisability.
- **Conclusion**: Saturated and animal fats associated with worse survival; plant-based and monounsaturated fats associated with improved outcomes in prostate cancer survivorship.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.30693 / PMID:42684704

## 2. Laterality of Breast Radiotherapy and Ischemic Heart Disease by Cardiovascular Risk Burden — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Network Open
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-01
- **Authors**: Chung SY et al.
- **Aims**: To evaluate whether the excess IHD risk from left-sided breast radiotherapy varies by baseline cardiovascular risk burden.
- **Method**: Retrospective nationwide cohort (Korean National Health Insurance; n=23,305 women); competing-risk Fine-Gray models; stratified by count of 6 CV risk factors; 2002–2018.
- **Key findings**: Left-sided RT associated with 11% higher overall IHD risk (sHR 1.11, 95% CI 1.01–1.22). Among patients with ≥3 CV risk factors, excess risk was 37% (sHR 1.37, 95% CI 1.10–1.71).
- **Strengths**: Large national registry with competing-risk methods; novel stratification by composite CV burden score identifies highest-risk subgroup.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective; limited radiation dose data; modern heart-sparing techniques not fully captured in early cohort years.
- **Conclusion**: Cardiac risk attributable to breast RT laterality is concentrated among patients with high CV burden, informing personalised survivorship planning.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.31356 / PMID:42684702

## 3. L-Glutamine Combined with Gemcitabine + Nab-Paclitaxel in Advanced Pancreatic Ductal Adenocarcinoma — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Nature Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-31
- **Authors**: Gong J et al.
- **Aims**: To determine safety and preliminary efficacy of clinical-grade L-glutamine added to standard first-line chemotherapy in advanced pancreatic cancer.
- **Method**: Open-label single-arm phase 1 trial (n=16); adaptive Bayesian dose-escalation (0.1–0.3 g/kg twice-daily oral L-glutamine); primary endpoint: recommended phase 2 dose.
- **Key findings**: ORR 44% (12.5% complete response); median OS 22 months vs ~9 months historical gemcitabine + nab-paclitaxel alone. Grade ≥3 treatment-related adverse events in 66.7%, primarily from chemotherapy.
- **Strengths**: First clinical translation of FDA-approved L-glutamine in pancreatic cancer; metagenomic and metabolomic profiling as exploratory endpoints illuminates mechanism.
- **Limitations**: Single-arm small sample (n=16); no randomised control; OS comparison is historical, not concurrent.
- **Conclusion**: L-Glutamine with gemcitabine + nab-paclitaxel is feasible and shows promising early efficacy; supports phase 2 investigation.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s43018-026-01225-z / PMID:42675216

## 4. Historical Redlining and Spatiotemporal Patterns in Breast Cancer Screening — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Network Open
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-01
- **Authors**: Rahman MA et al.
- **Aims**: To examine whether historical HOLC redlining grades are independently associated with breast cancer screening prevalence across space and time.
- **Method**: Census-tract cohort study (n=1,152 tracts); 2016–2024 CDC PLACES data; Bayesian spatiotemporal models adjusting for Social Vulnerability Index, neighbourhood, and geographic access.
- **Key findings**: Grade C and D tracts had 6% lower screening odds than grade A (OR ~0.94) after full adjustment. 61.7% of tracts showed residual spatial heterogeneity; screening peaked 2018–2019 and declined in 2024, remaining below the Healthy People 2030 target.
- **Strengths**: Spatiotemporal Bayesian approach captures residual geographic clustering; longitudinal 9-year window; adjustment for multiple contemporary confounders.
- **Limitations**: Ecological study (census tract), susceptible to aggregation bias; catchment area limited to one cancer centre in Kansas/Missouri.
- **Conclusion**: Historical redlining independently predicts lower breast cancer screening uptake, even after accounting for contemporary vulnerability, supporting place-based intervention strategies.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.30685 / PMID:42684705

## 5. Regional Variation in Bladder Cancer Clinical Trial Availability in the US — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Network Open
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-01
- **Authors**: Shah KU et al.
- **Aims**: To evaluate geographic disparities in bladder cancer clinical trial availability across US counties and their association with socioeconomic and epidemiologic factors.
- **Method**: Cross-sectional analysis of 436 bladder cancer trials (ClinicalTrials.gov, 2019–2025); zero-inflated negative binomial regression; county-level SVI, incidence, and mortality data.
- **Key findings**: Only 22.7% of US counties had any trial site. Low-SVI counties had 2.5× more trials than high-SVI counties (IRR 2.53). Counties with highest bladder cancer mortality had 20% fewer trials (IRR 0.80).
- **Strengths**: Comprehensive national trial registry; sophisticated zero-inflated model for count data; linkage of trial distribution to cancer burden and social vulnerability.
- **Limitations**: ClinicalTrials.gov may undercount industry trials; county-level analysis masks within-county variation; no patient-level enrolment data.
- **Conclusion**: Bladder cancer clinical trials are geographically concentrated in low-vulnerability, high-incidence counties, worsening access disparities where disease burden is greatest.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.32012 / PMID:42690658

## Appraisal notes

This week's digest is dominated by JAMA Network Open with four papers reflecting a surge in cancer survivorship epidemiology and health equity research. A notable methodological trend is the use of population-scale registry and administrative data with rigorous competing-risk or spatiotemporal Bayesian modelling to answer policy-relevant questions about who bears excess cancer risk and who lacks access to evidence-based care. The Nat Cancer phase 1 trial of L-glutamine stands out as a first clinical translation of a metabolic intervention in pancreatic cancer, though phase 2 randomised confirmation is essential before drawing conclusions.
