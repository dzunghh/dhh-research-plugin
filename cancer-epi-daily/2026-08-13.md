## 1. Spatial Organisation of Tumor-Infiltrating B-cells Informs ICI Response in CLDN18.2-Expressing Gastric Cancer — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Clinical Cancer Research
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-07
- **Authors**: Zhao JJ et al.
- **Aims**: To characterize the immune microenvironment of CLDN18.2-expressing gastric cancer and identify B-cell spatial features predicting ICI benefit.
- **Method**: Multi-cohort study; multiplex IHC in 103 patients (7.4M cells), validated in 5 transcriptomic cohorts (1,521 samples); cellular neighborhood and digital spatial profiling analyses.
- **Key findings**: CLDN18.2high tumors showed higher CD20+ B-cell density and humoral enrichment; tumor-compartment B-cell localization (vs. tertiary lymphoid structures) was associated with diminished ICI benefit.
- **Strengths**: Multi-cohort validation across 1,521 samples with orthogonal spatial platforms strengthens causal inference on immune architecture.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective; CLDN18.2 expression thresholds not fully standardized across assays.
- **Conclusion**: Spatial B-cell organization, not density alone, determines ICI benefit in CLDN18.2-expressing gastric cancer.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-26-1382 / PMID:42565557

## 2. SKYSCRAPER-03: Phase III Tiragolumab+Atezolizumab vs. Durvalumab in Locally Advanced Stage III NSCLC After Chemoradiation — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Annals of Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-06
- **Authors**: Wakelee HA et al.
- **Aims**: To evaluate consolidation tiragolumab+atezolizumab versus durvalumab in stage III unresectable NSCLC after platinum-based concurrent chemoradiation.
- **Method**: Phase III open-label RCT (N=829); primary endpoint IRF-assessed PFS in PD-L1+ patients; median follow-up 33.0 months.
- **Key findings**: Median PFS 19.4 vs. 16.6 months in PD-L1+ patients (HR 0.96; 95% CI 0.75–1.23; p=0.76); median OS not estimable vs. 54.8 months (HR 0.99). Primary endpoint not met.
- **Strengths**: Well-powered multicenter phase III RCT with long follow-up; OS data available for both subgroups.
- **Limitations**: Open-label design; PD-L1 cutoff selection for primary endpoint may have missed a responsive subgroup.
- **Conclusion**: Adding tiragolumab to atezolizumab did not improve PFS or OS versus durvalumab in stage III NSCLC.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.annonc.2026.07.414 / PMID:42562209

## 4. Charting Intratumor Heterogeneity from Bench to Bedside — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Nature Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-07
- **Authors**: Wan JCM et al.
- **Aims**: To synthesize current understanding of intratumor heterogeneity and tumor evolution, discussing evolution-aware diagnostic and therapeutic strategies.
- **Method**: Narrative review integrating multiregion/multiomic profiling, functional studies, and longitudinal liquid biopsy (ctDNA) data from multiple cancer types.
- **Key findings**: Genetic and non-genetic mechanisms both drive heterogeneity; longitudinal plasma ctDNA sampling reveals dynamic clonal evolution not captured by single-site biopsy; evolution-aware strategies show early promise.
- **Strengths**: Authoritative synthesis from TRACERx-affiliated team (McGranahan, Swanton); strong translational framework applicable to cancer epi cohort design.
- **Limitations**: Review design; prospective clinical validation of proposed evolution-aware strategies largely pending.
- **Conclusion**: Evolution-aware diagnostics and therapies are needed to address intratumor heterogeneity as a root cause of treatment resistance and metastasis.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s43018-026-01198-z / PMID:42567927
