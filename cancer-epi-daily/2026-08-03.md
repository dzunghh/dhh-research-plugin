## 1. Pragmatic Two-Stage Design for Clinical Trials With Treatment Selection — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-08
- **Authors**: Horton P et al.
- **Aims**: Develop a pragmatic two-stage design balancing sample efficiency and practicality for simultaneous treatment selection and efficacy evaluation.
- **Method**: Sequential two-stage adaptive design using stochastic optimisation of Kiefer-Weiss-inspired framework; benchmarked against fixed and multi-arm multi-stage designs.
- **Key findings**: Minimises weighted sum of expected patients, probability of selecting a bad arm, and global Type II error; outperforms comparators across varying error costs and treatment-response models.
- **Strengths**: Unified framework jointly handling treatment selection and efficacy evaluation with formal error-rate control.
- **Limitations**: Applicability beyond pharmaceutical development settings not discussed in abstract.
- **Conclusion**: Improves cost and practicality of clinical trials for medical treatments and pharmaceutical development.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70683 / PMID:42515853

## 2. A Bayesian Optimal Adaptive Clinical Trial Design for Sequentially Integrated Therapies — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-08
- **Authors**: Li Y et al.
- **Aims**: Introduce BIT design for complex diseases requiring sequential multi-phase therapies, connecting acute and downstream treatment outcomes.
- **Method**: Bayesian model linking acute-phase and subsequent-treatment outcomes; multiple interim analyses with adaptive futility and superiority stopping rules.
- **Key findings**: BIT controls family-wise type I error and maximises statistical power; simulation studies confirm excellent operating characteristics.
- **Strengths**: Handles multiple drug combinations and sequential treatment phases; R code provided for direct implementation.
- **Limitations**: Motivated by alcohol-associated hepatitis; direct applicability to cancer trials not yet demonstrated.
- **Conclusion**: BIT framework is broadly applicable to complex diseases requiring sequentially delivered integrated therapies.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70684 / PMID:42530482

## 3. Generalizing Findings in Nested Cohorts — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-05-25
- **Authors**: Naimi AI et al.
- **Aims**: Demonstrate proof-of-concept for generalizing associations from a nested cohort subset back to the original target population.
- **Method**: Inverse probability of selection weighting; nuMoM2b-HHS nested subset (N=4,486) generalised to nuMoM2b target cohort (N=9,920).
- **Key findings**: Obesity–preeclampsia RR: 2.0 (1.7–2.3) target, 2.2 (1.8–2.6) source, 2.0 (1.6–2.4) weighted back to target; estimates consistent.
- **Strengths**: Real-data proof of concept with transparent selection-weighting and explicit assumption discussion.
- **Limitations**: Single association; pregnancy cohort setting not directly cancer-epi; proof of concept only.
- **Conclusion**: Researchers should deploy selection-weighting methods when generalizing nested cohort findings to the target population.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1097/EDE.0000000000002012 / PMID:42525474

## 5. Bayesian Model Selection for Derived Responses — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-08
- **Authors**: Roy B et al.
- **Aims**: Adapt the WAIC model-selection criterion for derived non-linear response variables such as BMI.
- **Method**: Derived WAIC (D-WAIC) measuring out-of-sample predictive accuracy of derived quantities; compared bivariate growth model vs. spline BMI model.
- **Key findings**: D-WAIC enables direct comparison of models for original components vs. derived variable; fills gap where AIC/BIC/WAIC cannot compare across parameterisations.
- **Strengths**: Fills a methodological gap for joint vs. univariate derived-response model selection; simulation validated.
- **Limitations**: Illustrated only on BMI; not applied to cancer or survival outcomes.
- **Conclusion**: D-WAIC expands Bayesian model comparison to non-linear derived response variables.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70682 / PMID:42522120
