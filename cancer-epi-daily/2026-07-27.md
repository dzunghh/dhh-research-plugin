## 1. Clarifying risk-factor associations with quantitative breast tumor features: a pooled analysis of 24 studies — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-24
- **Authors**: Adams D et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate independent, dose-response associations between breast cancer risk factors and quantitative tumor features (ER, PR, HER2, KI67, TP53, grade).
- **Method**: Pooled analysis of 24 studies (15,731 invasive breast cancers); IHC on tissue microarrays; Bayesian False Discovery Probability threshold <0.2.
- **Key findings**: Nulliparity and later age at menopause associated with higher ER-positivity; current MHT use associated with lower grade (OR[grade1]=3.37, 95%CI 2.69–4.21 vs. never users). BMI associations differed by menopausal status.
- **Strengths**: Largest pooled case-level analysis of tumor markers to date; dose-response design; multi-continental consortia.
- **Limitations**: IHC assay variability across labs; HER2/KI67/TP53 associations did not meet evidence threshold.
- **Conclusion**: Reproductive and hormonal factors have independent, dose-response associations with ER, PR, and grade, clarifying the etiologic heterogeneity of breast cancer.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag244 / PMID:42498670

## 2. Colorectal cancer screening participation in childhood cancer survivors: a report from the French Childhood Cancer Survivors Study (FCCSS) — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: International Journal of Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-06-24
- **Authors**: Ouanounou J et al.
- **Aims**: Investigate CRC screening participation rates in childhood cancer survivors versus the general French population.
- **Method**: Retrospective cohort using the national health data system (SNDS) to identify fecal immunochemical tests in FCCSS; indirect standardization; multivariable logistic regression across three 2-year periods (2016–2021).
- **Key findings**: Participation was 20–23% across periods, substantially below the general population (SIR=0.62–0.72); higher medical expenses associated with lower participation (p<0.01).
- **Strengths**: National registry linkage, three temporal snapshots, large survivor cohort with known treatment history.
- **Limitations**: FIT-based screening only; no colonoscopy data; French health system may limit generalizability.
- **Conclusion**: Despite elevated CRC risk, childhood cancer survivors remain markedly underscreened; risk-adapted surveillance guidelines and educational initiatives are urgently needed.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/ije/dyag123 / PMID:42498270

## 3. Quantifying and adjusting for selection biases in the Norwegian Mother, Father and Child Cohort Study using population-wide individual-level registry information — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: International Journal of Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-06-24
- **Authors**: Rayner C et al.
- **Aims**: Quantify and correct for selection bias in the Norwegian MoBa cohort by comparing to population registers.
- **Method**: Inverse probability weighting (elastic-net regularized logistic regression) in 296,987 mothers; participation rate 29% at baseline, 12.5% at 8-year follow-up; three weighting strategies compared against registry-defined population.
- **Key findings**: Participation weights reduced bias on mean estimates by 93% and on exposure-outcome associations by 75%; attrition weights were less effective (50% on associations), especially for initial participation bias.
- **Strengths**: Population-level registry as ground truth; very large target population; novel head-to-head comparison of weighting approaches.
- **Limitations**: Norwegian-specific registry structure limits direct transferability; attrition weights insufficient when baseline sample is already unrepresentative.
- **Conclusion**: Participation weights substantially reduce selection bias, but attrition-only weighting inadequately corrects bias in observational cohorts with low baseline response.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/ije/dyag122 / PMID:42501752

## 4. Impact of diagnostic labels and management benefit-to-harms information for low-risk prostate pathology — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-24
- **Authors**: Bell KJL et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate whether alternative diagnostic labels and benefit-harm information shift treatment preferences away from overtreatment of low-risk prostate pathology.
- **Method**: Two parallel factorial (3×2) online randomized experiments; 1,340 males and 1,435 Australian females ≥50 years; three labels ("cancer", "neoplasm", "lesion") × two information levels; primary outcome: curative vs. conservative management preference.
- **Key findings**: "Neoplasm" + high benefit-harm information reduced curative-intent preference most (males: RD=−9.2%, 95%CI −15.1 to −3.3; females: RD=−12.6%, 95%CI −19.9 to −5.2).
- **Strengths**: RCT factorial design; large sample; both sexes; directly actionable communication intervention.
- **Limitations**: Hypothetical online experiment; Australian convenience sample; effects may differ in real clinical consultation.
- **Conclusion**: Using "neoplasm" with comprehensive benefit-harm information supports conservative management preferences and may mitigate overtreatment of Gleason Group 1 prostate pathology.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag248 / PMID:42496667

## 5. Izalontamab Brengitecan (Iza-Bren), a First-in-Class EGFR-HER3 Bispecific Antibody-Drug Conjugate in Extensive-Stage Small Cell Lung Cancer: Results From a Phase Ib Study — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Journal of Clinical Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-24
- **Authors**: Zhao Y et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate efficacy and safety of iza-bren (BL-B01D1) in relapsed extensive-stage small cell lung cancer.
- **Method**: Open-label, multicenter, dose-expansion phase Ib (NCT05194982); 52 patients; iza-bren 2.5 mg/kg days 1 and 8 per 3-week cycle; primary endpoints: ORR and safety.
- **Key findings**: ORR 48.1% (95%CI 34.0–62.4), mPFS 4.1 months, mOS 12.2 months overall; in 2nd-line (n=22) ORR 72.7%, mOS 15.0 months. Main toxicities were hematologic.
- **Strengths**: First-in-class bispecific ADC; strong ORR in a notoriously chemo-resistant tumor; phase III ongoing.
- **Limitations**: Non-randomized single-arm phase Ib; n=52; no comparative arm; primarily Chinese centers.
- **Conclusion**: Iza-bren demonstrates promising antitumor activity in relapsed ES-SCLC, particularly as second-line treatment; confirmatory phase III (NCT06500026) underway.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1200/JCO-26-00243 / PMID:42497381
