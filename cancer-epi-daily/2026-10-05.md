## 1. Obesity and Breast Cancer Survival in Asian American vs White Women — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: J Natl Cancer Inst
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-01
- **Authors**: John EM et al.
- **Aims**: Determine whether lower obesity in Asian American women explains their better survival after breast cancer vs non-Latina White women.
- **Method**: Prospective cohort (ARC Cohort, n=8,897); BMI, waist circumference, WHR, WHtR; multivariable Cox regression stratified by race/ethnicity.
- **Key findings**: Higher BMI raised all-cause mortality in both groups similarly (HR~1.1 per 5 kg/m²). After BMI adjustment, Asian American survival advantage persisted (HR=0.88, 95% CI=0.78–0.997).
- **Strengths**: Largest multiethnic breast cancer cohort studying multiple adiposity measures simultaneously.
- **Limitations**: Limited power for some subgroup analyses; adiposity measured at single time point.
- **Conclusion**: Differences in obesity do not explain the breast cancer survival advantage observed in Asian American women.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag348 / PMID:42826494

## 2. HPV Clearance as the Key Marker for CIN2 Regression and CIN3 Risk — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: J Natl Cancer Inst
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-01
- **Authors**: Cheung LC et al.
- **Aims**: Estimate prognostic value of histologic, cytologic, and virologic regression markers for subsequent CIN3+ risk after CIN2 diagnosis.
- **Method**: Electronic records from 5,938 women with CIN2 managed with delayed treatment at Kaiser Permanente Northern California; 3-year CIN3+ risk estimated.
- **Key findings**: HPV-negative results conferred lower subsequent CIN3+ risk than normal histology (2.8% vs 7.0%; p<0.001); combined HPV-negative + normal cytology: 1.3%.
- **Strengths**: Large real-world cohort with comprehensive multi-modal regression markers and HPV genotyping.
- **Limitations**: Single health system limits generalisability; no randomisation.
- **Conclusion**: HPV clearance is more informative than histological or cytological regression for guiding CIN2 surveillance and therapeutic trial endpoints.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag345 / PMID:42826514

## 3. Multi-Cancer Early Detection Blood Test: Methylation + Protein Classifier Performance — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiol Biomarkers Prev
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-02
- **Authors**: Gainullin VG et al.
- **Aims**: Assess clinical validity of a methylation and protein (MP) multi-cancer early detection blood test across 21 cancer types.
- **Method**: Large multi-centre prospective case-control study; 6,352 samples (1,438 cancer, 4,914 non-cancer); 21 tumour types; training/test split; 98.5% specificity threshold.
- **Key findings**: Overall sensitivity 50.9%; stage-stratified 15.4% (I), 38.0% (II), 67.8% (III), 85.5% (IV); excluding breast and prostate: 56.8% overall.
- **Strengths**: Prospectively collected multi-centre design; broad tumour type coverage including low-survival cancers lacking screening tests.
- **Limitations**: Industry-funded; low stage I sensitivity limits early detection utility; breast and prostate notably excluded from headline sensitivity.
- **Conclusion**: The MP classifier detects all 21 solid cancer types with clinically meaningful specificity, supporting further validation in screening-naive populations.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-0541 / PMID:42826075

## 4. AI Algorithm Quantifies Progressive Muscle Wasting from Prostate Cancer Systemic Therapies — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: J Natl Cancer Inst
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-01
- **Authors**: Shi C et al.
- **Aims**: Characterise body composition changes from advanced prostate cancer systemic therapies using an automated AI-based PET-CT algorithm.
- **Method**: Longitudinal analysis; 2,342 serial PET-CTs from 468 patients; AI quantified muscle density; linear mixed-effects models by treatment type.
- **Key findings**: ADT reduced muscle density by 9%; ARPIs added 6%; Lutetium-177 added further 6%—demonstrating cumulative muscle wasting across therapy lines.
- **Strengths**: Large longitudinal study with automated body composition quantification from routine clinical imaging.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective single-centre design; no patient-reported functional outcomes; causality not established.
- **Conclusion**: An automated PET-CT algorithm reveals progressive, treatment-driven sarcopenia that could guide early intervention in advanced prostate cancer.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag349 / PMID:42826502

## 5. Pre-diagnostic 27-Hydroxycholesterol and Sex Hormones Predict Mortality in Multiethnic Breast Cancer Survivors — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiol Biomarkers Prev
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-02
- **Authors**: Sangaramoorthy M et al.
- **Aims**: Examine associations of pre-diagnostic plasma 27HC, estrone, testosterone, and SHBG with mortality in multiethnic postmenopausal breast cancer survivors.
- **Method**: Prospective cohort; 1,009 postmenopausal women from the Multiethnic Cohort (1993–2017); plasma biomarkers measured ~6.7 years before diagnosis; Cox regression.
- **Key findings**: Highest vs lowest tertile 27HC inversely associated with all-cause mortality (HR=0.73); SHBG positively associated (HR=1.76); estrone inversely associated with BC-specific mortality (HR=0.37).
- **Strengths**: Racially/ethnically diverse cohort with pre-diagnostic biomarker data; first study of 27HC in this context.
- **Limitations**: Small sample for subgroup analyses by race/ethnicity or ER status; single pre-diagnostic blood draw.
- **Conclusion**: Pre-diagnostic 27HC, estrone, and SHBG are associated with mortality, highlighting cholesterol-hormone crosstalk as a prognostic pathway in breast cancer survivors.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-0180 / PMID:42826074

## Appraisal notes

Across all five papers, prospective cohort designs in diverse populations and data-adaptive methods are displacing single-biomarker, single-population studies.
