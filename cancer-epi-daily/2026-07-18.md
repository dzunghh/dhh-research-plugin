## 1. Cancer Type-Specific Contributions to the Reduction in Cancer Mortality Gap Between Black Individuals and White Individuals — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-16
- **Authors**: Jiang C et al.
- **Aims**: Examine cancer-type-specific contributions to the 30-year reduction in all-cancer excess mortality among Black vs White individuals in the US.
- **Method**: Cross-sectional temporal analysis of US national death certificate data (NCHS) comparing 1991–1995 vs 2019–2023 cancer deaths (~5 million).
- **Key findings**: Excess mortality rate in Black males fell 98.4/100k (driven by lung 31%, prostate 26%, esophageal 12%); in Black females 21.7/100k (colorectal 19%, cervical 17%, lung 15%). Breast cancer remains largest contemporary disparity for Black females (7.2/100k excess).
- **Strengths**: National data spanning 30 years with 5M+ cancer deaths; comprehensive cancer-type disaggregation by sex.
- **Limitations**: Death certificate data precludes adjustment for socioeconomic status or healthcare access.
- **Conclusion**: Meaningful progress across several cancers, but persistent breast and uterine corpus disparities require targeted equitable interventions.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamaoncol.2026.2400 / PMID:42461622

## 2. Progression-Free Survival in Metastatic Breast Cancer by Local Investigators vs Blinded Independent Central Review: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-16
- **Authors**: Ravani LV et al.
- **Aims**: Quantify discordance between investigator- and BICR-assessed PFS in HR+/HER2− metastatic breast cancer trials.
- **Method**: Systematic review and trial-level random-effects meta-analysis of 21 Phase 2/3 RCTs (n=9,165) in HR+/HER2− mBC post-CDK4/6 inhibitor.
- **Key findings**: No significant difference in PFS estimates (discrepancy index 1.02; 95% CI 0.95–1.10; P=0.57); absolute PFS difference in control arms only 0.44 months (P=0.01).
- **Strengths**: Comprehensive search through November 2025; 21 RCTs; quantifies a widely debated trialmethodology question with robust data.
- **Limitations**: Trial-level meta-analysis without individual patient data; 13 open-label trials introduce potential performance bias.
- **Conclusion**: Investigator and BICR PFS assessments yield comparable estimates, questioning the routine necessity of BICR in HR+/HER2− mBC trials.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamaoncol.2026.1571 / PMID:42461660

## 3. First-in-Human Testing of a Mutant KRAS Vaccine for Pancreatic Cancer Interception in High-Risk Cohorts — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Discovery
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-16
- **Authors**: Haldar SD et al.
- **Aims**: Assess safety, immunogenicity, and T-cell persistence of a 6-mutation KRAS peptide vaccine (mKRAS-VAX) in hereditary PDAC-predisposed individuals.
- **Method**: Phase I first-in-human trial (n=20 high-risk individuals with pancreatic anomaly); TCR sequencing for clonotype persistence tracking; median follow-up 16.5 months (NCT05013216).
- **Key findings**: 18/20 (90%) mounted mKRAS-specific T-cell responses; vaccine-induced clonotypes persisted up to 2 years; all AEs grade 1–2; zero PDAC cases over follow-up.
- **Strengths**: First human evidence of durable KRAS-specific T-cell immunity for cancer interception; novel prevention paradigm targeting precursor-stage disease.
- **Limitations**: Very small n=20 single-arm study; follow-up insufficient to assess PDAC incidence reduction.
- **Conclusion**: mKRAS-VAX is safe and generates durable T-cell immunity, supporting advancement of mutant KRAS vaccination for pancreatic cancer interception.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/2159-8290.CD-25-2245 / PMID:42458705

## 4. Development and External Validation of a Transcriptome-Based Multivariable Prediction Model for Treatment-Free Remission in Chronic Myeloid Leukemia — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Journal of Clinical Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-06-09
- **Authors**: Alcazer V et al.
- **Aims**: Develop and externally validate a blood transcriptomic signature predicting 2-year treatment-free remission in CML at TKI discontinuation.
- **Method**: DESeq2-based machine learning on multicenter STIM2 trial (n=96 training); external validation in independent real-world cohort (n=70); TCR/IgH repertoire analyses.
- **Key findings**: 50-gene signature achieved AUROC 0.83 (training) and 0.71 (external validation); high-TFR group enriched for myeloid immune cells and Hedgehog signaling.
- **Strengths**: External validation in independent real-world cohort; mechanistic biological insights alongside predictive performance.
- **Limitations**: Moderate sample size (n=166 total); model may not generalise beyond imatinib-treated patients.
- **Conclusion**: Transcriptomic profiling at TKI discontinuation can predict TFR and identifies biological pathways underlying sustained remission.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1200/JCO-25-02948 / PMID:42454992

## 5. PAPOLA-Mediated Hyperactive Polyadenylation Promotes Leukemogenesis and Leukemia Stem Cell Self-Renewal Through Metabolic Reprogramming — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Nature Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-15
- **Authors**: Guo S et al.
- **Aims**: Characterise the oncogenic role of poly(A) polymerase alpha (PAPOLA) in acute myeloid leukemia progression and stem cell maintenance.
- **Method**: Multi-model study integrating primary AML patient samples, leukemia cell lines, and mouse models; pharmacological inhibition with cordycepin; PAPOLA-GSTM2-HNE-DLD axis mechanistic dissection.
- **Key findings**: PAPOLA is upregulated in AML and associates with poor clinical outcomes; cordycepin suppressed metabolic reprogramming and leukemogenesis in vivo; GSTM2-HNE-DLD identified as druggable metabolic axis.
- **Strengths**: Multiple independent model systems; clinical patient outcome correlation; identifies novel, pharmacologically tractable target.
- **Limitations**: Mechanistic/pre-clinical study; no clinical trial data; murine models may not fully recapitulate human AML biology.
- **Conclusion**: Hyperactive polyadenylation is a core oncogenic mechanism in AML, with the PAPOLA-GSTM2-HNE-DLD axis as a promising therapeutic target.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s43018-026-01190-7 / PMID:42457944
