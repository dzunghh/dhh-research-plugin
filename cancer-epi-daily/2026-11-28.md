## 1. Randomized Phase II Clinical Trials in Rare Cancer Populations — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: J Clin Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-24
- **Authors**: Ballman KV et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate operating characteristics of single-arm vs. small randomized phase II designs for rare cancers with binary efficacy endpoints.
- **Method**: Simulation study (n≤100 patients); varied historical control uncertainty and systematic misspecification across single-arm and randomized designs.
- **Key findings**: Single-arm trials well-calibrated only when historical control is accurate and precise; misspecification inflated false-positive rates. Small randomized designs were largely insensitive to historical-control misspecification.
- **Strengths**: Rigorous simulation across multiple scenarios; illustrative sarcoma trial example grounded in real protocol.
- **Limitations**: Binary endpoints only; recruitment difficulties not modeled in simulations.
- **Conclusion**: Small randomized phase II designs should be considered when historical control values are uncertain in rare cancer settings.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1200/JCO-26-01504 / PMID:43077656

## 2. An Integrated Approach to Identify Multi-level Factors Associated with Aggressive Prostate Cancer and Poor Outcomes in Black/African American Men: the RESPOND Study — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiol Biomarkers Prev
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-24
- **Authors**: Hamilton AS et al.
- **Aims**: Describe design of RESPOND, integrating multilevel social and biological data to explain aggressive prostate cancer disparities in Black/African American men.
- **Method**: Prospective cohort (n=12,506 B/AA men) from population-based cancer registries in 7 states; linked cancer registry, survey, saliva (n=7,231), and tumor tissue (n=3,568) data with geospatial measures.
- **Key findings**: 12,506 participants recruited; multilevel data integrating germline/somatic variation with neighborhood stressors and social determinants collected.
- **Strengths**: Largest population-based cohort of B/AA men with PCa; integrates cancer registry, genomic, and geospatial data.
- **Limitations**: Volunteer recruitment component may introduce selection bias; outcome analyses require continued follow-up.
- **Conclusion**: RESPOND is a unique resource to identify biological and social drivers of aggressive prostate cancer and survival disparities in Black/AA men.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-0569 / PMID:43077106

## 3. Multicenter Randomized Phase II Trial of Maintenance Systemic Therapy Versus Local Consolidative Therapy Plus Maintenance Systemic Therapy for Limited Metastatic Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NRG-LU002) — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: J Clin Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-23
- **Authors**: Iyengar P et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate whether adding local consolidative therapy (LCT) to maintenance systemic therapy improves outcomes in oligometastatic NSCLC.
- **Method**: Multicenter randomized phase II trial (n=215, 68 sites); 1:2 randomization MST alone vs. LCT+MST; primary endpoint progression-free survival.
- **Key findings**: No PFS improvement with LCT addition (HR 0.93, P=.66); grade ≥3 pneumonitis significantly higher with LCT+MST (10% vs. 1%, P=.02).
- **Strengths**: Contemporary IO-based therapy background; large multicenter design across 68 sites; prespecified stratification by histology and IO use.
- **Limitations**: Phase II; heterogeneous oligometastatic definitions; LCT-associated toxicity may have confounded MST delivery.
- **Conclusion**: Adding LCT to IO-based MST did not improve survival in oligometastatic NSCLC; toxicity warrants careful patient selection.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1200/JCO-25-02395 / PMID:43071915

## 4. EPIK-B3: A phase 3 randomized trial of alpelisib and nab-paclitaxel in patients with advanced triple-negative breast cancer with PIK3CA mutation or PTEN loss — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Clin Cancer Res
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-24
- **Authors**: Sharma P et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate alpelisib plus nab-paclitaxel in advanced TNBC with PIK3CA mutation (Part A) or PTEN loss (Part B1).
- **Method**: Phase 3 randomized placebo-controlled trial Part A (n=102) and single-arm Part B1 (n=35); primary endpoints PFS and ORR; enrollment halted early due to slow recruitment.
- **Key findings**: No PFS benefit with alpelisib in PIK3CA-mutant TNBC (HR 1.49; 95% CI 0.92–2.41); ORR 14.3% in PTEN-loss cohort; baseline ctDNA fraction was prognostic.
- **Strengths**: International design; rigorous biomarker assessment; ctDNA fraction identified as prognostic regardless of treatment arm.
- **Limitations**: Part A halted early with incomplete accrual, limiting primary endpoint interpretation; hyperglycemia impacted alpelisib tolerability.
- **Conclusion**: Alpelisib plus nab-paclitaxel showed no PFS benefit in advanced PIK3CA-mutant TNBC; PI3K pathway targeting remains challenging in this subtype.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-26-1388 / PMID:43077437

## 5. Occupational night shift exposure and the risk of testicular germ cell tumours in adults: a French case-control study — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiol Biomarkers Prev
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-24
- **Authors**: Onyije FM et al.
- **Aims**: Assess whether occupational night shift work increases risk of testicular germ cell tumours in France.
- **Method**: French nationwide prospective case-control study (TESTIS, 2015–2018); 454 cases, 670 controls; conditional logistic regression for shift-work exposure metrics.
- **Key findings**: No association between night shift work and TGCT overall (OR 1.01, 95% CI 0.78–1.31); no dose-response with hours or duration of employment.
- **Strengths**: First study evaluating circadian disruption as a TGCT risk factor; multicenter design across 20 university hospitals; detailed occupational questionnaire.
- **Limitations**: Self-reported exposure history susceptible to recall bias; limited statistical power for subgroup analyses.
- **Conclusion**: Night shift work is not associated with increased TGCT risk; circadian disruption unlikely a major etiologic factor for this cancer.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-0263 / PMID:43077081
