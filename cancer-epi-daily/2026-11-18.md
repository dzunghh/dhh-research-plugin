## 1. Epigenetic heterogeneity in breast tumours informs diagnosis and prognosis — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Nature
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-11
- **Authors**: Furth N et al.
- **Aims**: Profile epigenetic intratumour heterogeneity in luminal breast cancer to identify signatures linked to diagnosis and prognosis.
- **Method**: Single-cell mass-cytometry epigenetics (CyTOF) on treatment-naive luminal tumours; validated via whole-genome DNA methylation deconvolution and cell-free nucleosome profiling in blood.
- **Key findings**: A recurrent basal-like subpopulation with an oncogenic epigenetic signature exists within luminal tumours. The epigenetic basal signature in blood predicts disease relapse risk.
- **Strengths**: Multi-modal single-cell and liquid biopsy validation across independent patient cohorts with direct clinical translational potential.
- **Limitations**: Cross-sectional design for the liquid biopsy cohort; relapse-prediction requires prospective validation.
- **Conclusion**: Epigenetic liquid biopsy can identify luminal breast cancer patients at high relapse risk, enabling early clinical intervention.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41586-026-11230-y / PMID:43015472

## 2. Suppression of epithelial proliferation and tumorigenesis by IgA — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Nature
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-11
- **Authors**: Donaldson GP et al.
- **Aims**: Determine whether IgA deficiency increases colorectal cancer susceptibility and identify the responsible epithelial mechanism.
- **Method**: Genetically controlled IgA-deficient mouse models across multiple endogenous colorectal cancer systems; CRISPR screening and co-immunoprecipitation mass spectrometry to identify IgA receptor DMBT1.
- **Key findings**: IgA deficiency increases colonic epithelial proliferation and tumour susceptibility; oral IgA treatment suppressed proliferation regardless of antigen-binding specificity via the DMBT1 receptor.
- **Strengths**: Multiple independent genetic models, reductionist culture systems, and in vivo rescue experiments providing strong mechanistic evidence.
- **Limitations**: Mouse models; human epidemiological validation of IgA deficiency as a colorectal cancer risk factor not provided.
- **Conclusion**: IgA and DMBT1 form a homeostatic axis tempering epithelial responses to microbiota, with potential relevance to colorectal cancer prevention.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41586-026-11264-2 / PMID:43015465

## 3. Early vs Routine Oral Nutritional Supplementation During Concurrent Chemoradiotherapy for Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma: A Randomized Clinical Trial — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-12
- **Authors**: Su L et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate the efficacy and cost utility of early oral nutritional supplementation (ONS) in nasopharyngeal carcinoma patients undergoing concurrent chemoradiotherapy (CCRT).
- **Method**: Open-label multicenter RCT across 6 Chinese tertiary hospitals (2021–2025); 216 patients randomised 1:1 to early vs routine ONS; primary endpoint >5% weight loss.
- **Key findings**: Early ONS reduced >5% weight loss (65.4% vs 80.7%; RR 0.81, 95% CI 0.68–0.96). Grade ≥3 radiation-induced oral mucositis halved (14.0% vs 26.6%).
- **Strengths**: Multicenter RCT with intent-to-treat analysis, cost-utility evaluation, and clinically meaningful endpoints.
- **Limitations**: Open-label design; short-term cost-utility only; Chinese tertiary hospital setting may limit generalisability.
- **Conclusion**: Early ONS at CCRT initiation significantly reduces clinically meaningful weight loss and severe oral mucositis in nasopharyngeal carcinoma, with potentially favourable cost utility.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamaoncol.2026.4535 / PMID:43018988

## 4. Lynch syndrome — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Lancet
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-12
- **Authors**: Ryan NAJ et al.
- **Aims**: Provide an integrated review of Lynch syndrome biology, diagnosis, prevention, and treatment across the continuum of care.
- **Method**: Comprehensive narrative and systematic review of Lynch syndrome epidemiology, molecular diagnostics, surveillance, chemoprevention, and immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy.
- **Key findings**: Lynch syndrome affects ~1 in 350–450 individuals; universal tumour MMR testing transforms case identification. Immune checkpoint inhibitors yield substantial benefit in MMR-deficient tumours.
- **Strengths**: Authoritative Lancet review integrating epidemiology, genomics, prevention, and precision oncology with equity considerations.
- **Limitations**: Review design limits causal inference; rapid evolution of immunotherapy evidence may date quickly.
- **Conclusion**: Lynch syndrome represents a preventable public health burden requiring equitable universal tumour testing, gene-specific surveillance, and precision immunotherapy.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S0140-6736(26)01600-4 / PMID:43020098
