## 1. Performance and safety of a multi-cancer early detection test: the PATHFINDER 2 study — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Nature Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-22
- **Authors**: Nabavizadeh N et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate clinical performance metrics and safety of a blood-based multi-cancer early detection (MCED) test in adults aged ≥50 without clinical cancer suspicion.
- **Method**: Prospective interventional study (n=35,878); co-primary endpoints of detection rate, PPV/NPV, sensitivity, specificity, cancer signal origin accuracy, and invasive-procedure safety over 12 months.
- **Key findings**: Cancer detection rate 0.54% (95% CI 0.47–0.63%); episode sensitivity 39.3% overall and 69.8% for a prespecified 12-cancer subgroup; CSO prediction accuracy 91.3%.
- **Strengths**: Largest prospective MCED interventional cohort to date; comprehensive safety evaluation with 12-month follow-up.
- **Limitations**: No randomisation or survival endpoints; 12-month follow-up insufficient to assess clinical utility.
- **Conclusion**: MCED test shows high specificity and CSO accuracy in an intended-use population; randomised trials with mortality endpoints are needed.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41591-026-04618-w / PMID:42773207

## 2. Childhood socioeconomic circumstances and adult mortality: a systematic review and meta-analysis — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Public Health
- **Pub date**: 2026-10 (EDAT 2026-09-22)
- **Authors**: Broadbent P et al.
- **Aims**: Synthesise evidence on associations between childhood socioeconomic conditions and adult all-cause and cause-specific mortality including cancer.
- **Method**: Systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis of 102 cohort studies (~8.5 million participants, 24 countries); ROBINS-E risk-of-bias; GRADE certainty; relative index of inequality computed.
- **Key findings**: Father's occupation disadvantage associated with all-cause mortality HR 1.26 (95% CI 1.18–1.35); lung cancer mortality HR 1.52; respiratory mortality HR 1.70–1.85.
- **Strengths**: PROSPERO-registered; largest synthesis to date; formal GRADE certainty assessment; standardised RII for cross-exposure comparison.
- **Limitations**: 50% of studies at high/very high risk of bias due to inadequate confounding control; certainty rated very low to moderate.
- **Conclusion**: Disadvantaged childhood socioeconomic circumstances increase adult mortality across diverse exposure types, supporting early-life intervention prioritisation.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S2468-2667(26)00187-8 / PMID:42772297

## 3. Lifestyle and chemotherapy shape functional aging trajectories in older breast cancer survivors and matched comparators — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-23
- **Authors**: Cespedes Feliciano EM et al.
- **Aims**: Quantify long-term functional trajectories in older breast cancer survivors versus matched cancer-free women, and assess effect modification by treatment and prediagnosis lifestyle.
- **Method**: Women's Health Initiative cohort (n=45,530; median age 72 years); repeated SF-36 physical function and grip strength/walking pace over 15 years; weighted regression with age-matched comparators.
- **Key findings**: Regional breast cancer associated with SF-36 deficit of −5.3 at year 10; chemotherapy recipients showed steepest early decline (−7.1 at year 1); lower-risk lifestyle predicted greater functional recovery.
- **Strengths**: Very large well-characterised cohort, 15-year follow-up, multiple functional outcomes, lifestyle effect modification rigorously tested.
- **Limitations**: Single cohort (WHI), predominantly white older women; treatment data self-reported.
- **Conclusion**: Breast cancer—especially chemotherapy—causes durable functional deficits; prediagnosis healthy lifestyle is a modifiable target for improving survivor outcomes.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag292 / PMID:42772761

## 4. Inflammatory biomarkers and risk of postmenopausal oestrogen receptor-positive breast cancer: a case-cohort analysis — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: British Journal of Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-19
- **Authors**: Albers FE et al.
- **Aims**: Estimate effects of circulating inflammatory biomarkers on postmenopausal ER-positive breast cancer risk.
- **Method**: Case-cohort study within the Melbourne Collaborative Cohort Study (1,223 females, 347 cases); weighted Poisson regression with robust variance estimator for risk ratios per doubling and by quartile.
- **Key findings**: Breast cancer risk increased per doubling in leptin (RR 1.13, 95% CI 1.03–1.25) and IL-10 (RR 1.14, 95% CI 1.01–1.29); CRP, IL-6, and IL-8 showed no significant associations.
- **Strengths**: Prospective case-cohort design within established cohort; multiple inflammatory markers measured simultaneously; appropriate weighted analysis.
- **Limitations**: Single cohort; moderate case numbers limit precision; residual confounding by adiposity possible.
- **Conclusion**: Leptin and IL-10 are associated with postmenopausal ER-positive breast cancer risk, supporting a role for systemic inflammation in breast carcinogenesis.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41416-026-03624-6 / PMID:42763311

## 5. Projected Cancer Burden among Sexual and Gender Minorities by Generation in the United States — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiology, Biomarkers & Prevention
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-22
- **Authors**: Xu CW et al.
- **Aims**: Estimate future cancer burden in sexual and gender minorities (SGM) across US generations through 2040–2044.
- **Method**: Nordpred age-period-cohort models applied to SEER-12 incidence data for breast, prostate, colorectal, lung, and melanoma; SGM population proportions from Gallup 2023–2024 surveys; Prophet sensitivity analysis.
- **Key findings**: At ages 55–59, five-year cancer burden projected 15× higher among Millennial vs Silent Generation SGMs (77,521 vs 5,134 cases); Millennials projected to account for 68.4% of SGM breast cancer cases by 2040–2044.
- **Strengths**: Multi-cancer APC projection with validated SEER database; sensitivity analysis confirms robustness; fills a major surveillance gap for an understudied population.
- **Limitations**: US-specific; SGM proportions from cross-sectional survey; Nordpred model assumptions may not capture recent trend acceleration.
- **Conclusion**: Substantial generational shift in SGM cancer burden projected, demanding oncology workforce and care-delivery preparation.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-0504 / PMID:42770946

## Appraisal notes

This week's digest is anchored by two landmark studies: PATHFINDER 2 (Nat Med, n=35,878) provides the most rigorous prospective evidence yet on blood-based multi-cancer early detection, while a PROSPERO-registered meta-analysis in Lancet Public Health quantifies childhood socioeconomic deprivation as a mortality risk factor including lung cancer. Methodologically, the week reflects a continued push towards population-level risk stratification—from cohort-based inflammatory biomarker profiling and large survivorship trajectories (WHI, 15 years) to APC projection modelling for under-characterised populations, together illustrating the field's broadening lens from aetiology to long-term survivorship and health equity.
