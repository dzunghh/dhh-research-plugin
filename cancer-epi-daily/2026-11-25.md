## 1. Prostate and Pelvic Nodal SBRT vs Prostate-Only SBRT for High-Risk Prostate Cancer: PACE-NODES Phase III RCT — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-20
- **Authors**: Pathmanathan A et al.
- **Aims**: Assess safety of pelvic nodal SBRT (PPN-SBRT) versus prostate-only SBRT (P-SBRT) for high-risk localised prostate cancer.
- **Method**: Phase 3 open-label RCT at 47 hospitals (UK, Ireland, NZ); n=1,066 evaluable; preplanned acute safety analysis.
- **Key findings**: PPN-SBRT increased grade ≥2 GI events within 12 weeks (28.0% vs 21.3%, OR 1.44, p=0.011); no significant difference in GU events (35.8% vs 40.2%).
- **Strengths**: Large international phase III RCT with patient-reported outcomes; toxicity resolved rapidly with no treatment-related deaths.
- **Limitations**: Primary endpoint (biochemical/clinical failure) not yet mature; efficacy data pending.
- **Conclusion**: PPN-SBRT is deliverable with acceptable acute toxicity; cancer control outcomes awaited for practice change.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00465-1 / PMID:43063962

## 2. Less Versus More Intensive Follow-up of Small Pulmonary Nodules: Cluster Randomized Pragmatic Trial — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Ann Intern Med
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-24
- **Authors**: Gould MK et al.
- **Aims**: Determine whether less intensive pulmonary nodule follow-up (current guidelines) is noninferior to more intensive strategies for small nodules ≤15 mm.
- **Method**: Unblinded cluster RCT, 24 clusters, n=34,686; mixed-effects logistic regression; noninferiority margin 5 percentage points.
- **Key findings**: 36.3% vs 27.7% of lung cancers exceeded 20 mm at diagnosis in less vs more intensive arms (7 percentage-point difference exceeding noninferiority margin, though non-significant).
- **Strengths**: Large pragmatic trial across diverse US health systems; real-world implementation of guideline-level protocols.
- **Limitations**: Suboptimal adherence, limited statistical power, residual confounding from cluster design.
- **Conclusion**: Less intensive follow-up does not meet noninferiority; current guidelines may allow preventable cancer progression.
- **ID**: DOI:10.7326/ANNALS-25-05646 / PMID:43071962

## 3. Progress in Reducing the Black-White Mortality Gap from 1993 to 2023 in the United States — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Ann Intern Med
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-24
- **Authors**: Sung H et al.
- **Aims**: Quantify cause-specific contributions to changes in excess Black vs White mortality rates over 30 years.
- **Method**: Cross-sectional temporal analysis of the National Mortality Database; age-standardised mortality rate differences for 24 causes.
- **Key findings**: All-cause excess mortality rate fell 56% in males and 68% in females; cancer reductions drove 38.2% of gains in males but maternal mortality worsened.
- **Strengths**: 30-year national data; granular decomposition by cause, sex, and age; American Cancer Society surveillance expertise.
- **Limitations**: Compares only 1993 and 2023 endpoints; peak EMR years vary by cause, affecting attribution.
- **Conclusion**: Major progress in Black-White cancer and HIV mortality disparities; persistent racial gaps in maternal death and diabetes require targeted action.
- **ID**: DOI:10.7326/ANNALS-26-01235 / PMID:43071964

## 4. Transcription Factor Subtype Governs Response and Resistance to DLL3-Directed T Cell Engagement in Small Cell Lung Cancer — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Cell
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-23
- **Authors**: Vasseur D et al.
- **Aims**: Determine whether SCLC transcription factor subtypes (ASCL1, NEUROD1, POU2F3) predict tarlatamab response and resistance.
- **Method**: cfChIP-seq liquid biopsy of circulating chromatin in patient plasma plus syngeneic immunocompetent mouse model of tarlatamab response.
- **Key findings**: ASCL1 tumors enriched for tarlatamab response; POU2F3 intrinsically resistant; acquired resistance via NEUROD1-high state with DLL3 downregulation.
- **Strengths**: Novel cfChIP-seq liquid biopsy approach enables non-invasive subtype inference; both clinical and preclinical validation.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective patient cohort; limited clinical sample size; causal direction of NEUROD1 shift not established.
- **Conclusion**: SCLC TF subtypes are clinically relevant predictive biomarkers for tarlatamab, supporting subtype-guided treatment strategies.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.ccell.2026.10.015 / PMID:43072366

## 5. Clinical Acquired Resistance to Mutant-Selective PI3Kα Inhibition in HR+ Metastatic Breast Cancer — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Discov
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-23
- **Authors**: Varkaris A et al.
- **Aims**: Characterize genomic landscape of acquired resistance to allosteric mutant-selective PI3Kα inhibitors in PIK3CA-mutant HR+ metastatic breast cancer.
- **Method**: Serial circulating tumor DNA analysis in 32 patients treated with zovegalisib or tersolisib; structural modelling and in vitro/in vivo functional assays.
- **Key findings**: ~70% acquired PI3K pathway alterations; secondary PIK3CA mutations (40%) clustered in allosteric binding pocket; orthosteric+allosteric PI3Kα combination showed striking synergy.
- **Strengths**: First clinical ctDNA characterisation of allosteric PI3Kα resistance; novel structural insights distinguish it from orthosteric resistance; in vivo validation.
- **Limitations**: Small cohort (n=32); predominantly observational; functional assays not exhaustive of all resistance mechanisms.
- **Conclusion**: Secondary PIK3CA allosteric pocket mutations are the dominant resistance mechanism; orthosteric/allosteric combination is a rational next-line strategy.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/2159-8290.CD-26-0390 / PMID:43070795
