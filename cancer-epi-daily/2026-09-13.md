## 1. Outbreak of Severe Methemoglobinemia during Maritime Migration — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: N Engl J Med
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-10
- **Authors**: Bernasconi L et al.
- **Aims**: Identify the cause of recurrent severe acquired methemoglobinemia clusters in maritime migrants arriving in Italy.
- **Method**: Coordinated clinical and toxicologic outbreak investigation; biologic samples from 39 patients and fuel from one vessel analyzed for methemoglobin-inducing agents.
- **Key findings**: 82 cases identified (March 2024–December 2025); median methemoglobin 44.6%. N-methylaniline (gasoline additive) found in 95% of tested patients and in vessel fuel at 1.1% concentration.
- **Strengths**: Toxicologic confirmation in near-all tested cases; systematic multi-site case ascertainment across Italian reception facilities.
- **Limitations**: Single country, specific migration route; not generalizable beyond fuel-contamination exposure contexts.
- **Conclusion**: Dermal N-methylaniline absorption from fuel-contaminated seawater is the likely cause; methylene blue availability at disembarkation sites is critical.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1056/NEJMoa2606487 / PMID:42715562

## 2. Sunlight Exposure and Breast Cancer Risk: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiol Biomarkers Prev
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-10
- **Authors**: Petrelli F et al.
- **Aims**: Examine the association between sunlight exposure and breast cancer incidence and survival, stratified by hormone-receptor subtype.
- **Method**: Random-effects meta-analysis of 31 epidemiologic studies; dose-response analysis; subgroup analyses by ER/PR/HER2 subtype and study design.
- **Key findings**: Higher sunlight exposure associated with lower breast cancer incidence (pooled OR 0.80, 95% CI 0.74–0.86); protection confined to ER-negative (OR 0.71) and triple-negative (OR 0.55) tumours; ~8% lower risk per 10 relative exposure units.
- **Strengths**: First meta-analysis to demonstrate receptor-subtype specificity; dose-response analysis adds biological plausibility.
- **Limitations**: Observational evidence with confounding and exposure misclassification; ambient UV proxies weaker than personal exposure measures.
- **Conclusion**: Sunlight may preferentially reduce ER-negative and triple-negative breast cancer risk, supporting further study of light environment in breast cancer aetiology.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-0666 / PMID:42720261

## 3. Associations Between Pre-diagnostic Plasma Metabolites and Ovarian Cancer: A Prospective Case-Cohort Analysis in the Cancer Prevention Study-3 — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiol Biomarkers Prev
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-10
- **Authors**: Pfluger BA et al.
- **Aims**: Identify pre-diagnostic plasma metabolites associated with ovarian cancer risk using untargeted metabolomics.
- **Method**: Case-cohort study (127 ovarian cancer cases; 1,811-person sub-cohort); Prentice-weighted Cox models; FDR-corrected pathway enrichment analysis of 868 metabolites.
- **Key findings**: No individual metabolite reached FDR < 0.2 in the full cohort; in postmenopausal women, 5 metabolites remained significant; lipid-related sub-pathways (bile acids, sphingomyelins) showed strongest enrichment.
- **Strengths**: Prospective design with pre-diagnostic samples; rigorous FDR correction; largest untargeted metabolomics study of ovarian cancer to date.
- **Limitations**: Relatively short median follow-up (2.4 years in cases); single cohort without external replication.
- **Conclusion**: Broad metabolite screening did not identify individual risk markers, but lipid pathway dysregulation and menopausal-status heterogeneity warrant further investigation.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-0544 / PMID:42720260
