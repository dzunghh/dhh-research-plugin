## 1. Genomic ascertainment of PALB2-related cancer predisposition — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-17
- **Authors**: Stewart DR et al.
- **Aims**: Quantify PALB2-associated cancer risk, age-specific penetrance, and all-cause/cancer-specific mortality using population-based genomic (not familial) ascertainment.
- **Method**: Case-control study in two population-based cohorts (UK Biobank n=469,580; Geisinger MyCode n=167,050) with exome sequencing and linked registry data; Cox proportional hazards models.
- **Key findings**: PALB2 P/LP variant prevalence 1:571 (UK) to 1:940 (Geisinger); HR for any cancer 1.7–3.6; all-cause mortality HR 1.61–1.67 vs non-carriers.
- **Strengths**: Two independent, population-based cohorts with registry linkage eliminate familial ascertainment bias.
- **Limitations**: Risk estimates lower than familially ascertained cohorts; residual confounding by lifestyle factors possible.
- **Conclusion**: Population-based genomic PALB2 screening identifies substantially elevated cancer risk and mortality, informing unselected genomic screening programs.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag429 / PMID:43046605

## 2. Fine-tuning of polygenic score to local data improves risk prediction and clinical implementation — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Nature Communications
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-23 (EDAT indexed 2026-11-18)
- **Authors**: Kim MS et al.
- **Aims**: Develop a machine learning framework to recalibrate externally derived polygenic risk scores (PRS) to local biobank data for cancer risk prediction.
- **Method**: Post-PRS calibration integrating multiple published PRSs via ML optimization, validated in MGB Biobank for breast cancer, prostate cancer, and coronary artery disease.
- **Key findings**: C-statistic improvements of 9.4% (prostate cancer) and 8.3% (breast cancer); breast cancer case enrichment improved from 7.1% to 20.8% using the locally optimized PRS.
- **Strengths**: Practical framework requiring no new GWAS discovery; scalable to diverse health systems without complex upstream algorithms.
- **Limitations**: Validated in one target cohort (MGB Biobank); generalizability to non-European populations not fully assessed.
- **Conclusion**: Local PRS calibration is a scalable epi-methodology advance enabling equitable clinical implementation of genomic cancer risk stratification.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41467-026-77770-z / PMID:43058116

## 3. Expansion and cytotoxicity of mucosal CAR+ T cells in enterocolitis associated with BCMA CAR-T cell therapy — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Nature Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-19
- **Authors**: Sainz TP et al.
- **Aims**: Characterize the incidence, pathology, and clonal dynamics of immune effector cell enterocolitis (IEC-EC) following cilta-cel CAR-T therapy for multiple myeloma.
- **Method**: Retrospective cohort (158 cilta-cel patients, MD Anderson); vector integration analysis, flow cytometry, TCR sequencing, RNA-seq on mucosal biopsies; two-center comparison.
- **Key findings**: IEC-EC incidence 5.1–5.7%; peripheral CAR T cell persistence 100-fold higher in affected patients; distinct clonal lymphoproliferative disorder, not peripheral T cell lymphoma.
- **Strengths**: Comprehensive multi-omic characterization of a novel, clinically important CAR-T complication; two-center validation.
- **Limitations**: Single primary center; limited sample size (9 cases); causal mechanism not fully established.
- **Conclusion**: IEC-EC is a duodenum-predominant, clonal CAR+ T cell disorder distinct from PTCL, with implications for surveillance and management after CAR-T therapy.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41591-026-04731-w / PMID:43058221

## 4. Association of emotional distress with immune checkpoint inhibition efficacy in melanoma — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-19
- **Authors**: van Dijk EJ et al.
- **Aims**: Determine whether emotional distress (ED) at start of immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) treatment is independently associated with survival in melanoma patients.
- **Method**: Retrospective cohort (555 melanoma patients, 2015–2025), validated Utrecht Symptom Diary questionnaire, Cox regression adjusted for performance status and comorbidities.
- **Key findings**: ED associated with impaired OS (HRadj 2.10, 95% CI 1.20–3.68) in anti-PD-1 monotherapy; RFS impaired in adjuvant setting (HRadj 2.29, 95% CI 1.36–3.85); no association seen with ipilimumab+nivolumab.
- **Strengths**: Validated psychometric tool, large contemporary cohort, multiple treatment settings and lines analyzed.
- **Limitations**: Single-center retrospective design; residual confounding by unmeasured performance status components possible.
- **Conclusion**: Emotional distress independently predicts impaired survival with anti-PD-1 monotherapy, supporting psycho-oncological screening as a clinical priority.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag459 / PMID:43055356

## 5. The influence of pre-diagnostic allostatic load on mortality after incident colorectal cancer — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: American Journal of Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-19
- **Authors**: Walts Z et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate associations between pre-diagnostic allostatic load—a composite immune/metabolic/cardiovascular stress biomarker—and mortality after colorectal cancer diagnosis.
- **Method**: Prospective cohort (Southern Community Cohort Study, 2002–2009; n=303 CRC cases with biomarker data), National Death Index linkage through 2020, multivariable-adjusted Cox models.
- **Key findings**: High allostatic load associated with all-cause mortality (HR 1.81, 95% CI 1.25–2.62) and CRC-specific mortality (HR 1.81, 95% CI 1.07–3.07); metabolic syndrome not associated with either outcome.
- **Strengths**: Pre-diagnostic biomarker samples from a prospective cohort enriched for Black and low-income participants; demonstrates advantage of allostatic load over metabolic syndrome.
- **Limitations**: Small subsample for allostatic load analysis (n=303); CRC-specific mortality estimate attenuated after health behavior adjustment.
- **Conclusion**: Allostatic load is a clinically actionable biomarker that outperforms metabolic syndrome in identifying CRC patients at elevated post-diagnosis mortality risk.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/aje/kwag296 / PMID:43053744
