## 1. Chemoradiotherapy versus short-course radiotherapy for response-adapted organ preservation in early-stage and intermediate-stage rectal cancer (STAR-TREC): 12-month results of an international, multicentre, open-label, parallel-group, randomised, phase 2/3 trial — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-23
- **Authors**: Bach SP et al.
- **Aims**: To assess whether LCCRT or SCRT increases organ preservation and reduces total mesorectal excision without compromising oncological outcomes in early-stage rectal cancer.
- **Method**: International multi-centre phase 2/3 RCT with patient-preference design; 5 European countries, 37 sites, 409 patients; 12-month TME-free survival primary endpoint.
- **Key findings**: 12-month TME-free survival 78.5% (LCCRT) vs 60.6% (SCRT), HR 1.90 (95% CI 1.29–2.81); grade 3–4 GI toxicity 2% (LCCRT) vs 8% (primary TME).
- **Strengths**: Robust international RCT design with patient-preference element improving generalisability across heterogeneous populations.
- **Limitations**: 12-month results are interim; definitive 30-month primary endpoint and functional outcomes still pending.
- **Conclusion**: LCCRT achieves superior short-term organ preservation over SCRT and may reduce surgical toxicity; longer follow-up is needed.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00228-7 / PMID:42633754

## 2. Reproducibility of overall survival in metastatic colorectal cancer randomized trials using ARCAD-Derived external control arms — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: J Natl Cancer Inst
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-22
- **Authors**: Raeisi M et al.
- **Aims**: To evaluate feasibility and validity of synthetic control arms (SCAs) derived from the ARCAD historical mCRC database for OS benchmarking in RCTs.
- **Method**: Retrospective validation using propensity score matching against 7 landmark mCRC RCTs (n=28,022 patients); pre-specified two-step benchmarking framework across first- to third-line settings.
- **Key findings**: ARCAD-derived SCAs reproduced control-arm OS with median absolute HR deviation 0.05; no significant Z-test differences (all P>0.05) across treatment lines.
- **Strengths**: Individual patient-level data, multi-line validation, large sample, pre-specified framework demonstrates reliable methodological reproducibility.
- **Limitations**: Residual confounding from unobserved variables and missing data; historical cohort may not reflect current standard-of-care practices.
- **Conclusion**: ARCAD-derived SCAs reliably reproduce OS endpoints, supporting their use when conventional RCT control arms are impractical.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag291 / PMID:42633557

## 3. Benchmarking female breast cancer survival in Africa, Central and South America, and Asia (SURVCAN-3) — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: J Natl Cancer Inst
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-22
- **Authors**: Zahwe M et al.
- **Aims**: To benchmark breast cancer prognosis using conditional survival estimates in low- and middle-income country (LMIC) settings across three continents.
- **Method**: Population-based cohort; 197,766 females with breast cancer (2008–2012) from 53 registries across 22 countries; 3-year age-standardised net survival using period approach.
- **Key findings**: Conditional survival consistently exceeded survival at diagnosis across all countries; largest gains in Zimbabwe (+11 pp) and Mauritius (+7 pp); inter-country inequalities persisted.
- **Strengths**: Largest population-based conditional survival analysis for breast cancer in LMICs; standardised methods across 53 registries.
- **Limitations**: Registry completeness varies; no treatment data available; 2008–2012 data may underestimate current survival improvements.
- **Conclusion**: Conditional survival reveals meaningful prognosis improvement after surviving the first year, but persistent geographic inequalities highlight early-care infrastructure gaps.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag294 / PMID:42633567

## 4. Expanding the phenotype of hereditary renal cell carcinoma syndromes: Implications for surveillance and management — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: J Natl Cancer Inst
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-24
- **Authors**: Carlo MI et al.
- **Aims**: To refine cancer risk estimates and phenotypes of hereditary RCC syndromes using large unselected pan-cancer sequencing cohorts.
- **Method**: Retrospective analysis of 32,728 cancer patients (MSK-IMPACT tumor-normal sequencing); burden testing for 7 RCC syndrome genes; UK Biobank case-control validation.
- **Key findings**: Germline pathogenic variants identified in 3.6% of RCC cases; 61.5% of carriers met clinical criteria only; BAP1 associated with hepatobiliary cancers, FLCN with colorectal cancer, validated in UK Biobank.
- **Strengths**: Large unselected pan-cancer cohort with independent UK Biobank validation and integrated clinical, IHC, and genomic data.
- **Limitations**: MSK referral bias; UK Biobank validation limited by small numbers; FH K477dup and MITF E318K associations inconclusive.
- **Conclusion**: Routine tumor-normal sequencing expands hereditary RCC phenotypes beyond classical clinical criteria and supports broader surveillance.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag290 / PMID:42635527

## 5. Anal cancer burden among people with HIV across US states, DC, and major metro areas — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: J Natl Cancer Inst
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-22
- **Authors**: Deshmukh AA et al.
- **Aims**: To characterise geographic variation in anal squamous cell carcinoma burden attributable to HIV infection across US states, DC, and major MSAs.
- **Method**: Linkage study (HIV/AIDS Cancer Match Study + US cancer registries + CDC National HIV Surveillance); 50 states + DC + MSAs; 2010–2019; annual anal SCC estimates with uncertainty intervals.
- **Key findings**: 20.1% of male anal SCC cases occurred in people with HIV; California highest (29.2%); geographic variation substantial across MSAs; only 1.4% in females.
- **Strengths**: Linkage of three national surveillance databases covering all 50 states and major metro areas over a 10-year period.
- **Limitations**: HIV attribution subject to misclassification; does not capture HPV vaccination trends or evolving antiretroviral therapy coverage over the period.
- **Conclusion**: Substantial geographic variation in HIV-attributable anal cancer burden supports targeted screening outreach in high-burden states and metro areas.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag285 / PMID:42633606
