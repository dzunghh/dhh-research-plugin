## 1. Effectiveness of opportunistic bilateral salpingectomy in preventing epithelial ovarian cancer: a population-based study — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: J Natl Cancer Inst
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-29
- **Authors**: Bahrami F et al.
- **Aims**: To assess whether opportunistic bilateral salpingectomy (OBS) reduces epithelial ovarian cancer risk compared with ovarian- and tubal-conserving hysterectomy or tubal ligation.
- **Method**: Population-based retrospective cohort (Ontario, Canada, 2011–2023); Cox proportional hazards regression; sensitivity analyses varying exclusion windows for pre-existing serous tubal intraepithelial carcinomas.
- **Key findings**: Among 196,839 individuals, OBS was associated with reduced any ovarian carcinoma risk (aHR 0.60, 95% CI 0.36–0.98); serous ovarian carcinoma aHR 0.47 (95% CI 0.22–0.99). Sensitivity analyses were broadly consistent.
- **Strengths**: Large population-based cohort with administrative health data; multiple sensitivity analyses to address pre-existing lesions; clinically actionable primary prevention endpoint.
- **Limitations**: Missing grade data required combining high- and low-grade serous carcinoma; residual confounding possible; relatively small event count (n=142) limits precision.
- **Conclusion**: OBS is associated with meaningful reduction in ovarian carcinoma risk, supporting its use as a primary prevention strategy during indicated pelvic surgeries.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag251 / PMID:42528271

## 2. Advancing oncology education in low-income and middle-income African settings: global insights for local action — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-01
- **Authors**: Shrestha S et al.
- **Aims**: To review existing oncology education programmes in Africa and identify effective strategies to address workforce gaps in LMICs.
- **Method**: Narrative review of curricula, fellowship programmes, mentorship networks, and technology-driven educational models (e-learning, telemedicine, AI-driven platforms, virtual reality).
- **Key findings**: Persistent gaps include funding deficits, language/cultural barriers, and policy misalignment; scalable opportunities identified in AI-driven adaptive learning and interdisciplinary international collaborations tailored to local needs.
- **Strengths**: Broad multi-institutional authorship spanning Africa, North America, and UK; practical focus on scalable interventions for resource-constrained settings.
- **Limitations**: Narrative (not systematic) review; evidence base for specific interventions is largely descriptive; no quantitative synthesis of programme outcomes.
- **Conclusion**: Sustainable, locally tailored, interdisciplinary collaborations aligned with national health policy are key to closing oncology education gaps in African LMICs.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00242-1 / PMID:42508437
