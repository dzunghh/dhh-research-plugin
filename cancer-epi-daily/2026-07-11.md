## 1. Loss of the USP22 Deubiquitylase Confers Resistance to Chemotherapy in Small Cell Lung Cancer — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Nature Communications
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-08
- **Authors**: Best S et al.
- **Aims**: Identify genetic drivers of chemoresistance in SCLC using in vivo CRISPR screening.
- **Method**: CRISPR deletion screen in SCLC patient-derived xenograft (PDX) models; mechanistic validation with epigenomic and transcriptomic assays.
- **Key findings**: USP22 loss (SAGA complex deubiquitylase) confers cisplatin-etoposide resistance; GLUT1 inhibition re-sensitises USP22-null tumours to chemotherapy.
- **Strengths**: Multiple PDX models with mechanistic epigenomic validation; actionable therapeutic target identified.
- **Limitations**: Only two chemosensitive PDX models; human translational potential of GLUT1 inhibition not yet tested.
- **Conclusion**: USP22 loss drives SCLC chemoresistance via attenuated DNA-damage response and glycolysis upregulation, with GLUT1 as a targetable vulnerability.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41467-026-75117-2 / PMID:42414273

## 2. Prognostic RNA-Splicing Archetypes in Breast Cancer Identified by Extended Pre-Training of Histopathology Foundation Models — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Nature Communications
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-07
- **Authors**: Fournier L et al.
- **Aims**: Discover recurrent tumour archetypes with prognostic value from routine H&E slides using tumour-specialised AI.
- **Method**: Extended pre-training of histopathology foundation models on invasive tumour tissue; unsupervised archetype discovery validated on TCGA and multi-cancer cohorts.
- **Key findings**: RNA splicing-associated archetypes predict poorer outcomes in HER2-positive and triple-negative breast cancer; reproducible across heterogeneous epithelial cancers.
- **Strengths**: Multi-cancer validation; systematic multi-scale biological evaluation; biologically interpretable outputs.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective TCGA-based validation; limited prospective external cohort testing.
- **Conclusion**: Tumour-specialised histopathology AI reveals RNA-splicing archetypes with robust prognostic value, enabling patient stratification from standard pathology slides.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41467-026-75217-z / PMID:42414307

## 3. Primary Sclerosing Cholangitis Displays Distinct Colonic Mucosa Topography Yet a Shared Mast Cell State With Ulcerative Colitis — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Nature Communications
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-07
- **Authors**: Tearle JL et al.
- **Aims**: Characterise immunological and microbial differences in PSC-UC colon vs UC to elucidate the elevated colorectal cancer risk.
- **Method**: Single-cell mRNA sequencing, antigen receptor sequencing, 16S rRNA, and spatial transcriptomics on biopsies from four colon regions in remission and relapse.
- **Key findings**: PSC-UC has enriched activated CD8 T and γδ T cells at right colon even in remission; TMEM176B mast cells enriched during relapse in both PSC-UC and UC, potentially pro-tumourigenic.
- **Strengths**: Multi-omic approach with paired remission/relapse samples and spatial resolution across colon regions.
- **Limitations**: Small sample size; biopsy-based design; remission status may underrepresent full inflammatory burden.
- **Conclusion**: PSC-UC and UC colons are fundamentally distinct but share a pro-tumourigenic mast cell programme during active disease, informing tailored cancer surveillance.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41467-026-75231-1 / PMID:42414303

## 4. Anti-PD-1 Plus Anti-CTLA-4 Blockade Overcomes Immune Exclusion in NSCLC Brain Metastases by Enhancing CD8 T Cell Responses and Promoting Tertiary Lymphoid Structure Formation — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Nature Communications
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-07
- **Authors**: Hosoya K et al.
- **Aims**: Characterise immune resistance in NSCLC brain metastases and evaluate efficacy of dual checkpoint blockade.
- **Method**: Clinical outcome analyses, paired human tissue profiling (scRNAseq, immunofluorescence), and syngeneic mouse brain-metastasis model.
- **Key findings**: Brain metastases are immune-excluded (fewer CTLs, fewer TLS than primary tumours); nivolumab+ipilimumab improves intracranial control and induces TLS-like structures vs monotherapy.
- **Strengths**: Integrated clinical, human tissue, and murine mechanistic evidence; CD8 depletion experiments confirm causal role.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective clinical analysis; limited human BrM biopsy pairs; syngeneic model may not fully recapitulate human biology.
- **Conclusion**: Dual PD-1/CTLA-4 blockade overcomes brain-metastasis immune exclusion via TLS induction and CD8 T cell recruitment, supporting combination ICI in intracranial disease.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41467-026-74782-7 / PMID:42414284

## 5. The Need for Another Tool: Australian Healthcare Professionals on the Use of Psilocybin for Existential Distress in People With Cancer — Score 2/6

- **Journal**: BMC Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-06
- **Authors**: Adler H et al.
- **Aims**: Investigate Australian HCP attitudes toward psilocybin-assisted therapy (PAT) for cancer-related existential distress.
- **Method**: Qualitative semi-structured interviews with 11 HCPs from major cancer centres; reflexive thematic analysis.
- **Key findings**: HCPs are interested in PAT but cite knowledge gaps and barriers; preference for multidisciplinary, culturally sensitive delivery models.
- **Strengths**: First Australian HCP perspective study on PAT in oncology; identifies key implementation barriers for future research.
- **Limitations**: Very small sample (n=11); single-country, single-context perspective; non-probabilistic sampling.
- **Conclusion**: PAT for cancer existential distress warrants further clinical investigation with ethically robust and culturally sensitive delivery frameworks.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1186/s12916-026-05024-2 / PMID:42410597
