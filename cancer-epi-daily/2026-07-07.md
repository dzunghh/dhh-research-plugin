## 1. De novo cancer-related mortality after solid organ transplantation in England: the EpCOT study — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Br J Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-01
- **Authors**: Stephens C et al.
- **Aims**: Quantify cancer-specific mortality risk among all solid organ transplant recipients in England over 23 years.
- **Method**: Population-based cohort study linking UK Transplant Registry, National Cancer Registry, Civil Registration of Deaths, and Hospital Episode Statistics; n=50,762 SOT recipients, 1992–2015.
- **Key findings**: Cancer caused 15.9% of all deaths; overall cancer SMR 2.12 (95% CI 2.05–2.19). Non-Hodgkin's Lymphoma had the highest SMR at 9.86 (95% CI 9.01–10.75).
- **Strengths**: Large national cohort with comprehensive multi-registry linkage spanning over two decades.
- **Limitations**: Registry-based design limits adjustment for immunosuppression regimen details and comorbidities.
- **Conclusion**: Cancer is the second leading cause of long-term mortality post-transplant, with risk stratified by cancer type and allograft.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41416-026-03529-4 / PMID:42387207

## 4. Epigenetic Liquid Biopsy Enables Universal Mutation-Agnostic Molecular Surveillance for High-Risk Neuroblastoma — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: Clin Cancer Res
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-01
- **Authors**: Gal-Mark N et al.
- **Aims**: Develop a methylation-based cfDNA liquid biopsy for universal surveillance of high-risk neuroblastoma regardless of mutational profile.
- **Method**: Whole-genome Oxford Nanopore sequencing; 72 neuroblastoma-specific differentially methylated regions identified; 25-marker panel validated across diagnostic, remission, and relapse samples.
- **Key findings**: cfDNA consistently detected at diagnosis and relapse but absent in remission and healthy controls; outperformed copy-number–based approaches for specificity.
- **Strengths**: Mutation-agnostic design enables universal applicability; longitudinal profiling allowed early molecular relapse detection.
- **Limitations**: Single-centre study; sample sizes at relapse were small; requires specialised Nanopore sequencing infrastructure.
- **Conclusion**: Methylation-based cfDNA deconvolution is a broadly applicable platform for pediatric cancer surveillance.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-26-0457 / PMID:42384765
