## 1. An alignment-free strategy for circulating tumor DNA detection and tumor fraction estimation from whole-genome sequencing data — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: PLoS Computational Biology
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-10
- **Authors**: Oroperv C et al.
- **Aims**: Develop a tumor-informed, alignment-free ctDNA detection tool (ctDNAmer) using k-mer analysis of unaligned WGS reads to estimate tumor fraction.
- **Method**: k-mer-based bioinformatic tool validated in 90 stage III colorectal cancer patients with 3-year postoperative follow-up (mean sequencing depth ~28×).
- **Key findings**: ctDNA detected in 77% (17/22) of recurring patients with 8-month median lead time vs. radiological imaging; Pearson correlation 0.897 between TF estimates and mean allele frequencies.
- **Strengths**: Avoids alignment biases; handles SNVs and structural variants in a unified framework; prospective cohort validation.
- **Limitations**: Single cancer type (CRC stage III); moderate sequencing depth may miss very low TF samples.
- **Conclusion**: ctDNAmer offers a scalable, alignment-free approach for postoperative ctDNA monitoring with clinically meaningful lead-time advantage.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1013356 / PMID:42574466

## 2. A fast numerical integration scheme for clonal expansion processes on graphs — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: PLoS Computational Biology
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-04
- **Authors**: Paterson C et al.
- **Aims**: Extend multi-stage clonal expansion (MSCE) cancer incidence models beyond linear chains to arbitrary directed graphs without stochastic simulation.
- **Method**: Numerical integration scheme for survival probabilities of first-order birth-death processes on directed graphs; validated on simulated incidence data.
- **Key findings**: Method correctly recovers model parameters from simulated cancer incidence data; avoids mean-field approximation errors that accumulate at old ages.
- **Strengths**: Generalisable to any graph topology; computationally efficient; expands scope of cancer epi modelling beyond linear MSCE.
- **Limitations**: Validated only on simulated data; real-world cancer datasets not yet tested.
- **Conclusion**: Provides a rigorous numerical method for fitting graphical clonal expansion models to cancer age-incidence curves.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1012784 / PMID:42550854

## 3. Liquid Biopsies for Cancer: A Translational Science Review — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: JAMA
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-10
- **Authors**: Mezzanotte-Sharpe J et al.
- **Aims**: Summarise evidence on circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) for monitoring cancer progression, residual disease detection, and treatment guidance.
- **Method**: Translational science review synthesising clinical studies and a meta-analysis of 1,725 urothelial carcinoma patients.
- **Key findings**: Elevated ctDNA associated with worse disease-free survival (HR 20.69; 95% CI 9.63–44.43); molecular relapse detected ~8.7 months earlier than imaging in colorectal cancer cohort.
- **Strengths**: Broad cancer-type coverage; clinically actionable synthesis in a high-visibility journal; quantifies lead-time advantage.
- **Limitations**: Review design; optimal ctDNA monitoring timing and cost-effectiveness remain undefined.
- **Conclusion**: ctDNA is a validated prognostic biomarker but clinical implementation protocols require prospective RCT-level evidence.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jama.2026.13898 / PMID:42574031

## 4. Cost-effectiveness of pirfenidone for patients with cancer and grade 2 or grade 3 radiation-induced lung injury: A modeling study — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: PLoS Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-05
- **Authors**: Pan QY et al.
- **Aims**: Assess cost-effectiveness of pirfenidone plus glucocorticoids vs. glucocorticoids alone for grade 2–3 radiation-induced lung injury (RILI) in cancer patients.
- **Method**: Markov model based on individual-level phase II RCT data (NCT03902509); 4-week cycles over 24 weeks; one-way and probabilistic sensitivity analyses.
- **Key findings**: Incremental 0.028 QALYs at incremental cost $590; ICER $20,956/QALY — well below $100,000/QALY WTP; 99.98% probability of cost-effectiveness.
- **Strengths**: RCT-grounded model; comprehensive sensitivity analysis; clinically actionable ICER with a clear WTP comparison.
- **Limitations**: Phase II trial with limited sample size; 24-week horizon may underestimate long-term effects.
- **Conclusion**: Pirfenidone plus glucocorticoids is cost-effective for RILI management, supporting its adoption pending phase III confirmation.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1371/journal.pmed.1004817 / PMID:42555672

## 5. Performance of PREMM5, clinical criteria, and immunohistochemistry for MMR proteins in genetic risk assessment of Mexican patients with colorectal cancer — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: PLoS ONE
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-04
- **Authors**: Rodríguez-Olivares JL et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate diagnostic accuracy of PREMM5, Amsterdam II, revised Bethesda criteria, and MMR immunohistochemistry for identifying germline pathogenic variant carriers in Mexican CRC patients.
- **Method**: Prospective enrollment of 208 CRC patients at two Mexican centres (Sep 2018–Oct 2024); NGS multigene panel as reference standard; ROC curve and sensitivity/specificity analysis.
- **Key findings**: Germline PVs in 32.2%; Lynch syndrome in 77.6% of carriers; PREMM5 AUC 0.822 (95% CI 0.746–0.898); revised Bethesda sensitivity 98.1%.
- **Strengths**: Multi-centre, prospective design; multigene panel as reference; first PREMM5 validation in a Latin American CRC cohort.
- **Limitations**: Two centres only; no population-based sampling; relatively short follow-up window for incident cancers.
- **Conclusion**: PREMM5 performs comparably to international validation cohorts in Mexico and supports multigene panel testing for CRC risk stratification.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1371/journal.pone.0354795 / PMID:42550852
