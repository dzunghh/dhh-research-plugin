## 1. Global uptake of essential cancer medicines with and without complex genomic diagnostics: a retrospective observational study — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-25
- **Authors**: Vokinger KN et al.
- **Aims**: Quantify uptake trends for WHO essential cancer medicines stratified by GDP level and diagnostic requirement across 71 countries (2002–2023).
- **Method**: Retrospective observational study using IQVIA MIDAS sales data; regression analyses comparing uptake by diagnostic category (IHC, complex genomics, none) across three GDP groups.
- **Key findings**: Medicines requiring complex genomic diagnostics had 65% lower standard-unit uptake than those without (coefficient −0.453; p<0.0001). Uptake was lowest in lower-GDP countries across all medicine types.
- **Strengths**: Large multinational scope (71 countries), 22-year longitudinal dataset from a standardised commercial source, robust regression stratification.
- **Limitations**: IQVIA MIDAS underrepresents low-income countries; observational associations only, not causal estimates.
- **Conclusion**: WHO EML inclusion alone is insufficient to ensure equitable patient access as precision oncology medicines proliferate on the essential medicines list.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00432-8 / PMID:42790449

## 2. Radiotherapy versus observation following surgical resection of WHO grade 2 atypical meningioma (ROAM/EORTC-1308): an international, multicentre, open-label, phase 3, randomised controlled trial — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Lancet
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-25
- **Authors**: Jenkinson MD et al.
- **Aims**: Assess whether adjuvant radiotherapy reduces recurrence after complete resection of newly diagnosed WHO grade 2 atypical meningioma.
- **Method**: Phase 3 RCT; 157 patients across 58 centres in 11 countries randomised 1:1 to 60 Gy fractionated radiotherapy vs. observation; minimum 4-year follow-up.
- **Key findings**: 5-year disease-free survival 79.9% (radiotherapy) vs. 64.3% (observation); HR 0.51 (95% CI 0.27–0.97; p=0.04). Grade 2–3 adverse events in 8% of treated patients.
- **Strengths**: First randomised trial for this tumour type; international multicenter design; 5-year follow-up with centralised MRI review.
- **Limitations**: Small sample (157 patients); open-label design; underpowered for overall survival.
- **Conclusion**: Adjuvant radiotherapy should be considered when prevention of recurrence is prioritised over potential late radiotherapy toxicity after complete meningioma resection.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S0140-6736(26)01804-0 / PMID:42790456

## 3. Balancing benefits, costs, and harms: policy implications of expanding cancer susceptibility testing from genetics clinics to the general population — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-25
- **Authors**: Turnbull C et al.
- **Aims**: Examine evidentiary and health-system challenges of scaling cancer susceptibility testing (including BRCA1/2, MLH1, MSH2, polygenic risk scores) from specialist clinics to population level.
- **Method**: Policy Review building on the Lancet Oncology Commission on cancer genomics; tiered, context-specific implementation framework proposed across clinical care and public health.
- **Key findings**: Evidence supports population testing for a small set of first-wave genes (BRCA1/2, MLH1, MSH2); most later genes on multigene panels have uncertain penetrance and no mortality-reduction evidence. Polygenic risk scores have limited discriminatory capacity and ancestry-dependent performance.
- **Strengths**: Systematic appraisal distinguishing gene classes; actionable tiered framework linking evidence quality to implementation context.
- **Limitations**: Narrative synthesis without formal meta-analysis; cost-effectiveness data sparse for most recommended interventions.
- **Conclusion**: A context-specific, tiered approach is needed to separate population-level susceptibility testing that provides net benefit from settings where use should remain restricted or research-only.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00390-6 / PMID:42790443

## 4. Accelerating equitable cancer genomics and precision oncology in health care and research: a Lancet Oncology Commission — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-25
- **Authors**: Casolino R et al.
- **Aims**: Define the global precision oncology landscape and analyse scientific, structural, and health-system challenges shaping equitable, effective, and sustainable delivery.
- **Method**: Lancet Oncology Commission; systematic multi-stakeholder analysis producing a value-based resource-stratified implementation framework, workforce competency framework, and monitoring and evaluation framework.
- **Key findings**: Access to cancer genomics remains profoundly unequal across and within countries. Consistent clinical benefit is confined to a few validated biomarker-treatment pairings; broader genomic profiling benefits only a minority of patients receiving matched therapy.
- **Strengths**: Global scope; multi-stakeholder authorship across high- and low-income settings; practical frameworks for implementation, monitoring, and data sharing.
- **Limitations**: Commission synthesis format; largely qualitative recommendations; limited quantitative modelling of implementation costs.
- **Conclusion**: Equitable, evidence-informed precision oncology is achievable with context-responsive frameworks, but requires urgent action on global access disparities and integration of robust value assessment.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00302-5 / PMID:42790448
