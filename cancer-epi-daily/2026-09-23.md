## 1. Per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (non-PFOS/PFOA) and liver cancer risk — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-17
- **Authors**: Watling CZ et al.
- **Aims**: Assess associations between nine less-studied PFAS compounds and liver cancer risk using pooled prospective cohort data.
- **Method**: Nested case-control study within 12 US prospective cohorts (853 matched case-control pairs); conditional logistic regression per 90th vs. 10th percentile.
- **Key findings**: PFHpA was positively associated with liver cancer (OR 1.78, 95% CI 1.26–2.52); males showed stronger association (OR 3.72). No significant associations for other eight PFAS.
- **Strengths**: Large pooled multi-cohort design with pre-diagnostic blood measures; sex- and subtype-stratified analyses.
- **Limitations**: Single blood measurement per subject; limited power for rare subtypes; residual confounding possible.
- **Conclusion**: Short-chain PFAS, particularly PFHpA, warrant further investigation as a hepatocarcinogen given continued consumer-product use.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag325 / PMID:42752878

## 2. Blood test trends vs single-threshold abnormality for cancer detection in primary care — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: PLoS Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-17
- **Authors**: Nicholson BD et al.
- **Aims**: Compare discrimination of longitudinal blood-test trends versus single abnormality thresholds for cancer detection among primary-care patients with unexpected weight loss.
- **Method**: Retrospective cohort (Clinical Practice Research Datalink, England); 275,205 UWL patients; Cox and joint-model analyses for 26 blood tests over 1–10-year windows.
- **Key findings**: Age/sex-adjusted trend models outperformed single-threshold models on 34 of 52 test-cancer combinations; highest AUC 0.82 for overall cancer.
- **Strengths**: Very large nationally representative primary-care dataset with linked cancer registry; systematic evaluation of 26 tests.
- **Limitations**: No adjustment for testing propensity bias; trend analysis limited to patients with ≥2 results.
- **Conclusion**: Incorporating longitudinal blood-test trends improves cancer discrimination beyond single-threshold alerts after age/sex adjustment.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1371/journal.pmed.1004956 / PMID:42752402

## 3. Early-adulthood BMI, mammographic density, and post-menopausal breast cancer risk — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: British Journal of Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-17
- **Authors**: Jauniaux B et al.
- **Aims**: Examine whether mammographic density mediates the inverse association between higher early-adulthood BMI and lower post-menopausal breast cancer risk.
- **Method**: Prospective cohort (UK PROCAS, 33,816 post-menopausal women); counterfactual mediation modelling with VAS % density and Volpara fibroglandular volume; median follow-up 10.4 years.
- **Key findings**: Higher early-adulthood BMI associated with lower breast cancer risk (HR per 5 kg/m²: 0.85); VAS density mediated ~60% of this association.
- **Strengths**: Objective mammographic density measurement; rigorous counterfactual mediation accounting for intermediate confounding.
- **Limitations**: Self-reported BMI at age 20; fibroglandular volume inconsistently associated, suggesting divergent pathways.
- **Conclusion**: Lower mammographic density may be a key mechanism linking higher early-adulthood BMI to reduced post-menopausal breast cancer risk.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41416-026-03593-w / PMID:42754665

## 4. Accelerometer-derived real-world sleep stages and incident disease risk (phenome-wide) — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: PLoS Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-17
- **Authors**: Luo J et al.
- **Aims**: Systematically map associations between objectively measured real-world sleep patterns and risk of 1,049 incident diseases, including cancers, in middle-aged and older adults.
- **Method**: UK Biobank cohort (95,559 participants); SleepNet algorithm on wrist accelerometry; phenome-wide Cox PH regression with restricted cubic splines; median follow-up 8.9 years.
- **Key findings**: Sleep patterns significantly associated with 156 diseases; extreme short sleep (<5h) associated with 37 of 41 adverse outcomes; optimal sleep 6–8 h for minimum risk in 69 phenotypes.
- **Strengths**: Largest real-world objectively measured sleep-stage study with phenome-wide scope; includes cancer phenotypes among 1,049 outcomes.
- **Limitations**: Observational design with residual confounding; accelerometry approximates but does not replace polysomnography.
- **Conclusion**: Maintaining 6–8 hours of sleep with high REM proportion is associated with broad disease risk reduction across organ systems.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1371/journal.pmed.1005213 / PMID:42752434

## Appraisal notes

This week's digest is anchored by strong epi-methodology designs: a 12-cohort nested case-control (PFAS/liver cancer), two prospective cohort mediation/discrimination analyses (blood-test trends and BMI/mammographic density), and a phenome-wide accelerometry study from UK Biobank. A recurring theme is the leveraging of large national biobanks and linked registries to move beyond single-exposure single-outcome analyses toward richer mechanistic and multi-phenotype frameworks. The PFAS finding on PFHpA is particularly policy-relevant given ongoing short-chain PFAS proliferation in consumer products.
