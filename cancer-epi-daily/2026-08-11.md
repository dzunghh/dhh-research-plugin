## 1. [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 in patients with PSMA-positive metastatic androgen pathway modulator-naive/sensitive prostate cancer (PSMAddition): a phase 3 randomised, controlled trial — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Lancet
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-06
- **Authors**: Tagawa ST et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate efficacy and safety of Lu-PSMA-617 combined with ADT plus ARPI in PSMA-positive metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer.
- **Method**: Phase 3 RCT, 1144 patients, 169 sites, 20 countries; 1:1 randomisation; primary endpoint radiographic progression-free survival (rPFS).
- **Key findings**: HR 0.72 (95% CI 0.58–0.90; p=0.0021) for rPFS, a 28% relative risk reduction; primary endpoint met. Grade 3+ AEs 51% vs 43% in control.
- **Strengths**: Large international RCT with centrally assessed endpoints across 20 countries; prespecified interim analysis.
- **Limitations**: Open-label design; median follow-up ~23.6 months; crossover from control arm permitted.
- **Conclusion**: Lu-PSMA-617 plus ADT/ARPI prolongs rPFS and may represent a new treatment option in mHSPC.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S0140-6736(26)01092-5 / PMID:42561994

## 2. Electronic Patient-Reported Outcome Monitoring With Alert-Based Interventions in Metastatic Breast Cancer: A Randomized Clinical Trial — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-06
- **Authors**: Karsten MM et al.
- **Aims**: Determine whether digital PRO monitoring with alert-based responses reduces fatigue in metastatic breast cancer patients.
- **Method**: Multicenter RCT, 909 patients, 52 German centers; weekly smartphone PRO questionnaires (EORTC CAT); nurse/physician alert within 48h of PRO deterioration.
- **Key findings**: Adjusted fatigue difference −5.4 points (95% CI −6.6 to −4.1), exceeding MCID (3.3); physical functioning difference +4.0, also exceeding MCID (3.2).
- **Strengths**: Large multicenter RCT with validated patient-centered instruments and 12-month follow-up.
- **Limitations**: German-only population; pre-randomisation baseline fatigue imbalance; PROs assessed after randomisation.
- **Conclusion**: Alert-based digital PRO monitoring achieves clinically meaningful reductions in fatigue and improved physical functioning in mBC.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamaoncol.2026.2661 / PMID:42560727

## 3. Longitudinal Change in Cardiac Function After Doxorubicin and Dexrazoxane: A Report From Children's Oncology Group ALTE11C2 — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Journal of Clinical Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-06
- **Authors**: Mobley EM et al.
- **Aims**: Assess long-term cardioprotective effect of dexrazoxane on left ventricular systolic function in childhood cancer survivors.
- **Method**: Multi-protocol COG cohort study, 895 patients, 2279 echocardiograms; generalised estimating equations + Cox PH models; blinded central remeasurement.
- **Key findings**: Dexrazoxane associated with LV z-score improvement of 0.4 (95% CI 0.2–0.5); HR for reduced LV function 0.58 at 1y and 0.54 at 5y; reduced screening needs in high-risk group.
- **Strengths**: Multiple prospective COG protocols, blinded central echocardiogram re-measurement, long follow-up (mean 5.9y, n=230 with ≥10y).
- **Limitations**: Not all echocardiograms centrally remeasured; variable follow-up duration; one protocol (P9754) not randomised.
- **Conclusion**: Dexrazoxane provides significant long-term cardioprotection and may reduce surveillance burden in doxorubicin-treated paediatric patients.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1200/JCO-26-00260 / PMID:42561216

## 4. Using polygenic risk scores to refine risk and outcomes in high-grade serous ovarian cancer in Australia — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiology, Biomarkers & Prevention
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-06
- **Authors**: Delahunty R et al.
- **Aims**: Assess PRS utility for predicting high-grade serous ovarian cancer risk and its association with chemotherapy response and survival.
- **Method**: Case-control study, 1097 HGSOC cases and 812 controls from Australian research programs; logistic regression and survival analysis.
- **Key findings**: Each SD increase in PRS associated with OR 1.40 (p<0.001); top 1% reached ~3% lifetime risk. PRS not significantly associated with chemotherapy response or survival.
- **Strengths**: First study linking PRS to both HGSOC risk and chemotherapy outcomes in the same cohort.
- **Limitations**: Australian population only; associations inconsistent across survival analyses; modest power for rare endpoints.
- **Conclusion**: PRS improves HGSOC risk stratification for prevention but is unlikely to serve as a therapeutic biomarker.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-25-1882 / PMID:42560169
