## 1. Surveillance Mammography and Second Primary Breast Cancer in Older Women — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Intern Med
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-02
- **Authors**: Richman IB et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate whether continued surveillance mammography >5 years post-diagnosis reduces risk of advanced second primary breast cancer in women aged ≥67.
- **Method**: Retrospective SEER-Medicare cohort (n=9,888); inverse probability of treatment weighting with cause-specific hazard models.
- **Key findings**: Advanced second primary BC cumulative incidence 1.65% vs 2.15% (HR 0.76, 95% CI 0.46–1.26); not statistically significant. Similar results across HR-positive and HR-negative subgroups.
- **Strengths**: Large population-based cohort using causal inference (IPTW); stratified by hormone receptor status; follow-up through 2021.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective design; residual confounding possible despite IPTW adjustment.
- **Conclusion**: Continued surveillance mammography beyond 5 years not associated with reduced advanced second primary breast cancer risk in older survivors.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamainternmed.2026.5465 / PMID:42970273

## 2. Protein Biomarkers of Lung Cancer Risk in Never-Smokers — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Netw Open
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-02
- **Authors**: Zahed H et al.
- **Aims**: Identify circulating protein biomarkers associated with lung cancer risk specifically among people who never smoked (PWNS).
- **Method**: Nested case-control study in 4 LC3 cohorts (180 matched pairs, 2821 proteins tested); replicated in UK Biobank (n=49,291).
- **Key findings**: CEACAM5 (OR 1.65, 95% CI 1.31–2.09) and CEACAM6 (OR 1.72, 95% CI 1.33–2.22) independently associated with lung cancer; both replicated in UK Biobank.
- **Strengths**: Multi-cohort discovery with large replication; FDR-adjusted; proteins distinct from smoking-related lung cancer markers.
- **Limitations**: Small discovery set (180 pairs); population diversity limited; larger studies needed to validate clinical utility.
- **Conclusion**: CEACAM5 and CEACAM6 are novel lung cancer risk biomarkers in never-smokers, supporting risk-stratified screening approaches.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.41494 / PMID:42970291

## 3. Global Burden of Bone Cancers, 1990–2023, with Forecasts to 2050 (GBD 2023) — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-29
- **Authors**: GBD 2023 Bone Cancer Collaborators et al.
- **Aims**: Estimate global incidence, mortality, and DALYs for bone cancers 1990–2023 and project trends to 2050.
- **Method**: GBD 2023 systematic analysis; standardized age-standardized rates and uncertainty intervals across 204 countries and territories.
- **Key findings**: 111,000 incident cases (95% UI 81,300–164,000), 76,600 deaths, 3.02 million DALYs globally in 2023; low-income countries highest mortality and DALY ASRs; forecasts indicate rising global caseload to 2050.
- **Strengths**: Comprehensive 33-year temporal analysis covering 204 countries; bimodal age distribution and regional breakdowns with 95% uncertainty intervals.
- **Limitations**: Data quality varies substantially across low-income settings; primary bone cancer subtypes not fully disaggregated.
- **Conclusion**: Bone cancer burden rising globally with marked geographic and income-related disparities, requiring targeted resource allocation especially in low-income settings.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamaoncol.2026.4274 / PMID:42955027

## 4. Emulated Target Trial: SABR Versus Surgery for Early-Stage NSCLC — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: J Clin Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-02
- **Authors**: Kagenaar E et al.
- **Aims**: Compare effectiveness of stereotactic ablative radiotherapy (SABR) vs surgical resection for early-stage NSCLC eligible for either treatment using an emulated target trial framework.
- **Method**: England National Cancer Registry data (2015–2020; n=12,546); emulated target trial design with instrumental variable analysis to reduce confounding by indication.
- **Key findings**: 3-year all-cause mortality 30.3% (SABR) vs 21.6% (surgery); mean absolute risk difference 8.8 pp (95% CI 1.4–16.2; p=0.02); non-significant difference in older subgroup (≥80y).
- **Strengths**: Largest comparative study to date; emulated trial + instrumental variable addresses confounding; real-world national registry data.
- **Limitations**: Residual confounding despite instrumental variable; eligibility criteria differ from randomized trials; instrumental variable assumptions not fully testable.
- **Conclusion**: Surgical resection remains preferred for eligible patients; SABR is a viable curative-intent alternative especially for older patients.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1200/JCO-26-00399 / PMID:42970669

## 5. Global Men's Health Epidemiology Within an Intersectional Framework — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Lancet
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-31
- **Authors**: Catto JWF et al.
- **Aims**: Provide an epidemiological overview of men's health using an intersectional framework informed by GBD 1990–2023 data.
- **Method**: GBD 1990–2023 mortality and DALY analysis across NCDs (including cancer), injuries, and infections; intersectional framework incorporating biological, social, and structural determinants.
- **Key findings**: Cancer and cardiovascular disease drive male excess mortality; marginalised men (incarcerated, homeless, low-income) have substantially worse outcomes; structural drivers including automation and violence will disproportionately affect men's future health.
- **Strengths**: GBD 2023 data (204 countries, 33 years); novel intersectional analytic lens; identifies actionable social determinants beyond sex disaggregation.
- **Limitations**: Review/framework article; primary data analysis limited to descriptive GBD trends; gender vs sex conceptualization constrained by GBD data structure.
- **Conclusion**: Men's excess cancer and CVD mortality reflects intersecting structural determinants that are largely preventable, requiring targeted policies beyond simple sex disaggregation.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S0140-6736(26)01490-X / PMID:42956774

## Appraisal notes

This week's digest highlights three methodological themes. First, causal inference in observational oncology is advancing: both the SEER-Medicare mammography cohort (IPTW) and the NSCLC emulated target trial (instrumental variable) represent growing sophistication in quasi-experimental methods applied to national registries. Second, proteomics-based risk biomarkers for never-smoker lung cancer (CEACAM5/6) mark a shift toward molecular epidemiology for populations excluded from standard screening criteria. Third, global burden analyses (GBD 2023 bone cancer; men's health intersectional framework) increasingly incorporate uncertainty quantification and structural determinants, moving cancer surveillance closer to policy-actionable precision.
