## 1. A quantitative approach to evidence triangulation: development of a framework to address rigour and relevance — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: European Journal of Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-16
- **Authors**: Shapland CY et al.
- **Aims**: Develop a formal framework for quantitatively integrating evidence from multiple study designs with explicit bias adjustments.
- **Method**: Framework development combining RCTs, Mendelian randomization and multivariable regression via bias-adjusted meta-analysis; illustrated on beta-carotene and CHD.
- **Key findings**: After bias adjustment, all three designs agreed on no strong causal effect of beta-carotene on CHD (adjusted RR=0.99, 95% CI 0.82–1.21). Unadjusted observational studies falsely suggested a protective effect (RR=0.83).
- **Strengths**: Explicit relevance and rigour assessments bring structured quantification to previously qualitative triangulation practice.
- **Limitations**: Bias direction and magnitude estimates remain subjective; external validity depends on quality of prior knowledge about each design's biases.
- **Conclusion**: The framework advances causal inference by enabling formal, reproducible synthesis across heterogeneous study designs.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1007/s10654-026-01484-1 / PMID:43037922

## 2. Nationwide Assessment of Trends, Characteristics, and Outcomes of Pregnancy for Cancer Survivors — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Network Open
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-02
- **Authors**: Matsuo K et al.
- **Aims**: Assess temporal trends and maternal morbidity at delivery among past and current cancer survivors in the US.
- **Method**: Nationwide cross-sectional study using National Inpatient Sample; 28.4 million hospital deliveries 2016–2023; multivariable Poisson models.
- **Key findings**: Deliveries among past cancer survivors increased from 30.1 to 45.6 per 10 000 (AAPC=6.3%). Severe maternal morbidity was 1.45× higher for past survivors and 3.80× higher for current survivors versus no-cancer group.
- **Strengths**: Very large nationally representative sample spanning 8 years with rigorous competing-risk considerations.
- **Limitations**: Cross-sectional design; no long-term follow-up of neonatal outcomes; residual confounding by cancer type and treatment details.
- **Conclusion**: Rising deliveries among past cancer survivors, with substantially elevated maternal morbidity, support mandatory preconception evaluation and tertiary-care referral.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.44168 / PMID:43037985

## 3. Cardiovascular Risk Factors and Incident Cardiovascular Disease Among Cancer Survivors in Korea — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiology, Biomarkers & Prevention
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-16
- **Authors**: Moon SJ et al.
- **Aims**: Examine cancer-type-specific associations between cardiovascular risk factors and incident CVD in Korean cancer survivors.
- **Method**: Nationwide cohort (n=148,537) across 5 cancer types (breast, colorectal, gastric, liver, lung); cause-specific Cox regression with death as competing risk; 2012–2021.
- **Key findings**: Heavy smoking associated with high CVD risk in breast (aHR 1.93) and colorectal cancer survivors. Severe obesity associated with incident IHD across all cancer types, most strongly in liver and lung cancer.
- **Strengths**: Large nationwide registry with competing-risk methodology; cancer type-specific stratification adds clinical granularity.
- **Limitations**: Residual confounding from treatment exposures (cardiotoxic chemotherapy, radiotherapy) not fully captured.
- **Conclusion**: Cardiovascular risk factor patterns differ meaningfully by cancer type, supporting tailored survivorship care strategies.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-0732 / PMID:43037640

## 4. Eligibility and real-world use of adjuvant chemotherapy in colon cancer: a Dutch nationwide study — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-16
- **Authors**: Shkurti J et al.
- **Aims**: Quantify adherence to guideline-recommended adjuvant chemotherapy (ACT) and factors driving non-adherence in resected non-metastatic colon cancer.
- **Method**: Population-based cohort using Netherlands Cancer Registry 2005–2020 (n=44,471); logistic/multivariable regression for adherence predictors.
- **Key findings**: Only 57.8% of guideline-eligible patients initiated ACT. Non-adherence strongly associated with age ≥75 (only 29.9% received ACT), comorbidity, female sex, right-sided tumors, and non-elective surgery.
- **Strengths**: Complete nationwide registry; long study period enabling trend analysis; clinical and hospital-level covariates.
- **Limitations**: Eligibility criteria based on available registry variables may misclassify some patients; reasons for non-adherence not captured.
- **Conclusion**: Substantial underuse of ACT highlights system-level and patient-level barriers requiring targeted quality-improvement interventions.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag432 / PMID:43037074

## 5. Antibiotics counteracting tumor-promoting dysbiosis in colorectal cancer — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Cell
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-13
- **Authors**: Zitvogel L et al.
- **Aims**: Assess whether targeted antibiotics can overcome Fusobacterium nucleatum-driven resistance to immune checkpoint blockade in metastatic colorectal cancer.
- **Method**: Commentary on a clinical trial combining pulsed anaerobe-directed metronidazole with anti-PD-1 antibody plus VEGF receptor inhibitor in Fn-positive MSS metastatic CRC.
- **Key findings**: The triple regimen achieved encouraging progression-free survival without inducing global dysbiosis; selective pathobiont dismantling preserved immunotherapy efficacy.
- **Strengths**: Addresses key resistance mechanism in immunotherapy-refractory CRC subtype; clinically actionable approach.
- **Limitations**: Commentary on a single trial; sample size not reported; long-term effects on microbiome uncertain.
- **Conclusion**: Pulsed, targeted antibiotic co-therapy may selectively reverse tumor-promoting dysbiosis without systemic immune compromise.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.ccell.2026.10.013 / PMID:43025423
