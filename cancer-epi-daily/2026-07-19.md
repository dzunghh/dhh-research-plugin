## 1. Geographic and Social Equity in Population-Wide Genomic Screening — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Netw Open
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-01
- **Authors**: Sonawane K et al.
- **Aims**: Assess whether an implementation-science-guided statewide program achieves equitable population reach for cancer-relevant Tier 1 genomic screening.
- **Method**: Cross-sectional analysis of 82,420 adults in In Our DNA SC; coverage and positivity stratified by rural-urban continuum codes and Social Vulnerability Index quartiles.
- **Key findings**: Urban/lower-SVI counties had significantly higher screening rates (>1000/100k vs ~840/100k in rural areas); HBOC positivity was higher in peri-urban areas; Lynch syndrome and FH positivity did not differ by SVI.
- **Strengths**: Statewide program, all 46 counties included, 50k+ completed screens, multi-channel recruitment.
- **Limitations**: Single-state cross-sectional design; clinical and behavioral outcomes not yet evaluated.
- **Conclusion**: Informatics-enabled implementation frameworks can achieve broad genomic screening reach, but rural/disadvantaged access gaps persist.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.22743 / PMID:42440321

## 2. Colorectal Cancer Treatment Delay Thresholds and Metastasis Risk — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Netw Open
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-01
- **Authors**: Nguyen CM et al.
- **Aims**: Identify pathway-specific treatment delay thresholds associated with 3-year metastasis risk in non-metastatic colorectal cancer.
- **Method**: Retrospective cohort (Optum Clinformatics, n=11,927); XGBoost identified optimal time-to-treatment-initiation thresholds; Fine-Gray competing-risk models estimated associations.
- **Key findings**: For surgery + adjuvant therapy, TTI ≥4 days raised metastasis risk (sHR 1.27); for neoadjuvant + surgery, TTI ≥68 days nearly tripled risk (sHR 2.66); trimodality pathway showed no TTI effect.
- **Strengths**: Large insured cohort, machine-learning threshold detection, competing-risk methodology, pathway stratification.
- **Limitations**: Administrative claims cannot capture clinical nuance; insured population limits generalisability; residual confounding by disease severity.
- **Conclusion**: Pathway-tailored TTI benchmarks outperform uniform delay thresholds in predicting metastasis risk in CRC.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.23057 / PMID:42446881

## 3. Bevacizumab and Platinum Choice in Pembrolizumab-Treated Advanced Cervical Cancer — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Netw Open
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-01
- **Authors**: Farolfi A et al.
- **Aims**: Compare overall survival by platinum agent and bevacizumab use in advanced cervical cancer patients receiving first-line pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy.
- **Method**: Comparative effectiveness study; propensity-score-matched retrospective multinational cohort from TriNetX Global Collaborative Network (n=1,931).
- **Key findings**: Cisplatin vs carboplatin showed equivalent OS (HR 0.98; 95% CI 0.81–1.18); bevacizumab improved 12-month OS (74.8% vs 64.5%) but the benefit attenuated by 24 months.
- **Strengths**: Multinational real-world data, propensity score matching, pre-specified safety outcomes including fistula and pulmonary embolism.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective design; short median follow-up (~11 months); TriNetX data lacks granular pathological staging.
- **Conclusion**: Carboplatin can substitute cisplatin with equal effectiveness; bevacizumab confers early OS benefit warranting prospective confirmation.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.23166 / PMID:42446878

## 4. Allogeneic CD70-Targeted CAR T-Cell Therapy for Advanced Renal Cell Carcinoma: Phase I TRAVERSE Trial — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: J Clin Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-14
- **Authors**: Srour SA et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate safety and antitumor activity of allogeneic CAR T-cell product ALLO-316 in ICI/VEGFR-refractory clear cell renal cell carcinoma.
- **Method**: Phase Ia/b dose-escalation trial (TRAVERSE; NCT04696731); modified 3+3 design; 51 patients; lymphodepletion ± anti-CD52 antibody.
- **Key findings**: ORR 17.4% overall, 25.0% in Phase Ib, and 31.3% in patients with CD70 tumour proportion score ≥50%; grade ≥3 CRS in 2%, severe neurotoxicity in 0%.
- **Strengths**: First proof-of-concept for allogeneic CAR T in solid tumours; long median follow-up (28.8 months); CD70 biomarker enrichment analysis.
- **Limitations**: Small Phase I sample (n=51), heterogeneous lymphodepletion regimens, no comparator arm.
- **Conclusion**: ALLO-316 has a manageable safety profile with encouraging efficacy in CD70-positive ccRCC, supporting further development.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1200/JCO-26-00388 / PMID:42447427

## 5. Neonatal Outcomes Following SSRI Use During Pregnancy: A Target Trial Emulation — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Netw Open
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-01
- **Authors**: Aref L et al.
- **Aims**: Apply target trial emulation to estimate the causal effect of SSRI continuation vs discontinuation during pregnancy on neonatal outcomes.
- **Method**: Cohort study emulating two target trials (periconception and early-pregnancy) in 1,014 linked mother-neonate EHR records; causal inference via intention-to-treat framework.
- **Key findings**: SSRI continuation associated with lower Apgar scores at 1 min (MD −0.39) and 5 min (MD −0.28), and increased meconium risk (OR 1.73); no significant increase in NICU admission or congenital anomalies.
- **Strengths**: Target trial emulation minimises comparator bias; linked EHR enables neonatal outcome ascertainment; methodologically instructive for pharmacoepidemiology.
- **Limitations**: Single academic centre, limited power for rare outcomes (congenital anomalies), no long-term infant follow-up.
- **Conclusion**: SSRI continuation causes impaired neonatal adaptation but not severe complications; illustrates target trial emulation as a template for drug safety causal inference.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.22790 / PMID:42440319
