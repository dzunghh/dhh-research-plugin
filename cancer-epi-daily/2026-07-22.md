## 1. Enfortumab Vedotin and Pembrolizumab in Cisplatin-Eligible Bladder Cancer — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: N Engl J Med
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-23
- **Authors**: Galsky MD et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate perioperative enfortumab vedotin–pembrolizumab (EV+P) vs neoadjuvant cisplatin-gemcitabine in cisplatin-eligible muscle-invasive bladder cancer.
- **Method**: Phase III open-label multicenter RCT (n=808); primary endpoint event-free survival; median follow-up 33.6 months.
- **Key findings**: 2-yr event-free survival 79.4% vs 66.2% (HR 0.53, 95% CI 0.41–0.70); pathological complete response 55.8% vs 32.5% (P<0.001).
- **Strengths**: Phase III RCT with overall survival as key secondary endpoint; large multicenter population, long follow-up.
- **Limitations**: Open-label design; grade ≥3 adverse events higher with EV+P (75.7% vs 67.2%).
- **Conclusion**: Perioperative EV+P significantly improves survival over cisplatin-gemcitabine, establishing a new standard of care.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1056/NEJMoa2601486 / PMID:42485627

## 2. Everything All at Once: On Choosing an Estimand for Multi-component Environmental Exposures — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-22
- **Authors**: Rudolph KE et al.
- **Aims**: Propose a causal estimand and nonparametric estimator for quantifying effects of multi-component environmental exposure mixtures on health outcomes.
- **Method**: Causal inference framework using stochastic shift interventions; fully nonparametric ML estimation; applied to CHAMACOS pesticide-hypertension longitudinal cohort.
- **Key findings**: Flexible estimand captures mixture-component interactions; ML approach avoids parametric misspecification; step-by-step code provided for replication.
- **Strengths**: Novel causal estimand for mixture research; fully nonparametric; open reproducible code; applicable to longitudinal settings.
- **Limitations**: Requires positivity assumption; complex implementation; primary application is hypertension, not cancer.
- **Conclusion**: Proposed estimand and ML estimator provide a rigorous, flexible framework for causal inference in environmental mixture epidemiology.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1097/EDE.0000000000002022 / PMID:42484456

## 3. Nesting a Target Study within a Target Trial: A Framework for Evaluating Intervention Effects on Disparities — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-21
- **Authors**: Sun X et al.
- **Aims**: Present a novel TS+TT causal framework nesting a target study within a target trial to generate ethically grounded evidence on intervention effects on health disparities.
- **Method**: Stratified enrollment ensuring social-group covariate balance; randomization within groups; semiparametric G-computation extended to continuous stochastic interventions and time-to-event outcomes.
- **Key findings**: TS+TT yields a principled disparity estimand grounded in allowability; demonstrated via pulse oximeter bias and racial treatment disparities in clinical care.
- **Strengths**: Integrates ethics-based allowability concept; extends G-computation for continuous interventions; versatile framework for disparity research.
- **Limitations**: Emulation requires appropriate observational data; social group classification can be complex; example is not cancer-specific.
- **Conclusion**: TS+TT offers a policy-relevant causal framework to evaluate whether interventions reduce or exacerbate health disparities.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1097/EDE.0000000000002024 / PMID:42479539

## 4. Occupational Benzene Exposure and Risk of Lung Cancer: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Eur J Epidemiol
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-21
- **Authors**: Seyyedsalehi MS et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate the association between occupational benzene exposure and lung cancer incidence and mortality via systematic review and meta-analysis.
- **Method**: Systematic review (PubMed, SCOPUS, EMBASE) and meta-analysis of 44 cohort and case-control studies; random-effects model; stratified analyses by study design, smoking adjustment, and exposure level.
- **Key findings**: No overall association (RR=1.00, 95% CI 0.93–1.07); smoking-adjusted studies RR=1.17 (95% CI 1.11–1.24); dose-response for high-level exposure (p-trend=0.03).
- **Strengths**: 44 independent studies; updated to 2024; dose-response analysis; publication bias excluded (p=0.16).
- **Limitations**: High heterogeneity (p≤0.001); benzene exposure misclassification likely; sparse high-exposure stratum data.
- **Conclusion**: Overall evidence null, but smoking-adjusted analyses and dose-response suggest occupational benzene may increase lung cancer risk at high exposures.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1007/s10654-026-01400-7 / PMID:42479407

## 5. The Effect of Older Age and Stage on Risk of Recurrence and Mortality Following Curative Resection, in a National Cohort of Patients with Colorectal Cancer — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Br J Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-21
- **Authors**: Almilaji O et al.
- **Aims**: Estimate recurrence, CRC-related mortality, and non-CRC mortality to 5 years in older (>75) vs younger CRC patients after curative resection.
- **Method**: National cohort linkage study; 50,480 curative resections in England (2014–2020); cause-specific cumulative incidence from linked cancer, treatment, and administrative datasets.
- **Key findings**: 5-yr recurrence-free survival 66.2% (age >75) vs 77.4% (age ≤75); non-CRC death risk among older patients (14.2%) equalled CRC death risk (13.3%).
- **Strengths**: Large national cohort (n=50,480); multiple linked administrative datasets; competing risks analysis; 5-year follow-up.
- **Limitations**: Recurrence ascertained via diagnostic/treatment codes, no imaging validation; selection bias toward fitter older patients chosen for surgery.
- **Conclusion**: Stage is the strongest recurrence predictor; older CRC patients are equally at risk of dying from non-cancer causes as from cancer recurrence.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41416-026-03553-4 / PMID:42481685
