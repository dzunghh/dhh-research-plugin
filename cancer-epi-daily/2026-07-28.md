## 1. Addition of irinotecan to chemoradiotherapy as preoperative treatment for locally advanced rectal cancer (ARISTOTLE): a multicentre, open-label, phase 3, randomised controlled trial — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-08
- **Authors**: Sebag-Montefiore D et al.
- **Aims**: To determine whether adding irinotecan to capecitabine-chemoradiotherapy improves disease-free survival in MRI-defined locally advanced rectal cancer.
- **Method**: Phase 3 RCT at 75 UK centres (n=564), 78-month median follow-up, modified ITT analysis; pre-specified stratification by centre, mesorectal fascia, and metastatic status.
- **Key findings**: 36-month DFS 68% (irinotecan) vs 67% (standard; HR 0.91, 95% CI 0.68–1.23; p=0.54). Grade ≥3 AEs substantially higher with irinotecan (78% vs 52%).
- **Strengths**: Large multi-centre RCT with 78-month follow-up and pre-specified stratification provides robust definitive null finding.
- **Limitations**: Open-label design; irinotecan arm had lower protocol-drug completion rates, potentially underestimating efficacy.
- **Conclusion**: Irinotecan addition to capecitabine-chemoradiotherapy does not improve DFS and should not be adopted in locally advanced rectal cancer.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00132-4 / PMID:42508427

## 2. Intravesical cretostimogene grenadenorepvec oncolytic immunotherapy in high-risk, BCG-unresponsive, non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer with carcinoma in situ (BOND-003 Cohort C): a single-arm, phase 3 trial — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-08
- **Authors**: Tyson MD et al.
- **Aims**: To evaluate efficacy and safety of intravesical cretostimogene in BCG-unresponsive high-risk NMIBC with carcinoma in situ.
- **Method**: Single-arm international phase 3 trial, 41 centres (North America, Asia, Australia), n=112, 25.8-month median follow-up; centrally confirmed complete response as primary endpoint.
- **Key findings**: Complete response at any time in 75% (83/110; 95% CI 66.3–83.2). No grade 3/4 treatment-related AEs; no treatment-related deaths.
- **Strengths**: Centrally confirmed primary endpoint, international multicentre design, and 25-month median follow-up strengthen internal validity.
- **Limitations**: Single-arm design precludes direct comparative inference; modest sample size.
- **Conclusion**: Cretostimogene delivers clinically meaningful anti-tumour response with manageable toxicity, advancing bladder-sparing treatment for BCG-unresponsive disease.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00194-4 / PMID:42508433

## 3. Postmastectomy chest wall radiotherapy for breast cancer (SUPREMO): 5-year quality-of-life results from a randomised, controlled, phase 3 trial — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-08
- **Authors**: Velikova G et al.
- **Aims**: To assess the 5-year QoL impact of adjuvant chest wall radiotherapy in intermediate-risk breast cancer after mastectomy.
- **Method**: QoL substudy of a phase 3 RCT (n=989 UK consenting patients), pre-specified EORTC instruments, repeated measures mixed-effects models.
- **Key findings**: Chest wall symptoms significantly worse with radiotherapy at 5 years (effect estimate 1.99; 95% CI 0.36–3.62; p=0.017). No significant differences in overall QoL, fatigue, physical function, anxiety, or depression.
- **Strengths**: Pre-specified QoL endpoints, four validated instruments, 80% consent rate, and 5-year longitudinal follow-up.
- **Limitations**: UK-only substudy with ~25% questionnaire attrition at year 5; not powered for subgroup analyses.
- **Conclusion**: Post-mastectomy chest wall radiotherapy causes persistent but isolated chest wall symptoms without broader QoL detriment at 5 years.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00122-1 / PMID:42508432

## 4. Oral health and diabetes: a systematic review and meta-analysis — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Public Health
- **Pub date**: 2026-08
- **Authors**: Botelho J et al.
- **Aims**: To synthesise longitudinal evidence on bidirectional associations between diabetes and oral diseases, including oral cancer.
- **Method**: PRISMA systematic review and meta-analysis of 28 longitudinal prospective studies (16 countries, >300,000 participants); random-effects meta-analysis using restricted maximum likelihood; Newcastle-Ottawa Scale quality assessment.
- **Key findings**: Periodontitis associated with 18–25% higher incidence of type 2 diabetes; tooth loss associated with HR 1.12–1.20 for incident T2DM. No longitudinal evidence identified for oral cancer.
- **Strengths**: Large evidence base (>300k participants), four-database search including grey literature, PRISMA-compliant, WHO-sponsored.
- **Limitations**: High heterogeneity across included studies; no longitudinal evidence available to synthesise for oral cancer specifically.
- **Conclusion**: Bidirectional periodontitis–diabetes associations support integrating oral health into interdisciplinary diabetes prevention and management.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S2468-2667(26)00149-0 / PMID:42480567

## 5. Consensus statements from the first Gynecologic Cancer InterGroup Cervical Cancer Consensus Conference in Clinical Research — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-08
- **Authors**: Kataria B et al.
- **Aims**: To develop harmonised consensus statements on cervical cancer clinical trial design, unmet needs, and research priorities.
- **Method**: Structured international consensus conference (Dublin, Oct 2024); 33 GCIG member groups plus patient advocates and LMIC representatives; 16 statements across 4 topic groups; debate-and-vote consensus process.
- **Key findings**: All 16 statements reached consensus, covering diagnosis/staging, early-stage, locally advanced, and persistent/recurrent disease; unmet needs in trial harmonisation across resource settings identified.
