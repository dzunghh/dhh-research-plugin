## 1. Efficacy and Toxicity of Antibody-Drug Conjugates in Older Adults with Breast Cancer — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-23
- **Authors**: Margolis S et al.
- **Aims**: Characterise ADC efficacy and toxicity specifically in older adults (≥65 years) with breast cancer, a population underrepresented in trials.
- **Method**: Systematic review and meta-analysis of 20 phase 2/3 RCTs (n=13,055; 18% aged ≥65) comparing ADC vs. non-ADC therapies; pooled HRs for PFS/OS by age stratum.
- **Key findings**: ADCs improved PFS in older (pHR 0.64, 95% CI 0.51–0.79) and younger adults (pHR 0.58, 95% CI 0.51–0.66) with no significant age-by-treatment interaction (ratio of HRs 1.10, 95% CI 0.91–1.33). Age-stratified toxicity data were unavailable in 17/20 trials.
- **Strengths**: Largest pooled analysis of age-stratified ADC data; highlights critical under-reporting gap in oncology RCTs.
- **Limitations**: Only 3 trials reported age-stratified safety data; early-stage disease data insufficient for pooling.
- **Conclusion**: ADC efficacy appears preserved in older adults with metastatic breast cancer, but evidence base for safety in this group remains inadequate.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag399 / PMID:42920260

## 2. Modeling the Survival Impact of the Inflation Reduction Act Out-of-Pocket Cap in Multiple Myeloma — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-23
- **Authors**: Zhao H et al.
- **Aims**: Predict survival gains from the 2025 IRA $2,000 annual OOP cap for Medicare Part D beneficiaries with newly diagnosed multiple myeloma.
- **Method**: Two independent CISNET natural-history simulation models parameterised with SEER-Medicare data (2012–2021); simulated life-years gained under realistic and best-case IMiD adherence scenarios.
- **Key findings**: Realistic scenario: 0.17–0.18 life-years gained (aged 66–75) and 0.20–0.26 (≥76), translating to 4,164–5,005 population-level life-years in 2025. Best-case: 26,893–32,347 LYG.
- **Strengths**: Dual-model approach reduces single-model uncertainty; uses SEER-Medicare for real-world parameterisation.
- **Limitations**: Modelling assumes stable treatment guidelines; does not account for comorbidity heterogeneity or non-adherence unrelated to cost.
- **Conclusion**: The IRA OOP cap is projected to deliver meaningful survival gains for Medicare beneficiaries with multiple myeloma, warranting real-world validation.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag396 / PMID:42924351

## 3. A Clinical Model Incorporating Early Lymphocyte Dynamics in HER2-Positive Breast Cancer — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-24
- **Authors**: Bergstrom CP et al.
- **Aims**: Develop a scalable biomarker model using routine peripheral blood absolute lymphocyte count (ALC) changes to predict pathologic complete response (pCR) after neoadjuvant therapy in HER2+ breast cancer.
- **Method**: Retrospective discovery cohort (n=154) and separate validation cohort (n=83); multivariable logistic regression incorporating baseline ALC, post-cycle-1 ALC change, age, ER/PR status, HER2 IHC score, and tumour stage.
- **Key findings**: Model AUC 0.86 (discovery) and 0.82 (validation); pCR rate ranged from 12% in lowest-probability quartile to 79% in highest.
- **Strengths**: Uses universally available routine bloods; two-cohort design with independent validation.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective, single-institution; small cohorts; does not include pertuzumab or T-DM1 regimens.
- **Conclusion**: Early ALC dynamics offer a simple, scalable predictor of neoadjuvant response in HER2+ breast cancer that could guide early treatment adaptation.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag400 / PMID:42921756

## 4. Employment after Curative Breast Cancer Treatment: The Breast Cancer and the Workforce Cohort Study — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-23
- **Authors**: Blinder VS et al.
- **Aims**: Quantify post-treatment employment rates and identify sociodemographic predictors of work status in a diverse urban breast cancer cohort.
- **Method**: Prospective cohort (n=489 pre-diagnosis employed women, stage I–III, NYC); surveys at baseline, 4 months post-treatment, 1 year, and 2 years; multivariable logistic regression.
- **Key findings**: 68.6% working at 4 months, rising to 77.3% at 2 years; Black, Chinese, and Latina women had significantly higher cancer-attributable job-loss rates vs. white peers; chemotherapy (OR not specified) and demands at work were independently inversely associated.
- **Strengths**: Prospective design, diverse NYC cohort, comparison to race/ethnicity-matched cancer-free peers removes baseline unemployment confounding.
- **Limitations**: Single-city, single treatment era; self-reported employment; limited power for subgroup analysis by cancer stage.
- **Conclusion**: Breast cancer disproportionately disrupts employment for minority women; paid sick leave and employer accommodations are key policy levers.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag384 / PMID:42924356

## 5. Clinical Performance and User-Informed Refinement of an Indigenous HPV Self-Collection Device for Cervical Cancer Screening in Resource-Constrained Settings — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiology, Biomarkers & Prevention
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-22
- **Authors**: Singhal S et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate iterative clinical performance of three successive CERVICHECK™ HPV self-collection device prototypes against a physician-collected reference standard in Indian women.
- **Method**: Prospective study, n=756 (743 paired samples); self-collected samples tested with indigenous PCR-based HPV assay; reference standard Cobas 6800; primary endpoint CIN2+ sensitivity.
- **Key findings**: Final prototype (P3) achieved 93.6% clinical sensitivity for CIN2+ (95% CI 78.6–99.2) vs. 40.0% with prototype P1; relative clinical sensitivity 1.0 vs. physician collection.
- **Strengths**: Iterative user-informed design with clear performance milestones; relevant to global cervical cancer elimination goals.
- **Limitations**: Single-centre India study; CIN2+ cases small (n not explicitly stated per prototype); no cost-effectiveness analysis.
- **Conclusion**: User-informed iterative refinement of a low-cost indigenous HPV self-collection device achieved clinical performance equivalent to physician sampling for CIN2+ detection.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-0211 / PMID:42911102
