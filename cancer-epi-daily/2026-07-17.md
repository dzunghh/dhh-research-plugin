## 1. Continuous or Fixed-Duration Maintenance Therapy in Multiple Myeloma — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: N Engl J Med
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-16
- **Authors**: Kumar S et al.
- **Aims**: Determine whether indefinite vs 2-year fixed-duration lenalidomide maintenance improves overall survival in standard-risk newly diagnosed multiple myeloma (no upfront transplant).
- **Method**: Phase 3 RCT (ENDURANCE; NCT01863550); 516 randomized; median follow-up 86 months; ECOG-ACRIN multicenter.
- **Key findings**: No significant OS difference (7-year OS 68.6% vs 69.0%, P=0.93); grade ≥3 non-hematologic events 48.2% vs 31.5% for indefinite vs fixed-duration.
- **Strengths**: Phase 3 RCT with long-term follow-up (86 months), 80% power, multicenter design.
- **Limitations**: Restricted to standard-risk patients; excludes those receiving upfront autologous stem-cell transplantation.
- **Conclusion**: Fixed 2-year lenalidomide maintenance is non-inferior to indefinite therapy in OS while substantially reducing toxicity.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1056/NEJMoa2600157 / PMID:42456135

## 2. Contemporary Hormonal Contraception and Colorectal Cancer in Premenopausal Women: Nationwide Cohort Study — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: BMJ
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-15
- **Authors**: Lange IH et al.
- **Aims**: Assess effect of contemporary hormonal contraceptive types on colorectal cancer risk in reproductive-age women.
- **Method**: Nationwide Danish register-based cohort study (n=1,956,948; 24.5M person-years, 1995–2021); adjusted incidence rate ratios by contraceptive type and duration.
- **Key findings**: Current/recent use vs never: IRR 0.94 (95% CI 0.83–1.06); >10 years use IRR 0.86 (95% CI 0.62–1.18); no substantial effect by contraceptive type.
- **Strengths**: Near-total national coverage, 24.5 million person-years, active comparisons by product type and duration of use.
- **Limitations**: Observational design; residual confounding possible; some subgroup estimates imprecise due to small cell sizes.
- **Conclusion**: Contemporary hormonal contraception does not substantially alter colorectal cancer risk, unlikely to explain rising incidence in young women.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1136/bmj-2026-100065 / PMID:42457240

## 3. Recurrence Score Gene Group Components and Outcomes in the RxPONDER Trial (SWOG S1007) — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: J Natl Cancer Inst
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-15
- **Authors**: Abdou Y et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate Recurrence Score gene group components (proliferation, ER, HER2, invasion) by ethnicity and their association with outcomes in node-positive HR+/HER2- breast cancer.
- **Method**: Secondary analysis of RxPONDER RCT; 3,102 women with ethnicity and gene-expression data; multivariable Cox models for IDFS and distant recurrence-free interval.
- **Key findings**: Non-Hispanic Black women had significantly higher proliferation scores vs Non-Hispanic White (p=0.003); IDFS HR for NHB vs NHW 1.41 (95% CI 0.98–2.03, non-significant); Asian women had improved IDFS (HR 0.63, 95% CI 0.43–0.91).
- **Strengths**: Large RCT-embedded dataset, pre-specified gene-group analysis, ethnicity-stratified multivariable models.
- **Limitations**: Small NHB subgroup (n=145) limits precision; observational gene-expression component within trial.
- **Conclusion**: Gene-group components differ by ethnicity beyond composite RS, supporting biology-specific investigation to address outcome disparities.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag221 / PMID:42458791

## 4. Prevalence of Industry Ties in the US Cancer Ecosystem: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: J Natl Cancer Inst
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-15
- **Authors**: Kim CJ et al.
- **Aims**: Estimate prevalence of financial conflicts of interest across six domains of the US cancer ecosystem.
- **Method**: Systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis; Ovid MEDLINE, Embase, Scopus, Web of Science, HADB; 36 eligible studies published since 2015.
- **Key findings**: >50% COI prevalence across all six domains; pooled industry sponsorship in trials 69% (95% CI 62–77%); payments to oncologists 61% (95% CI 47–75%).
- **Strengths**: Comprehensive multi-domain analysis, formal meta-analysis with pooled estimates, multi-database search.
- **Limitations**: High heterogeneity between studies; publication bias possible; restricted to studies from 2015 onward.
- **Conclusion**: Financial conflicts of interest exceed 50% across the US cancer ecosystem, warranting close monitoring to protect clinical decision-making.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag175 / PMID:42448352

## 5. Perioperative Tislelizumab Plus Chemotherapy for Locally Advanced Gastric Cancer: A Randomized, Prospective Phase 2 Trial — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Cell
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-15
- **Authors**: Xu Z et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate whether tsMHC-II expression predicts benefit from adding tislelizumab to perioperative chemotherapy in locally advanced gastric/GEJ cancer.
- **Method**: Biomarker-stratified, randomized, multicenter phase 2 trial (Mountain-02; NCT06374901); 136 patients; primary endpoint major pathological response rate in tsMHC-II+ stratum.
- **Key findings**: tsMHC-II+ patients: mPR 61.8% vs 26.5% chemotherapy alone (p=0.003); no significant benefit in tsMHC-II- subgroup; pCR 32.4% vs 5.9% (p=0.006) in tsMHC-II+ combination arm.
- **Strengths**: Pre-specified biomarker-stratified randomized design, multicenter, novel predictive marker prospectively tested.
- **Limitations**: Phase 2 only; small per-subgroup N; tsMHC-II biomarker requires independent validation in larger studies.
- **Conclusion**: tsMHC-II is a promising predictive biomarker for perioperative immunotherapy in gastric cancer warranting phase 3 validation.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.ccell.2026.06.015 / PMID:42456653
