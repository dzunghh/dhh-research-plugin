## 1. Subclinical cholestasis is a hallmark of gut dysbiosis causing resistance to cancer immunotherapy — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Cell
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-29
- **Authors**: Mallard de La Varende AL et al.
- **Aims**: Identify how antibiotic-associated gut dysbiosis metabolically mediates resistance to PD-1 immune checkpoint blockade.
- **Method**: Murine antibiotic/Enterocloster models with metabolomics (bile acid profiling); validated in 6 independent patient cohorts receiving ICI.
- **Key findings**: Dysbiosis causes tauro-conjugated bile acid (TCDCA/T-βMCA) accumulation and loss of secondary BAs; elevated γGT (subclinical cholestasis) predicted poor ICI survival in multivariate analyses across all 6 cohorts.
- **Strengths**: Integrates mechanistic murine work with multi-cohort translational clinical validation; identifies novel actionable biomarker.
- **Limitations**: Causal reversal (FMT/fexaramine) demonstrated only in mice; γGT is non-specific and may be confounded by liver comorbidities.
- **Conclusion**: Subclinical cholestasis (γGT elevation) is a clinically measurable predictor of ICI resistance linked to gut microbiome dysbiosis.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.ccell.2026.06.022 / PMID:42526445

## 2. Cemiplimab and Fianlimab With Neoadjuvant Chemotherapy in Early-Stage High-Risk ERBB2-Negative Breast Cancer: The I-SPY2 Randomized Clinical Trial — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-30
- **Authors**: Isaacs C et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate dual PD-1+LAG-3 checkpoint blockade (cemiplimab+fianlimab) added to neoadjuvant chemotherapy in ERBB2-negative high-risk breast cancer.
- **Method**: Adaptive Bayesian platform RCT (I-SPY2); n=78 intervention vs n=350 historical control; pCR primary endpoint; ImPrint immune biomarker sub-analysis.
- **Key findings**: pCR 44% vs 21% (all ERBB2-neg); 53% vs 29% (TNBC); ImPrint-positive patients derived greatest benefit; adrenal insufficiency in 21%.
- **Strengths**: Adaptive design enables biomarker-stratified signal detection across receptor subtypes efficiently; multi-site US platform with standardized endpoints.
- **Limitations**: Historical rather than concurrent control; per-subtype sample sizes are small; no long-term survival data yet.
- **Conclusion**: Dual PD-1+LAG-3 inhibition approximately doubles pCR rates in ERBB2-negative breast cancer, with ImPrint positivity as a predictive biomarker warranting phase 3 confirmation.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamaoncol.2026.2576 / PMID:42530945

## 3. Transarterial Chemoembolization Plus Thermal Ablation in Unresectable Hepatocellular Carcinoma: The Phase 3 TORCH Randomized Clinical Trial — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-30
- **Authors**: Lyu N et al.
- **Aims**: Determine whether sequential TACE plus thermal ablation improves clinical outcomes vs TACE alone in liver-confined unresectable HCC.
- **Method**: Phase 3 open-label RCT; n=241 BCLC stage B HCC patients; primary endpoint PFS per RECIST v1.1; secondary OS, untreatable PFS, safety.
- **Key findings**: Median PFS 17.7 vs 7.3 months (HR 0.47, 95% CI 0.34–0.65, p<.001); median OS 88.6 vs 35.1 months (HR 0.50, p<.001).
- **Strengths**: Phase 3 RCT with long data maturity; large OS benefit magnitude; subgroup analysis by tumor burden score aids patient selection.
- **Limitations**: Single-country two-centre study (China); no systemic therapy comparator arm; open-label design risks procedural bias.
- **Conclusion**: TACE combined with thermal ablation is superior to TACE alone in liver-confined unresectable HCC and could revise locoregional treatment standards.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamaoncol.2026.2366 / PMID:42530948

## 4. Investigation of a prostate cancer radiation response signature in the breast cancer SweBCG91RT randomized trial — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Clinical Cancer Research
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-30
- **Authors**: Zhao SG et al.
- **Aims**: Test whether PORTOS, a 24-gene RT response signature validated in prostate cancer RCTs, predicts radiotherapy benefit in breast cancer.
- **Method**: Biomarker analysis of SweBCG91RT RCT (adjuvant whole-breast RT vs observation; n=765 tumour samples); gene expression profiling; 10-year cumulative recurrence incidence primary endpoint.
- **Key findings**: Significant PORTOS×RT interaction (p=0.049); top 75% PORTOS achieved SHR=0.46 (p<0.001) with RT vs SHR=0.73 (ns) in bottom 25%; higher PORTOS correlated with RT-related breast pain.
- **Strengths**: First biomarker to predict RT efficacy AND toxicity across randomised trials in two cancer types; orthogonal to existing breast cancer signatures ARTIC/POLAR.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective post-hoc biomarker analysis; interaction p-value marginal; no prospective breast cancer validation yet.
- **Conclusion**: PORTOS is the first cross-tumour-type radiation response biomarker with dual predictive utility for benefit and toxicity, ready for prospective breast cancer validation.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-26-1455 / PMID:42531378

## 5. Neoadjuvant olaparib and pembrolizumab combination therapy in the treatment of patients with HRD-positive advanced ovarian cancer: A pilot study — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Clinical Cancer Research
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-30
- **Authors**: Harano K et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate feasibility and activity of neoadjuvant olaparib ± pembrolizumab in HRD-positive advanced ovarian cancer.
- **Method**: Pilot study; Cohort 1 olaparib monotherapy (n=10), Cohort 2 olaparib+pembrolizumab (n=20); scRNA-seq tumour microenvironment profiling; ORR primary endpoint.
- **Key findings**: ORR 50% (Cohort 1) vs 70% (Cohort 2, p=0.28); BRCA1/2-mutated ORR 84.6% with combination; combination increased CD8+ T-cell infiltration without unexpected toxicities.
- **Strengths**: First neoadjuvant PARP+ICI combination data with single-cell immune profiling; mechanistic insight into olaparib-mediated immune priming.
- **Limitations**: Small non-randomised pilot (n=30); ORR difference non-significant; STING pathway activation not confirmed in clinical samples.
- **Conclusion**: Olaparib+pembrolizumab shows immune-activating potential in HRD-positive ovarian cancer, but definitive efficacy requires a randomised trial.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-25-4887 / PMID:42531373

## Appraisal notes

This week's digest is dominated by high-quality randomised evidence: two phase 3/platform RCTs (TORCH HCC, I-SPY2 breast) and a secondary biomarker analysis of an RCT (SweBCG91RT), reflecting a maturing trial pipeline in locoregional and immunotherapy-combination strategies. A methodological theme is predictive biomarker development across journal tiers — ImPrint for dual checkpoint blockade and PORTOS for radiotherapy generalise beyond their discovery cancer types, illustrating growing interest in pan-cancer treatment response signatures. The gut microbiome–ICI resistance axis (Cancer Cell) adds mechanistic epidemiology: γGT as a routinely available clinical lab value for ICI prognosis represents a potentially scalable, low-cost biomarker if validated prospectively.
