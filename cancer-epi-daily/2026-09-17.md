## 1. Phenotypic and Genotypic Risk Factors for Uveal Melanoma in a High-Ambient UV Radiation Environment — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiol Biomarkers Prev
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-11
- **Authors**: Palmer JM et al.
- **Aims**: To identify genetic and phenotypic determinants of uveal melanoma (UM) risk using a large population-based cohort in Queensland, Australia.
- **Method**: Case-control study (485 UM cases, 43,724 QSkin cohort controls) with polygenic risk scores and Mendelian randomization for pigmentation traits.
- **Key findings**: Blue/light eye color, inability to tan, and high nevus density strongly associated with UM risk; MR confirmed causal roles for lighter eye color, freckling, and reduced tanning ability.
- **Strengths**: Large control base (43k), integrates MR causal inference with classic epidemiology for a rare cancer.
- **Limitations**: Cases recruited via specialty clinics; potential ascertainment bias and limited to Queensland.
- **Conclusion**: Genetic evidence supports causal roles for pigmentation traits in UM susceptibility, informing targeted risk counselling.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-0734 / PMID:42725865

## 2. SHBG as a Breast Cancer Risk Biomarker in Premenopausal Women? Results from a Pooled Analysis and Meta-Analysis — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: J Natl Cancer Inst
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-11
- **Authors**: Serrano D et al.
- **Aims**: To evaluate whether SHBG level predicts breast cancer risk in premenopausal women via individual patient data and meta-analysis.
- **Method**: Individual patient pooled analysis from two tamoxifen chemoprevention trials plus meta-analysis of 11 studies (35,167 premenopausal women, 2,302 BC events).
- **Key findings**: Higher SHBG associated with 40% lower BC hazard in high-risk premenopausal cohort (HR=0.60; 95% CI 0.38–0.96); general premenopausal meta-analysis non-significant (SOR=0.92, 95% CI 0.78–1.09).
- **Strengths**: Individual patient data from trials; menopausal stratification; contrasts high-risk vs. general population.
- **Limitations**: Few studies stratified by menopausal status; high-risk trial population may not generalize.
- **Conclusion**: SHBG is a prognostic biomarker in high-risk premenopausal women but does not confirm risk prediction in the general population.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag318 / PMID:42726095

## 3. Ancestry-Dependent Immunologic and Prognostic Effects Characterize the Prostate Cancer Urinary Proteome — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: J Natl Cancer Inst
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-11
- **Authors**: Ha A et al.
- **Aims**: To quantify ancestry-associated differences in the urinary proteome of localized prostate cancer patients.
- **Method**: Cross-sectional proteomics in 329 patients (135 White, 194 Black), controlling for age, PSA, and cISUP grade.
- **Key findings**: 110 proteins differed significantly between groups; immune pathways particularly dysregulated; Black patients' proteome harboured higher-grade features despite grade matching.
- **Strengths**: Largest ancestry-stratified urinary proteomics dataset in prostate cancer; addresses health-equity gap in biomarker development.
- **Limitations**: Single-institution study; residual confounding by unmeasured lifestyle/diet factors possible.
- **Conclusion**: Ancestry-associated urinary proteomic differences must be addressed to develop race-equitable prostate cancer biomarkers.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag329 / PMID:42730607

## 4. Association of Low Serum Vitamin B12 Concentration with Colorectal Cancer Risk and All-Cause Mortality: A Global Multi-Institutional Retrospective Cohort Study — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiol Biomarkers Prev
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-11
- **Authors**: Tseng YT et al.
- **Aims**: To evaluate low serum vitamin B12 as a risk factor for colorectal cancer incidence and all-cause mortality.
- **Method**: Propensity score-matched retrospective cohort (184,126 pairs) from TriNetX Global Network (2006–2020); landmark, E-value, and negative-control analyses applied.
- **Key findings**: Low B12 (<200 pg/mL) associated with higher CRC risk (HR 1.16, 95% CI 1.09–1.23) and all-cause mortality (HR 1.16, 95% CI 1.14–1.18); CRC association attenuated at 3- and 5-year landmarks.
- **Strengths**: Very large global dataset; propensity matching; landmark and negative-control sensitivity analyses.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective design; single B12 measurement; CRC association not robust at longer follow-up.
- **Conclusion**: Low B12 is robustly linked to mortality; CRC association is suggestive but requires prospective confirmation.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-0761 / PMID:42725864

## 5. Association of Area-Level Broadband Access and Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer Diagnosis and Guideline-Concordant Care in the US — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiol Biomarkers Prev
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-11
- **Authors**: Fan Q et al.
- **Aims**: To examine whether area-level broadband access is associated with NSCLC stage at diagnosis, timely treatment, and guideline-concordant care.
- **Method**: Retrospective cohort (525,114 NSCLC patients, NCDB 2018–2022); ZIP-code broadband quartiles; multivariable logistic regression stratified by metropolitan status and pandemic period.
- **Key findings**: Highest vs. lowest broadband quartile linked to early-stage diagnosis (AOR 1.18–1.21), timely treatment (AOR 1.13–1.14), and guideline-concordant care (AOR 1.11–1.14); rural pandemic-era benefit most pronounced.
- **Strengths**: Very large national sample (525k); pre/during pandemic comparison; rural vs. urban stratification.
- **Limitations**: Area-level broadband metric (ZIP code); may reflect broader SES rather than broadband independently.
- **Conclusion**: Broadband access is a significant digital social determinant of NSCLC care quality, particularly amplified by telehealth expansion in rural areas.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-0708 / PMID:42726080

## Appraisal notes

This week's digest highlights the growing integration of Mendelian randomization and polygenic risk scores into cancer epidemiology, as seen in the uveal melanoma study combining classic case-control design with genomic causal inference. A second theme is equity: both the ancestry-stratified prostate cancer proteomics and the broadband/NSCLC studies draw attention to structural and biological factors that shape differential cancer outcomes, reflecting an important methodological shift toward health-equity–sensitive study design. The pooled SHBG analysis exemplifies careful stratification by population type (high-risk trial participants vs. general population), a distinction that should become standard when translating biomarker findings to screening guidelines.
