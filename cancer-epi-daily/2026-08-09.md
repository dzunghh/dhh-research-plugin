## 1. Priorities for breast cancer risk assessment in UK women under age 50 — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: British Journal of Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-04
- **Authors**: Dennison RA et al.
- **Aims**: To understand women's preferences for different breast cancer risk assessment strategies in UK primary care for those under 50.
- **Method**: Cross-sectional survey with discrete choice experiment (n=893), conditional logistic regression over six risk-assessment attributes.
- **Key findings**: Participants preferred proactive invitation-based multifactorial risk assessment over current family-history-only care; 20 of 21 alternative approaches had >92% probability of being preferred.
- **Strengths**: Quantified real-world preferences for multifactorial BOADICEA/CanRisk-based models; large and well-powered sample.
- **Limitations**: UK primary care context; survey preferences may not translate directly to actual uptake behaviour.
- **Conclusion**: Proactive, population-wide multifactorial breast cancer risk assessment is acceptable and preferred by younger women in primary care.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41416-026-03546-3 / PMID:42547566

## 2. Trends and correlates of smokeless tobacco use among Indian women: Evidence from three NFHS rounds 2005–2021 — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: PLoS One
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-03
- **Authors**: Barik M et al.
- **Aims**: To compare smokeless tobacco (SLT) prevalence trends and associated factors among Indian reproductive-age women across three survey rounds.
- **Method**: Repeated cross-sectional analysis of three nationally representative NFHS rounds (n up to 724,115); multivariable logistic regression per wave.
- **Key findings**: SLT prevalence fell from 8.4% (2005–06) to 3.8% (2019–21); household smoking exposure was a persistent predictor (AOR ~1.85 in NFHS-5).
- **Strengths**: Three rounds of very large nationally representative surveys; temporal trend analysis captures policy-period effects.
- **Limitations**: Limited to women 15–49; cross-sectional design precludes causal inference on determinants.
- **Conclusion**: SLT use is declining in India but geographic and sociodemographic disparities remain, requiring targeted oral-cancer-prevention efforts in high-burden states.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1371/journal.pone.0353704 / PMID:42545929

## 3. Prevalence and associated factors of Leukemia in Africa: A systematic review and meta-analysis — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: PLoS One
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-03
- **Authors**: Gedfie S et al.
- **Aims**: To determine pooled leukemia prevalence and key risk factors across African countries through systematic review.
- **Method**: PRISMA-compliant meta-analysis of 15 studies (n=42,884) using random-effects model in STATA; subgroup analyses by country, year, and population; Egger's test for publication bias.
- **Key findings**: Pooled leukemia prevalence 5.09% (95% CI 4.02–6.17); rural residency (OR 56.98) and older age (OR 3.90) were significant predictors.
- **Strengths**: First pan-African pooled estimate; country- and period-level subgroup analyses highlight geographic variation.
- **Limitations**: Very high heterogeneity (I²=97.1%); only 15 eligible studies; prevalence metric incomparable across clinical versus community settings.
- **Conclusion**: Leukemia burden in Africa is substantial and unevenly distributed, requiring strengthened cancer registries and targeted surveillance.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1371/journal.pone.0354814 / PMID:42545965
