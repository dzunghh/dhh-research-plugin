## 1. Benign Breast Diagnoses and Future Breast Cancer Risk — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiol Biomarkers Prev
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-18
- **Authors**: Sattayapiwat O et al.
- **Aims**: Estimate risk associations between 38 specific benign breast biopsy diagnoses and future invasive breast cancer and DCIS.
- **Method**: Retrospective registry cohort of 131,075 biopsy episodes from 8 BCSC sites (1994–2022); Fine-Gray competing risk models; negative-screening control group (N=1,599,694).
- **Key findings**: Lobular carcinoma in situ conferred 5.61-fold invasive cancer risk; proliferative lesions with atypia yielded invasive HRs of 2.76–4.85; associations declined over time.
- **Strengths**: Largest granular BBD-cancer linkage study; multi-site design, competing risk methodology, median 7.5-year follow-up.
- **Limitations**: Pathological classification variation across sites; no individual genomic risk data.
- **Conclusion**: BBD-cancer risk varies widely within histologic categories, supporting tailored screening and prevention strategies.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-0640 / PMID:42611528

## 2. Ovarian Reserve Predicts Chemotherapy Benefit in Breast Cancer (RxPONDER) — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Ann Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-24
- **Authors**: Kalinsky K et al.
- **Aims**: Assess whether ovarian reserve markers predict adjuvant chemotherapy benefit in premenopausal HR+/HER2- node-positive breast cancer with recurrence score ≤25.
- **Method**: Pre-specified biomarker analysis of Phase 3 SWOG S1007 (RxPONDER) RCT; 1,556 women <55 years; Cox regression with treatment-by-marker interaction testing.
- **Key findings**: Women with AMH ≥10 pg/mL (64%) showed superior IDFS with chemotherapy (HR 0.46, p=0.00012); those with AMH <10 pg/mL had no chemotherapy benefit (HR 1.27).
- **Strengths**: Phase 3 RCT, prospectively collected pre-treatment samples, pre-specified analysis, large N.
- **Limitations**: AMH assay standardization varies across labs; findings limited to premenopausal patients aged <55.
- **Conclusion**: Ovarian reserve (AMH) outperforms menopause status in predicting chemotherapy benefit, enabling more precise patient selection.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.annonc.2026.05.697 / PMID:42613139

## 3. Urine-Based Multiplex Assay for Urothelial Cancer Detection in Gross Hematuria — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: J Natl Cancer Inst
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-17
- **Authors**: Lotan Y et al.
- **Aims**: Prospectively validate Oncuria-Detect as a non-invasive urothelial cancer detection tool in gross hematuria patients.
- **Method**: Prospective multicenter cohort; 450 participants across 9 US/Japan centers (2016–2025); sensitivity and NPV vs BladderChek and urine cytology; cystoscopy as reference standard.
- **Key findings**: Oncuria-Detect sensitivity 82.6% vs BladderChek 16.4% and cytology 35.7%; NPV 87.2%.
- **Strengths**: Prospective multicenter international design; head-to-head comparison with established tests; NIH-funded.
- **Limitations**: 10-year enrollment window may mask temporal variability; UC prevalence 21.6% may not generalise.
- **Conclusion**: Oncuria-Detect substantially outperforms existing urine tests for non-invasive bladder cancer risk stratification.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag282 / PMID:42608795

## 4. RAS-Mediated Resistance in Oncogene-Driven Lung Cancer — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Ann Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-18
- **Authors**: Facchinetti F et al.
- **Aims**: Characterise RAS alterations as off-target resistance mechanisms across oncogene-driven lung cancers and evaluate RAS inhibitor combinations preclinically.
- **Method**: Retrospective analysis of 590 patients using paired tissue and liquid biopsies; two patient-derived KRAS-mutant osimertinib-resistant models tested with zoldonrasib and daraxonrasib in vitro and in vivo.
- **Key findings**: RAS alterations in 11.2% of osimertinib progressors; more common at lorlatinib vs 2G ALK inhibitor resistance (14.7% vs 5%, p=0.044); combination therapy showed synergistic preclinical activity.
- **Strengths**: Large multi-tumor-type cohort; dual biopsy strategy; functional patient-derived model validation.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective; preclinical combination data only; awaits clinical trial confirmation.
- **Conclusion**: RAS inhibitor combinations with driver-targeted agents show strong preclinical rationale, supporting clinical investigation.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.annonc.2026.08.004 / PMID:42612796

## 5. Childhood Residence Near Coldwater Creek Radiation Contamination and Cancer Mortality — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Int J Epidemiol
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-18
- **Authors**: Leung M et al.
- **Aims**: Examine whether childhood proximity to Manhattan Project-contaminated Coldwater Creek, Missouri, was associated with long-term mortality.
- **Method**: Historical cohort: Baby Tooth Survey (1958) linked to the National Death Index; 43,876 eligible participants; Cox regression adjusted for sex, birth year, census-tract socioeconomics.
- **Key findings**: Closer creek residence associated with higher all-cause mortality (HR 1.05 per 10 km, p=0.01) and suggestive radiosensitive cancer mortality increase (HR 1.10, p=0.05).
- **Strengths**: Large unique historical cohort; NDI linkage; census-tract socioeconomic adjustment; novel exposure-mortality dataset.
- **Limitations**: Residential distance used as ecological radiation proxy; no individual dosimetry; residual confounding possible.
- **Conclusion**: Childhood residence near Coldwater Creek was associated with higher mortality, with suggestive cancer signal warranting further study.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/ije/dyag166 / PMID:42613029
