## 1. Obesity phenotypes, omics signatures, and risk of adverse liver outcomes — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: J Natl Cancer Inst
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-21
- **Authors**: Zhao L et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate associations of obesity phenotypes and multi-omics signatures with liver cancer and adverse liver outcomes.
- **Method**: UK Biobank cohort (n=451,463), Cox regression, elastic-net NMR metabolomics and Olink proteomics signatures.
- **Key findings**: Metabolically unhealthy obesity (MUO) yielded the highest liver cancer risk (HR up to 3.68); MUO omics signatures showed stronger associations (HR 1.58–6.13).
- **Strengths**: Very large sample, dual-omics platform, 11-year follow-up, internally validated signatures.
- **Limitations**: UK Biobank non-representative; liver outcomes from registry records may underascertain mild disease.
- **Conclusion**: MUO omics profiles reveal metabolic dysfunction beyond adiposity as a key driver of liver cancer risk.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag242 / PMID:42482169

## 2. Design-Outcome Concordance in Targeted-Therapy Trials in Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: J Natl Cancer Inst
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-20
- **Authors**: Mastrantoni L et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate concordance between design assumptions and observed effects in phase III NSCLC targeted-therapy RCTs.
- **Method**: Systematic review of 45 RCTs (2005–2025), meta-analysis, retrodesign analysis (power, type S error, exaggeration ratio), RMST vs HR.
- **Key findings**: Observed pooled HR was 0.47 vs assumed 0.64; observed-to-expected HR ratio 0.76; 41% of trials had <1% probability of exceeding planned HR.
- **Strengths**: First comprehensive retrodesign meta-analysis in NSCLC; combines frequentist, Bayesian, and RMST frameworks.
- **Limitations**: Restricted to PFS; heterogeneous trial populations limit cross-trial comparisons.
- **Conclusion**: NSCLC trials systematically underestimate targeted therapy effects; retrodesign metrics and RMST should be routinely reported.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag241 / PMID:42477876

## 3. Metabolomic signatures of persistent smoking effects and lung cancer risk among former smokers — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiol Biomarkers Prev
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-20
- **Authors**: Le DH et al.
- **Aims**: Develop a metabolite signature capturing persistent smoking effects and assess its prospective association with lung cancer risk in former smokers.
- **Method**: Nested case-control in SMHS + SCCS cohorts (n=1,732 total); elastic-net regression to derive MSPS; conditional logistic regression.
- **Key findings**: 16-metabolite MSPS associated with lung cancer risk (OR=1.44 per SD, 95%CI:1.15–1.81), independent of pack-years and cessation duration; consistent across White and Black participants.
- **Strengths**: Multi-cohort, multi-racial validation; MSPS captures residual risk beyond standard smoking metrics.
- **Limitations**: Association non-significant in Asian subgroup; external metabolomics validation not yet performed.
- **Conclusion**: MSPS enables refined lung cancer risk stratification in former smokers beyond conventional smoking history.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-25-2011 / PMID:42474280

## 4. Prospective national precision medicine trial of blood-based molecular profiling in Cancer of Unknown Primary (CUP-COMP) — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Br J Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-20
- **Authors**: Conway AM et al.
- **Aims**: Assess feasibility of integrating liquid biopsy molecular profiling with a national Molecular Tumour Board into CUP clinical pathways.
- **Method**: Multicenter prospective phase 2 trial (n=117, 7 UK sites); blood- and tissue-based profiling; MTB-guided treatment; 12-month follow-up.
- **Key findings**: Blood profiling succeeded in 93% vs tissue 61%; MTB identified actionable alterations in 63%; 26% received precision medicine; median OS 9.5 months.
- **Strengths**: First national-scale liquid biopsy trial in CUP; demonstrates clinical feasibility of liquid biopsy over tissue.
- **Limitations**: Non-randomized; low tumour fraction (<1%) in 43% limited actionable alteration detection.
- **Conclusion**: Liquid biopsy is highly feasible in CUP and a viable complement to tissue profiling for molecular tumour board-guided treatment.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41416-026-03519-6 / PMID:42477427

## 5. Dalpiciclib plus endocrine therapy in HR+/HER2− advanced breast cancer with visceral crisis — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Nat Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-21
- **Authors**: Mo H et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate dalpiciclib plus endocrine therapy efficacy and safety in HR+/HER2− advanced breast cancer patients experiencing visceral crisis.
- **Method**: Multicenter, nonrandomized phase 2 trial (n=53); primary endpoint: 6-month survival rate; exploratory cfDNA analysis.
- **Key findings**: 6-month survival rate 92.5% (95%CI:81.8–97.9); ORR 26.4%; median PFS 11.2 months; low baseline monocyte-derived cfDNA associated with worse OS (HR=4.79).
- **Strengths**: Addresses a high-mortality, trial-excluded subgroup; introduces cfDNA 'molecular crisis' biomarker concept.
- **Limitations**: Single-arm, non-randomized; exploratory cfDNA analysis underpowered; no comparator arm.
- **Conclusion**: Dalpiciclib plus ET shows promising survival activity in visceral crisis, with cfDNA as a potential molecular stratifier for future trials.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s43018-026-01208-0 / PMID:42481711
