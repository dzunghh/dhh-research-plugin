## 1. Occupational Solar Ultraviolet Radiation Exposure and Incident Ocular Melanoma Risk in Two Large-Scale Nordic Cohorts — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-10
- **Authors**: Turner MC et al.
- **Aims**: To investigate whether occupational solar UVR exposure increases ocular melanoma risk.
- **Method**: Prospective cohort study in Danish DOC*X (n=2.95M) and Norwegian Nor-Work (n=2.45M) linked to national cancer registries using a quantitative job-exposure matrix for UVR.
- **Key findings**: 814 total ocular melanoma cases; in Nor-Work, upper quartiles of UVR showed IRRs 1.63–1.80 vs. lowest quartile, with similar elevations for uveal melanoma (IRR 1.35–1.45).
- **Strengths**: Massive population-scale cohorts, objective quantitative exposure matrix, registry-confirmed outcomes.
- **Limitations**: Non-monotonic dose-response trend; inconsistent results between the two cohorts.
- **Conclusion**: Some positive associations exist between occupational UVR and ocular melanoma, warranting refined exposure assessment in future large-scale studies.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag419 / PMID:43006889

## 2. Decoding Neutrophil Spatial Heterogeneity and Clinical Implications in Breast Cancer — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Cell
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-09
- **Authors**: Xu YZ et al.
- **Aims**: To map neutrophil spatial patterns in breast tumors and link them to clinical outcomes.
- **Method**: AI-powered computational pathology (n=1,113) integrated with single-cell spatial omics (n=104) and functional experiments in TNBC cohorts.
- **Key findings**: Tumor-proximal neutrophils associated with favorable prognosis; immune-cell-proximal neutrophils predicted immunotherapy response; combined spatial biomarker improved TNBC stratification.
- **Strengths**: Large multi-platform cohort, orthogonal experimental validation, translational clinical readout.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective design; spatial omics cohort relatively small (n=104).
- **Conclusion**: Neutrophil spatial context is clinically informative and can be captured routinely with AI-driven pathology.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.ccell.2026.10.005 / PMID:43003078

## 3. A Stroma-Sensing TGF-β Blockade Reprograms CAF-Myeloid Niches to Induce Durable Tumor Immunity — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Cell
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-09
- **Authors**: Kang DW et al.
- **Aims**: To develop a matrix-targeted TGF-β inhibitor overcoming fibrotic immune exclusion in tumors.
- **Method**: Preclinical study using fibronectin EDB-binding peptide fused to soluble TGF-β receptor II (FEBP-sTβRII) in syngeneic mouse tumor models with transcriptomic and functional assays.
- **Key findings**: FEBP-sTβRII induced CAF-myeloid reprogramming, T cell infiltration, and long-term antitumor immune memory; superior to systemic TGF-β blockade with fewer peripheral side effects.
- **Strengths**: Mechanistically thorough, spatially targeted approach with minimal off-target effects demonstrated.
- **Limitations**: Preclinical only; no human data; pancreatic cancer focus limits generalizability.
- **Conclusion**: Localized TGF-β blockade via tumor-matrix targeting converts immune-excluded fibrotic tumors into immunotherapy-responsive ecosystems.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.ccell.2026.10.007 / PMID:43003077

## 4. Long-term Fatigue Dimensions in the Multidimensional Fatigue Inventory (MFI) up to Five Years After Radiotherapy in Men Diagnosed with Non-Metastatic Prostate Cancer — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: British Journal of Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-09
- **Authors**: Heumann P et al.
- **Aims**: To characterize multidimensional fatigue trajectories up to 5 years post-radiotherapy in prostate cancer survivors.
- **Method**: Prospective cohort (international REQUITE study, n=1,097) with latent class growth analysis and logistic regression for 5-dimension MFI over 5 years.
- **Key findings**: 72% reported fatigue in ≥1 dimension across follow-up; pre-treatment fatigue (OR 4.7–9.0), depression (OR 2.5), diabetes (OR 1.8–2.2), and obesity (OR 1.8–2.2) predicted long-term fatigue.
- **Strengths**: Prospective international multi-centre design; multidimensional fatigue assessment; long follow-up.
- **Limitations**: Restricted to radiotherapy-treated men; no comparison arm with other treatment modalities.
- **Conclusion**: Pre-treatment fatigue and comorbidities are strong prognostic markers for long-term fatigue, supporting early multidimensional assessment in prostate cancer survivorship.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41416-026-03626-4 / PMID:43003921

## 5. Increasing Cancer Incidence in Iowa: A 22-Year Regional Comparative Analysis with the Seven Surrounding States — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiology, Biomarkers & Prevention
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-09
- **Authors**: Liu Q et al.
- **Aims**: To identify site- and stage-specific drivers of Iowa's rising cancer incidence relative to surrounding states.
- **Method**: Population-based cancer surveillance analysis (2001–2022) using joinpoint regression on age-standardized incidence and mortality rates from Iowa Cancer Registry and SEER.
- **Key findings**: Iowa overall IR increased 0.6%/year from 2013; in 2022 Iowa IRs exceeded surrounding states by 11% (males) and 6% (females); prostate cancer drove 43.5% of male excess.
- **Strengths**: 22-year population-based surveillance; systematic comparison with seven bordering states; stage-specific stratification.
- **Limitations**: Descriptive design cannot establish causal drivers; screening behaviour comparisons limited by self-reported data.
- **Conclusion**: Iowa's rising incidence, particularly at localised stage, warrants sustained preventive and screening policy attention, especially for prostate and breast cancers.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-0788 / PMID:43002126

## Appraisal notes

This week's picks reflect two dominant themes: spatial tumour biology and cancer surveillance epidemiology. The two Cancer Cell papers demonstrate a methodological convergence of AI-powered computational pathology with spatial omics, offering both mechanistic insight and practical clinical stratification tools. On the epidemiological side, the Nordic ocular melanoma cohort study exemplifies the power of job-exposure matrices linked to population registries for rare cancer aetiology, while the Iowa cancer surveillance analysis highlights how regional divergence in incidence trends can reveal screening artefacts and emerging risk factor disparities even in mature surveillance systems.
