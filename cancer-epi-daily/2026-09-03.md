## 1. Creating outdoor air pollution exposome metrics for assessing breast and lung cancer risks in the United States — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiol Biomarkers Prev
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-01
- **Authors**: Boyle J et al.
- **Aims**: Develop cancer-specific air pollution exposome metrics to identify where and what pollutants drive breast and lung cancer incidence in the US.
- **Method**: Cross-sectional geospatial analysis combining RSEI emissions model and CACES land-use regression data (2005–2020) at census-tract level, weighted by meta-analytic cancer-specific risk estimates.
- **Key findings**: Cancer-specific metrics (APBC, APLC) showed positive skew with highest values in large cities and transportation corridors. Both varied significantly by race, ethnicity, and housing with strong spatial clustering.
- **Strengths**: Novel multi-source exposome approach captures industrial, transport, and power-plant emissions simultaneously; covers entire continental US over 15 years.
- **Limitations**: Cross-sectional design; cancer-specific risk weights derived from meta-analyses introducing potential measurement error.
- **Conclusion**: These novel metrics can identify cancer-relevant air pollution constituents and inform strategies to reduce incidence disparities.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-0493 / PMID:42678813

## 2. Tumor-Secreted ADAMTSL4 Activates Latent TGFβ1 to Drive Cancer Cachexia — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Discov
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-01
- **Authors**: Machado J et al.
- **Aims**: Identify tumor-derived circulating factors mediating cancer cachexia in colorectal and lung cancer patients.
- **Method**: Preclinical mouse models with Adamtsl4 overexpression/deletion combined with patient biomarker validation; genetic and pharmacological TGFβ signalling blockade.
- **Key findings**: ADAMTSL4 overexpression converted non-cachexia-inducing tumors into cachexia-inducing tumors; its deletion spared fat/muscle and reduced cachexia severity. ADAMTSL4 levels correlated with TGFβ-driven atrophy signatures in cancer patients.
- **Strengths**: Converging genetic, pharmacological, and clinical evidence; identifies upstream regulator enabling selective therapeutic targeting.
- **Limitations**: Primarily preclinical; patient validation cohort size not reported in abstract; human causal data limited.
- **Conclusion**: ADAMTSL4 is an upstream regulator of TGFβ1 activation in cancer cachexia and a potential therapeutic target.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/2159-8290.CD-26-0045 / PMID:42678278

## 3. Mining Stored-Specimen Studies for Information about Cancer Natural History — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiol Biomarkers Prev
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-01
- **Authors**: Lange J et al.
- **Aims**: Assess whether retrospective stored-blood-specimen studies can be used to infer cancer natural history parameters alongside preclinical test sensitivity.
- **Method**: Methodological/theoretical analysis examining identifiability of transition rates in multistate natural history models within a Bayesian framework using case-control subsamples from large cohort studies.
- **Key findings**: Key transition rates are estimable within a Bayesian framework leveraging prior sensitivity information from diagnostic studies; parameter identifiability depends on model complexity.
- **Strengths**: Directly relevant to multicancer early detection test evaluation; proposes a practical Bayesian framework for a data-scarce problem.
- **Limitations**: Theoretical analysis only; formal empirical study of identifiability across varying model complexity still needed.
- **Conclusion**: Stored-specimen studies can be harnessed to infer cancer natural history if Bayesian identifiability conditions are carefully met.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-0731 / PMID:42676126

## 5. Cancer Screening in the Postpandemic Era: Lessons from Disruption and a Path Forward — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiol Biomarkers Prev
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-01
- **Authors**: Coronado GD et al.
- **Aims**: Outline actionable strategies for sustaining cancer screening continuity during future public health emergencies based on COVID-19 pandemic lessons.
- **Method**: Commentary drawing on PROSPR consortium data across 10 US health systems documenting cervical, colorectal, and lung screening declines during April–May 2020.
- **Key findings**: Screening dropped up to 96% in April–May 2020; systems with mailed fecal testing recovered faster; home-based screening infrastructure proved critical.
- **Strengths**: Multi-system PROSPR consortium data provides a national picture; directly translatable policy recommendations.
- **Limitations**: Commentary design; long-term cancer outcome impacts of screening disruption not modelled.
- **Conclusion**: Embedding home-based testing, telehealth, and risk-stratified prioritisation as standard practice will protect screening continuity for vulnerable populations.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-0825 / PMID:42676125
