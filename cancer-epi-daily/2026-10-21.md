## 1. Time trends of HPV-attributable head and neck cancers: the BROADEN study — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-19
- **Authors**: Waterboer T et al.
- **Aims**: To assess HPV-attributable fractions (HPV-AF) of head and neck cancers across 7 countries over two periods (2008–2009 and 2018–2019).
- **Method**: Retrospective multi-country study (n=6,043, 88 hospitals); central HPV-DNA, HPV-E6*I mRNA, and p16INK4a testing to determine attributability.
- **Key findings**: HPV-AF in oropharyngeal cancer increased significantly across all countries; range 0–44.2% in 2008–2009 to 20–51.7% in 2018–2019. Other head and neck cancer sites showed low HPV-AF (≤5%) with no trend.
- **Strengths**: Largest multi-country HPV-attribution study to date with rigorous central laboratory testing.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective design; completeness of archival tissue may vary by site and time period.
- **Conclusion**: Rising HPV-attributable oropharyngeal cancer burden across Europe and Asia informs global vaccine and prevention strategies.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag375 / PMID:42895305

## 2. Novel framework for the validation of intermediate clinical endpoints in oncology real-world data — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-19
- **Authors**: Labaki C et al.
- **Aims**: To develop and test a framework for validating surrogate endpoints using real-world data (RWD) in metastatic breast cancer.
- **Method**: Retrospective cohort study; US Flatiron Health nationwide database, n=19,164; Kendall's tau and R² to evaluate surrogacy of rwPFS, TTNT, TTLA, and TTEFL for overall survival.
- **Key findings**: rwPFS met both surrogacy conditions (Kendall's τ ≥0.5; R²≥0.7 for group effects). TTNT and TTLA met individual-level but not group-level surrogacy conditions.
- **Strengths**: Large nationwide RWD sample; rigorous two-condition surrogacy framework applicable beyond breast cancer.
- **Limitations**: Industry-supported; Flatiron data may underrepresent rural/low-resource populations.
- **Conclusion**: rwPFS is a strong surrogate for OS in metastatic breast cancer RWD; framework provides a blueprint for other cancer types.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag390 / PMID:42898006

## 3. Effects of private health insurance coverage gains on colorectal cancer outcomes: a modeling study — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-20
- **Authors**: van den Puttelaar R et al.
- **Aims**: To estimate the impact of providing private health insurance to currently uninsured Americans on colorectal cancer incidence, mortality, and costs (2025–2055).
- **Method**: MISCAN-Colon microsimulation model adapted to uninsured population (ages 40–100); national age-specific estimates for screening, stage distribution, survival by insurance status.
- **Key findings**: Insurance gains could avert up to 8.9% of CRC cases and 30.7% of deaths per 100,000 by 2055; total costs increase by 3.7% due to higher screening expenditure.
- **Strengths**: Policy-relevant microsimulation with validated model; long horizon captures delayed prevention benefits.
- **Limitations**: Model assumptions on behavioral uptake may not reflect real-world implementation; modeled population may not capture all uninsured subgroups.
- **Conclusion**: Expanding private insurance substantially reduces CRC mortality disparities, though net costs increase modestly due to screening expansion.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag395 / PMID:42899410

## 4. Effectiveness of a Multisite Oncology Financial Navigation Intervention: The LIFT Clinical Trial — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Journal of Clinical Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-16
- **Authors**: Wheeler SB et al.
- **Aims**: To evaluate effectiveness and implementation of financial navigation (FN) across diverse oncology practice settings to reduce financial toxicity (FT) among cancer survivors.
- **Method**: Pragmatic hybrid II implementation-effectiveness trial; 8 sites (4 rural, 4 non-rural); n=233 FT-positive cancer patients; pre-post change in FACIT-COST score as primary outcome.
- **Key findings**: FACIT-COST scores improved by mean 8.1 points post-FN (p<.0001); significant at both rural (6.3 points) and non-rural sites (9.3 points); ~75% uptake among FT-positive patients.
- **Strengths**: Pragmatic real-world multi-site design including rural populations; high intervention uptake.
- **Limitations**: No control arm; pre-post design cannot rule out regression to the mean; 21 patients died before completion.
- **Conclusion**: Financial navigation is effective across diverse care settings and should be considered standard practice for cancer survivors with moderate-to-severe financial toxicity.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1200/JCO-26-00617 / PMID:42885694

## 5. Hypofractionated Versus Conventional Postmastectomy Radiotherapy With IMN Irradiation for High-Risk Breast Cancer (HypoMast-IMNI) — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Journal of Clinical Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-19
- **Authors**: Wang X et al.
- **Aims**: To evaluate whether hypofractionated PMRT (42.56 Gy/16 fractions) is noninferior to conventional PMRT (50 Gy/25 fractions) including internal mammary node irradiation in high-risk breast cancer.
- **Method**: Phase III randomized noninferiority trial; n=1,494 patients; primary endpoint 5-year locoregional recurrence (LRR); median follow-up 58.1 months.
- **Key findings**: 5-year LRR 1.7% vs 2.2% (HR 0.69, 95% CI 0.32–1.51), meeting noninferiority criterion (p=0.017); grade 3 acute skin toxicity significantly lower with HypoRT (1.7% vs 12.2%).
- **Strengths**: Large phase III RCT with 5-year follow-up; no ischemic cardiac events; favorable acute toxicity.
- **Limitations**: Single-institution Chinese cohort; may limit generalizability to Western populations with different tumor biology.
- **Conclusion**: 16-fraction HypoRT is noninferior for locoregional control and offers superior acute toxicity versus conventional fractionation in high-risk breast cancer requiring IMN irradiation.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1200/JCO-25-02843 / PMID:42896129

## Appraisal notes

This week's digest reflects a strong methodological theme across studies. The BROADEN and LIFT studies exemplify rigorous multi-site epidemiology addressing viral cancer causation and health equity respectively, while the Flatiron/JNCI surrogate-endpoint framework advances RWD methodology for regulatory and trial contexts. The insurance–CRC modeling study and the HypoMast-IMNI RCT both carry important cancer prevention and treatment implications, with the latter providing level I evidence for a fractionation change already underway in many centers.
