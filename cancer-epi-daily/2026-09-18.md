## 1. Statin Initiation and Mortality After Colorectal Cancer Treatment — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Netw Open
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-01
- **Authors**: Woo HT et al.
- **Aims**: Estimate the association between statin initiation after CRC treatment and mortality using target trial emulation to address time-related biases.
- **Method**: Nationwide Korean NHI claims cohort (n=88,516); clone-censor-weight target trial emulation; stabilized inverse probability of censoring weights; 60-month follow-up.
- **Key findings**: Statin initiation associated with modestly lower CRC-specific mortality (HR 0.95, 95% CI 0.92–0.99); effect substantially attenuated vs conventional observational estimates.
- **Strengths**: Large nationwide cohort; target trial emulation explicitly corrects immortal time and prevalent user biases; positive-control cohort validates approach.
- **Limitations**: Claims data lack cancer stage and lifestyle covariates; results may not generalise beyond the Korean setting.
- **Conclusion**: The statin survival benefit in CRC is modest once time-related biases are corrected, cautioning against overinterpretation of conventional observational studies.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.33680 / PMID:42742942

## 2. Healthy Lifestyle and Cancer Risk: A Pan-Cancer Analysis in the UK Biobank Prospective Cohort — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Br J Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-15
- **Authors**: Ding J et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate associations between a composite healthy lifestyle score (HLS) and risk of 27 cancer types, quantifying population attributable fractions (PAFs).
- **Method**: UK Biobank prospective cohort (n=287,745); HLS across smoking, alcohol, diet, physical activity, BMI; Cox proportional hazards models; PAF estimation; median follow-up 11.5 years.
- **Key findings**: HLS adherence (4–5 vs 0–1 behaviours) associated with reduced risk of 13/27 cancer types (HRs 0.12–0.79); smoking and overweight linked to the largest number of cancer types.
- **Strengths**: Very large prospective cohort; 27 cancer types; PAF quantification; five lifestyle components combined into single score.
- **Limitations**: Self-reported lifestyle data; healthy volunteer bias in UK Biobank; potential residual confounding.
- **Conclusion**: Smoking cessation and weight control are the highest-yield cancer prevention priorities across multiple cancer types.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41416-026-03613-9 / PMID:42744862

## 3. CpG Hypermethylation and WNT/AP-1 Cooperativity Define the Epigenetic Landscape and a Clinical Subgroup of High-Risk Pediatric Adrenocortical Carcinoma — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Nat Commun
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-14
- **Authors**: Fincke VE et al.
- **Aims**: Identify molecular risk groups and therapeutic vulnerabilities in pediatric adrenocortical tumors through integrated multi-omic profiling.
- **Method**: Multi-omic analysis (DNA methylation, transcriptomics, chromatin accessibility, spatial deconvolution) of 214 pediatric adrenocortical tumors; entinostat functional validation.
- **Key findings**: Four distinct risk groups identified; high-risk subgroup defined by CpG island hypermethylation, chromosomal instability, WNT/AP-1 co-activation, and dismal survival.
- **Strengths**: Comprehensive multi-omic design across 214 samples; in vitro therapeutic validation; spatial intercellular crosstalk characterized.
- **Limitations**: Rare cancer limits sample size; predominantly German cohort; functional validation limited to cell-line models.
- **Conclusion**: CpG hypermethylation with WNT/AP-1 cooperativity marks a high-risk pediatric ACC subgroup with actionable epigenetic vulnerabilities.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41467-026-77225-5 / PMID:42736301

## 4. Preferences for AI Technologies to Detect Skin Cancer in Primary Care: A UK-Wide Discrete Choice Experiment — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Br J Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-11
- **Authors**: Jones OT et al.
- **Aims**: Examine preferences of GPs, patients, and the public for AI technology attributes for skin cancer detection in UK primary care.
- **Method**: UK-wide online discrete choice experiment (DCE; n=2,302); alternative-specific conditional logit regression; attributes: false negative/positive rates, cost, location, skin tone coverage, guideline status.
- **Key findings**: False negative rate was the most important attribute across all groups; all stakeholders preferred AI used by healthcare professionals in GP settings over home-based AI.
- **Strengths**: Three distinct stakeholder groups; equity dimension (skin tone training coverage) explicitly modelled; rigorous DCE design.
- **Limitations**: Online survey may under-represent marginalised groups; stated preferences may not predict real-world uptake.
- **Conclusion**: AI skin cancer tools should minimise false negatives and train on diverse skin tones to secure stakeholder acceptance and safe implementation.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41416-026-03611-x / PMID:42728369

## 5. Viral Mimicry Escape as a Necessary Feature of Malignant Transformation — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: Nat Rev Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-15
- **Authors**: Chen R et al.
- **Aims**: Establish viral mimicry escape — suppression of transposable-element-derived immunogenic nucleic acids — as a fundamental step in cancer initiation and progression.
- **Method**: Systematic narrative review synthesising evidence on transposable element derepression, epigenetic dysregulation, and innate immune evasion across cancer types.
- **Key findings**: Emerging tumors accumulate immunogenic nucleic acids that can trigger antiviral responses; cancers escape via epigenetic repression, RNA editing, and dampened interferon signaling.
- **Strengths**: Unifying conceptual framework covering DNA methylation, histone, splicing, and RNA processing; strong translational framing for immunotherapy combinations.
- **Limitations**: Review article; universal necessity of viral mimicry escape across all cancer types not yet experimentally confirmed.
- **Conclusion**: Framing viral mimicry escape as necessary for malignant transformation opens a therapeutic window for combining epigenetic agents with immunotherapy.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41568-026-00977-1 / PMID:42744865
