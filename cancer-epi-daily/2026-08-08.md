## 1. Mathematical Biomarkers of Adaptive Therapy Outcomes in Prostate Cancer — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-06
- **Authors**: Gallagher K et al.
- **Aims**: Develop and validate mathematical biomarkers from first-cycle PSA dynamics to predict outcomes under adaptive therapy in prostate cancer.
- **Method**: Retrospective modeling study using a 2-population differential equation model validated in two independent clinical trial cohorts (n=53 total: 40 CSPC, 13 mCRPC).
- **Key findings**: Adaptive therapy score from first-cycle PSA strongly predicted TTP in CSPC (HR 0.49; 95% CI 0.31–0.76; p=0.002); outperformed standard PSA metrics for OS prediction in mCRPC (Spearman ρ=0.76 for TTP, p=0.002).
- **Strengths**: Cross-cohort validation in two independent trials spanning different disease stages and treatment regimens strengthens generalizability.
- **Limitations**: Small mCRPC cohort (n=13) limits statistical power; retrospective design precludes causal inference on clinical outcomes.
- **Conclusion**: Mechanism-based mathematical biomarkers accurately stratify prostate cancer patients for personalized adaptive therapy scheduling.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamaoncol.2026.2781 / PMID:42560686

## 2. Tumoral Extracellular Vesicles and Particles Reprogram Interstitial Macrophages in the Lung to Promote Vascular Permeability and Metastasis — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Nature Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-05
- **Authors**: Dror S et al.
- **Aims**: Elucidate how tumor-derived extracellular vesicles and particles (EVPs) promote vascular permeability during premetastatic niche formation.
- **Method**: Mechanistic mouse study using EVP administration, macrophage depletion experiments, NF-κB/JAK-STAT pathway analysis, and integrin profiling; validated with human colorectal cancer EVPs.
- **Key findings**: EVPs induce vascular leakiness within 1 h via interstitial macrophage NF-κB/JAK-STAT activation and IL-6 secretion; EVP-associated integrin α5 (ITGα5) is the key mediator driving this axis.
- **Strengths**: Multi-model mechanistic dissection with human CRC EVP validation extends preclinical findings toward clinical relevance.
- **Limitations**: Mouse models may not fully recapitulate human tumour microenvironment; human validation limited to in vitro.
- **Conclusion**: An EVP–macrophage–IL-6 axis drives premetastatic vascular permeability, positioning ITGα5 as a potential anti-metastatic therapeutic target.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s43018-026-01209-z / PMID:42557402

## 3. Fc-Optimized GITR Antibody Enhances a CD4 T Cell–Dendritic Cell Crosstalk to Promote Antitumor Immunity — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Nature Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-05
- **Authors**: Avraham Y et al.
- **Aims**: Determine how Fc domain engineering affects antitumor efficacy of anti-GITR monoclonal antibodies via FcγR-mediated immune mechanisms.
- **Method**: Fc protein and glycan engineering combined with humanized mouse tumor models; characterized FcγR-dependent regulatory T cell depletion and CD4/DC crosstalk.
- **Key findings**: An optimized Fc scaffold enhanced antitumor efficacy through Treg depletion and mutual CD4 T cell–dendritic cell activation, leading to direct CD4 cytotoxicity and amplified CD8 responses.
- **Strengths**: Systematic dissection of multiple FcγR mechanisms in humanized models provides a rational design framework for next-generation agonistic mAbs.
- **Limitations**: Humanized mouse models have immune reconstitution limitations; clinical translation of Fc engineering effects remains unvalidated in patients.
- **Conclusion**: Fc optimization of anti-GITR mAbs harnesses complementary FcγR pathways to substantially improve antitumor immune responses.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s43018-026-01207-1 / PMID:42557403

## 5. Weight Loss Responses to Pharmacotherapy in Breast Cancer Survivors: Results from the Adaptive Nutrition and Exercise Weight Loss (A-NEW) Study — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: Clinical Cancer Research
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-04
- **Authors**: Sheng JY et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate addition of naltrexone/bupropion (Contrave) for breast cancer survivors with minimal weight loss after behavioural intervention.
- **Method**: Single-arm phase II study (n=55); adaptive design escalating to pharmacotherapy for non-responders (<5% weight loss) at 2 months; primary endpoint ≥5% weight loss at 6 months.
- **Key findings**: 42% of pharmacotherapy recipients achieved ≥5% weight loss at 6 months; physical function and pain improved with ≥5% weight loss (p=0.03, p=0.02); HbA1c and triglycerides improved in pharmacotherapy arm.
- **Strengths**: Adaptive intervention design efficiently identifies non-responders; cardiometabolic endpoints add clinical breadth beyond weight alone.
- **Limitations**: Single-arm design without control group; small pharmacotherapy cohort (n=38); 74% gastrointestinal adverse event rate limits tolerability conclusions.
- **Conclusion**: Adaptive pharmacotherapy for behavioural non-responders produces clinically meaningful weight loss and cardiometabolic benefits in breast cancer survivors.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-26-0270 / PMID:42550849
