## 1. Timing of Mental Disorders in Relation to Cancer-Specific Mortality in Cancer Patients — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-27
- **Authors**: Li M et al.
- **Aims**: To evaluate whether mental disorders before or after cancer diagnosis are associated with cancer-specific mortality.
- **Method**: Cohort study of 22,281 UK Biobank participants linked to cancer and death registries; period-specific longitudinal inverse probability weighting with weighted Cox models.
- **Key findings**: Post-diagnosis mental disorders (years 2–5) strongly associated with cancer mortality (HR 2.19, 95% CI 1.97–2.43); associations strongest during years 2–5 across five cancer sites.
- **Strengths**: Large biobank-registry linkage; sophisticated IPW approach minimising survival and confounding bias; multi-site analyses.
- **Limitations**: UK Biobank is not population-representative; residual confounding possible despite weighting.
- **Conclusion**: Findings support routine mental-health screening and intervention throughout cancer survivorship care.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag289 / PMID:42658001

## 2. Peripubertal Pregnancies and Associations With Health and Developmental Outcomes in Offspring During Late Adolescence and Adulthood: Sibling-Comparison Register Study — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: International Journal of Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-18
- **Authors**: Ahuja V et al.
- **Aims**: To assess long-term health and developmental outcomes in adult offspring of peripubertal mothers using sibling comparison to strengthen causal inference.
- **Method**: Swedish national register cohort (1964–2018); 2.46 million offspring; sibling fixed-effect models to account for unmeasured familial confounding.
- **Key findings**: Conventional analyses showed poorer cognitive function (β −0.54) and stress resilience (β −0.24) in offspring of <16-year mothers; sibling-comparison models attenuated adverse associations to null or reversed them.
- **Strengths**: National register linkage; very large sample; explicit causal-inference framework via sibling comparison eliminates shared familial confounding.
- **Limitations**: Conscription outcomes restricted to male offspring; findings may not generalise beyond Swedish context.
- **Conclusion**: Apparent adverse offspring outcomes from teenage pregnancy are largely attributable to shared familial characteristics, not direct perinatal causation.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/ije/dyag153 / PMID:42658719

## 3. Cancer Biologics Utilization After Biosimilar Entry and Financial Implications For Payers and Patients — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-27
- **Authors**: Liu X et al.
- **Aims**: To assess market dynamics and financial implications of biosimilar entry for anticancer biologics bevacizumab, rituximab, and trastuzumab.
- **Method**: Retrospective cohort using Merative MarketScan commercial and Medicare databases (July 2019–December 2024); n=14,655 patients categorised by biosimilar use pattern.
- **Key findings**: Biosimilar-only users had $3,820 lower mean monthly payer costs and $39.50 lower out-of-pocket costs; reference-product ASP declined 3.8% annually; market share fell ~30% annually.
- **Strengths**: Large multi-payer database spanning the full biosimilar entry period; granular payer and patient cost data.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective design; clinical outcomes not assessed; database may underrepresent some community-practice settings.
- **Conclusion**: Biosimilar entry in oncology substantially reduced payer costs and patient financial burden, supporting policies facilitating biosimilar adoption.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamaoncol.2026.3128 / PMID:42658505

## 4. Salvage Hypofractionated Accelerated Versus Standard Radiotherapy for Biochemical Recurrence After Radical Prostatectomy: A Phase III Randomized Clinical Trial — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Journal of Clinical Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-25
- **Authors**: Song Y et al.
- **Aims**: To compare hypofractionated RT (65 Gy/26 fr) vs conventional RT (66 Gy/33 fr) for biochemical recurrence after radical prostatectomy.
- **Method**: Phase III RCT; n=316 patients; primary endpoint biochemical progression-free survival (bPFS) at 4 years.
- **Key findings**: 4-year bPFS 80.1% (HYPO) vs 78.1% (CONV), non-superior; grade ≥2 GI toxicity higher in HYPO arm without endorectal balloon (7.9% vs 0.7%).
- **Strengths**: Randomized design; long median follow-up (52.6 months); patient-reported quality-of-life outcomes included.
- **Limitations**: Non-inferiority margins not formally pre-specified; endorectal balloon use inconsistent across participants.
- **Conclusion**: Hypofractionated salvage RT is a viable alternative with comparable oncologic outcomes and acceptable toxicity when an endorectal balloon is used.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1200/JCO-25-02234 / PMID:42641124

## 5. Addressing the Impact of Tobacco and Alcohol Use on Cancer-Related Health Outcomes — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-26
- **Authors**: Zhou H et al.
- **Aims**: To synthesise evidence linking tobacco and alcohol to cancer and develop actionable public-health recommendations.
- **Method**: Multidisciplinary workshop commentary integrating epidemiological, policy, and clinical evidence across five thematic sessions.
- **Key findings**: Synergistic tobacco–alcohol effects on cancer incidence highlighted; tobacco-control policy lessons applied to alcohol reduction strategies with specific cessation interventions reviewed.

- **Conclusion**: Integrated public-health strategies modelled on tobacco control are urgently needed to reduce alcohol-attributable cancer burden.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag138 / PMID:42644806
