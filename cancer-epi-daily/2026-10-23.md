## 1. Childhood Cancer Risk After Assisted Reproductive Technology — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: JAMA
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-21
- **Authors**: Mose K et al.
- **Aims**: To examine associations between ART and childhood cancer risk, overall and by ART modality and cancer type.
- **Method**: Nationwide Danish cohort of 1,086,136 live-born children (1996–2012), median 20-year follow-up; Cox proportional hazards models; secondary analyses using infertility-history controls.
- **Key findings**: ART-conceived children had higher cancer incidence (HR 1.26, 95% CI 1.07–1.48); frozen embryo transfer showed the highest risk (HR 2.18, 95% CI 1.45–3.29).
- **Strengths**: Large nationally representative registry cohort; long follow-up; secondary infertility-control analyses distinguish procedure vs infertility effects.
- **Limitations**: Residual confounding by indication; low absolute risk numbers for rare cancer subtypes.
- **Conclusion**: Frozen embryo transfer and ICSI, but not standard IVF, are associated with increased childhood cancer risk; absolute risks remain low.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jama.2026.18862 / PMID:42905692

## 2. Association of Circulating Eosinophils With Systemic Immune Activation and Reduced Recurrence in HR+/HER2− Breast Cancer — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: J Clin Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-20
- **Authors**: Li ZQ et al.
- **Aims**: To evaluate circulating eosinophil count as a noninvasive biomarker for recurrence risk in HR+/HER2− breast cancer.
- **Method**: Multimodal discovery cohort (N=472) plus propensity score overlap–weighted independent validation cohort (N=23,170); integrated multiomics and spatial digital pathology.
- **Key findings**: Higher eosinophil counts correlated with improved recurrence-free and distant metastasis-free survival; eosinophils added prognostic value beyond the 21-gene recurrence score and predicted CDK4/6 inhibitor benefit.
- **Strengths**: Large independent validation cohort with propensity score weighting; multiomics and in vivo mechanistic validation.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective discovery cohort; predominantly single-institution; requires prospective validation.
- **Conclusion**: Preoperative circulating eosinophil count is a clinically applicable liquid biopsy biomarker for recurrence and CDK4/6 inhibitor response in HR+/HER2− breast cancer.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1200/JCO-26-00261 / PMID:42900864

## 3. Effectiveness of a Structured Template to Improve Completeness of Reporting Randomised Trial Protocols — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: BMJ
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-20
- **Authors**: Blanco D et al.
- **Aims**: To evaluate whether a SPIRIT 2025–structured manuscript template improves completeness of reporting in randomised trial protocols.
- **Method**: Randomised controlled trial (N=70 manuscripts, 1:1 allocation) at BMJ Open; blinded outcome assessors; 11-item SPIRIT completeness score as primary outcome.
- **Key findings**: Intervention improved completeness score by 0.61 points (95% CI 0.20–1.01); largest gains in recruitment, data collection, and data access items; revision times unchanged.
- **Strengths**: Randomised design with pre-registration and blinded assessment; directly relevant to epi and clinical-trial reporting quality.
- **Limitations**: Small sample size (35 per arm); single-journal setting; only 40% survey response rate.
- **Conclusion**: A SPIRIT-based template during manuscript revision modestly improves protocol reporting completeness without extending revision time.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1136/bmj-2026-100917 / PMID:42901609

## 4. Helicobacter pylori Infection — Advances, Challenges, and the Road Ahead — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: N Engl J Med
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-22
- **Authors**: Li H et al.
- **Aims**: To review the history, pathogenesis, global burden, screening strategy, and emerging treatment trends for H. pylori infection in the context of gastric cancer prevention.
- **Method**: Narrative review synthesising 40 years of H. pylori research including epidemiological screening data and antibiotic resistance surveillance.
- **Key findings**: H. pylori eradication remains the primary modifiable risk factor for gastric cancer prevention; antibiotic resistance is rising globally; no approved vaccine exists.
- **Strengths**: NEJM authoritative review covering global burden and prevention framework; authored by Nobel Laureate co-discoverer (Marshall BJ).
- **Limitations**: Narrative review with inherent selection bias; limited quantitative synthesis.
- **Conclusion**: Standardised H. pylori screening and eradication is the most scalable approach to primary gastric cancer prevention pending vaccine development.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1056/NEJMra2414530 / PMID:42906088
