## 1. Projected impact of population-level multi-cancer early detection screening: a discrete event simulation analysis — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-06
- **Authors**: Aswani N et al.
- **Aims**: To project population-level mortality reductions achievable by liquid biopsy-based multi-cancer early detection (MCED) screening across ten cancer types.
- **Method**: Patient-level discrete event simulation calibrated to SEER incidence (ages 18–80, 1939–2001 birth cohorts), stratified by sex and race, modelling early detection benefit by stage-shifting and sojourn time.
- **Key findings**: Under ideal early detection conditions, pooled cancer mortality fell 41.0% (6.42% → 3.79%). Overdiagnosis ranged 10.9%–28.9% (lung highest); prostate and ovarian showed largest site-specific mortality reductions.
- **Strengths**: Large-scale cross-cohort calibration to SEER; flexible framework extensible to emerging MCED test performance data.
- **Limitations**: Assumes no lead-time bias harms, ideal detection sensitivity; does not model harms of false positives or downstream diagnostic cascades.
- **Conclusion**: MCED screening could halve cancer mortality under ideal conditions, but realised gains depend on actual test sensitivity and overdiagnosis management.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag356 / PMID:42836653

## 2. Chemotherapy enhances cancer vaccine efficacy and expands stem-like TCF1⁺CD8⁺ T cells — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Cell
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-02
- **Authors**: Noblecourt L et al.
- **Aims**: To determine whether chemotherapy synergizes with therapeutic cancer vaccines and identify the underlying immune mechanism.
- **Method**: Heterologous prime-boost viral vector vaccine combined with multiple chemotherapy regimens in murine models; validated in patient samples across cancer types; TCF1 disruption via genetic and pharmacological approaches.
- **Key findings**: CarboTaxol and cyclophosphamide acted as immunological adjuvants by expanding stem-like TCF1⁺CD8⁺ T cells independently of tumour presence. Triple combination (vaccine + CarboTaxol + anti-PD-1) further improved tumour control and survival.
- **Strengths**: Mechanistic clarity using genetic disruption; cross-species and multi-cancer patient validation.
- **Limitations**: Pre-clinical murine models dominate; clinical patient data is cross-sectional, not randomised.
- **Conclusion**: TCF1-dependent chemo-immunological adjuvancy provides a mechanistic rationale for vaccine + chemotherapy + PD-1 blockade clinical trials.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.ccell.2026.09.006 / PMID:42826713

## 3. Chromatin accessibility trajectories reveal pre-cancerous epigenetic priming and an acid-driven immunotherapy-resistant gastric cancer subtype — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Cell
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-06
- **Authors**: Wang T et al.
- **Aims**: To map epigenetic dynamics across multistep gastric tumorigenesis and identify chromatin-defined subtypes guiding immunotherapy response.
- **Method**: ATAC-seq chromatin accessibility atlas of 650 multistage samples from 439 patients; unsupervised subtype discovery; FOSL2/H+K-ATPase axis validated by genetic depletion and vonoprazan.
- **Key findings**: Three epigenomic subtypes identified; pseudo-gastric subtype is intrinsically immunotherapy-resistant via FOSL2-driven acid milieu suppressing CD8⁺ T cells. Vonoprazan sensitized this subtype to immunotherapy.
- **Strengths**: Large multistage atlas; actionable drug target (clinically available acid blocker).
- **Limitations**: Single-centre Chinese cohort; functional validation in patient-derived models limited.
- **Conclusion**: Epigenomic subtyping of gastric cancer identifies a drug-resistant subtype reversible by proton pump inhibition, with immediate translational potential.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.ccell.2026.09.004 / PMID:42838052

## 4. Efficacy of Cryotherapy in Preventing Taxane-Induced Neuropathy: The CryoPac Randomised Controlled Trial — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Annals of Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-06
- **Authors**: Lendorf ME et al.
- **Aims**: To assess whether frozen gloves and socks (cryotherapy) during taxane infusion reduce chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy (CIPN) incidence.
- **Method**: Multicentre RCT (n=268 randomised; n=217 analysed), 1:1 cryotherapy vs. usual care; primary endpoint TNS ≥2 at end of treatment; secondary endpoints EORTC QLQ-CIPN20, CTCAE, quantitative sensory testing.
- **Key findings**: Primary endpoint (CIPN incidence TNS ≥2) did not significantly differ (74% vs 84%, P=0.071). Cryotherapy reduced tingling and numbness in hands/feet and tactile disturbance on monofilament test (all P<0.025).
- **Strengths**: Registered RCT with quantitative sensory testing; multicentre design; patient-reported and objective endpoints.
- **Limitations**: High dropout (51/268); compliance only 67%; primary endpoint narrowly missed, potentially underpowered.
- **Conclusion**: Cryotherapy did not reduce CIPN incidence by TNS criteria but improved sensory symptom burden; longer follow-up may reveal persistent benefit.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.annonc.2026.09.014 / PMID:42838271

## 5. 21-Gene recurrence score assay in pN2-3 early-stage breast cancer: a National Cancer Database study — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-06
- **Authors**: Valenza C et al.
- **Aims**: To assess adjuvant chemotherapy benefit in hormone receptor-positive/HER2-negative pN2-3 breast cancer patients with 21-gene recurrence score ≤25.
- **Method**: Retrospective NCDB cohort (2010–2020); 2,058 patients with RS ≤25; propensity score-weighted Cox regression for overall survival; E-value reported for unmeasured confounding.
- **Key findings**: Chemotherapy was associated with longer overall survival (PS-weighted HR 0.62; 95% CI 0.48–0.81; P=0.0002). Five-year OS was 86% in tested vs. 79% in untested patients.
- **Strengths**: Large national database; propensity-weighting and E-value analysis strengthen causal inference claims.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective design with high risk of confounding by indication; E-value lower bound 1.58 suggests residual confounding possible.
- **Conclusion**: Chemotherapy benefit was observed in RS ≤25 pN2-3 breast cancer in this highly selected cohort, but randomised trial evidence is needed before practice change.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag374 / PMID:42836707

## Appraisal notes

This week's batch reflects two converging themes: epigenomic and immunological precision in cancer treatment (TCF1-based chemo-vaccine synergy, chromatin-defined gastric subtypes) alongside population-level epi-modelling innovations (MCED simulation, 21-gene RS in underexplored node-positive disease). The MCED discrete event simulation stands out for methodological rigour — calibrated to SEER across race/sex strata — and may become a reference framework as blood-test performance data from trials like PATHFINDER mature. The CryoPac RCT is a timely contribution given the absence of approved CIPN prevention strategies, though its underpowered primary endpoint underscores the difficulty of pragmatic oncology trials with complex patient-side compliance.
