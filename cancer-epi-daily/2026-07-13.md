## 1. Quality, Cost, and Timeliness of Cancer Treatment in Medicare Advantage and Traditional Medicare — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Intern Med
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-13
- **Authors**: Mitchell AP et al.
- **Aims**: Determine whether Medicare Advantage (MA) vs Traditional Medicare (TM) enrollment affects receipt of optimal guideline-concordant cancer treatment and treatment costs.
- **Method**: Retrospective cohort (n=35,245) using Medicare claims 2016–2019, covering diverse cancer scenarios; IPTW balancing with generalized estimating equations clustered by physician.
- **Key findings**: MA beneficiaries had similar likelihood of optimal treatment (adjusted RR 0.99; 95% CI 0.97–1.02) and 6% lower treatment costs (adjusted cost ratio 0.94; 95% CI 0.91–0.97), saving ~$931 per patient.
- **Strengths**: Large population-based sample covering varied cancer types, robust confounding control via IPTW and physician-level clustering.
- **Limitations**: Claims-based; excludes non-pharmacologic treatment variation; snapshot of 2016–2019 pre-dates recent MA reforms.
- **Conclusion**: Medicare Advantage delivers equivalent cancer treatment quality at meaningfully lower cost compared with Traditional Medicare.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamainternmed.2026.2864 / PMID:42440324

## 2. Gastric Cancer HIPEC vs Systemic Therapy Alone — PERISCOPE II Phase 3 RCT — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-08
- **Authors**: Quik JSE et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate whether gastrectomy + cytoreductive surgery + HIPEC improves overall survival over systemic therapy alone in gastric cancer with limited peritoneal metastases.
- **Method**: Multicentre randomised phase 3 RCT (n=102; 8 European centres); 1:1 allocation, stratified by centre/histology/peritoneal extent; ITT primary analysis; closed early after futility interim.
- **Key findings**: Median OS 15.7 months (HIPEC arm) vs 16.6 months (systemic therapy arm); HR 1.10 (95% CI 0.69–1.74), p=0.70; three treatment-related deaths in the HIPEC arm vs none.
- **Strengths**: First RCT to prospectively test HIPEC+CRS against systemic therapy alone; long median follow-up of 67 months; multicentre European design.
- **Limitations**: Closed prematurely (n=102 of planned n=136) due to unplanned futility interim; underpowered for definitive conclusions.
- **Conclusion**: Gastrectomy with CRS and HIPEC provides no survival advantage and increased morbidity compared with systemic therapy alone for gastric cancer with limited peritoneal metastases.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00144-0 / PMID:42419336

## 3. Circadian PER3 Genotypes and Prostate Cancer Radiotherapy Timing Interact to Affect Late Toxicity — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Clin Cancer Res
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-13
- **Authors**: Webb AJ et al.
- **Aims**: Test whether circadian rhythm gene genotypes interact with time-of-day of radiotherapy to modify late rectal bleeding and urinary incontinence in prostate cancer.
- **Method**: Prospective multinational observational cohort (REQUITE; n=1,760 prostate cancer patients receiving external beam RT); LASSO + logistic regression on n=877 with complete genotype and RT-timing data; 2-year follow-up.
- **Key findings**: PER3 rs696305 C/C carriers reduce predicted rectal bleeding risk from 11% (09:00) to 5% (17:00); rs172933 heterozygotes reduce urinary incontinence risk from 15% to 5% avoiding midday treatment.
- **Strengths**: Replicates prior breast cancer chronomodulation findings; large multinational prospective cohort; clinically actionable without new infrastructure.
- **Limitations**: RT timing was not randomised; n=877 complete data may limit power for subgroup analyses.
- **Conclusion**: PER3 genotype modifies the effect of radiotherapy timing on late toxicity, supporting genotype-guided chronomodulation as a low-cost strategy to reduce prostate cancer treatment side-effects.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-26-0268 / PMID:42440347

## 4. ctDNA Tumor Fraction as a Predictive Biomarker for Immunotherapy Regimen Selection in Advanced NSCLC — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Clin Cancer Res
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-13
- **Authors**: Dall'Olio FG et al.
- **Aims**: Assess whether ctDNA tumor fraction (TF) predicts differential benefit from immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) alone vs ICB plus chemotherapy in advanced NSCLC.
- **Method**: Retrospective analysis of US nationwide clinicogenomic database (CGDB; n=965 aNSCLC), validated in independent Gustave Roussy cohort (n=283 with paired PET); hybrid capture liquid biopsy.
- **Key findings**: ctDNA TF ≥5% predicted ICB+chemotherapy benefit (HR for rwPFS 0.58 [95% CI 0.41–0.82], p=0.002); TF correlated with total metabolic tumour volume (rho=0.46, p<0.001).
- **Strengths**: Large real-world database with independent prospective validation; correlates liquid biopsy with imaging biomarkers.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective real-world design; heterogeneous treatment practices across centres; confounding by lines of therapy.
- **Conclusion**: ctDNA tumor fraction integrates disease burden and biology, identifying patients likely to benefit from treatment intensification with chemo-immunotherapy.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-25-3917 / PMID:42440365

## 5. Durvalumab Plus Gemcitabine-Cisplatin in Advanced Biliary Tract Cancer: TOPAZ-1 4-Year Follow-Up — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-09
- **Authors**: Oh DY et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate 4-year overall survival and long-term safety of durvalumab plus gemcitabine-cisplatin (GemCis) vs placebo plus GemCis in advanced biliary tract cancer.
- **Method**: Post-hoc analysis of the global Phase 3 TOPAZ-1 RCT (n=685; 17 countries); data cutoff Feb 2025 (median follow-up 56.9 months for durvalumab arm); ITT OS analysis.
- **Key findings**: 48-month OS rate 11.8% (durvalumab+GemCis) vs 4.3% (placebo+GemCis); median OS 13.0 vs 11.4 months; HR 0.75 (95% CI 0.64–0.88); safety profiles were comparable between arms.
- **Strengths**: Longest follow-up from the pivotal biliary tract cancer immunotherapy trial; confirms durable survival benefit at 4 years.
- **Limitations**: Post-hoc analysis without pre-specified statistical plan; no biomarker enrichment data.
- **Conclusion**: Durvalumab plus GemCis sustains a meaningful long-term survival advantage as first-line treatment for advanced biliary tract cancer, with manageable safety.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamaoncol.2026.2204 / PMID:42424063

## Appraisal notes

This week's digest spans three prominent methodological threads. First, high-quality RCT reporting dominated the highest-tier journals: the PERISCOPE II null result definitively closes off a widely used but unproven surgical strategy for gastric peritoneal metastases, while TOPAZ-1's extended follow-up cements durvalumab as a durable standard of care in biliary tract cancer. Second, pharmacogenomic epidemiology is maturing toward clinical translation: the REQUITE circadian genotype × RT-timing interaction study illustrates how prospective cohort data can identify cheap, actionable treatment modifications without new drug development. Third, the Medicare Advantage cancer care study exemplifies the growing rigour of health services research in oncology, applying IPTW on a >35,000-patient national claims dataset to address a directly policy-relevant question about insurance design and cancer outcomes.
