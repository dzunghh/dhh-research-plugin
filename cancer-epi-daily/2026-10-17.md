## 1. Mutability Dependence of Breast Cancer Metastasis and Latency to Recurrence — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Discovery
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-13
- **Authors**: Lawrence-Paul MR et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate functional relevance of mutations specific to breast cancer metastases and their role in determining latency to recurrence.
- **Method**: Genomic analysis of 662 paired and ~6,000 unpaired primary/metastatic tumors; APOBEC mutagenesis burden; relative mutation-timing framework.
- **Key findings**: >50% of inter-patient variation in metastatic latency explained by timing of driver mutation acquisition before vs. after dissemination; APOBEC enriched in metastasis-specific mutations; 95% of patients had metastasis-specific mutations in ≥1 of 10 key pathways.
- **Strengths**: Largest paired genomic dataset for metastatic breast cancer; novel analytical framework linking mutation timing to clinical outcome.
- **Limitations**: Observational design; causal mechanisms inferred from correlation; limited survival data.
- **Conclusion**: Post-dissemination driver mutation accumulation governs metastatic outgrowth and latency in breast cancer.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/2159-8290.CD-25-2090 / PMID:42867316

## 2. Validation of Immunoscore for Prognostic Stratification in HPV-associated Oropharyngeal Cancer: An International Multicenter Study — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Clinical Cancer Research
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-13
- **Authors**: Nguyen DH et al.
- **Aims**: Validate the Immunoscore (CD3+/CD8+ density) for risk stratification in HPV-associated oropharyngeal squamous cell carcinoma (OPSCC).
- **Method**: International multicenter cohort, n=191 HPV+ OPSCC; training cohort + 3 independent validation cohorts; Cox proportional hazards for DFS, OS, and TTR.
- **Key findings**: IS-Low independently predicted worse DFS (HR 9.03; 95% CI 4.02–20.31); combined IS-clinical C-index 0.82 vs. 0.70 clinical-only (p<0.0001).
- **Strengths**: First IS validation in HPV-OPSCC; 4-cohort validation including prospective; standardized IS methodology.
- **Limitations**: Small individual cohort sizes; heterogeneous treatment regimens across centres.
- **Conclusion**: Immunoscore outperforms clinical staging and enables biomarker-guided risk-adapted treatment in HPV-OPSCC.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-26-1776 / PMID:42868274

## 3. Differential Impact of Tumor-Derived ESR1 Mutations on Elacestrant Benefit in Metastatic Breast Cancer — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Clinical Cancer Research
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-13
- **Authors**: Sledge GW et al.
- **Aims**: Determine whether specific ESR1 mutation variants differentially affect treatment benefit from elacestrant (oral SERD) in ER+ metastatic breast cancer.
- **Method**: Real-world cohort of 3,344 ESR1-mutated tumors; time-on-treatment from insurance claims; Cox proportional hazards; whole-transcriptome pathway analysis.
- **Key findings**: ESR1-L536 mutations conferred significantly shorter time-on-treatment vs. Y537/D538G (HR 2.09, p=0.001); multiple concurrent ESR1 mutations associated with worse outcomes (adjusted HR 1.62, p=0.038).
- **Strengths**: Large real-world genomic + clinical dataset; variant-level granularity; transcriptomic functional validation.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective design; time-on-treatment is a surrogate endpoint; referral bias in genomically-profiled cohort.
- **Conclusion**: ESR1 variants are not functionally equivalent; variant-specific stratification is needed to optimize SERD patient selection.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-26-2215 / PMID:42868272

## 4. Use of Circulating MicroRNAs to Identify High-Risk Adenomas Among FIT+ Patients Undergoing Screening Colonoscopy — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiology, Biomarkers & Prevention
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-13
- **Authors**: Sutherland RL et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate whether circulating plasma miRNAs improve risk stratification of FIT-positive individuals for high-risk adenomas (HRAs) to guide colonoscopy prioritisation.
- **Method**: 398 FIT+ participants (160 HRA, 238 controls); NanoString nCounter miRNA profiling; LASSO regression; 5 risk prediction models assessed by AUC, bootstrap validation, and decision curve analysis.
- **Key findings**: LASSO+Baseline model achieved optimism-corrected AUC 0.82 vs. 0.61 for baseline alone; 95.0% sensitivity and 56.7% PPV at 20% risk threshold.
- **Strengths**: Rigorous model validation (bootstrap, DeLong's test, calibration, DCA); clinically actionable performance metrics.
- **Limitations**: Single Canadian screening cohort; modest sample size; external validation not yet performed.
- **Conclusion**: Circulating miRNAs substantially improve HRA identification among FIT+ patients, warranting further evaluation in CRC screening programs.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-0233 / PMID:42867948

## 5. Joint Associations of Abdominal Obesity, General Obesity, and Smoking with Bladder Cancer Risk Among Postmenopausal Women: A Nationwide Cohort Study — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiology, Biomarkers & Prevention
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-13
- **Authors**: Han M et al.
- **Aims**: Investigate independent and joint associations of waist circumference, BMI, and smoking with bladder cancer risk in postmenopausal women.
- **Method**: Nationwide Korean cohort of 1,336,820 postmenopausal women; median 12.4-year follow-up; multivariable Cox models; joint waist circumference × BMI × smoking analysis.
- **Key findings**: Smoking HR 1.83 (95% CI 1.53–2.19); concurrent obesity (abdominal) and smoking HR 2.27 (95% CI 1.71–3.01); obesity alone elevated risk even among never-smokers.
- **Strengths**: Exceptionally large nationwide cohort with long follow-up; joint-exposure analyses highlight multiplicative risk.
- **Limitations**: National screening data may have selection bias; Korean-specific obesity criteria limit global generalisability.
- **Conclusion**: Obesity and smoking jointly substantially amplify bladder cancer risk in postmenopausal women, supporting combined prevention strategies.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-0285 / PMID:42867947

## Appraisal notes

This week's digest highlights two complementary themes: tumour-intrinsic heterogeneity as a determinant of clinical trajectories (APOBEC mutation timing governing metastatic latency; ESR1 variant-specific SERD benefit; Immunoscore immune stratification in HPV-OPSCC) and population-scale cancer prevention epidemiology (bladder cancer risk in 1.3M Korean women; miRNA-enhanced colorectal cancer screening in FIT+ individuals). Methodologically, the APOBEC/mutability paper demonstrates how large genomic databases can be repurposed to address classical epidemiological questions about disease onset and latency. The miRNA screening paper exemplifies rigorous risk prediction model development, with bootstrap calibration and decision curve analysis increasingly standard in biomarker epidemiology.
