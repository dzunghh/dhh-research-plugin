## 1. Obesity and Cancer Risk in Young Adults in Korea — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Annals of Internal Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-27
- **Authors**: Yun JS et al.
- **Aims**: Assess associations between obesity (BMI and waist circumference) and early-onset cancer risk in young Korean adults.
- **Method**: Nationwide population-based cohort, Korean National Health Insurance Service database 2009–2021, n=6,313,338 aged 20–39 years, mean follow-up 11.2 years.
- **Key findings**: 10-year cumulative incidence rose from 1.84% (normal weight) to 2.62% (class II obesity); abdominal obesity amplified risk beyond BMI category, especially thyroid, endometrial, and ovarian cancers.
- **Strengths**: Exceptionally large nationally representative cohort with 151,893 incident cancer cases; BMI and waist circumference jointly analysed.
- **Limitations**: Observational design; unmeasured confounding including smoking burden; sample limited to screening participants.
- **Conclusion**: Obesity in young Korean adults is independently associated with elevated early-onset cancer risk persisting into midlife, supporting early weight management for cancer prevention.
- **ID**: DOI:10.7326/ANNALS-25-02612 / PMID:42932883

## 2. First-Line Pembrolizumab vs Chemotherapy for dMMR Advanced Endometrial Cancer (KEYNOTE-C93) — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: The Lancet
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-26
- **Authors**: Slomovitz BM et al.
- **Aims**: Compare first-line pembrolizumab monotherapy versus carboplatin plus paclitaxel in mismatch repair-deficient advanced or recurrent endometrial cancer.
- **Method**: Phase 3 RCT (KEYNOTE-C93/GOG-3064/ENGOT-en15), 122 centres in 24 countries, n=299, 1:1 randomisation; dual primary endpoints PFS and OS.
- **Key findings**: Median PFS 38.2 vs 8.4 months (HR 0.57, p=0.0002); grade ≥3 treatment-related AEs 18% vs 51%.
- **Strengths**: Global multicentre Phase 3; dramatic PFS benefit with markedly better tolerability, enabling chemotherapy-free first-line treatment.
- **Limitations**: OS did not reach significance at interim analysis; pembrolizumab not compared against current IO plus chemotherapy standard of care.
- **Conclusion**: First-line pembrolizumab provides clinically meaningful PFS improvement with superior tolerability over chemotherapy in dMMR endometrial cancer.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S0140-6736(26)02095-7 / PMID:42933458

## 3. Neoadjuvant Short-Course RT + Camrelizumab + CAPOX for Locally Advanced Rectal Cancer (UNION) — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: The Lancet Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-25
- **Authors**: Tao K et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate event-free survival from a prespecified interim analysis of the UNION Phase 3 trial in locally advanced rectal cancer.
- **Method**: Open-label Phase 3 RCT, 9 Chinese hospitals, n=231; short-course RT (25 Gy/5 fr) + camrelizumab + CAPOX vs long-course chemoradiotherapy; median follow-up 36.7 months.
- **Key findings**: 3-year EFS 91.3% vs 77.4% (HR 0.33, p=0.0029); previously reported pCR rate 39.8% vs 15.3%.
- **Strengths**: Positive on both pCR and EFS endpoints; safety profile comparable to standard of care.
- **Limitations**: Single-country (China) trial; does not compare against contemporary total neoadjuvant therapy strategies.
- **Conclusion**: Short-course RT combined with immunotherapy and chemotherapy substantially improves event-free survival in locally advanced rectal cancer.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00446-8 / PMID:42927765

## 4. Sacituzumab Tirumotecan vs Treatment of Physician's Choice in Previously Treated Endometrial Cancer (TroFuse-005) — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: The Lancet
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-26
- **Authors**: Lorusso D et al.
- **Aims**: Compare TROP2-directed ADC sacituzumab tirumotecan (sac-TMT) versus physician's choice chemotherapy in endometrial cancer after platinum-based chemotherapy and immunotherapy.
- **Method**: Phase 3 RCT, 242 gynaecological oncology centres, 31 countries, n=776; dual co-primary endpoints PFS and OS; data cutoff March 2026.
- **Key findings**: Median PFS 7.2 vs 3.9 months (HR 0.59, p<0.0001); median OS 19.0 vs 12.1 months (HR 0.68, p=0.0001).
- **Strengths**: Global trial meeting both PFS and OS co-primary endpoints; broadly inclusive (any MMR status, any TROP2 expression level).
- **Limitations**: Grade ≥3 adverse events more frequent with sac-TMT (69% vs 57%); high use of platelet-elevating agents required.
- **Conclusion**: Sac-TMT significantly improves both PFS and OS over chemotherapy in post-platinum/IO endometrial cancer, establishing a new standard of care.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S0140-6736(26)02099-4 / PMID:42933460

## 5. Izalontamab Brengitecan vs Platinum-Based Chemotherapy in EGFR-Mutated Advanced NSCLC (PANKU-Lung01) — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: The Lancet
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-26
- **Authors**: Hong S et al.
- **Aims**: Compare izalontamab brengitecan (iza-bren), a first-in-class EGFR/HER3 bispecific ADC, versus platinum-based chemotherapy in EGFR-mutated NSCLC after 3rd-generation EGFR TKI progression.
- **Method**: Phase 3 RCT (PANKU-Lung01), 73 Chinese sites, n=432; primary endpoint BICR-assessed PFS; median follow-up 14.6 months.
- **Key findings**: Median PFS 11.1 vs 5.7 months (HR 0.40, p<0.0001); grade ≥3 TRAEs 79% vs 62%, predominantly haematological.
- **Strengths**: First Phase 3 evidence for a bispecific ADC class; strong PFS separation; definitive primary analysis.
- **Limitations**: China-only population limits global generalisability; OS data immature; higher haematological toxicity than chemotherapy.
- **Conclusion**: Iza-bren significantly prolongs PFS over chemotherapy in EGFR TKI-resistant NSCLC, providing first Phase 3 evidence for bispecific antibody-drug conjugates.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S0140-6736(26)02026-X / PMID:42933461

## Appraisal notes

This week's digest is dominated by landmark Phase 3 trials published simultaneously in The Lancet and Lancet Oncology, featuring antibody-drug conjugates (sac-TMT, iza-bren), immunotherapy monotherapy (pembrolizumab), and immunotherapy–chemoradiotherapy combinations — reflecting the field's accelerating shift toward biomarker-driven and targeted treatment strategies across multiple tumour types. The single strong epidemiological study (obesity and young-adult cancer risk in 6.3 million Koreans) reinforces the growing cancer burden attributable to metabolic risk factors in East Asian populations, with sex-differential and site-specific patterns warranting further study. A notable methodological trend is the adoption of BICR-assessed dual PFS+OS co-primary endpoints as the emerging gold standard in gynaecological oncology Phase 3 trials, enhancing regulatory and clinical validity.
