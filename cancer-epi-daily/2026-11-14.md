## 1. US Geographical Disparities in Young-Onset Colorectal Cancer Mortality, 1990–2023 — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: J Natl Cancer Inst
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-10
- **Authors**: Wu JF et al.
- **Aims**: Examine age-standardized mortality trends and geographic variation in young-onset CRC (ages 15–49) across US states using GBD 2023 data.
- **Method**: Ecological analysis; GBD 2023 estimates; Joinpoint regression; population attributable fraction (PAF) for modelled risk factors.
- **Key findings**: ASMR 3.25/100,000 in 2023 with >2-fold state variation; mean APC 1.06%; 10/13 highest-ASMR states in the South; 65.5% of deaths attributable to behavioral/metabolic risks.
- **Strengths**: 34-year longitudinal analysis, validated GBD data source, risk attribution quantifies modifiable drivers.
- **Limitations**: Ecological design; GBD estimates carry modelling uncertainty; PAFs cannot infer individual-level causation.
- **Conclusion**: Young-onset CRC mortality hotspots cluster in the South, driven by behavioral and metabolic factors, informing targeted state-level interventions.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag420 / PMID:43006908

## 2. Pretreatment Cognitive Screening, Treatment Tolerability, and Early Mortality in Older Adults with Advanced Cancer — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: J Natl Cancer Inst
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-10
- **Authors**: Mohamed M et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate whether abnormal pretreatment cognitive screening predicts treatment tolerability and mortality in older adults (≥70 y) with advanced cancer.
- **Method**: Secondary analysis of the usual-care arm, GAP70+ cluster-RCT (NCT02054741); multivariable logistic and Cox regression; n=368.
- **Key findings**: 32% had abnormal cognitive screening; OR=1.79 (95% CI 1.04–3.10) for unplanned hospitalization; HR=1.94 (95% CI 1.24–3.03) for 6-month mortality.
- **Strengths**: RCT-embedded cohort, multivariable adjustment for geriatric variables, prospective design minimises recall bias.
- **Limitations**: Secondary analysis of usual-care arm limits causal inference; Mini-Cog is brief; Asian/Black subgroup effects not fully reported.
- **Conclusion**: Abnormal cognitive screening predicts early mortality and hospitalisation, supporting routine Mini-Cog for risk stratification before initiating systemic therapy in older patients.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag417 / PMID:43006917

## 3. Prevalence and Actionability of MTAP Loss in Driver Oncogene-Positive and Oncogene-Negative Lung Cancer — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Clin Cancer Res
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-10
- **Authors**: Sakai T et al.
- **Aims**: Determine MTAP loss prevalence by IHC in NSCLC across driver-alteration subgroups and assess PRMT5 inhibitor activity.
- **Method**: Retrospective IHC in 243 NSCLC specimens (132 early-stage, 111 metastatic); PRMT5 inhibitor testing in 22 MTAP-deleted cell lines with TKI combinations.
- **Key findings**: MTAP loss in 17% of NSCLC overall; 24% in oncogene-driven vs 14% in driver-negative tumors; TKI + PRMT5 inhibitor combination showed greater activity than monotherapy.
- **Strengths**: Paired tumor-lymph node analysis, diverse driver subgroups (EGFR/KRAS/ALK), functional validation in multiple cell lines.
- **Limitations**: Single institution; cell-line-only functional data; p=0.314 for driver subgroup comparison (underpowered for subgroup inference).
- **Conclusion**: MTAP loss occurs in ~17% of NSCLC including 24% of oncogene-driven tumors, supporting PRMT5 inhibitor + TKI combination trials in this molecular subset.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-26-2695 / PMID:43008131

## 4. Treatment of Stage IB to III Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma: CSCO-ASCO Guideline Update — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: J Clin Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-10
- **Authors**: Chen YP et al.
- **Aims**: Update the 2021 CSCO-ASCO guideline on chemoradiotherapy for non-metastatic NPC, incorporating new RCT evidence on RT technique, chemotherapy sequencing, and immunotherapy.
- **Method**: Systematic review and multinational Expert Panel consensus; RCTs, meta-analyses, and prospective studies reviewed.
- **Key findings**: GP and TPF as preferred induction regimens; MRI-guided IMRT as standard; PD-1 inhibitors improve disease control but OS data remain immature; metronomic capecitabine recommended for high-residual-risk patients.
- **Strengths**: International multidisciplinary panel, transparent ASCO process, comprehensive evidence synthesis integrating Asian and Western RCTs.
- **Limitations**: Most trial data from Asia-Pacific populations; OS data for immunotherapy immature; guideline applicability in low-resource settings limited.
- **Conclusion**: Risk-adapted chemotherapy sequencing around IMRT is now evidence-based for NPC; routine PD-1 inhibitor addition cannot yet be recommended pending OS data.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1200/JCO-26-02648 / PMID:43008242

## 5. Advancing Gastric Cancer Prevention in the United States: Perspectives from a Congressional Forum — Score 3/6

- **Pub date**: 2026-11-10
- **Authors**: Hyun CS et al.
- **Aims**: Propose a multidisciplinary policy framework to implement established gastric cancer prevention strategies in US routine practice.
- **Method**: Policy commentary synthesizing a congressional forum with clinicians, researchers, advocates, and policymakers, integrated with published evidence.

## Appraisal notes
