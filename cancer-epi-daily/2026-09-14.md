## 1. LymphGen-Sig: Integrating Genetic and Transcriptional States to Predict Therapeutic Response in Diffuse Large B-Cell Lymphoma — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Journal of Clinical Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-09
- **Authors**: Tumuluru S et al.
- **Aims**: Develop a gene-expression classifier (LGsig) that harmonises genetic and non-genetic dimensions of DLBCL for subtype-specific treatment prediction.
- **Method**: Nearest shrunken centroid classifier trained on 764 paired genomic/transcriptomic samples; validated against POLARIX trial (n=678) comparing pola-R-CHP vs R-CHOP.
- **Key findings**: LGsig classified all DLBCLs including previously unclassified cases; identified subtypes with distinct survival benefit from pola-R-CHP (improvement over existing LymphGen tool).
- **Strengths**: Large external validation in a randomised trial; integrates transcriptional and genomic dimensions; clinically actionable output.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective transcriptomic data from archival POLARIX samples; generalisability to routine FFPE workflows not fully established.
- **Conclusion**: LGsig advances DLBCL precision medicine by enabling comprehensive molecular classification tied to specific treatment benefits.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1200/JCO-26-00451 / PMID:42715516

## 2. Commensal Nakaseomyces glabratus Migrates into Prostate Tumors to Accelerate Cancer Progression — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Nature Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-09
- **Authors**: Lai P et al.
- **Aims**: Determine whether gut commensal fungus Nakaseomyces glabratus contributes to castration-resistant prostate cancer (CRPC) progression.
- **Method**: Multi-level study: clinical sample correlation (fecal, blood, tumor), oral gavage mouse models, mechanistic Dectin-2/PMN-MDSC pathway analysis, and intestinal hydrogel intervention.
- **Key findings**: N. glabratus enriched in CRPC patients and correlated with poor overall survival; ADT increases gut permeability, facilitating fungal translocation that drives immunosuppression via PMN-MDSCs; hydrogel blockade reduced tumor infiltration.
- **Strengths**: Clinical-to-mechanistic breadth; novel therapeutic target (intestinal barrier); intervention validated in vivo.
- **Limitations**: Mouse models may not fully recapitulate human CRPC biology; human sample sizes not reported in abstract.
- **Conclusion**: Gut-to-tumor translocation of N. glabratus promotes endocrine resistance by enhancing immunosuppressive microenvironment in prostate cancer.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s43018-026-01229-9 / PMID:42716968

## 3. UniCure: A Multi-Modal Model for Predicting Personalized Cancer Therapy Response — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Cell
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-09
- **Authors**: Chen Z et al.
- **Aims**: Build a multi-modal foundation model to predict personalized drug-induced transcriptomic responses and rank therapies across diverse patient contexts.
- **Method**: Biological and chemical foundation models combined; trained on 1.9 million transcriptomic perturbation profiles (>22,000 compounds, 166 cell types, 24 tissues); fine-tuned on 345 patient-derived tumor-like cluster profiles; validated on 396 clinical profiles.
- **Key findings**: UniCure accurately predicted dose-dependent and combination drug responses; generalised from bulk to single-cell data; enabled patient-level stratification validated experimentally.
- **Strengths**: Massive training corpus; multi-context generalisability; experimental validation in patient-derived models.
- **Limitations**: Reliance on transcriptomic data; clinical validation sample size (n=396) modest relative to model complexity.
- **Conclusion**: UniCure provides a practical framework for translating preclinical transcriptomic data into personalised therapeutic strategies.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.ccell.2026.07.010 / PMID:42716007

## 4. Neoadjuvant Pertuzumab Biosimilar HLX11 Versus Reference Pertuzumab for Early-stage or Locally Advanced Breast Cancer: A Phase 3 Equivalence Trial — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Clinical Cancer Research
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-09
- **Authors**: Zhang J et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate whether pertuzumab biosimilar HLX11 is equivalent to reference pertuzumab in neoadjuvant HER2+/HR− breast cancer treatment.
- **Method**: Randomised double-blind phase 3 equivalence trial (n=908); 1:1 allocation to HLX11 or EU-pertuzumab plus trastuzumab/docetaxel; primary endpoint BICR-assessed total pathological complete response (tpCR).
- **Key findings**: tpCR rates 46.3% vs 45.8% (relative risk 1.01, 90% CI 0.90–1.14); equivalence criteria met; no meaningful differences in safety, PK, or immunogenicity.
- **Strengths**: Large pre-specified RCT; blinded central review; adjuvant switching analysis included.
- **Limitations**: Limited to HER2+/HR− subtype; long-term outcomes not reported in this analysis.
- **Conclusion**: HLX11 demonstrates therapeutic equivalence to reference pertuzumab, supporting its use as a lower-cost alternative to expand treatment access.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-26-1604 / PMID:42714872

## 5. Ceralasertib plus Durvalumab in RAS Mutant and RAS Wild-Type Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer: Two Cohorts of the Phase II National Lung Matrix Trial — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: Clinical Cancer Research
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-09
- **Authors**: Middleton G et al.
- **Aims**: Assess activity of ATR inhibitor ceralasertib plus anti-PD-L1 durvalumab in ICB-resistant KRAS mutant and wild-type NSCLC.
- **Method**: Prospective parallel-cohort phase II platform trial (National Lung Matrix Trial); Bayesian primary outcome estimation of 24-week durable clinical benefit and objective response.
- **Key findings**: 24-week DCB 37.7% (J1/KRAS) vs 29.3% (NAJ/wild-type); two objective responses both in KRAS mutant; trial underpowered (target n=30 not reached per cohort).
- **Strengths**: Platform trial design; biomarker-stratified enrollment; Bayesian analytical framework.
- **Limitations**: Did not reach target recruitment (funding closure); modest differences between cohorts not statistically significant.
- **Conclusion**: Ceralasertib/durvalumab shows modest activity in ICB-resistant NSCLC; KRAS mutation alone insufficient to predict benefit from this combination.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-26-1365 / PMID:42714874

## Appraisal notes

This week's digest highlights a convergence of precision oncology tools with mechanistic epidemiology: LymphGen-Sig and UniCure both demonstrate that integrating multi-omic layers (transcriptomic + genomic, or transcriptomic + chemical) substantially improves patient-level prediction over single-modality approaches. The Nakaseomyces glabratus paper is a standout methodologically — linking gut mycobiome dysbiosis (driven by androgen-deprivation therapy) to prostate tumour immunosuppression through a translocation mechanism, with a therapeutic proof-of-concept — reflecting growing sophistication in host–microbiome cancer-risk research. The biosimilar equivalence trial rounds out the set with direct health-access implications for HER2+ breast cancer globally.
