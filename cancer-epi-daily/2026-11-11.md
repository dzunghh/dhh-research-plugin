## 1. The risk of gastric MALT lymphoma after Helicobacter pylori eradication treatment — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-06
- **Authors**: Leijonmarck W et al.
- **Aims**: Determine whether H. pylori eradication reduces risk of developing gastric MALT lymphoma.
- **Method**: Population-based cohort study; 567,984 individuals in 4 Nordic countries; SIR vs. background population; 18-year follow-up using nationwide registries.
- **Key findings**: Risk elevated in first 1–5 years post-eradication (SIR 2.65, 95% CI 1.52–4.31); indistinguishable from background at 6–18 years (SIR 0.96, 95% CI 0.39–1.99).
- **Strengths**: Unprecedented cohort size (567,984); near-complete 18-year follow-up via linked registries across 4 countries.
- **Limitations**: H. pylori eradication confirmed by prescription, not test-of-cure; observational design.
- **Conclusion**: H. pylori eradication normalises gastric MALT lymphoma risk after six years, supporting preventive treatment strategies.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag422 / PMID:42992951

## 2. Multi-cancer risk prediction in asymptomatic adults using urinary glycosaminoglycan profiling — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiology, Biomarkers & Prevention
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-06
- **Authors**: Gatto F et al.
- **Aims**: Investigate whether urinary glycosaminoglycan (GAGome) profiles predict multi-cancer risk in asymptomatic adults.
- **Method**: Case-cohort study within Lifelines Cohort (Netherlands); 5,436 asymptomatic adults; 827 incident cancers within 5 years; Cox proportional hazards modeling.
- **Key findings**: Per-SD GAGome increase HR = 1.66 (95% CI 1.60–1.73); 5-year risks: 0.18% (Low), 2.3% (Intermediate), 7.6% (High), 32% (Very High) groups.
- **Strengths**: Prospective case-cohort design; multi-cancer endpoint; urine-based non-invasive biomarker.
- **Limitations**: Single-cohort validation; clinical utility and cost-effectiveness not established.
- **Conclusion**: Urinary GAGome profiling identifies high-risk individuals for personalised cancer surveillance beyond current screening criteria.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-1059 / PMID:42993180

## 3. Text message reminders vs patient navigation for bowel cancer screening uptake: a three-arm RCT — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: British Journal of Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-05
- **Authors**: Siddique B et al.
- **Aims**: Compare text message reminders (TMR) versus patient navigation (PN) to increase bowel cancer screening uptake.
- **Method**: Three-arm RCT (n = 3,129) in London among non-completers; ITT and per-protocol logistic regression analysis.
- **Key findings**: TMR and PN both improved uptake vs usual care (6.5% and 7.5% vs 2.0%); PN more effective than TMR for men (14.5% vs 6.7%, p < 0.001).
- **Strengths**: Pre-registered three-arm RCT; large sample; first direct TMR vs PN comparison in cancer screening.
- **Limitations**: Single-city London sample; 26-week follow-up; potential self-selection in per-protocol analysis.
- **Conclusion**: Patient navigation should be prioritised over text reminders for men to reduce bowel cancer screening disparities.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41416-026-03645-1 / PMID:42988857

## 4. Treatment-related neutropenia as predictor of palbociclib response in the PALLAS trial — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-05
- **Authors**: Fanucci K et al.
- **Aims**: Assess whether treatment-related neutropenia grade identifies patients benefiting from adjuvant palbociclib.
- **Method**: Exploratory analysis of Phase III PALLAS trial (5,736 HR+ breast cancer patients); high-grade vs low-grade neutropenia vs ET-alone; time-dependent Cox regression for 5-year iDFS.
- **Key findings**: High-grade neutropenia (HGN) group had superior iDFS vs ET-alone (HR 1.19, 95% CI 1.01–1.40, p = 0.04), persisting after adjustment for race, BMI, prior chemotherapy.
- **Strengths**: Large Phase III trial dataset; clinically actionable pharmacodynamic biomarker; multivariate adjustment.
- **Limitations**: Post-hoc exploratory analysis; neutropenia influenced by BMI and race, introducing confounding.
- **Conclusion**: High-grade neutropenia may serve as a predictive biomarker for palbociclib efficacy in adjuvant HR+ breast cancer.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag416 / PMID:42986066

## 5. Long-term fatigue trajectories up to five years after radiotherapy in non-metastatic prostate cancer (REQUITE study) — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: British Journal of Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-04
- **Authors**: Heumann P et al.
- **Aims**: Characterise long-term fatigue trajectories and predictors up to 5 years post-radiotherapy in prostate cancer patients.
- **Method**: Prospective international cohort (REQUITE; 1,758 men); EORTC QLQ-C30 fatigue; latent class growth analysis; logistic regression for 2-year fatigue predictors.
- **Key findings**: 35% experienced clinically important fatigue at ≥1 timepoint; four trajectory classes (low 45%, moderate 42%, elevated-decreasing 8%, increasing 6%); diabetes associated with increasing fatigue trajectory.
- **Strengths**: Large international multi-centre cohort; validated instrument; novel multi-trajectory classification.
- **Limitations**: Radiotherapy-only cohort; self-reported fatigue; no objective fatigue measures.
- **Conclusion**: Diabetes and baseline fatigue predict long-term increasing fatigue, supporting risk-stratified survivorship monitoring.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41416-026-03644-2 / PMID:42982989
