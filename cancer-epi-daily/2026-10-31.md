## 1. Assessment of comprehensiveness of national cancer control plans using a multiagent artificial intelligence model: a global analysis — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-11
- **Authors**: Kumar CJ et al.
- **Aims**: Assess comprehensiveness of national cancer control plans (NCCPs) across 50 countries using a validated multiagent AI model (PRISM).
- **Method**: Cross-sectional policy analysis; PRISM (Qwen2.5-VL-72B + Llama 4 Scout) applied to 50 NCCPs; 12-element/76-sub-element framework validated via two-stage Delphi with 67 experts.
- **Key findings**: Median overall comprehensiveness 2.78/5 (IQR 2.25–3.09); Europe highest (2.96), western Pacific lowest (2.21). Financing (1.88) and outcomes (1.83) had lowest element scores.
- **Strengths**: First global AI-driven policy analysis of NCCPs; Delphi-validated framework; covers all WHO regions and income groups.
- **Limitations**: Single time-point analysis; excluded draft, sub-national, and superseded plans; AI may miss policy nuance.
- **Conclusion**: NCCP comprehensiveness varies substantially globally; scalable AI appraisal tools could standardise cross-country monitoring and strengthen cancer control planning.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00281-0 / PMID:42933439

## 2. Zamtocabtagene autoleucel, a tandem CD20-CD19-directed CAR T-cell therapy, for second line treatment of large B-cell lymphoma in patients ineligible for transplantation (DALY 2-EU) — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-11
- **Authors**: Borchmann P et al.
- **Aims**: Compare zamtocabtagene autoleucel (zamto-cel) vs R-GemOx chemoimmunotherapy in transplant-ineligible relapsed/refractory large B-cell lymphoma.
- **Method**: Open-label multicentre phase 2 RCT (DALY 2-EU; 45 centres, 12 European countries); 1:1 randomisation; primary endpoint event-free survival by masked independent review committee.
- **Key findings**: Median event-free survival 6.21 months (zamto-cel) vs 2.53 months (R-GemOx; HR 0.39, 95% CI 0.27–0.58; p<0.0001). Grade ≥3 adverse events 83% vs 86%.
- **Strengths**: First RCT of CAR T-cell therapy in transplant-ineligible patients; multicenter, stratified randomisation, masked IRC review.
- **Limitations**: Open-label; phase 2 (n=160); short vein-to-vein time may limit global generalisability.
- **Conclusion**: Zamto-cel supports consideration as a second-line standard-of-care for transplant-ineligible relapsed/refractory large B-cell lymphoma.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00290-1 / PMID:42933440

## 3. ENTRÉE Lung: An Open-label Platform Study Investigating the Safety and Pharmacokinetics of Belrestotug Combinations in Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Clin Cancer Res
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-26
- **Authors**: Spigel DR et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate safety, pharmacokinetics, and preliminary efficacy of dostarlimab + belrestotug (anti-TIGIT) ± nelistotug (anti-CD96) in post-PD-(L)1/platinum advanced NSCLC.
- **Method**: Open-label international platform trial (NCT03739710) with dose-escalation/expansion safety cohorts and one randomised arm (n=62 total).
- **Key findings**: Grade ≥3 TRAEs in 17%/6%/14% across arms; no clinically meaningful anti-tumour activity; one partial response observed.
- **Strengths**: First published data on triple TIGIT+CD96+PD-1 checkpoint blockade; multicentre, multinational design with randomised arm.
- **Limitations**: Small N, open-label, heavily pre-treated population; negative efficacy results limit interpretability.
- **Conclusion**: The anti-TIGIT + anti-CD96 + anti-PD-1 triplet was well tolerated but showed no meaningful activity in this setting, informing future immunotherapy combination strategies.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-25-4851 / PMID:42931848

## 4. Dual VEGF and PD-(L)1 Blockade: Facts and Hopes — Score 2/6

- **Journal**: Clin Cancer Res
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-26
- **Authors**: Terme M et al.
- **Aims**: Synthesise the biological rationale and clinical evidence for anti-VEGF-A/PD-(L)1 combination therapy across tumour types.
- **Method**: Narrative review; summarises approved combinations and discusses disappointing results in colorectal cancer and glioblastoma.
- **Key findings**: Anti-VEGF/PD-(L)1 approved in renal, hepatocellular, NSCLC, and endometrial cancers; limited activity in CRC and GBM despite immunological rationale.
- **Strengths**: Comprehensive cross-tumour synthesis; discusses tumour microenvironment mechanisms and emerging strategies.
- **Limitations**: Narrative review; no systematic search; heterogeneous patient populations across cited trials.
- **Conclusion**: Dual VEGF/PD-(L)1 blockade has proven value in select cancers; understanding resistance in CRC/GBM is the next frontier.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-25-3556 / PMID:42932566

## 5. Breast cancer treatment: When less is more — Score 1/6

- **Journal**: PLoS Med
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-26
- **Authors**: Starowicz K et al.
- **Aims**: Argue that precision oncology's greatest opportunity may be identifying patients who can safely receive less treatment to avoid overtreatment.
- **Method**: Editorial/commentary synthesising evidence on de-escalation in breast cancer systemic therapy.
- **Key findings**: Overtreatment causes severe side effects with limited incremental benefit; precision tools now enable risk-stratified de-escalation strategies.
- **Strengths**: Timely perspective on overtreatment in the era of precision oncology; highlights patient quality-of-life considerations.
- **Limitations**: Editorial with no primary data; specific de-escalation criteria not provided.
- **Conclusion**: Identifying patients for safe de-escalation of breast cancer therapy is as important as finding new treatment targets in precision oncology.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1371/journal.pmed.1005284 / PMID:42932385
