## 1. Sacituzumab Tirumotecan vs Chemotherapy in TNBC: Final OS Analysis of Phase III OptiTROP-Breast01 — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Annals of Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-12-31
- **Authors**: Xu B et al.
- **Aims**: Confirm the sustained overall survival (OS) benefit of sacituzumab tirumotecan (sac-TMT) over chemotherapy in previously treated advanced triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC).
- **Method**: Phase III RCT (n=263), 1:1 randomization, OS key secondary endpoint; RPSFT adjustment for 27.8% crossover to ADC in control arm; median follow-up 29.4 months.
- **Key findings**: Median OS 14.7 vs 9.9 months (HR 0.60, 95% CI 0.45–0.80); 30-month OS rates 26.8% vs 13.2%.
- **Strengths**: Extended follow-up with RPSFT crossover adjustment; patient-reported outcome analyses across five HRQoL domains.
- **Limitations**: Single-country (China) population; small subgroup sizes limit heterogeneity analysis.
- **Conclusion**: sac-TMT delivers a clinically meaningful, sustained OS advantage with no new safety signals in advanced TNBC.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.annonc.2026.10.098 / PMID:42933638

## 2. Polygenic and Classical Risk Integration Reshapes Breast Cancer Screening in 9,761 Asian Women — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: BMC Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-30
- **Authors**: Li J et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate risk-stratified mammography screening using polygenic risk score (PRS) plus Gail model in Asian women, where prospective evidence is absent.
- **Method**: Multicentre prospective cohort BREATHE (n=4,513 women aged 35–59), validated in two independent case cohorts (SGBCC, MyBrCa; combined n=9,761); absolute 5-year risk threshold 0.9%.
- **Key findings**: Among women 40–49 recommended annual mammography, risk stratification downgraded 38.7% to no screening; combined PRS+Gail captured 68–69% of cases vs ~54% with PRS alone.
- **Strengths**: First prospective multicentre evidence for PRS-integrated screening in Asian women; cross-validated in two independent cohorts from Singapore and Malaysia.
- **Limitations**: Limited events in BREATHE (n=41); external validation outside Singapore/Malaysia needed.
- **Conclusion**: Integrating PRS with classical risk factors substantially reshapes screening recommendations; PRS-missed cancers display biologically aggressive profiles requiring future tool refinement.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1186/s12916-026-05334-5 / PMID:42963548

## 3. EHR Phenotype Choice and Real-World Outcome Estimates: Three Breast Cancer Definitions in TriNetX — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: PLoS One
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-30
- **Authors**: Chiang JK et al.
- **Aims**: Quantify how EHR-based breast cancer phenotype definitions affect real-world outcome estimates in the TriNetX Global Collaborative Network.
- **Method**: Retrospective benchmarking cohort; three operational phenotypes (Dx1, Dx2, DxTx) applied to ≥1.4M patients; pairwise propensity score matching on 13 covariates; outcomes: all-cause mortality, ED visits; negative-control outcomes used.
- **Key findings**: More restrictive definitions showed higher mortality hazard (HR 1.26–1.32 for DxTx vs Dx1); negative-control outcomes near null confirming phenotype-specific differences.
- **Strengths**: Very large EHR sample from global network; use of negative-control outcomes strengthens causal inference validity.
- **Limitations**: Single database; associations reflect selection/ascertainment differences, not treatment effects.
- **Conclusion**: Breast cancer EHR phenotypes are not analytically interchangeable; phenotype specification should be a reported core analytic decision.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1371/journal.pone.0361883 / PMID:42961971

## 4. COG Long-Term Follow-Up Guideline for Cancer Genetics Evaluation in Childhood Cancer Survivors — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: The Lancet Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-11
- **Authors**: Janardan SK et al.
- **Aims**: Develop a standardized, evidence-based guideline for cancer genetics evaluation in childhood cancer survivors at high risk for cancer predisposition syndromes (CPS).
- **Method**: Evidence-based policy review; systematic literature synthesis integrated into Children's Oncology Group Long-Term Follow-Up Guidelines; multidisciplinary expert panel.
- **Key findings**: Up to 12% of childhood cancer survivors harbour an underlying CPS; new guideline identifies high-risk survivors warranting genetics referral and germline testing.
- **Strengths**: First standardized COG guideline addressing genetics integration into survivorship care; endorsed by a major cooperative group.
- **Limitations**: Evidence base sparse for rare CPS; implementation across varied healthcare settings will be challenging.
- **Conclusion**: New COG guideline standardizes CPS identification in childhood cancer survivors to improve tailored screening and outcomes.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00340-2 / PMID:42933447

## 5. Curative-Intent Management of De Novo Metastatic Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma: International Consensus Guideline — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: The Lancet Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-11
- **Authors**: Guo QJ et al.
- **Aims**: Provide evidence-based international consensus recommendations for curative-intent treatment of de novo metastatic nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC).
- **Method**: Consensus-building by 54 experts from 46 centres in 18 countries; 32 recommendations across 7 clinical domains; strong (≥80% agreement) or conditional (67–79%) classification.
- **Key findings**: Curative intent appropriate for fit patients with ≤3 metastatic lesions in ≤2 organs; multimodality treatment (immunochemotherapy + locoregional RT + metastasis-directed therapy + maintenance) recommended.
- **Strengths**: Largest and most geographically diverse international consensus for NPC; endorsed by 17 oncology organisations.
- **Limitations**: Evidence quality variable across domains; most supporting studies are observational or from single regions.
- **Conclusion**: First international consensus guideline provides a standardized framework for curative-intent NPC management and identifies evidence gaps.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00347-5 / PMID:42933448
