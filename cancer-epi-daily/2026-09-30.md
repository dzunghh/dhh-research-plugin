## 1. Global burden of cancer attributable to infections in 2024: a worldwide incidence analysis — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-01
- **Authors**: Rumgay H et al.
- **Aims**: Estimate the global incidence of cancer cases attributable to 12 Group 1 carcinogenic infectious agents in 2024.
- **Method**: Worldwide incidence analysis applying population-attributable fractions to GLOBOCAN 2024 data, stratified by sex, country, age, UN subregion, and World Bank income group.
- **Key findings**: ~2.3 million cancer cases (12% of all cancers) were infection-attributable in 2024; H. pylori (760 000) and HPV (750 000) accounted for the largest shares. Eastern Asia had the highest regional burden (990 000 cases, ASIR 31.9/100 000).
- **Strengths**: Comprehensive IARC-led global estimates covering 12 infectious agents with latest GLOBOCAN data and detailed stratification.
- **Limitations**: PAF estimates rely on exposure prevalence data of variable quality across low-income countries.
- **Conclusion**: Infection control strategies—vaccination, test-and-treat, and screening—remain underused yet evidence-based levers for global cancer prevention.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00307-4 / PMID:42805198

## 2. Hypofractionated versus conventional fractionated postmastectomy radiotherapy for high-risk breast cancer (CHN HYPOPMRT): 10-year outcomes from a phase 3 RCT — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-01
- **Authors**: Sun GY et al.
- **Aims**: Assess long-term (10-year) efficacy and adverse events of hypofractionated vs conventional postmastectomy radiotherapy in high-risk breast cancer.
- **Method**: Randomised, non-inferiority, phase 3 trial (n=810 women); 43.5 Gy/15 fractions vs 50 Gy/25 fractions; median follow-up 11.5 years.
- **Key findings**: 10-year locoregional recurrence was 12.0% (hypo) vs 10.3% (conventional); HR 1.19 (95% CI 0.79–1.82). No grade 4–5 late events or brachial plexopathy in either arm.
- **Strengths**: Longest follow-up RCT for postmastectomy hypofractionation with prespecified 10-year analysis and comprehensive late-toxicity assessment.
- **Limitations**: Single-centre Chinese cohort; no data on race/ethnicity; 94% stage III limits generalisability.
- **Conclusion**: Hypofractionated postmastectomy radiotherapy provides durable locoregional control with no increase in severe late toxicity, supporting broader adoption.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00297-4 / PMID:42805199

## 3. Prostate Cancer Screening and Likelihood of Benefit in Veterans Affairs and Fee-for-Service Medicare — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Internal Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-28
- **Authors**: Bryant AK et al.
- **Aims**: Benchmark PSA testing and biopsy rates in VA versus fee-for-service Medicare primary care by likelihood of benefit from screening.
- **Method**: Retrospective cohort study using national linked VA-Medicare data (FY2004–2023); 1.41 million dually enrolled male veterans ≥65 years; adjusted Poisson regression.
- **Key findings**: Among men ≥80 years, VA had lower adjusted PSA testing (187.9 vs 267.9/1000 person-years, IRR 0.70) and biopsy rates (IRR 0.67). VA testing was higher among men aged 65–69 (IRR 1.10).
- **Strengths**: Large national linked dataset with 20-year follow-up spanning multiple guideline eras; life-expectancy stratification enables meaningful value-of-care assessment.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective design; cannot establish causality; Medicare Advantage members excluded, limiting generalisability to some older adults.
- **Conclusion**: VA primary care aligns PSA screening more closely with likelihood of benefit than FFS Medicare, particularly in men least likely to benefit.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamainternmed.2026.4660 / PMID:42804174

## 4. Diagnostic yield of cancer predisposition in a nationwide prospective childhood acute leukemia cohort — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Nature Communications
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-28
- **Authors**: Taylan F et al.
- **Aims**: Assess diagnostic yield and clinical utility of whole-genome sequencing (WGS) for cancer predisposition in childhood acute leukemia.
- **Method**: Nationwide, prospective, population-based Swedish cohort (n=181 children); three-pronged strategy: systematic phenotyping, 189-gene germline WGS panel, and tumor sequencing of positive cases; trio-WGS in high-suspicion families.
- **Key findings**: Pathogenic germline alterations in 9/181 (5%): 6 (3.3%) leukemia predisposition syndromes and 3 (1.6%) solid tumor predisposition syndromes. Findings led to surveillance changes in 8/9 and treatment modifications in 4/9.
- **Strengths**: Nationwide, prospective, uniform WGS application; high actionability of findings supports clinical validity.
- **Limitations**: Small sample (n=181); single country; modest diagnostic yield limits immediate policy translation.
- **Conclusion**: Systematic germline WGS in childhood leukemia is feasible and actionable despite modest overall yield; panel breadth should reflect local resources.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41467-026-78170-z / PMID:42806009

## 5. Unraveling the lexicon of complex mutational phenomena in human cancers — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Nature Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-25
- **Authors**: Yang T et al.
- **Aims**: Provide a comprehensive, harmonised lexicon of complex genomic mutational phenomena in cancer and clarify definitional ambiguities.
- **Method**: Systematic review and perspective synthesising 15 years of cancer genomics literature on phenomena including kataegis, chromothripsis, chromoplexy, and related structural events.
- **Key findings**: Multiple mutational phenomena are associated with distinct molecular mechanisms, drug resistance signatures, and clinical outcomes; current nomenclature carries significant definitional ambiguity.
- **Strengths**: Authoritative synthesis from leading mutational signatures group (Alexandrov lab); directly addresses reproducibility gaps in cancer genomics nomenclature.
- **Limitations**: Perspective article; not a primary data study; mechanistic understanding of several phenomena remains incomplete.
- **Conclusion**: Standardising mutational phenomena nomenclature is essential for reproducible cancer genomics research and clinical translation.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s43018-026-01242-y / PMID:42791387
