## 1. SBRT plus abiraterone acetate and ADT versus abiraterone acetate and ADT in oligometastatic castrate-resistant prostate cancer (ARTO): long-term, unplanned overall survival analysis of an open-label, randomised, phase 2 trial — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-07
- **Authors**: Francolini G et al.
- **Aims**: Assess whether adding metastasis-directed SBRT to abiraterone+ADT improves long-term overall survival in oligometastatic CRPC.
- **Method**: Multicenter open-label phase 2 RCT (16 Italian centres, n=157), ITT analysis, median follow-up 53 months.
- **Key findings**: Median OS 50 months (control) vs not reached (SBRT arm); HR 0.55 (95% CI 0.33–0.92), p=0.021.
- **Strengths**: Registered multicenter RCT with long follow-up; ITT design; stratified randomisation.
- **Limitations**: Unplanned OS analysis; small sample (n=157); single-country (Italy); no race/ethnicity data collected.
- **Conclusion**: Adding SBRT to systemic therapy yields a significant OS benefit in oligometastatic CRPC.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00178-6 / PMID:42372744

## 2. Leveraging primary care data to understand military veteran health in England: feasibility study and matched-control comparison of recorded conditions — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: BMC Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-06-30
- **Authors**: Almeida-Meza P et al.
- **Aims**: Validate veteran identification in CPRD and compare recorded prevalence of health conditions vs matched non-veterans.
- **Method**: Matched cohort study in CPRD (n=122,484 veterans; n=244,573 non-veterans); Poisson regression for adjusted prevalence ratios; GP validation of veteran status.
- **Key findings**: Veterans had higher recorded prevalence for PTSD (aPR 16.43), prostate cancer (aPR 1.83, 95% CI 1.73–1.94), and COPD (aPR 1.83, 95% CI 1.77–1.89).
- **Strengths**: Very large nationally representative matched cohort; validated record-linkage methodology; comprehensive multi-condition coverage.
- **Limitations**: Cross-sectional prevalence; recording bias probable (veteran conditions may be more systematically captured); no causal inference possible.
- **Conclusion**: CPRD-based linkage is a valid and scalable platform for UK veteran cancer and health surveillance.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1186/s12916-026-05005-5 / PMID:42380894

## 3. Comprehensive CYP2D6 genotyping improves phenotype classification and highlights methodological pitfalls in tamoxifen-treated breast cancer — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: BMC Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-01
- **Authors**: Alkebbeh L et al.
- **Aims**: Expand CYP2D6 genetic profiling in Syrian breast cancer patients and evaluate its impact on phenotype classification and tamoxifen outcomes.
- **Method**: Retrospective cohort; strip-based genotyping plus PCR for copy-number variation; NGS validation; time-to-event (DFS) analysis.
- **Key findings**: 19% of patients were reclassified with expanded genotyping; no significant overall CYP2D6–DFS association; stage II subgroup HR 3.59 (95% CI 2.34–67.48, imprecise).
- **Strengths**: Demonstrates that allele dropout in conventional assays leads to systematic misclassification; NGS validation.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective; small sample; Syrian-specific population; very wide CIs in subgroup analyses limit interpretability.
- **Conclusion**: Comprehensive genotyping is methodologically necessary for pharmacogenetic studies but clinical utility in this cohort remains unproven.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1186/s12885-026-16440-1 / PMID:42380819
