## 1. Communicating Uncertainties About the Benefits and Risks of New Cancer Drugs: Two Randomized Clinical Trials — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Intern Med
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-12
- **Authors**: Cherla A et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate whether communicating common uncertainties about new cancer drug benefits/risks affects patient hypothetical treatment decisions.
- **Method**: Two online RCTs (n=6,272 US adults, census-matched for age, sex, race, education) testing plain-language uncertainty statements on drug-taking decisions.
- **Key findings**: Communicating uncertainties reduced hypothetical drug-taking by 10.1 pp (95% CI: −11.7 to −8.5). Dose-dependent effect observed — each additional uncertainty further reduced willingness to take the drug.
- **Strengths**: Demographically diverse census-matched sample; two complementary RCTs enabling dose-response assessment of uncertainty communication.
- **Limitations**: Hypothetical decisions may not reflect real-world drug uptake; online panel may not fully represent clinical decision-making contexts.
- **Conclusion**: Communicating drug uncertainty reduces patient willingness to take new cancer drugs, with a dose-dependent effect suggesting cumulative impact.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamainternmed.2026.4969 / PMID:42862585

## 2. Development and Validation of a Blood Test Informed Risk Prediction Model for Lung Cancer — The Copenhagen Lung Cancer Risk Model (CPH-Lung) — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: J Clin Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-12
- **Authors**: Skov-Jeppesen SM et al.
- **Aims**: Develop and externally validate a blood test-augmented model (CPH-Lung) to improve lung cancer risk stratification prior to image-based screening.
- **Method**: Prospective nested case-control study in two population cohorts (CGPS training n=689 cases/1,924 controls; CCHS external validation n=3,195 ever-smokers).
- **Key findings**: CPH-Lung AUC 0.783 vs PLCOm2012 AUC 0.720 in validation (p=0.006); sensitivity 57.6% vs 39.2% in women at 77.7% specificity. Detected younger cases (mean age 68.2 vs 76.5 years).
- **Strengths**: External validation in independent population cohort; improvement most pronounced in women and younger smokers underserved by current criteria.
- **Limitations**: Danish population limits generalisability; blood biomarker availability varies; limited to 6-year risk horizon.
- **Conclusion**: Blood test-augmented CPH-Lung model substantially outperforms PLCOm2012 for lung cancer risk stratification, especially in women.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1200/JCO-25-02326 / PMID:42863103

## 3. Sofetabart Mipitecan, an Antibody Drug Conjugate Targeting Folate Receptor Alpha, in High Grade Ovarian Cancer: Results From the First-in-Human Phase 1 Study — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Ann Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-08
- **Authors**: Ray-Coquard I et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate safety, efficacy, and pharmacokinetics of novel FRα-targeting ADC sofetabart mipitecan (Sofe-M) in heavily pretreated high-grade ovarian cancer.
- **Method**: Multicenter open-label phase 1 dose-escalation trial (n=105 HGOC patients) including those with FRα <75% expression and prior mirvetuximab soravtansine exposure.
- **Key findings**: Overall response rate 50.5% (95% CI: 40.5–60.4%); 57.9% ORR in prior mirvetuximab-exposed patients. No keratopathies or grade ≥3 neuropathy; median PFS 8.1 months.
- **Strengths**: Includes patients with prior ADC exposure and low FRα expression; multicenter design with durable clinical activity across FRα expression levels.
- **Limitations**: Single-arm phase 1 design without comparator; higher toxicity rate at 4 mg/kg (56.4% grade ≥3 TEAEs) limits dosing flexibility.
- **Conclusion**: Sofe-M demonstrates robust anti-tumour activity regardless of FRα expression or prior ADC exposure, supporting 3 mg/kg as recommended phase 3 dose.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.annonc.2026.10.001 / PMID:42849725

## 4. Randomized Trial of Hypofractionated Postmastectomy Radiation Therapy in Women With Breast Reconstruction (RT CHARM, Alliance A221505) — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: J Clin Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-12
- **Authors**: Poppe MM et al.
- **Aims**: Establish noninferiority of 16-fraction hypofractionated PMRT versus conventional 25-fraction PMRT in women undergoing breast reconstruction after mastectomy.
- **Method**: Multicenter noninferiority RCT (n=898) stratified by reconstruction type and timing; primary composite endpoint was reconstruction complications at 24 months.
- **Key findings**: 24-month reconstruction complication rates 12.3% vs 10.2% (difference −2.1%, 95% CI: −6.5 to 2.2), meeting noninferiority margin. No significant difference in 36-month locoregional recurrence.
- **Strengths**: Large Alliance multicenter trial with median 3.9-year follow-up; robust stratification by reconstruction type enabling subgroup analysis.
- **Limitations**: Actual complication rates lower than assumed 25%; primarily implant-based reconstructions; longer-term cosmetic outcomes not reported.
- **Conclusion**: Hypofractionated 16-fraction PMRT is noninferior to conventional PMRT for breast reconstruction complication rates, enabling shorter treatment courses.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1200/JCO-26-00474 / PMID:42863096

## 5. How Would You Screen This 26-Year-Old Woman for Cervical Cancer? Grand Rounds Discussion From Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: Ann Intern Med
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-13
- **Authors**: Smith CC et al.
- **Aims**: Debate optimal cervical cancer screening strategies for young women in light of updated HPV primary testing guidelines and self-collected specimen options.
- **Method**: Expert case-based Grand Rounds discussion comparing primary HPV testing vs cytology, incorporating updated ACS, WPSI, and draft USPSTF 2026 recommendations.
- **Key findings**: Updated guidelines prefer primary HPV testing as the preferred strategy; self-collected specimens are now an accepted option. Half of cervical cancers occur in underscreened or never-screened women.
- **Strengths**: Integrates multiple updated national guidelines; explicitly addresses health equity dimensions and screening disparities across race and geography.
- **Limitations**: Expert opinion/discussion format, not primary data; recommendations may not apply uniformly across different healthcare settings or resource levels.
- **Conclusion**: Primary HPV testing with self-collection is the preferred cervical cancer screening approach under updated guidelines, particularly for underserved populations.
- **ID**: DOI:10.7326/ANNALS-26-02904 / PMID:42863118
