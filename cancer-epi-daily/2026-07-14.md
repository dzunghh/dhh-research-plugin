## 1. Setmelanotide for the Treatment of Acquired Hypothalamic Obesity — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: N Engl J Med
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-09
- **Authors**: Miller JL et al.
- **Aims**: To evaluate setmelanotide (MC4R agonist) versus placebo for weight reduction in acquired hypothalamic obesity over 52 weeks.
- **Method**: Phase 3 multicenter RCT (2:1 allocation, n=120, age 4–66) with BMI change as primary endpoint.
- **Key findings**: Setmelanotide reduced BMI by −16.5% vs +3.3% placebo (P<0.001); hunger scores also improved significantly (P=0.009).
- **Strengths**: First phase 3 RCT for acquired hypothalamic obesity; large effect size across paediatric and adult age groups.
- **Limitations**: Open-label phase after 52 weeks not reported; adverse events (skin hyperpigmentation, nausea) in 100% of treatment group.
- **Conclusion**: Setmelanotide provides clinically meaningful and statistically significant BMI reduction in this rare, cancer-treatment-related obesity syndrome.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1056/NEJMoa2512275 / PMID:42418774

## 2. Comparative Effects of Drugs for Adults with Overweight or Obesity: Network Meta-Analysis — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: BMJ
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-08
- **Authors**: Nong K et al.
- **Aims**: To synthesise comparative benefits and harms of 19 anti-obesity drugs across 24 outcomes to guide clinical decision-making.
- **Method**: Systematic review and network meta-analysis (262 RCTs, 99,791 participants; frequentist random-effects + Bayesian dose-response models; GRADE).
- **Key findings**: Tirzepatide produced greatest weight loss (−14.9% vs lifestyle at 1 year); only subcutaneous semaglutide reduced all-cause mortality (RR 0.81) and MI (RR 0.72).
- **Strengths**: PROSPERO-registered, 262 RCTs, GRADE certainty ratings for all outcomes; includes emerging agents not covered by prior NMAs.
- **Limitations**: Most cardiovascular benefit driven by high-risk cardiovascular trial populations; quality of life improvements below minimal important difference for all drugs.
- **Conclusion**: Tirzepatide and CagriSema produce largest weight reductions but evidence for cardiometabolic benefit remains largely limited to semaglutide.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1136/bmj-2026-372161 / PMID:42419792

## 3. Long-Term Physical Activity and Dementia Risk in US Adults: Prospective Cohort Study — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Public Health
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-09
- **Authors**: Li Y et al.
- **Aims**: To examine associations between long-term physical activity and dementia incidence, objective cognitive function, and subjective cognitive decline.
- **Method**: Two prospective US cohorts (NHS + HPFS; n>107,000 combined), physical activity assessed every 2–4 years; 4-year lag for reverse causation; multivariable Cox and RR models.
- **Key findings**: Highest vs lowest quartile total PA: HR 0.72 (95% CI 0.68–0.76) for dementia; associated with ~1.15 fewer years of age-related global cognitive differences.
- **Strengths**: Long follow-up, validated self-report in well-established cohorts, 4-year lag reduces reverse causation, both objective and subjective cognitive endpoints.
- **Limitations**: Self-reported physical activity; NHS female-only for objective cognitive testing; cannot rule out residual confounding.
- **Conclusion**: Higher long-term physical activity independently associated with lower dementia risk and better cognitive performance across multiple validated endpoints.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S2468-2667(26)00123-4 / PMID:42425117

## 4. Advances in Systemic Therapies for Advanced Prostate Cancer — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: BMJ
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-08
- **Authors**: Childs DS et al.
- **Aims**: To summarise contemporary management of advanced prostate cancer from high-risk biochemical recurrence through mCRPC.
- **Method**: Narrative review synthesising landmark trials and meta-analyses covering ARPI use, PARP inhibitors, and Lu-PSMA-617.
- **Key findings**: Earlier ARPI use improves outcomes in mHSPC; mCRPC management now stratified by prior treatment and genomic biomarkers; Lu-PSMA-617 role established in PSMA-positive mCRPC.
- **Strengths**: Comprehensive, multidisciplinary perspective including cardiac, haematologic, and bone toxicity management.
- **Limitations**: Narrative (not systematic) review; evidence hierarchy and GRADE not applied; rapidly evolving field may not capture latest data.
- **Conclusion**: Personalised prostate cancer management guided by advanced imaging and genomic biomarkers is now feasible across the disease continuum.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1136/bmj-2025-085211 / PMID:42419784

## 5. Decision Tree Model to Predict One-Year Survival in Ambulatory Patients with Advanced Cancer — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: PLoS One
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-14
- **Authors**: Hiratsuka Y et al.
- **Aims**: To develop a one-year survival prediction model using objective clinical parameters in patients with advanced cancer.
- **Method**: Secondary analysis of a Korean prospective cohort (n=200 advanced cancer patients); recursive partitioning analysis (RPA) using three variables (chemotherapy response, CRP-albumin ratio, LDH).
- **Key findings**: 4-node decision tree achieved AUC 0.749 (95% CI 0.696–0.800) with Brier score 0.161 and calibration slope 0.99 after 200 bootstrap resamples.
- **Strengths**: Three readily available predictors; high calibration accuracy; bootstrap-corrected AUC; clinically actionable for palliative care referral timing.
- **Limitations**: Single-centre, n=200, external validation not yet performed; restricted to clinician-selected patients predicted ≤1 year survival.
- **Conclusion**: Simple RPA-based model predicts one-year cancer survival accurately with three objective biomarkers, pending external validation.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1371/journal.pone.0353195 / PMID:42447165
