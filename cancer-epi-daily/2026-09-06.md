## 1. Pulmonary Nodules — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: N Engl J Med
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-03
- **Authors**: Callister MEJ et al.
- **Aims**: Provide evidence-based clinical guidance on risk stratification and management of solid and subsolid pulmonary nodules.
- **Method**: Systematic narrative review synthesising CT imaging protocols, risk-prediction models, biopsy strategies, and surgical thresholds.
- **Key findings**: Solid nodules stable ≥2 years are considered benign; subsolid nodules require longer stability. Intermediate-risk nodules warrant PET-CT or biopsy.
- **Strengths**: Comprehensive NEJM review covering all nodule types with graded management pathways applicable globally.
- **Limitations**: Review design; no new primary data; resource availability for PET-CT varies internationally.
- **Conclusion**: Pulmonary nodule management must balance timely cancer diagnosis against unnecessary invasive procedures in benign disease.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1056/NEJMcp2515063 / PMID:42685318

## 2. Serum Soluble Fas Levels and Incidence of Biliary Tract Cancer in a Nested Case-Control Study — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiol Biomarkers Prev
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-04
- **Authors**: Adachi Y et al.
- **Aims**: Assess whether pre-diagnostic serum soluble Fas (sFas) is prospectively associated with extrahepatic biliary tract cancer (eBTC) incidence.
- **Method**: Prospective nested case-control study within Japan Collaborative Cohort (39,242 participants); 73 cases, 215 matched controls; conditional logistic regression.
- **Key findings**: Highest sFas tertile had OR 3.27 (95% CI 1.37–7.80) for eBTC; elevated risk observed in both gallbladder and bile duct subsites (P-trend 0.04 and 0.02).
- **Strengths**: Pre-diagnostic blood samples in a large prospective cohort minimise reverse causation; robust nested case-control design.
- **Limitations**: Only 73 cases; restricted to Japanese population limiting generalisability; single-country cohort.
- **Conclusion**: Elevated serum sFas may predict future biliary tract cancer risk; replication in independent large cohorts is warranted.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-0782 / PMID:42696027

## 3. Tumor Evolution Shapes Metastatic Competence in Colorectal Cancer — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Cell
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-01
- **Authors**: Verhagen MP et al.
- **Aims**: Characterise how distinct genomic evolutionary trajectories determine organ-specific metastatic competence in colorectal cancer.
- **Method**: Commentary on Manca et al. integrating genomic alterations, primary tumour location, and clinical variables across 7,000+ colorectal cancers.
- **Key findings**: Distinct patterns of tumour evolution shape metastatic organ tropism; primary location and genomic profile jointly determine metastatic risk.
- **Strengths**: Largest genomic–metastasis integration analysis for CRC; potential to refine surveillance protocols clinically.
- **Limitations**: Commentary piece; statistical methods and full study design of underlying work not detailed here.
- **Conclusion**: Tumour evolutionary framework may transform surveillance strategies for colorectal cancer by predicting metastatic behaviour.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.ccell.2026.07.013 / PMID:42679810

## 4. TIME in Motion: How Response Takes Shape — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Cell
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-01
- **Authors**: Chin WL et al.
- **Aims**: Highlight that dynamic tumour immune microenvironment (TIME) remodelling, rather than static baseline state, predicts immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) response.
- **Method**: Commentary on Lin et al.'s longitudinal pan-cancer single-cell atlas tracking immune-state trajectories during ICI therapy.
- **Key findings**: Directional immune-state changes during therapy are more informative than baseline composition; pan-cancer scope validates across tumour types.
- **Strengths**: Paradigm-shifting reframing of ICI biomarker strategy from static to temporal; pan-cancer single-cell atlas scope.
- **Limitations**: Commentary; primary sample sizes, statistical approaches and validation sets not directly reported.
- **Conclusion**: Temporal immune profiling should replace static baseline assessment as the standard biomarker framework for ICI response prediction.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.ccell.2026.06.016 / PMID:42679809
