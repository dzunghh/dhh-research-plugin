## 1. Durvalumab With Radiation Therapy in cCRT-Ineligible Locally Advanced NSCLC (DART) — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Journal of Clinical Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-16
- **Authors**: Rimner A et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate efficacy and safety of durvalumab plus definitive RT (without chemotherapy) in LA-NSCLC patients ineligible for concurrent chemoradiotherapy.
- **Method**: Multicenter single-arm prospective phase II; RT + durvalumab 1500 mg q4w for up to 12 months; primary endpoint 2-year PFS.
- **Key findings**: 2-year PFS 39% (met pre-specified threshold of >36%); 2-year OS 54%; grade 3/4 AEs in 21%, grade 5 in 7%.
- **Strengths**: Addresses an unmet clinical need in elderly/frail patients; prospective multicenter design with predefined endpoints.
- **Limitations**: Single-arm (no randomized comparator); small sample (n=58); 31% early durvalumab discontinuation limits generalizability.
- **Conclusion**: Durvalumab + RT is a promising option for cCRT-ineligible LA-NSCLC, supporting further randomized investigation.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1200/JCO-25-02517 / PMID:42462186

## 2. Breast Cancer Follow-Up and Surveillance After Primary Treatment: ASCO Guideline Update — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Journal of Clinical Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-16
- **Authors**: Nahleh Z et al.
- **Aims**: Provide updated recommendations for breast cancer follow-up and surveillance after primary treatment, incorporating a risk-based approach.
- **Method**: Systematic review of RCTs + modified Delphi consensus process; ASCO Expert Panel convened for formal recommendation development.
- **Key findings**: Risk-based surveillance frequency recommended; only 1 RCT identified for mammography guidance; blood biomarkers and supplemental imaging guidance provided.
- **Strengths**: Comprehensive systematic review; international expert panel; formal modified Delphi consensus for low-evidence domains.
- **Limitations**: Sparse RCT evidence (most recommendations consensus-based); limited data on risk-stratified follow-up intervals.
- **Conclusion**: Risk-stratified follow-up with mammography and physical examination remains standard; closer surveillance warranted for locally advanced or residual-disease patients.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1200/JCO-26-01700 / PMID:42462191

## 3. Immune Implications and Therapeutic Opportunities of Tumor Glycosylation — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Nature Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-15
- **Authors**: Hatinguais R et al.
- **Aims**: Review how aberrant tumor glycosylation suppresses anti-tumor immunity and identify glycan-targeting therapeutic strategies.
- **Method**: Narrative review synthesizing tumor glycan biology, immune evasion mechanisms, and emerging clinical and preclinical therapeutic data.
- **Key findings**: Tumor glycans engage immune inhibitory receptors and modify checkpoint function; glycan-targeting agents show promise as precision cancer medicines adjuvanting immunotherapy.
- **Strengths**: Comprehensive mechanistic overview of an emerging field; bridges basic biology and therapeutic applications.
- **Limitations**: Review design; clinical evidence for glycan-targeting agents remains early-stage and largely preclinical.
- **Conclusion**: Glycosylation is a near-universal cancer hallmark with growing therapeutic relevance for enhancing immunotherapy efficacy.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s43018-026-01200-8 / PMID:42457945
