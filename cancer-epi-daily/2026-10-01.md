## 1. A transient MXD4-dependent proliferative pause enables survival of brain micrometastases — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Cell
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-28
- **Authors**: García-Gómez P et al.
- **Aims**: To identify how metastasis-initiating cells survive early organ adaptation stress during brain colonization.
- **Method**: In vivo genetic disruption of MXD4 in cancer models; analysis of human brain micrometastases; patient-derived organotypic culture experiments.
- **Key findings**: MXD4 drives a transient proliferative pause before vascular co-option; genetic MXD4 disruption prevents micrometastasis progression to macrometastases in multiple models.
- **Strengths**: Multi-system validation including spontaneous models, patient cohorts, and clinically available drugs in organotypics.
- **Limitations**: Mechanisms upstream of MXD4 activation in different cancer types not fully characterised.
- **Conclusion**: The MXD4-dependent pause is a targetable vulnerability to prevent brain metastasis progression and post-surgical relapse.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.ccell.2026.09.002 / PMID:42805170
