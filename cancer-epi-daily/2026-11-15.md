## 1. Blood-based RNA biomarker for the early detection of ovarian cancer — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Nature Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-10
- **Authors**: Wilhelm J et al.
- **Aims**: To evaluate Tu-stroma, a tumor-associated lncRNA in serum extracellular vesicles, as a circulating biomarker for pre-invasive (STIC) and early-stage high-grade serous ovarian carcinoma.
- **Method**: Multi-center diagnostic cohort study (n=506) comparing serum EV Tu-stroma levels across STIC, HGSC stages I–IV, benign adnexal lesions, and healthy controls using ROC analysis.
- **Key findings**: Tu-stroma discriminated STIC from controls with AUC=0.99 vs CA125 AUC=0.59 (P<0.001); for HGSC vs controls AUC=0.99 vs 0.96; no elevation in 160 nonovarian malignancies.
- **Strengths**: Large multi-site cohort including premenopausal women; disease-context specificity validated across inflammatory comparators and nonovarian cancers.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective design; prospective real-world validation in consecutively enrolled cohorts still required.
- **Conclusion**: Tu-stroma is a highly specific blood-based RNA biomarker candidate for the earliest histopathological stages of ovarian cancer, dramatically outperforming CA125.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41591-026-04732-9 / PMID:43009642

## 2. Risovalisib for PIK3CA-mutated recurrent or persistent ovarian clear cell carcinoma — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-10
- **Authors**: Li J et al.
- **Aims**: To evaluate anti-tumour activity and safety of the PI3Kα inhibitor risovalisib in PIK3CA-mutated recurrent/persistent ovarian clear cell carcinoma (OCC).
- **Method**: Single-arm, multicentre, phase 2 trial across 39 centres in China and Japan; 93 patients; primary endpoint objective response rate by blinded independent review.
- **Key findings**: Objective response rate 35% (95% CI 25–46) in efficacy analysis set (n=84); grade ≥3 adverse events included rash (32%), hyperglycaemia (30%); no treatment-related deaths.
- **Strengths**: First targeted therapy trial in a molecularly-defined rare subtype; multi-national, blinded independent central review.
- **Limitations**: Single-arm design without comparator; 68% of patients had ≥2 prior lines limiting generalisability.
- **Conclusion**: Risovalisib demonstrates clinically meaningful activity in PIK3CA-mutated OCC, supporting it as a potential novel treatment for this lethal rare cancer.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00337-2 / PMID:43008753

## 3. Treatment of Recurrent and/or Metastatic Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma: CSCO and ASCO Joint Guideline — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Journal of Clinical Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-10
- **Authors**: Jiang W et al.
- **Aims**: To provide evidence-based recommendations for first- and subsequent-line systemic therapy, maintenance therapy, locoregional consolidation, and salvage local therapy for recurrent/metastatic NPC.
- **Method**: CSCO–ASCO joint multidisciplinary expert panel; systematic literature review of RCTs, meta-analyses, and prospective/retrospective studies with consensus-based interpretation.
- **Key findings**: Platinum-based chemotherapy plus PD-1 inhibitor is the preferred first-line regimen; maintenance PD-1 inhibition up to 2 years recommended after disease control.
- **Strengths**: First joint CSCO–ASCO guideline providing international consensus; covers all relevant lines of therapy and local approaches.
- **Limitations**: Consensus-based interpretation introduces expert-panel heterogeneity; evidence largely from Asian-endemic populations.
- **Conclusion**: This joint guideline standardises evidence-based treatment for recurrent/metastatic NPC globally, endorsing PD-1 inhibitor combinations as the new standard of care.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1200/JCO-26-02650 / PMID:43008245

## 4. High tissue factor (CD142) expression identifies a poor-prognosis subgroup of esophageal and esophagogastric junction adenocarcinoma — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: BMC Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-10
- **Authors**: Bedau T et al.
- **Aims**: To characterise tissue factor (TF/CD142) expression, its prognostic value, and molecular context in esophageal (EAC) and esophagogastric junction (EGJ) adenocarcinoma.
- **Method**: Tissue microarray of 1,006 resected EAC/EGJ adenocarcinomas (1996–2020) with TF immunohistochemistry H-scoring; multivariable Cox regression; median follow-up 67 months.
- **Key findings**: TF-high (H-score >100) in 22.4% of tumours; independently associated with shorter OS (HR 1.67, 95% CI 1.32–2.11; P<0.001); inversely associated with CLDN18.2, positively with MET.
- **Strengths**: Large population-based cohort; long follow-up; adjustment for multiple clinicopathological factors; paired primary/metastasis comparison.
- **Limitations**: Single-centre TMA; H-score >100 threshold requires prospective validation; causal relationship unstated.
- **Conclusion**: TF-high is an independent adverse prognostic biomarker in EAC/EGJ, defining a distinct molecularly targetable subgroup for prospective ADC trials.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1186/s12885-026-17336-w / PMID:43009849

## 5. Geometry-based gene-expression signatures detect cancer treatment responders in clinical trials — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: BMC Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-10
- **Authors**: Chachólski W et al.
- **Aims**: To evaluate whether geometrical/topological data analysis (TDA) of tumour gene-expression signatures can classify and predict treatment responders in a randomised cancer trial.
- **Method**: Retrospective analysis of JAVELIN Renal 101 phase III RCT gene-expression data (n=726); leave-one-out out-of-sample testing; comparison against three published gene-expression signatures.
- **Key findings**: Out-of-sample prediction accuracy 82.9% (AUC=0.880, 95% CI 0.855–0.905); predicted responders gained +2.54 months RMST vs non-responders +0.44 months (adjusted interaction P=0.004).
- **Strengths**: Novel TDA methodology outperformed all three published signatures; rigorous out-of-sample testing with 20,000 permutations; full open-source trial dataset.
- **Limitations**: Validated on a single trial (renal cell carcinoma); co-expressed module structure not externally replicated.
- **Conclusion**: Topological gene-expression signatures enable accurate identification of treatment responders, demonstrating superior detection of treatment effect heterogeneity over conventional signatures.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1186/s12885-026-17312-4 / PMID:43009859
