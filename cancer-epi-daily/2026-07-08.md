## 1. Differential impact of proton pump inhibitors and antibiotics on immunotherapy efficacy after chemoradiotherapy in locally advanced NSCLC: a post-hoc analysis of the PACIFIC trial — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-03
- **Authors**: Brunetti L et al.
- **Aims**: To assess whether baseline PPI or antibiotic exposure attenuates durvalumab benefit in unresectable stage III NSCLC after chemoradiotherapy.
- **Method**: Post-hoc analysis of the PACIFIC phase 3 RCT (N=660) with 62.4-month median follow-up; competing-risks regression for PFS and OS by PPI/antibiotic exposure subgroups.
- **Key findings**: PPI exposure associated with shorter PFS (9.4 vs 17.2 months; HR 1.57, p<0.0001) and OS (33.0 vs 57.9 months; HR 1.66, p<0.0001) with durvalumab; no effect in placebo arm (interaction p<0.0001).
- **Strengths**: Large international RCT dataset with 5-year follow-up enabling reliable subgroup inference; significant treatment interaction confirms drug-specific effect.
- **Limitations**: Post-hoc, not pre-specified; PPI/antibiotic use may confound by indication (sicker patients); microbiome data unavailable to confirm mechanism.
- **Conclusion**: Baseline PPI exposure significantly attenuates durvalumab efficacy, suggesting gut microbiome modulation may be actionable in stage III NSCLC immunotherapy.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00191-9 / PMID:42398520

## 2. Generalizable AI predicts immunotherapy outcomes across cancers and treatments — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Nature Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-03
- **Authors**: Shen W et al.
- **Aims**: To develop a pan-cancer AI model predicting immune checkpoint inhibitor response from tumor transcriptomes that generalises across cancer types and treatments.
- **Method**: COMPASS, a concept bottleneck transformer trained on 10,184 tumors (33 cancer types), validated on 16 clinical cohorts spanning 7 cancers and 6 ICIs; survival analyses and mechanistic concept attribution.
- **Key findings**: COMPASS improved accuracy by 8.5% and AUPRC by 15.7% on average vs 22 competing methods; responders had HR=4.7 for overall survival (P<0.0001); model generalised to cancer types/treatments absent from fine-tuning.
- **Strengths**: Largest pan-cancer ICI predictor to date; biologically grounded concept bottleneck enables mechanistic interpretation; broad external validation.
- **Limitations**: Trained on bulk transcriptomes (not single-cell); prospective validation in interventional trials not yet done.
- **Conclusion**: COMPASS provides generalizable, interpretable ICI response prediction, identifying actionable resistance programs (TGFβ signaling, CD4T dysfunction) for trial design.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41591-026-04502-7 / PMID:42399673

## 3. Clinical Validation of a Multiplex Urine Biomarker Assay for Surveillance of Recurrent Bladder Cancer — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Clinical Cancer Research
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-02
- **Authors**: Lotan Y et al.
- **Aims**: To evaluate Oncuria-Monitor for detecting recurrent non-muscle invasive bladder cancer during surveillance as a non-invasive alternative to cystoscopy.
- **Method**: Prospective, blinded multi-center study (6 US/Japan sites, n=300, 1,248 serial urine samples) compared to cystoscopy + histopathology; training and validation cohorts with 2-year follow-up.
- **Key findings**: Oncuria-Monitor achieved 85.6% sensitivity and 93.0% NPV in the validation cohort vs 20.0% sensitivity for BladderChek and 36.9% for urine cytology; NNE=3 (same as cystoscopy).
- **Strengths**: Prospective design, international sites, blinded analysis, head-to-head comparison with standard tools; clinically actionable NPV for rule-out strategy.
- **Limitations**: 25% cystoscopy reduction estimated but not directly tested; real-world implementation costs not assessed.
- **Conclusion**: Oncuria-Monitor supports a risk-adapted bladder cancer surveillance strategy that could safely reduce cystoscopy in ~25% of visits.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-26-1407 / PMID:42391037

## 4. Cardiac Risk After Heart-Sparing Breast Radiotherapy — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-02
- **Authors**: Quirk S et al.
- **Aims**: To compare whole-heart versus left anterior descending artery (LAD) radiation dose metrics for predicting cardiac risk after breast radiotherapy.
- **Method**: Cross-sectional study (n=4,908; 2,223 left-sided) treated 2008–2018 at a Canadian tertiary centre; competing-risks regression adjusted for cardiovascular risk factors; automated dosimetry via CT planning.
- **Key findings**: Maximum LAD dose ≥12 Gy EQD2 independently associated with higher cardiac risk (SHR=1.81; 95% CI 1.04–3.16; p=0.04); mean heart dose ≥2 Gy was not (p=0.99); C-index 0.58 vs 0.53.
- **Strengths**: Large cohort with median 10.8-year follow-up; automated segmentation enables reproducible dosimetry; data-driven cut-point analysis.
- **Limitations**: Single-centre retrospective design; limited ability to adjust for all cardiac risk factors over follow-up.
- **Conclusion**: LAD-based dosimetric planning should replace whole-heart metrics for cardiac risk assessment in breast radiotherapy.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamaoncol.2026.2066 / PMID:42390858

## 5. Worsening frailty and declining quality of life in older survivors of non-metastatic breast cancer — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-03
- **Authors**: Minami CA et al.
- **Aims**: To quantify the association between clinically significant frailty worsening one year post-diagnosis and long-term quality of life decline in older breast cancer survivors.
- **Method**: Prospective cohort study from the Women's Health Initiative (n=1,061, age ≥65); validated claims-based frailty index at 1 year post-diagnosis; QoL assessed 4–6 years post-diagnosis; multivariable logistic regression.
- **Key findings**: 19.5% experienced clinically significant frailty worsening; worsening frailty independently associated with long-term QoL decline (OR 1.48; 95% CI 1.07–2.04).
- **Strengths**: Large WHI cohort with multi-site representation; validated claims-based frailty tool; long QoL follow-up window.
- **Limitations**: QoL based on single 10-point scale; claims-based frailty may miss nuanced phenotype; residual confounding by treatment intensity possible.
- **Conclusion**: Frailty worsening during breast cancer treatment predicts long-term QoL decline, supporting integration of frailty assessment into survivorship care for older women.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag218 / PMID:42402217

## Appraisal notes

This week's digest is dominated by two methodological currents: the translational validation of AI-driven biomarkers (COMPASS and Oncuria-Monitor) moving from discovery toward clinical deployment, and the growing recognition that treatment-context factors — gut microbiome modifiers (PPIs, antibiotics) and host frailty — substantially modulate cancer outcomes independent of tumour biology. The PACIFIC post-hoc analysis is notable as one of the first RCT-level datasets to confirm a treatment-specific (not just prognostic) attenuation effect of PPIs on immunotherapy, lending epidemiological weight to what had been largely observational signal.
