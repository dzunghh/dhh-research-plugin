## 1. Germline CDK12 Variants as Rare Drivers of Lethal Metastatic Prostate Cancer — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Discovery
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-02
- **Authors**: Tolmeijer SH et al.
- **Aims**: To determine whether germline CDK12 truncating variants drive aggressive metastatic prostate cancer, challenging the prior assumption that CDK12 mutations are exclusively somatic.
- **Method**: Retrospective genomic cohort study of 4,535 metastatic prostate cancer patients; germline CDK12 enrichment tested against gnomAD V4.1.0 (n=807,162) and V2.1.1 controls using odds ratios with 95% CIs.
- **Key findings**: Germline CDK12 truncating variants identified in 5/4,535 patients (0.1%); enriched vs gnomAD V4.1.0 controls (OR 11.4, 95% CI 3.6–27.8) and V2.1.1 non-cancer controls (OR 29.6, 95% CI 6.8–28.6).
- **Strengths**: Large multi-center cohort, two independent population-based control datasets, family pedigree validation confirming germline inheritance.
- **Limitations**: Very small case count (n=5) due to variant rarity; retrospective design limits causal inference; family history data incomplete for most cases.
- **Conclusion**: Germline CDK12 truncating variants are a rare but verifiable hereditary driver of lethal metastatic prostate cancer, warranting inclusion in germline testing panels.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/2159-8290.CD-26-0084 / PMID:42392870

## 2. metilene: Multi-Condition Differentially Methylated Region Detection with Auto-Classification — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Nature Communications
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-04
- **Authors**: Zhu Z et al.
- **Aims**: To develop a fast, scalable DMR detection algorithm that operates across multiple sample conditions simultaneously, including unsupervised sample clustering.
- **Method**: Genome segmentation method using pairwise methylation difference signals across user-labeled or unlabeled groups; benchmarked on simulated data and diverse human datasets including pancreatic tissue.
- **Key findings**: metilene accurately detects DMRs in supervised and unsupervised modes; in pancreatic cancer tissue, identified DMRs enriched for NFKB-NFAT regulatory programs suggesting an altered epigenetic landscape.
- **Strengths**: Novel unsupervised mode removes need for predefined group labels; open-source; validated on multiple real and simulated human datasets.
- **Limitations**: Unsupervised clustering performance in highly heterogeneous clinical cohorts not fully characterized; pancreatic cancer application is exploratory.
- **Conclusion**: metilene provides an interpretable, multi-condition DMR framework enabling new epigenetic stratifications and regulatory discovery in complex cancer datasets.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41467-026-74931-y / PMID:42401582

## 3. Longitudinal Comorbidity Effects on Brain Structure and Cognition in Older Breast Cancer Survivors — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-03
- **Authors**: Casagrande CC et al.
- **Aims**: To evaluate longitudinal gray matter volume changes and cognitive outcomes in breast cancer survivors aged ≥60 years, with focus on comorbidity burden as a modifying factor.
- **Method**: Prospective longitudinal cohort; n=64 survivors vs n=62 non-cancer controls; structural MRI + neuropsychological testing at baseline, 12- and 24-month follow-up; linear mixed models with FDR correction.
- **Key findings**: Survivors showed frontal, thalamic, and limbic gray matter decline vs controls over 24 months; those with ≥3 comorbidities had the lowest frontal volume at all timepoints; survivors failed to show practice effects in attention/processing speed (group-by-time p=0.030).
- **Strengths**: Longitudinal design with active controls, multi-domain cognitive assessment, brain age estimation, direct comorbidity-volume-cognition pathway analysis.
- **Limitations**: Small sample (n=64 survivors), likely underpowered for subgroup analyses; single-institution; chemotherapy regimen heterogeneity not fully modeled.
- **Conclusion**: Older breast cancer survivors undergo accelerated gray matter decline amplified by comorbidity burden, underscoring the need for targeted cognitive monitoring in this high-risk group.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag219 / PMID:42402209
