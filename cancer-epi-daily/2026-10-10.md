## 1. Interventions to Improve Follow-Up Colonoscopy After Abnormal FIT/FOBT: Meta-Analysis — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Internal Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-05
- **Authors**: Chua WY et al.
- **Aims**: To evaluate patient- and system-level interventions that improve colonoscopy completion after an abnormal FIT/FOBT result.
- **Method**: Systematic review and meta-analysis (PRISMA); 21 studies, 19,613 patients; random-effects pooling.
- **Key findings**: Patient navigation (RR 1.24), patient reminders (RR 1.32), clinician reminders (RR 1.36), and automated referrals (RR 1.68) all significantly increased colonoscopy completion. Pooled completion rose from 47.8% (control) to 69.1% (intervention).
- **Strengths**: Large, diverse sample; comprehensive multi-database search; sensitivity analyses reduce heterogeneity for key interventions.
- **Limitations**: Moderate-to-high heterogeneity across intervention types; automated referral evidence base limited to few studies.
- **Conclusion**: Multiple scalable interventions can substantially improve colorectal cancer screening follow-up, with automated referrals showing the greatest effect.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamainternmed.2026.4827 / PMID:42832233

## 2. Clinical and Lifetime Natural History of Oral Cancer: Implications for Screening — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-07
- **Authors**: Cheung LC et al.
- **Aims**: To estimate oral cancer clinical and lifetime natural history parameters to empirically inform screening intervals and surveillance.
- **Method**: Cohort analysis of the Kerala Oral Cancer Screening Trial (n=41,225); Kaplan-Meier for clinical history and semi-Markov multistate models for lifetime natural history.
- **Key findings**: Visual inspection sensitivity 89.2% over two screens; dual screen-positivity conferred 32-fold risk stratification (PPV 4.13% vs. cNPV 0.13%). Mean sojourn from precancer to early-stage cancer was 19.3 years; early- to late-stage was 2.7 years.
- **Strengths**: Very large population-based trial with 11-year follow-up; rigorous multistate modeling; policy-actionable intervals derived.
- **Limitations**: Single setting (Kerala, India); restricted to ever-chewers/smokers/drinkers; generalisability uncertain.
- **Conclusion**: 10-year screening intervals are safe for double screen-negatives; intensive surveillance warranted for screen-positives with precancer.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag360 / PMID:42842394

## 3. NRG-BR002: Metastasis-Directed Ablation vs Systemic Therapy Alone for Oligometastatic Breast Cancer (RCT) — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Journal of Clinical Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-07
- **Authors**: Chmura SJ et al.
- **Aims**: To determine whether metastasis-directed ablation (SBRT/surgery) improves progression-free survival versus first-line systemic therapy alone in oligometastatic breast cancer.
- **Method**: Randomized phase II/III multicenter trial (n=125 eligible); 1:1 allocation, primary endpoint PFS.
- **Key findings**: Median PFS 23.0 months (no ablation) vs 19.5 months (ablation); HR 0.92 (95% CI 0.57–1.47); trial did not proceed to phase III. OS also non-significant (HR 1.07). No grade 5 toxicities.
- **Strengths**: Multicenter US/Canadian RCT; prospective CTC biomarker exploratory analysis; clinically important negative result.
- **Limitations**: Small sample (n=125); underpowered for OS; heterogeneous metastatic phenotypes may dilute effects.
- **Conclusion**: Adding metastasis-directed ablation to first-line systemic therapy does not improve PFS or OS; routine SBRT outside clinical trials is not supported.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1200/JCO-26-00201 / PMID:42842849

## 4. Advances in Systemic Treatment of Breast Cancer — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: New England Journal of Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-08
- **Authors**: Hurvitz SA et al.
- **Aims**: To review major advances in systemic breast cancer therapy since 2020, including targeted therapies, ADCs, and immunotherapy across subtypes.
- **Method**: Narrative review synthesising clinical trial evidence on CDK4/6 inhibitors, PI3K inhibitors, oral SERDs, ADCs, and immune checkpoint inhibitors.
- **Key findings**: Trastuzumab deruxtecan is reshaping HER2+ treatment paradigms; oral SERDs and ADCs transform management after endocrine resistance; immunotherapy improves outcomes in TNBC early and late disease.
- **Strengths**: Comprehensive, authoritative synthesis in highest-impact journal; integrates biomarker-guided treatment strategies and circulating tumour DNA use.
- **Limitations**: Narrative design; selection bias in evidence included; does not quantitatively synthesise trial outcomes.
- **Conclusion**: Breast cancer systemic therapy has diversified substantially, with ongoing challenges in treatment sequencing, predictive biomarker identification, and equitable access.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1056/NEJMra2500695 / PMID:42842904

## 5. Neoadjuvant Niraparib for HRD-Positive High-Grade Serous Ovarian Cancer (NANT Phase 2) — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: The Lancet Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-07
- **Authors**: Liu D et al.
- **Aims**: To evaluate chemotherapy-free neoadjuvant niraparib monotherapy in HRD-positive advanced ovarian cancer unsuitable for primary debulking surgery.
- **Method**: Multicentre single-arm phase 2 trial (n=67); 2 cycles niraparib before interval debulking surgery; co-primary endpoints objective response rate and R0 resection rate.
- **Key findings**: Objective response rate 63% (95% CI 47–76%); R0 resection rate 80% (95% CI 64–91%). Grade 3-4 thrombocytopenia in 48% of patients.
- **Strengths**: First dedicated study of PARP inhibitor neoadjuvant monotherapy in HRD+ ovarian cancer; clinically meaningful R0 rate with 37-month median follow-up.
- **Limitations**: Single-arm, no randomised comparator; small sample (n=67); all Han Chinese, limiting generalisability.
- **Conclusion**: Neoadjuvant niraparib shows anti-tumour activity enabling R0 resection in HRD+ ovarian cancer but requires randomised validation against platinum-taxane chemotherapy.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00387-6 / PMID:42843394

## Appraisal notes

This week's digest is dominated by actionable translational findings bridging epidemiology and clinical practice: a large meta-analysis quantifying scalable colonoscopy follow-up interventions, and a methodologically rigorous natural history model providing the first empirical basis for extended oral cancer screening intervals. Notably, a well-powered negative RCT (NRG-BR002) challenges the growing use of metastasis-directed SBRT in oligometastatic breast cancer outside clinical trials, reinforcing the importance of randomised evidence before widespread adoption of novel treatment strategies.
