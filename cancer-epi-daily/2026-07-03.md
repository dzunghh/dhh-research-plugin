## 1. Trastuzumab Rezetecan versus Pyrotinib plus Capecitabine for HER2-Positive Metastatic Breast Cancer (HORIZON-Breast01) — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-01
- **Authors**: Yao H et al.
- **Aims**: To compare trastuzumab rezetecan vs pyrotinib+capecitabine in HER2+ metastatic breast cancer post-trastuzumab/taxane.
- **Method**: Phase 3 RCT interim analysis; 287 Chinese patients; 1:1 randomisation; primary endpoint PFS by blinded central review.
- **Key findings**: Median PFS 30.6 vs 8.3 months (HR 0.22, 95% CI 0.15–0.34; p<0.0001); 12-month PFS rate 84.7% vs 35.5%.
- **Strengths**: Blinded independent central review; active standard-of-care comparator; pre-specified interim analysis with dramatic effect size.
- **Limitations**: Single-country (China) population; open-label design; interim data only; no OS results reported.
- **Conclusion**: Trastuzumab rezetecan significantly improved PFS over pyrotinib+capecitabine, representing a potential new treatment option.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00193-2 / PMID:42385760

## 2. Dose Escalation with Intraoperative Radiotherapy in Newly Diagnosed Glioblastoma (INTRAGO-II) — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-01
- **Authors**: Giordano FA et al.
- **Aims**: To evaluate whether adding intraoperative radiotherapy (30 Gy) to standard chemoradiotherapy improves outcomes in glioblastoma.
- **Method**: Open-label, multicentre, phase 3 RCT; 298 patients; 18 centres in 7 countries; masked centralised review.
- **Key findings**: Median PFS 11.0 vs 11.4 months (HR 1.1, 95% CI 0.85–1.44; p=0.47); no improvement with intraoperative RT.
- **Strengths**: International multicentre design; pre-specified primary endpoint; masked centralised outcome review; 7-country enrolment.
- **Limitations**: Open-label assignment inherent to intraoperative intervention; race/ethnicity data not collected.
- **Conclusion**: Intraoperative radiotherapy dose escalation did not improve progression-free survival in resectable newly diagnosed glioblastoma.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00235-4 / PMID:42372746

## 3. Perioperative Systemic Therapy versus Surgery Alone for Resectable Colorectal Peritoneal-Only Metastases (CAIRO6) — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-01
- **Authors**: Rovers KP et al.
- **Aims**: To compare perioperative systemic therapy plus CRS-HIPEC vs CRS-HIPEC alone in resectable colorectal peritoneal-only metastases.
- **Method**: Open-label, phase 3 RCT; 358 patients; 10 Dutch/Belgian tertiary centres; mITT analysis; 41-month median follow-up.
- **Key findings**: Median OS 44 vs 39 months (HR 0.85, 95% CI 0.62–1.15; p=0.28); perioperative chemo not superior to surgery alone.
- **Strengths**: Phase 3, pre-specified primary OS endpoint; patient organisation involved in design; multicentre, multi-country.
- **Limitations**: Power recalculation for OS was post-hoc; heterogeneous HIPEC regimens across centres; open-label design.
- **Conclusion**: Perioperative systemic therapy cannot be recommended for all patients with resectable colorectal peritoneal-only metastases.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00085-9 / PMID:42372745

## 4. Breast Cancer Survival in Asian American Patients — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Netw Open
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-01
- **Authors**: Gomez SL et al.
- **Aims**: To examine clinical, lifestyle, and sociodemographic contributors to breast cancer mortality differences across Asian ethnic groups vs White women.
- **Method**: Pooled cohort from 4 epidemiologic studies; Cox proportional hazards models; n=8994 women (3973 Asian, 5021 White); mean 12.6-year follow-up.
- **Key findings**: Chinese (HR 0.77), Filipina (HR 0.81), and Japanese (HR 0.71) females had lower all-cause mortality vs White women after full multivariable adjustment; differences persisted by nativity.
- **Strengths**: Disaggregated by specific Asian ethnicity and nativity; long follow-up; multi-registry population-based design.
- **Limitations**: California/Hawaii populations limit US generalisability; no granular treatment protocol data; residual confounding possible.
- **Conclusion**: Several Asian ethnic groups had lower adjusted breast cancer mortality than White females, with nativity modifying some associations.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.21041 / PMID:42384382

## 5. A Simple and Powerful Test of Vaccine Waning — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Am J Epidemiol
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-01
- **Authors**: Perenyi G et al.
- **Aims**: To develop a formal causal test assessing whether vaccine (or treatment) efficacy is constant vs waning over time at the individual level.
- **Method**: Causal estimand–based hypothesis test under interpretable assumptions; validated via simulation and BNT162b2 COVID-19 RCT re-analysis; two variants use summary data only.
- **Key findings**: Test rejected null of no waning in BNT162b2 trial (prior analyses had not established waning); power gain over classical approaches demonstrated.
- **Strengths**: Formal causal framework; applicable to published summary data without individual-level access; interpretable assumptions clearly stated.
- **Limitations**: Illustrated primarily in infectious-disease vaccine context; extension to cancer immunotherapy settings requires further validation.
- **Conclusion**: This causal test detects waning treatment effects with greater power than existing methods, with broad applicability to cancer and vaccine trials.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/aje/kwag118 / PMID:42383737

## Appraisal notes

This week's digest is dominated by high-quality phase 3 RCTs from Lancet Oncology, with two reporting important null results (CAIRO6 and INTRAGO-II) that definitively close off escalation strategies in colorectal peritoneal metastases and glioblastoma respectively. A methodological highlight is the American Journal of Epidemiology causal test for waning treatment effects, which addresses a longstanding gap in how vaccine and immunotherapy trials quantify durability — directly applicable to cancer immunotherapy settings. The pooled cohort study of breast cancer survival in Asian Americans exemplifies rigorous cancer epidemiology, using disaggregated ethnicity and nativity data to reveal survival patterns unexplained by standard clinical and sociodemographic factors.
