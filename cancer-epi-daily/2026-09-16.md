## 1. COVID-19 Vaccination Around ICI Start and Survival in Cancer Patients — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Nature Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-10
- **Authors**: Salem JE et al.
- **Aims**: Assess whether COVID-19 mRNA vaccination timing relative to immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) initiation affects survival in cancer patients.
- **Method**: Nationwide pharmacoepidemiology using French SNDS (n=95,015 ICI starters); IPTW for pre-ICI vaccination; target trial emulation for post-ICI vaccination arm.
- **Key findings**: Pre-ICI mRNA vaccination associated with lower early all-cause mortality (HR 0.90, 95% CI 0.87–0.94 at 1–3 months); post-ICI vaccination showed similar early benefit (HR 0.84, 0.80–0.88 at 3 months). No significant association beyond 12 months for either.
- **Strengths**: Large nationwide cohort (95k), rigorous causal methods (IPTW + target trial emulation), non-mRNA vaccine comparison as active comparator.
- **Limitations**: Residual healthy-vaccinee bias possible; no randomisation; cause-specific death follow-up truncated at end-2023.
- **Conclusion**: Early survival benefit is transient and likely reflects healthy-vaccinee effects rather than a specific mRNA–ICI synergy.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41591-026-04640-y / PMID:42722885

## 2. Phenomic Profiles and Disease Patterns of 1.9 Million Participants from Our Future Health — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Nature Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-10
- **Authors**: Straub VJ et al.
- **Aims**: Characterise the phenotypic composition, cancer prevalence, and representativeness of the Our Future Health (OFH) UK prospective cohort.
- **Method**: Cross-sectional description of >1.9M adults using self-report, EHR, cancer registry, and death records; validation against UK Biobank (r=0.78 disease correlation) and national estimates.
- **Key findings**: Cancer prevalence followed expected age gradients; lung cancer rates below national estimates; underrepresentation of most deprived and minority ethnic groups confirmed. All 33 self-reported conditions replicated directionality in UK Biobank.
- **Strengths**: Largest UK prospective cohort to date (aiming for 5M); multi-source EHR + cancer registry linkage enabling robust cancer phenotyping.
- **Limitations**: Underrepresents most deprived and several minority ethnic groups; self-report bias for mental health conditions; recruitment still ongoing.
- **Conclusion**: OFH provides a scalable population platform for cancer epidemiology; known recruitment biases must be addressed to ensure generalisability.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41591-026-04602-4 / PMID:42722883

## 3. PRO Symptom Monitoring and Survival in Lung Cancer (SYMPRO-Lung Trial) — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-11
- **Authors**: Hassing MJ et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate whether weekly electronic patient-reported outcome (PRO) symptom monitoring improves long-term overall survival in real-world lung cancer care.
- **Method**: Post-hoc survival analysis of a stepped-wedge cluster-RCT across 14 Dutch centres (n=446, stages I–IV); Cox mixed-effects model adjusted for stage, histology, ECOG; median follow-up 50 months.
- **Key findings**: PRO monitoring associated with improved OS (HR 0.80, 95% CI 0.63–1.00, p=0.049); median OS 26 vs. 21 months (5-month difference). No significant progression-free survival benefit (HR 0.86, p=0.18).
- **Strengths**: Stepped-wedge RCT design in routine care; Netherlands Cancer Registry linkage for long-term survival; real-world stage I–IV inclusion.
- **Limitations**: Post-hoc analysis of quality-of-life–primary trial; marginal p-value; 13% excluded due to consent/linkage issues.
- **Conclusion**: Weekly electronic PRO monitoring in routine clinical practice is associated with a clinically meaningful 5-month OS benefit in lung cancer patients.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00305-0 / PMID:42727592

## 4. Driver-Agnostic MPZL1 CAR-T Cell Therapy Targeting Amplified Surface Proteins — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Nature Communications
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-10
- **Authors**: Jiménez-Vázquez S et al.
- **Aims**: Identify pan-cancer, driver-agnostic CAR-T targets among amplified surface-protein genes and validate MPZL1 as a lead candidate.
- **Method**: Systematic genomic screen of amplified surface-protein genes; anti-MPZL1 monoclonal antibody and CAR-T engineering; efficacy tested in vitro, xenograft, autochthonous mouse, and patient-derived tissue explant models.
- **Key findings**: MPZL1 amplified in up to 75% of patients with certain solid tumours; CAR-T cells selectively eliminated MPZL1-positive tumour cells with antitumour activity across all four preclinical model types.
- **Strengths**: Multi-model preclinical validation (4 model types); generalizable framework for exploiting amplified surface proteins across tumour types.
- **Limitations**: Entirely preclinical; therapeutic window, toxicity, and clinical activity in humans remain undemonstrated.
- **Conclusion**: MPZL1 is a viable pan-cancer CAR-T target, and the amplified surface-protein approach offers a broadly applicable strategy for immunotherapy target discovery.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41467-026-77465-5 / PMID:42722692

## 5. KEYNOTE-826: Pembrolizumab Plus Chemotherapy for Cervical Cancer — 5-Year Data — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-10
- **Authors**: Hasegawa K et al.
- **Aims**: Report 5-year efficacy and safety of pembrolizumab plus platinum-based chemotherapy in previously untreated recurrent/metastatic cervical cancer.
- **Method**: Exploratory ad hoc analysis of phase 3 double-blind RCT KEYNOTE-826 (n=617, 151 sites, 19 countries); median follow-up 59.1 months.
- **Key findings**: OS benefit sustained at 5 years (HR 0.62, 95% CI 0.50–0.76 in PD-L1 CPS≥1; HR 0.64, 0.53–0.78 in ITT). No new safety signals; no additional treatment-related deaths since final analysis.
- **Strengths**: Large multinational phase 3 RCT; longest follow-up reported for pembrolizumab in this indication; PD-L1 subgroup analyses.
- **Limitations**: Post-hoc exploratory analysis with no pre-specified formal hypothesis testing at 5 years.
- **Conclusion**: Five-year data confirm durable OS benefit, reinforcing pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy as the standard first-line treatment for recurrent/metastatic cervical cancer.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamaoncol.2026.3547 / PMID:42720936

## Appraisal notes

This week's digest is dominated by high-rigour pharmacoepidemiological and large-cohort methods: the COVID-19/ICI paper exemplifies target-trial emulation applied to a >95k real-world cancer population, while Our Future Health illustrates the growing role of multi-source EHR linkage in characterising cancer phenomes at national scale. The SYMPRO-Lung result adds evidence that patient-facing digital tools embedded in routine care can produce meaningful survival gains, marking a shift toward integrating outcomes research into trial design through stepped-wedge approaches.
