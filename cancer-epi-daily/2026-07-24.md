## 1. HCT-ASCT vs Non-Myeloablative Consolidation in Primary CNS Lymphoma (MATRix/IELSG43) — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Lancet
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-22
- **Authors**: Illerhaus G et al.
- **Aims**: To compare thiotepa-based HCT-ASCT vs non-myeloablative R-DeVIC consolidation after uniform MATRix induction in newly diagnosed primary CNS lymphoma.
- **Method**: Open-label randomized phase 3 trial; 56 hospitals in 5 European countries; n=229 randomized; primary endpoint progression-free survival; median follow-up 45.3 months.
- **Key findings**: 3-year PFS 78% (HCT-ASCT) vs 51% (R-DeVIC), HR 0.43 (95% CI 0.27–0.68), p=0.0003; HCT-ASCT also superior for overall survival.
- **Strengths**: Largest randomized PCNSL trial to date; multicenter, multinational, preregistered (NCT02531841).
- **Limitations**: Open-label design; restricted to fit patients aged 18–70; higher adverse events in HCT-ASCT arm (14.6 vs 9.3 per patient).
- **Conclusion**: HCT-ASCT establishes a new standard of care as preferred consolidation in fit PCNSL patients after MATRix induction.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S0140-6736(26)00917-7 / PMID:42486133

## 2. Hypofractionated vs Standard Locoregional Radiotherapy in High-Risk Breast Cancer (DBCG Skagen Trial 1) — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: J Clin Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-23
- **Authors**: Offersen BV et al.
- **Aims**: To test noninferiority of 40Gy/15fx vs 50Gy/25fx locoregional radiotherapy in node-positive high-risk breast cancer, with lymphedema as the primary endpoint.
- **Method**: Phase III noninferiority RCT; 17 centers; n=2,963 consented, n=2,908 ITT; noninferiority margin +5% excess lymphedema; median cancer-outcome follow-up 5.25 years.
- **Key findings**: 3-year lymphedema 8.0% vs 9.4%, OR 0.84 (95% CI 0.62–1.14); no differences in locoregional or distant recurrence, breast cancer mortality, or all-cause mortality.
- **Strengths**: Large multicenter design with comprehensive toxicity and oncological outcomes; accrual 2015–2021.
- **Limitations**: Cancer outcomes follow-up may miss late events; evolving RT techniques during accrual period.
- **Conclusion**: 40Gy/15fx is noninferior to 50Gy/25fx for locoregional RT in high-risk breast cancer, supporting hypofractionation as standard.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1200/JCO-25-02705 / PMID:42492022

## 3. Total Neoadjuvant Therapy With Long-Course Radiotherapy vs CRT in High-Risk Locally Advanced Rectal Cancer (TNTCRT) — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: J Clin Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-23
- **Authors**: Wang X et al.
- **Aims**: To compare doublet-LC TNT (long-course RT plus uninterrupted doublet chemo induction/concurrent/consolidation) vs conventional neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy in high-risk locally advanced rectal cancer.
- **Method**: Multicenter phase III RCT; n=458; high-risk features required (cT4, cN2, MRF involvement, or cT3c-d + EMVI); primary endpoint disease-free survival; median follow-up 51 months.
- **Key findings**: 3-year DFS 74.8% vs 66.0%, HR 0.674 (95% CI 0.489–0.929), p=0.016; pCR 26.4% vs 9.8% (p<0.001); MFS HR 0.655 (p=0.014).
- **Strengths**: Phase III design with event-driven DFS endpoint; comprehensive high-risk subgroup; preregistered (NCT03177382).
- **Limitations**: Grade ≥3 neoadjuvant adverse events higher in doublet arm (27.6% vs 8.6%); primarily Chinese population; open-label.
- **Conclusion**: Doublet-LC TNT improves DFS, MFS, and pCR with manageable toxicity, establishing a new standard TNT option for high-risk LARC.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1200/JCO-25-03110 / PMID:42492015

## 4. Pan-Cancer Single-Cell Multimodal Atlas of 60 Cancer Cell Lines — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Nat Commun
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-23
- **Authors**: Xu Z et al.
- **Aims**: To build a pan-cancer single-cell atlas linking gene-regulatory programs to cancer cell states, microenvironment composition, and therapeutic response.
- **Method**: snRNA-seq (240,957 profiles) + snATAC-seq (223,347 profiles) across 60 cell lines spanning 16 tissue origins and 20 cancer types; integrative chromatin-transcriptome analysis.
- **Key findings**: Conserved EMT axis spans tissue origins; copy-number-driven TF amplification drives state reprogramming; acral melanoma shows inflammation-suppressive program vs inflamed cutaneous melanoma (JAK-STAT as central discriminator).
- **Strengths**: Large-scale multimodal atlas; rare subtype inclusion (acral melanoma); links cell-line regulatory programs to patient cohort outcomes.
- **Limitations**: Cell lines have limited external validity compared to primary tumors; culture conditions alter in vivo gene expression.
- **Conclusion**: Pan-cancer regulatory atlas reveals EMT and JAK-STAT as cross-tissue drivers of cancer state plasticity, with potential therapeutic implications.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41467-026-75360-7 / PMID:42493507

## 5. Microbial Engraftment and Immune Regulation During FMT + Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor Therapy in Cancer — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: Nat Commun
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-23
- **Authors**: Ninkov M et al.
- **Aims**: To synthesize evidence from clinical trials of FMT combined with ICIs and build a mechanism-based framework explaining variable clinical outcomes.
- **Method**: Narrative review integrating clinical trial data on FMT + ICI; mechanism-based analysis of gut microbiota functional programs and immune regulation in oncology.
- **Key findings**: Specific microbial functional programs buffer or lower thresholds for immune dysregulation; donor selection, longitudinal monitoring, and trial design implications outlined.
- **Strengths**: Clinical trial synthesis with mechanistic framework; directly relevant to cancer immunotherapy practice and trial design.
