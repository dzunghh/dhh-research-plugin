## 1. Obesity Phenotypes, Omics Signatures, and Risk of Adverse Liver Outcomes — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-21
- **Authors**: Zhao L et al.
- **Aims**: To evaluate how obesity phenotypes and multi-omics signatures associate with liver cancer and other adverse liver outcomes in a large UK cohort.
- **Method**: Population-based cohort (UK Biobank, n=451,463, median follow-up 11 years); Cox models; internally validated NMR metabolomic and Olink proteomic signatures via elastic-net regression.
- **Key findings**: General, central, and metabolically unhealthy obesity associated with HRs 1.53–3.68 for liver outcomes; MUO omics signatures even stronger (HRs 1.58–6.13 across outcomes). FABP4, albumin, glutamine among top biomarkers.
- **Strengths**: Large prospective cohort; combined metabolomic and proteomic signatures internally validated.
- **Limitations**: UK Biobank limits generalisability; omics measured at single baseline time point.
- **Conclusion**: Metabolic dysfunction beyond adiposity is a key driver of liver cancer risk, with omics profiles clarifying molecular pathways.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag242 / PMID:42482169

## 2. Dalpiciclib Plus Endocrine Therapy in Advanced Breast Cancer With Visceral Crisis — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Nature Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-21
- **Authors**: Mo H et al.
- **Aims**: To evaluate dalpiciclib (CDK4/6 inhibitor) plus endocrine therapy in HR+/HER2− advanced breast cancer patients with visceral crisis.
- **Method**: Multicenter nonrandomized phase 2 trial (DARVIN, n=53); primary endpoint 6-month survival rate; exploratory liquid biopsy analysis.
- **Key findings**: 6-month survival rate 92.5% (95% CI 81.8–97.9); median PFS 11.2 months; ORR 26.4%, DCR 79.2%. Low baseline monocyte-derived cfDNA predicted worse OS (HR 4.79).
- **Strengths**: Targets underserved clinical scenario; integrates ctDNA biomarker for prognostic stratification.
- **Limitations**: Single-arm nonrandomized design; n=53 limits subgroup analysis; no direct chemotherapy comparison.
- **Conclusion**: Dalpiciclib plus ET shows clinically meaningful survival in breast cancer visceral crisis, with cfDNA as a potential prognostic biomarker.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s43018-026-01208-0 / PMID:42481711

## 3. Global Trends in the Morbidity Gap 1990–2023: GBD Study 2023 — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Public Health
- **Pub date**: 2026-08 (Aug 2026)
- **Authors**: Hay SI et al.
- **Aims**: To comprehensively estimate changes in the morbidity gap (life expectancy minus HALE) globally from 1990 to 2023 by country, sex, and SDI quintile.
- **Method**: Systematic GBD 2023 analysis across 204 countries; YLD decomposition by cause and risk factor; 250+ posterior draws for uncertainty quantification.
- **Key findings**: Morbidity gap increased 8.8→10.7 years (+21.9%); 14.5% of life spent in poor health in 2023; musculoskeletal, mental, and sensory disorders accounted for 57.4% of unhealthy years; high BMI and fasting glucose were top risk factors.
- **Strengths**: 204-country coverage, uncertainty intervals on all estimates, sex-stratified decomposition.
- **Limitations**: HALE estimates uncertain for low-data regions; morbidity gap framing cannot capture distribution within countries.
- **Conclusion**: Survival gains are outpacing reductions in non-fatal health loss globally; extending healthspan must become a central public health priority.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S2468-2667(26)00098-8 / PMID:42480564

## 4. Designing Efficient Multicenter Trials With a Maximin Strategy — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-08 (Aug 2026)
- **Authors**: Candel MJJM et al.
- **Aims**: To derive optimal and maximin sample size designs for two-treatment multicenter trials with continuous outcomes, under unknown variance parameters.
- **Method**: Analytic derivation of optimal designs balancing allocation ratio, center count, and within-center sample size; maximin approach for worst-case parameter scenarios; R Shiny app.
- **Key findings**: Maximin designs guarantee pre-specified power across plausible parameter ranges; both fixed and optimizable design scenarios derived; meaningful budget savings demonstrated empirically.
- **Strengths**: Provides practical R Shiny tool; addresses fixed-center and fixed-sample-size constraints common in real trials.
- **Limitations**: Framework restricted to two-treatment designs with continuous outcomes.
- **Conclusion**: Maximin multicenter designs offer robust power guarantees while minimizing sample size or budget, with freely available tools.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70677 / PMID:42479089

## 5. IIW-GEE With Irregularly Measured Longitudinal Data and Informative Dropout — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Statistics in Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-08 (Aug 2026)
- **Authors**: Stefan G et al.
- **Aims**: To extend the inverse-intensity-weighted GEE (IIW-GEE) framework to correct for informative dropout in longitudinal studies with irregular visits.
- **Method**: Extended IIW-GEE incorporating dropout propensity weights; simulation studies across varied dropout scenarios; illustrated using STAR*D depression trial data.
- **Key findings**: Standard IIW-GEE is substantially biased when dropout is informative; proposed extension significantly reduces bias in simulations. Disease trajectory was overestimated ~15–25% in STAR*D when dropout ignored.
- **Strengths**: Both simulation and real-data validation; addresses a pervasive bias in observational and trial longitudinal analyses.
- **Limitations**: Assumes correctly specified dropout model; tested on a single real-world application.
- **Conclusion**: Extended IIW-GEE markedly reduces bias from informative dropout in irregular longitudinal studies and should be preferred over standard IIW-GEE in such settings.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1002/sim.70674 / PMID:42479297
