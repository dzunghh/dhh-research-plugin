## 1. Perioperative chemotherapy and SBRT versus chemotherapy alone in high-risk localised pancreatic cancer (MASTERPLAN) — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-23
- **Authors**: Oar A et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate whether adding SBRT to perioperative chemotherapy reduces locoregional recurrence in high-risk localised PDAC.
- **Method**: Open-label phase II RCT across 14 hospitals in Australia/NZ; 74 patients randomised 2:1 to chemo+SBRT vs chemo alone; 40 Gy in 5 fractions.
- **Key findings**: 12-month locoregional recurrence 20.2% vs 52.0% (SHR 0.44, 95%CI 0.22–0.88; p=0.022); grade 3+ AEs 70% vs 64% (NS).
- **Strengths**: Prospective RCT with real-time SBRT quality assurance; stratified randomisation; 3.2-year median follow-up.
- **Limitations**: Closed early due to slow accrual (74 of 120 planned), reducing power for secondary endpoints.
- **Conclusion**: SBRT added to perioperative chemotherapy significantly reduces locoregional recurrence without meaningful toxicity increase, warranting a phase III trial.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00532-2 / PMID:42914998

## 2. Active surveillance versus standard surgery after nCRT for oesophageal cancer: 5-year results of the SANO trial — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Ann Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-23
- **Authors**: Panday SSGG et al.
- **Aims**: Report 5-year OS outcomes of active surveillance versus standard surgery in oesophageal cancer patients achieving complete clinical response after nCRT.
- **Method**: Multicentre stepped-wedge cluster-RCT (2017–2021); 309 patients; Cox regression and competing-risk analysis; median follow-up 76 months.
- **Key findings**: 5-year OS not significantly different (50% vs 54%; HR 1.17, 95%CI 0.77–1.77); distant metastases higher in surveillance arm (46% vs 29%; p<0.01); 43% avoided oesophagectomy.
- **Strengths**: Longest follow-up of any organ-preservation RCT in oesophageal cancer; competing-risk analysis for metastases.
- **Limitations**: Cluster-randomised design limits individual-level causal inference; includes only complete clinical responders.
- **Conclusion**: 5-year OS remains non-significantly different, supporting active surveillance as a feasible strategy despite higher distant metastases in selected patients.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.annonc.2026.10.1290 / PMID:42918421

## 3. Overall survival with adjuvant ribociclib plus NSAI in HR+/HER2- early breast cancer: 6-year follow-up of NATALEE — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Ann Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-23
- **Authors**: Slamon DJ et al.
- **Aims**: Report prespecified 6-year OS analysis of adjuvant ribociclib + nonsteroidal aromatase inhibitor vs NSAI alone in high-risk HR+/HER2- EBC.
- **Method**: Phase III RCT (NATALEE); 1:1 randomisation; ribociclib 400 mg (3 years) + NSAI vs NSAI alone; median OS follow-up 66.5 months.
- **Key findings**: OS HR 0.809 (95%CI 0.661–0.989; p=0.019); 6-year ΔOS 2.1%; iDFS HR 0.741 (p<0.0001), 6-year ΔiDFS 5.3%.
- **Strengths**: Prespecified analysis; consistent OS benefit across subgroups; large internationally representative trial.
- **Limitations**: Descriptive OS analysis not powered for OS; no blinding.
- **Conclusion**: Adjuvant ribociclib + NSAI confers a statistically significant OS benefit at 6 years, establishing the first CDK4/6 inhibitor OS signal in early breast cancer.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.annonc.2026.10.1288 / PMID:42918423

## 4. Final results of phase III ENERGIZE trial: perioperative nivolumab plus GC versus GC alone in muscle-invasive bladder cancer — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Ann Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-23
- **Authors**: Sonpavde G et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate perioperative nivolumab plus gemcitabine-cisplatin vs GC alone in previously untreated resectable MIBC.
- **Method**: Phase III RCT (CA017-078/ENERGIZE); 653 patients; co-primary endpoints pCR and EFS; ctDNA exploratory biomarker analysis; median follow-up 54.5 months.
- **Key findings**: pCR 39.1% vs 29.6% (Δ9.5%) but EFS not improved (HR 0.94, p=0.67); ctDNA positivity/clearance strongly prognostic for EFS and OS.
- **Strengths**: Large phase III with long follow-up and prospective ctDNA biomarker collection in >400 patients.
- **Limitations**: EFS not improved despite pCR improvement; ctDNA analysis remains exploratory.
- **Conclusion**: Perioperative nivolumab did not improve EFS in MIBC despite higher pCR; ctDNA emerges as a robust prognostic biomarker.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.annonc.2026.10.096 / PMID:42918420

## 5. Double-blind phase III KEYNOTE-866: perioperative pembrolizumab plus neoadjuvant GC in cisplatin-eligible muscle-invasive bladder cancer — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Ann Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-23
- **Authors**: Bedke J et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate whether perioperative pembrolizumab added to neoadjuvant cisplatin-gemcitabine and cystectomy improves EFS in cisplatin-eligible MIBC.
- **Method**: Double-blind phase III RCT (KEYNOTE-866); 907 patients 1:1; neoadjuvant + adjuvant pembrolizumab or placebo; median follow-up 60.9 months.
- **Key findings**: EFS HR 0.88 (95%CI 0.71–1.10; p=0.14, non-significant); pCR 44.4% vs 35.8% (Δ8.9%); OS HR 1.00.
- **Strengths**: Double-blind design with 907 patients; longest follow-up (60.9 months) of any perioperative PD-1 bladder trial.
- **Limitations**: Primary endpoint not met; OS HR of 1.00 indicates no survival benefit from pembrolizumab.
- **Conclusion**: Perioperative pembrolizumab did not improve EFS in cisplatin-eligible MIBC, contrasting with enfortumab vedotin combinations.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.annonc.2026.10.106 / PMID:42918422

## Appraisal notes

This week's picks are dominated by high-quality phase III/II RCTs reporting mature (5–6 year) oncology outcomes, with SANO and NATALEE providing landmark long-term data on organ preservation and CDK4/6 inhibition respectively. A notable methodological trend is the integration of ctDNA as a prognostic biomarker in surgical MIBC trials (ENERGIZE, and implicitly KEYNOTE-866), which may inform future adaptive trial designs. The convergence of two large phase III bladder cancer trials (ENERGIZE and KEYNOTE-866) both failing to demonstrate EFS benefit with PD-1 inhibition added to GC challenges the perioperative immunotherapy paradigm for this indication and signals the field toward enfortumab vedotin-based approaches.
