## 1. Small area estimation of hysterectomy prevalence and cervical cancer screening in the U.S. states and counties — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiology, Biomarkers & Prevention
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-18
- **Authors**: Liu B et al.
- **Aims**: Produce state- and county-level small area estimates of cervical cancer screening (CCS) corrected for hysterectomy prevalence across the U.S.
- **Method**: Small area model combining NHIS and BRFSS data (2012–2016, 2021–2023); internal and external validation against cancer registry data.
- **Key findings**: Substantial geographic variation in CCS observed across 3,142 U.S. counties; state-level CCS negatively correlated with cervical cancer incidence (r=−0.36, p<0.0001 for 2012–2016) and mortality.
- **Strengths**: National county-level coverage with dual-data-source modeling; hysterectomy-corrected denominators reduce misclassification.
- **Limitations**: Modeled estimates, not direct measurements; some residual hysterectomy-related misclassification persists.
- **Conclusion**: County-level hysterectomy-corrected CCS maps identify underserved communities for targeted cervical cancer prevention.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-0535 / PMID:43049535

## 2. Association Between Geographic Access to Lung Cancer Screening and Early-Stage Diagnosis — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiology, Biomarkers & Prevention
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-18
- **Authors**: Lian M et al.
- **Aims**: Examine whether geographic access to lung cancer screening (LCS) centers is associated with early-stage lung cancer diagnosis.
- **Method**: SEER data (2015–2019), patients aged 55–80; two-step floating catchment area access metric; modified Poisson regression with county-level clustering.
- **Key findings**: Lowest LCS access quartile was 12% less likely to achieve early-stage diagnosis vs. highest quartile (PR=0.88, 95% CI 0.84–0.93); benefit concentrated in less-deprived counties and the Western U.S.
- **Strengths**: Large national SEER dataset; validated spatial access metric; systematic effect modification analyses by SES, region, and transportation.
- **Limitations**: County-level ecological design; LCS utilization rates not directly measured.
- **Conclusion**: Geographic access to LCS centers is necessary but not sufficient for equitable early detection; socioeconomic context strongly modifies the benefit.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-1148 / PMID:43049537

## 3. Immune protection against anogenital HPV in Rwandan women with and without HIV following the introduction of quadrivalent HPV vaccination — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-17
- **Authors**: Castle PE et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate long-term population effectiveness of national HPV vaccination in Rwandan women with HIV (WWH) versus women without HIV (WWoH).
- **Method**: Cross-sectional analysis of 4 sub-cohorts (n=3,058); vaginal/anal HPV genotyping; anti-HPV16 ELISA as vaccination proxy; multivariable logistic regression.
- **Key findings**: Highest anti-HPV16 antibody quartile associated with ~77% lower anogenital HPV16/18 prevalence (aOR=0.23, 95% CI 0.13–0.38); protection was comparable in WWH (aOR=0.23) and WWoH (aOR=0.24).
- **Strengths**: Large population-based sample in real-world national programme; serological validation of vaccination proxy overcomes self-report bias.
- **Limitations**: Cross-sectional design; self-reported vaccination status as primary exposure measure.
- **Conclusion**: Rwanda's national HPV vaccination programme confers comparable cervical cancer prevention potential in women regardless of HIV status.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag430 / PMID:43046617

## 4. Molecular profiling of repeated self-sampled blood reveals dynamic immune phenotypes in young adults — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Nature Communications
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-14
- **Authors**: Bendes A et al.
- **Aims**: Characterize longitudinal immune and proteome dynamics using dried blood spot (DBS) self-sampling linked to infection and vaccination exposures.
- **Method**: Longitudinal DBS cohort (n=808, ~2,400 samples); 365 inflammation proteins profiled; genome-wide pQTL mapping; registry-linked infection/vaccination exposures.
- **Key findings**: 664 pQTLs identified across 65 proteins; stable anti-IFN autoantibodies (IFNAR1, IFNGR2) associated with longer COVID-19 symptoms; temporal protein shifts post-infection (LAP3) and post-vaccination (TIMP3).
- **Strengths**: Large longitudinal sample with objective registry-linked exposures and genomic validation; scalable DBS self-sampling method.
- **Limitations**: Young adult cohort (24–27 years) limits generalizability to older or cancer-susceptible populations.
- **Conclusion**: Population-based DBS self-sampling enables high-throughput molecular epidemiology and captures short-term immune variability relevant to future cancer-epi proteomics cohorts.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41467-026-78987-8 / PMID:43034200

## 5. Association of food consumption patterns with height growth of children and adolescents: A population-based observational study over four decades of food transitions in China — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: PLoS Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-16
- **Authors**: Li T et al.
- **Aims**: Identify food consumption patterns associated with greater height in Chinese children and adolescents across 40 years of dietary transition.
- **Method**: Observational study (~1.7 million students, 1985–2019); elastic net, WQS regression, PCA for pattern identification; ML with SHAP to derive Food Consumption-Height (FCH) score.
- **Key findings**: FUEL pattern (fruits, nuts, eggs, milk) most strongly and consistently associated with greater height (ρ=0.518, 95% CI 0.453–0.580); calcium density positively associated with height across all three periods.
- **Strengths**: Massive sample spanning four decades; ML-derived FCH score validated in independent Guangzhou cohort; sustainability analysis included.
- **Limitations**: Observational ecological design; population-level food data prevent individual-level causal inference.
- **Conclusion**: Nutrient quality, particularly calcium-rich FUEL pattern, is associated with child height and low environmental impact over four decades of dietary change.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1371/journal.pmed.1004958 / PMID:43038250

## Appraisal notes

This week's picks reflect a strong spatial-epidemiology theme: two Cancer Epidemiol Biomarkers Prev studies apply small area estimation and GIS methods to U.S. cancer screening gaps, underscoring growing interest in geographic equity in early detection. The Rwanda HPV vaccination paper adds real-world vaccine-effectiveness evidence in an HIV-positive sub-population rarely captured in trial data. The molecular-epi and nutritional-epi entries (papers 4 and 5) represent methodological highlights — DBS self-sampling with pQTL mapping and ML-driven dietary scoring respectively — both applicable to future cancer-risk cohort design.
