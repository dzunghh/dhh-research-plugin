## 1. EMERALD-3: Durvalumab+Tremelimumab±Lenvatinib+TACE vs TACE in Embolisation-Eligible HCC — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-01
- **Authors**: Kudo M et al.
- **Aims**: To evaluate STRIDE immunotherapy ± lenvatinib added to TACE in embolisation-eligible hepatocellular carcinoma.
- **Method**: Global, open-label, phase 3 RCT; n=760 across 177 sites in 21 countries; 1:1:1 randomisation.
- **Key findings**: STRIDE+lenvatinib+TACE improved PFS vs TACE (13.0 vs 9.8 months; HR 0.70, 95% CI 0.57–0.86; p=0.0007). Overall survival HR 0.84 (p=0.18), not yet mature.
- **Strengths**: Large multinational RCT with two pre-planned data cutoffs and stratified randomisation.
- **Limitations**: OS benefit not yet demonstrated; high rate of serious adverse events (64%) in triple-combination arm.
- **Conclusion**: STRIDE+lenvatinib+TACE offers a significant PFS benefit and is a potential new standard for embolisation-eligible HCC.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00278-0 / PMID:42636832

## 2. ALINA Trial: HRQoL and Safety of Adjuvant Alectinib vs Chemotherapy in Resected ALK+ NSCLC — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-01
- **Authors**: Dziadziuszko R et al.
- **Aims**: To report health-related quality-of-life and safety from the phase 3 ALINA trial of adjuvant alectinib.
- **Method**: Phase 3 open-label RCT; n=257; SF-36v2 HRQoL assessment over 2 years; standardised NCI CTCAE v5.0 safety grading.
- **Key findings**: Clinically meaningful HRQoL improvements in multiple SF-36v2 domains at week 12; maintained at week 96 near population norms. Fewer discontinuations (5% vs 13%).
- **Strengths**: Pre-specified, validated HRQoL instrument; 2-year follow-up; benchmark against population norms.
- **Limitations**: HRQoL was exploratory endpoint; different follow-up durations between arms (24.8 vs 3.7 months).
- **Conclusion**: Adjuvant alectinib maintains HRQoL over 2 years, reinforcing its standard-of-care role in resected ALK+ NSCLC.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00283-4 / PMID:42636834

## 3. Ifebemtinib+Garsorasib in Pretreated KRASmut Metastatic Colorectal Cancer: Phase 1b/2 Trial — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-01
- **Authors**: Song Z et al.
- **Aims**: To evaluate ifebemtinib (FAK inhibitor) plus garsorasib (KRAS G12C inhibitor) in KRAS-mutated mCRC.
- **Method**: Multicentre phase 1b/2 study; single-arm (n=15) and randomised (n=36) components; ORR primary endpoint.
- **Key findings**: ORR 46.7% in single-arm; 38.9% vs 16.7% in randomised (difference 22.2%; p=0.068, not significant). Manageable grade 3–4 toxicity profile.
- **Strengths**: First randomised data for FAK+KRAS inhibitor combination in mCRC; rigorous response assessment.
- **Limitations**: Small sample (n=51), non-significant primary endpoint, all-Asian cohort limits generalisability.
- **Conclusion**: Ifebemtinib+garsorasib showed promising but statistically non-significant activity in KRAS-mutated mCRC, warranting further evaluation.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00175-0 / PMID:42636835

## 4. SHR-A2102 (Nectin-4 ADC) in Advanced Solid Tumours: Phase 1 Trial — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-01
- **Authors**: Zhong R et al.
- **Aims**: To determine safety, recommended phase 2 dose, and preliminary activity of SHR-A2102, a novel nectin-4-directed ADC.
- **Method**: Multicentre, single-arm phase 1 dose-escalation trial; n=395; Bayesian Optimal Interval design; multiple solid tumour types.
- **Key findings**: No maximum tolerated dose reached; recommended phase 2 dose identified. Grade 3–4 treatment-related adverse events in 54%; treatment-related deaths in 2 patients (<1%).
- **Strengths**: Large phase 1 cohort (n=395) across diverse tumour types; rigorous BOIN design; detailed pharmacokinetics.
- **Limitations**: Single-country (China) study; no efficacy comparator; single-arm design limits conclusions.
- **Conclusion**: SHR-A2102 demonstrated promising activity and manageable toxicity across multiple solid tumours, supporting ongoing phase 2/3 evaluation.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00238-X / PMID:42636836

## 5. Tumour-Infiltrating Lymphocytes and Breast Radiotherapy: International Working Group Perspectives — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-01
- **Authors**: Gunster JLB et al.
- **Aims**: To synthesise evidence on TILs as biomarkers mediating radiotherapy response in breast cancer.
- **Method**: Narrative review with international consensus from the Immuno-Oncology Biomarker Working Group; evidence synthesis across prognostic and predictive TIL data.
- **Key findings**: Pre-treatment TILs are prognostic in breast cancer; evidence for TIL-radiotherapy interaction is scarce. Standardised TIL reporting in RT trials is urgently needed.
- **Strengths**: International multidisciplinary consensus; actionable call for standardised biomarker reporting in radiation oncology.
- **Limitations**: Scarce clinical evidence for TIL-RT interaction; most data from systemic therapy contexts; narrative review susceptible to selection bias.
- **Conclusion**: TILs represent a promising predictive biomarker for radiotherapy individualisation in breast cancer, but rigorous prospective trials with standardised reporting are required.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00255-X / PMID:42636842

## Appraisal notes
