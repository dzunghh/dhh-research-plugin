## 1. Epstein-Barr Virus-Attributable Cancers Among Children and Adolescents in Europe and North America — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: J Natl Cancer Inst
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-01
- **Authors**: Volesky-Avellaneda KD et al.
- **Aims**: Estimate the proportion and number of EBV-attributable cancers in those aged ≤19 years in Europe and North America in 2022.
- **Method**: Systematic review and meta-analysis (53 studies, random-effects models) of EBV prevalence in Burkitt lymphoma, Hodgkin lymphoma, and nasopharyngeal cancer multiplied by GLOBOCAN incidence data.
- **Key findings**: EBV was attributable to 2.8% (≈1,166 cases) of 41,366 childhood cancers; prevalence was 33.4% in Hodgkin lymphoma, 19.7% in Burkitt lymphoma, and 96.8% in nasopharyngeal cancer.
- **Strengths**: Large multi-study synthesis using GLOBOCAN incidence; separate age- and sex-stratified estimates.
- **Limitations**: High heterogeneity (I²=75.6%) in Hodgkin lymphoma studies; limited data from non-Western regions.
- **Conclusion**: Nearly 3% of childhood cancers in high-income regions are EBV-attributable, highlighting vaccine prevention potential.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag344 / PMID:42820761

## 2. Cancer and the Microbiome: From Association to Intervention — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Lancet Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-01
- **Authors**: Moradi A et al.
- **Aims**: Synthesise evidence linking the intestinal microbiome to anticancer therapy outcomes and evaluate therapeutic modulation strategies.
- **Method**: Narrative review of clinical and preclinical evidence across ICI-treated cancers (melanoma, NSCLC, RCC), covering FMT, probiotics, diet, and live biotherapeutic products.
- **Key findings**: Higher gut microbial diversity and immunostimulatory composition associate with improved ICI response and survival; antibiotics and PPIs linked to inferior outcomes.
- **Strengths**: Comprehensive scope across multiple cancer types and intervention modalities; introduces mycobiome as an emerging component.
- **Limitations**: Most intervention trials are early phase with heterogeneous endpoints; causal mechanisms remain incompletely established.
- **Conclusion**: Microbiome modulation is a clinically promising but still investigational strategy to improve ICI outcomes in cancer.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00326-8 / PMID:42805210

## 3. Immunotherapy-Based Therapies in Pediatric Hodgkin Lymphoma: Potential to Narrow the Gap in Long-Term Survival — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: J Natl Cancer Inst
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-01
- **Authors**: Rogers JR et al.
- **Aims**: Project lifetime survival outcomes after contemporary brentuximab vedotin- and nivolumab-containing regimens for pediatric advanced-stage Hodgkin lymphoma.
- **Method**: Microsimulation model integrating COG AHOD1331 and SWOG S1826 trial data with Childhood Cancer Survivor Study and national databases; sensitivity analyses for immunotherapy late mortality.
- **Key findings**: Expected survival at age 50 improved from 86% (historical) to 92–93% (contemporary regimens); gains persisted unless immunotherapy-related late mortality exceeded ~8%.
- **Strengths**: Synthesises multiple high-quality trial and registry data sources; explicit uncertainty quantification via sensitivity analysis.
- **Limitations**: Long-term immunotherapy toxicities remain unknown; model depends on trial-derived survival estimates.
- **Conclusion**: Contemporary immunotherapy regimens may substantially narrow the long-term survival gap while reducing late-effect burden in pediatric HL.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag353 / PMID:42820770

## 4. Point-of-Care Models for Genetic Testing Among Patients at Risk of Hereditary Cancer: A Systematic Review — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiol Biomarkers Prev
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-01
- **Authors**: Heald B et al.
- **Aims**: Summarise genetic testing models used by non-genetics providers in point-of-care settings and their clinical outcomes.
- **Method**: Systematic review of PubMed and Embase (2014–2024), 48 articles from 46 studies; outcomes included uptake, turnaround time, cascade testing.
- **Key findings**: Testing uptake ranged 49–100% in cancer patients; 19/20 comparison-group studies showed similar or higher uptake with point-of-care versus referral-based approaches; 10/11 noted shorter turnaround times.
- **Strengths**: Comprehensive dual-database search; covers diverse delivery models and cancer types.
- **Limitations**: Most studies were in breast/ovarian/prostate cancer at academic-affiliated clinics; post-test counselling and cascade testing inconsistently reported.
- **Conclusion**: Point-of-care genetic testing models are feasible and improve access and efficiency, though equitable implementation in broader settings needs further study.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-0615 / PMID:42820680

## 5. Unwelcome Natural Experiments: Cancer Etiologic Insights from Disaster-Exposed Populations — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiol Biomarkers Prev
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-01
- **Authors**: Ecsedy EM et al.
- **Aims**: Synthesise cancer epidemiology insights derived from catastrophic disasters including radiation events, industrial accidents, and the 9/11 World Trade Center attacks.
- **Method**: Perspective/narrative review linking World Trade Center responder data (4,815 males, 1.8-fold cancer risk) with historical disaster cohorts from Hiroshima, Chernobyl, Seveso, Bhopal, and Vietnam.
- **Key findings**: WTC responders aged 20–44 at time of attacks had the highest cancer risk; across disasters, data clarified organ specificity, dose-response, and age-at-exposure effects with long latency.
- **Strengths**: Draws cross-disaster lessons with policy implications for sustained cohort investment; contextualises new WTC data within historical canon.
- **Limitations**: Narrative synthesis without formal meta-analysis; disaster populations are inherently heterogeneous.
- **Conclusion**: Epidemiologists have a responsibility to ensure sustained long-term follow-up of disaster cohorts to fully characterise cancer consequences.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-1029 / PMID:42817712

## Appraisal notes

This week's digest is dominated by synthesis studies—three systematic reviews/meta-analyses and one simulation model—reflecting the methodological maturity of cancer epidemiology. A notable thematic thread is the role of host and environmental modifiers of cancer risk: the gut microbiome shaping ICI therapy outcomes and EBV as a quantifiable infectious driver of paediatric cancers both point toward pathogen- and microbiome-targeted prevention strategies. The natural-experiment commentary underscores an enduring methodological challenge in epi: leveraging unplanned exposures ethically and rigorously while acknowledging the non-comparability of disaster-affected cohorts.
