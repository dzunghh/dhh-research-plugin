## 1. Effect of two common GVHD prophylaxis regimens on the gut microbiome in a randomized phase 3 trial — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Nature Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-12
- **Authors**: Wirbel J et al.
- **Aims**: Determine whether gut microbiome diversity at neutrophil engraftment predicts non-relapse mortality after allogeneic HCT, comparing PTCy/MMF vs Tac/MTX prophylaxis.
- **Method**: Randomized phase 3 companion study (BMTCTN 1801) with 304 participants; 2575 longitudinal stool samples analysed by metagenomics.
- **Key findings**: No association found between engraftment microbiome diversity and non-relapse mortality (P=0.314). Lower baseline diversity associated with shorter overall survival (P=0.007).
- **Strengths**: Largest prospective randomized microbiome-HCT study; rigorous longitudinal sampling across multicenter trial.
- **Limitations**: Primary endpoint not met; exploratory analyses hypothesis-generating only; limited generalisability beyond HCT populations.
- **Conclusion**: Prophylaxis regimens independently shape the microbiome and immune compartments, complicating microbiome-targeted intervention design.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41591-026-04712-z / PMID:43020901

## 2. A single-cell lung cancer atlas reveals cellular and molecular drivers of metastasis and clinical outcome — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Nature Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-12
- **Authors**: Jurmeister P et al.
- **Aims**: Build a multicenter single-cell atlas of NSCLC to identify molecular drivers of metastasis and predictors of clinical outcome.
- **Method**: scRNA-seq atlas (LuCaS+) of 2.5M nuclei from 454 clinically annotated patients; independent validation in 90 additional patients.
- **Key findings**: Metastatic tumor subpopulations linked to oncogenic signaling, immune evasion, and EMT identified. Integrating tumor-immune-stromal profiles improved survival prediction across all stages.
- **Strengths**: Largest NSCLC single-cell atlas with long-term follow-up and independent multicenter validation.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective clinical annotation; surgical resection biases toward resectable disease; functional validation limited.
- **Conclusion**: LuCaS+ provides a clinically anchored single-cell resource for biology-driven personalised therapies in NSCLC.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s43018-026-01260-w / PMID:43020953

## 3. Targeting F. nucleatum with metronidazole plus tislelizumab and fruquintinib in MSS colorectal cancer: A multicenter phase 2 study — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Cell
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-13
- **Authors**: Jiang S et al.
- **Aims**: Test whether adding metronidazole to anti-PD-1 and VEGFR inhibitor improves outcomes in Fusobacterium nucleatum-positive microsatellite-stable metastatic CRC.
- **Method**: Multicenter single-arm phase 2 trial (NCT06356597); n=33; metagenomic profiling; FMT experiments in germ-free mice.
- **Key findings**: Primary endpoint met: median PFS 6.0 months; ORR 19.4%; DCR 83.9%. FMT from responders sensitised germ-free mice to treatment, enhancing CD8+ T-cell differentiation.
- **Strengths**: First clinical demonstration of bacterium-targeted therapy sensitising immunotherapy in hard-to-treat MSS CRC; mechanistic FMT validation.
- **Limitations**: Small single-arm study (n=33); no randomized comparison; PFS endpoint requires larger confirmatory trial.
- **Conclusion**: Pulsed anaerobe-directed antibiotic combined with immunotherapy is a promising strategy for Fusobacterium nucleatum-positive MSS CRC.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1016/j.ccell.2026.10.012 / PMID:43025422

## 4. Epithelium stratifies via nucleation and growth induced by foam-geometric instability — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Nature Communications
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-13
- **Authors**: Fukamachi S et al.
- **Aims**: Determine the physical mechanism by which an epithelial monolayer transitions to a multilayer (stratification), including in intestinal cancer transformation.
- **Method**: Cell culture experiments combined with 3D vertex modeling; validated in embryonic skin development and intestinal cancer transformation models.
- **Key findings**: Stratification proceeds through dispersed nucleation then lateral expansion driven by foam-geometric mechanical instability. Concept applied to intestinal carcinogenesis.
- **Strengths**: Mechanistic insight from both experimental and computational approaches; cancer transformation validation adds translational relevance.
- **Limitations**: Basic biology focus; direct cancer-epi applicability requires further clinical translation; intestinal cancer model is a secondary application.
- **Conclusion**: Epithelial stratification is a physically driven nucleation-growth transition, offering a new framework for understanding early cancer transformation.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41467-026-78964-1 / PMID:43026291

## 5. The antioxidant addiction of cancer — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: Nature Reviews Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-13
- **Authors**: Chandel NS et al.
- **Aims**: Examine how tumour stage-specific antioxidant pathway dependencies create therapeutic vulnerabilities exploitable to treat tumours and prevent metastasis.
- **Method**: Narrative review and perspective synthesising ROS biology, ferroptosis, and antioxidant pathway evidence across cancer types and stages.
- **Key findings**: Broad dietary antioxidant supplementation lacks clinical benefit and may cause harm. Selective targeting of specific antioxidant pathways (stage-dependent) is the emerging strategy.
- **Strengths**: Comprehensive synthesis across cancer biology, clinical trials, and mechanistic data; identifies actionable therapeutic targets.
- **Limitations**: Review/perspective format; no primary data; clinical translation of selective antioxidant targeting remains early-stage.
- **Conclusion**: Stage-specific antioxidant dependencies are exploitable vulnerabilities; targeted antioxidant inhibition is preferable to broad supplementation.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41568-026-00997-x / PMID:43026262
