## 1. Azacitidine-Venetoclax or Induction Chemotherapy for Acute Myeloid Leukemia — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: N Engl J Med
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-03
- **Authors**: Fathi AT et al.
- **Aims**: Compare azacitidine plus venetoclax versus induction chemotherapy as first-line therapy in induction-eligible AML patients.
- **Method**: Multicenter Phase 2 RCT (n=172), 1:1 randomization, primary endpoint event-free survival; FLT3/NPM1-mutant patients excluded.
- **Key findings**: Median EFS 14.5 vs 6.2 months (HR 0.57, 95% CI 0.39–0.84; P=0.002); grade ≥3 infection 28% vs 41%.
- **Strengths**: Randomized multicenter design with pre-specified primary endpoint and 21.9-month median follow-up.
- **Limitations**: Phase 2, relatively small sample; excludes common favorable-risk mutations.
- **Conclusion**: Azacitidine-venetoclax significantly extends EFS over standard induction chemotherapy in adverse-risk AML.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1056/NEJMoa2602804 / PMID:42685316

## 2. Neoadjuvant Anbenitamab and HB1801 in ERBB2-Positive Breast Cancer: A Phase 3 Randomized Clinical Trial — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-03
- **Authors**: Li J et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate whether neoadjuvant anbenitamab (ERBB2-biparatopic antibody) plus HB1801 improves pathological complete response vs standard trastuzumab-pertuzumab-docetaxel.
- **Method**: Multicenter Phase 3 registration RCT (n=521), stage II–III HER2+ breast cancer, primary endpoint tpCR by blinded independent review.
- **Key findings**: tpCR 62.4% vs 51.2% (absolute difference 11.4 pp, P=0.004); grade 3–4 adverse events similar (29.3% vs 28.3%).
- **Strengths**: Large Phase 3 registrational trial with independent adjudication; consistent benefit across subgroups.
- **Limitations**: Conducted solely in China; long-term survival follow-up not yet available.
- **Conclusion**: Novel ERBB2-biparatopic antibody combination significantly improves pCR with comparable toxicity in early HER2+ breast cancer.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamaoncol.2026.3329 / PMID:42690668

## 3. Molecularly Guided Therapy Versus Continued Chemotherapy in Unfavorable Cancer of Unknown Primary: Updated CUPISCO Study — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: J Clin Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-03
- **Authors**: Krämer A et al.
- **Aims**: Report updated efficacy and safety of comprehensive genomic profiling–guided molecularly guided therapy vs platinum chemotherapy in unfavorable cancer of unknown primary.
- **Method**: International Phase 2 RCT (n=436, 3:1 MGT vs chemo), median follow-up 37 months; primary endpoint progression-free survival.
- **Key findings**: Updated median PFS 6.1 vs 4.4 months (HR 0.75; P=.017); OS 15.2 vs 12.8 months (HR 0.79; P=.069).
- **Strengths**: Largest RCT to date in CUP; comprehensive genomic profiling integrated at diagnosis; extended follow-up confirms primary finding.
- **Limitations**: Open-label; underpowered for OS; heterogeneous actionable alterations limit subgroup analysis.
- **Conclusion**: CGP-guided molecularly targeted therapy maintains a PFS benefit over chemotherapy in unfavorable CUP beyond 3 years of follow-up.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1200/JCO-25-02974 / PMID:42691436

## 4. Daraxonrasib for Previously Treated RAS-Mutant Non-Small-Cell Lung Cancer — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: N Engl J Med
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-03
- **Authors**: Arbour KC et al.
- **Aims**: Assess safety and antitumor activity of daraxonrasib (oral pan-RAS multiselective inhibitor) in previously treated RAS-mutant NSCLC.
- **Method**: Phase 1–2 multicenter dose-escalation and dose-expansion study (n=136 at ≤300 mg); primary endpoint safety; secondary endpoints ORR and duration of response.
- **Key findings**: ORR 37% at 300 mg; grade ≥3 adverse events in 54%; rash, diarrhea, and pneumonia most common serious events.
- **Strengths**: First-in-class pan-RAS inhibitor demonstrating >30% ORR in historically undruggable pan-RAS NSCLC population.
- **Limitations**: Single-arm Phase 1–2, no comparator arm; heterogeneous RAS mutation subtypes analyzed together.
- **Conclusion**: Daraxonrasib shows meaningful antitumor activity in RAS-mutant NSCLC with substantial but manageable toxicity, supporting ongoing Phase 3 evaluation.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1056/NEJMoa2504059 / PMID:42685317

## 5. A Phase II Randomized Study of Proton Therapy vs IMRT in Patients with Unilateral Head and Neck Cancer — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: J Natl Cancer Inst
- **Pub date**: 2026-09-03
- **Authors**: Dee EC et al.
- **Aims**: Compare acute toxicity of proton beam radiotherapy versus IMRT in postoperative unilateral head and neck cancer.
- **Method**: Multicenter Phase 2 RCT (n=98), 1:1 randomization to PBRT or IMRT at 60 Gy in 30 fractions; primary endpoint grade ≥2 oral mucositis at 3 months.
- **Key findings**: Grade ≥2 oral mucositis 7.4% vs 22.7% (P=0.03); dysgeusia 9.3% vs 31.8% (P=0.005); 3-year survival similar.
- **Strengths**: Randomized design, multicenter, patient-reported outcomes alongside clinician-graded toxicity.
- **Limitations**: Small sample (n=98) with wide confidence intervals for PROs; underpowered for survival outcomes.
- **Conclusion**: Proton therapy significantly reduces acute mucosal and taste toxicity vs IMRT in postoperative unilateral head and neck radiotherapy.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag257 / PMID:42690689

## Appraisal notes

This week's digest is dominated by high-impact Phase 2–3 randomized trials testing precision oncology strategies: a practice-changing NEJM AML trial demonstrating venetoclax-based combinations outperform conventional induction, and a JAMA Oncology Phase 3 trial validating a novel ERBB2-biparatopic antibody in early breast cancer. A recurring theme is the actionability of genomic heterogeneity — the CUPISCO CUP trial and daraxonrasib KRAS data both illustrate how broad molecular profiling and multi-target inhibition are reshaping treatment of historically intractable malignancies. Epidemiological methods representation was limited this window, with the cohort and EHR-based papers appearing in lower-tier journals outside the final five.
