## 1. SKYSCRAPER-01: Tiragolumab + Atezolizumab in PD-L1-High NSCLC — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: J Clin Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-27
- **Authors**: Peters S et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate tiragolumab + atezolizumab vs placebo + atezolizumab in untreated PD-L1-high locally advanced/metastatic NSCLC.
- **Method**: Phase III RCT (SKYSCRAPER-01); N=521, 1:1 randomisation; dual primary endpoints INV-PFS and OS.
- **Key findings**: Median OS 23.1 vs 16.9 months (HR 0.87, 95% CI 0.7–1.1, p=0.22); INV-PFS HR 0.78 (non-significant); primary endpoints not met.
- **Strengths**: Large global RCT with mature OS data at median 17.9 months follow-up.
- **Limitations**: Both co-primary endpoints missed significance; PD-L1 assay heterogeneity may have affected patient selection.
- **Conclusion**: Anti-TIGIT addition to atezolizumab provides no statistically significant survival benefit in PD-L1-high NSCLC.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1200/JCO-25-02777 / PMID:42507968

## 2. STELLAR Trial 5-Year Outcomes: Short-Course RT + Chemotherapy vs Long-Course CRT in Rectal Cancer — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: J Clin Oncol
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-27
- **Authors**: Tang Y et al.
- **Aims**: Report 5-year outcomes comparing short-course radiotherapy-based total neoadjuvant therapy (TNT) vs standard long-course chemoradiotherapy (CRT) in locally advanced rectal cancer.
- **Method**: Phase III RCT (STELLAR); median follow-up 68.7 months; survival analysis by Kaplan–Meier and Cox regression.
- **Key findings**: 5-year OS 78.1% (TNT) vs 69.7% (CRT), HR 0.739 (95% CI 0.550–0.993); DFS HR 0.849 (non-significant). High-risk subgroup showed improved OS (HR 0.663, 95% CI 0.469–0.937).
- **Strengths**: Mature 5-year follow-up from a large multicentre Chinese Phase III RCT.
- **Limitations**: Single-country population may limit generalisability; DFS benefit did not reach significance.
- **Conclusion**: SCRT-based TNT confers a durable OS advantage over CRT, particularly in high-risk locally advanced rectal cancer.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1200/JCO-25-02387 / PMID:42507974

## 3. Racial and Ethnic Disparities in Cause of Death for Clear Cell Renal Cell Carcinoma — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Nat Commun
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-28
- **Authors**: Xie Y et al.
- **Aims**: Analyse trends and contributors of racial/ethnic disparities in causes of death among patients with clear cell renal cell carcinoma.
- **Method**: Population-based cohort using SEER (2000–2019); competing-risks framework; multivariable Cox regression.
- **Key findings**: Black patients had HR 1.15 (95% CI 1.09–1.22) vs whites; AIAN patients HR 1.25 (1.10–1.43); cardiovascular mortality disparities between Black and white patients widened over time.
- **Strengths**: Large registry spanning 20 years with competing-risk analysis; identifies modifiable contributors including stage, surgery, income, and geography.
- **Limitations**: Administrative data limit granularity on treatment quality and comorbidity burden.
- **Conclusion**: Persistent and widening racial/ethnic mortality disparities in renal cell carcinoma highlight the need for targeted equity interventions.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41467-026-70709-4 / PMID:42521664

## 4. Effectiveness of Incentives on Cancer Screening Among Indigenous Populations: Systematic Review — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiol Biomarkers Prev
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-28
- **Authors**: Khalaj N et al.
- **Aims**: Investigate the effectiveness of financial and non-financial incentives on cancer screening participation in Indigenous populations.
- **Method**: PRISMA-compliant systematic review; 4-database search (inception–Sept 2025); 35 included studies appraised with Mixed-Methods Appraisal Tool.
- **Key findings**: Education programmes improved screening rates by 11.7–370%; outreach with personalised follow-up improved participation by >31%; co-designed, culturally tailored interventions were most effective.
- **Strengths**: Comprehensive multi-database search; dual independent reviewers; mixed-methods quality appraisal.
- **Limitations**: Heterogeneity in incentive types and outcomes; limited high-quality RCT evidence within the included studies.
- **Conclusion**: Culturally co-designed incentive programmes are most effective for improving cancer screening uptake in Indigenous communities.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-25-2071 / PMID:42518200

## 5. Pooled Multinomial Logistic Regression for Parametric G-Computation with Competing Events — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-05-01 (EDAT: 2026-07-26–08-01)
- **Authors**: Neuroth LM et al.
- **Aims**: Introduce pooled multinomial logistic regression as a simplified alternative to multiple logistic models for parametric g-computation in the presence of competing events.
- **Method**: Methodological paper; proof-of-concept in the Women's Interagency HIV Study (N=1,164); parametric g-computation estimating marginal 2-year risks.
- **Key findings**: Identical estimates to standard multiple-logistic g-computation; 2-year risk difference for HAART initiation -12.5% (95% CI -18.0, -7.0%) with historical injection drug use.
- **Strengths**: Simplifies competing-events g-computation to a single model fit; directly applicable to cancer survival analyses.
- **Limitations**: Proof-of-concept only; real-data validation in cancer settings not yet conducted.
- **Conclusion**: A single pooled multinomial logit model adequately replaces multiple logistic models in g-computation, reducing analytic complexity.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1097/EDE.0000000000001995 / PMID:42525475
