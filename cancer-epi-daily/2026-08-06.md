## 1. Racial Differences in Sonographic Evaluation in Suspected Cases of Endometrial Neoplasia — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Network Open
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-03
- **Authors**: Rotenberg O et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate racial/ethnic differences in transvaginal ultrasound (TVU) diagnostic performance for endometrial neoplasia detection.
- **Method**: Prospective cohort study (n=1,833 women ≥50 years) at a large urban academic center; TVU followed by sonohysterogram-directed biopsy.
- **Key findings**: Black women had significantly lower TVU completion rates vs White women (75.7% vs 88.9%; RR 0.85; 95% CI 0.80–0.90); disparity disappeared after excluding women with fibroids.
- **Strengths**: Large diverse prospective cohort; covers Black, Hispanic, and White women; rigorous final-outcome ascertainment.
- **Limitations**: Single academic center; may not represent community or rural settings.
- **Conclusion**: Fibroid-driven endometrial inaccessibility, not diagnostic inaccuracy, underpins TVU completion disparities in Black women with suspected endometrial neoplasia.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.27283 / PMID:42550505

## 2. Leveraging Expression Quantitative Trait Loci Information in Single-Cell Resolution to Identify Cell-Specific Genes for Basal Cell Carcinoma — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: PLOS ONE
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-04
- **Authors**: Du M et al.
- **Aims**: Identify cell-type-specific susceptibility genes for basal cell carcinoma by integrating GWAS with single-cell eQTL data.
- **Method**: BCC GWAS (17,416 cases, 375,455 controls) integrated with 12-immune-cell-type sc-eQTL data (OneK1K) using OTTERS + ACAT-O for single-cell TWAS.
- **Key findings**: scTWAS identified 207 non-redundant BCC susceptibility genes vs only 35 by bulk TWAS; cell-specific immune programs (MHC-II antigen presentation in CD4ET, innate immunity in monocytes) were missed by tissue-averaged analysis.
- **Strengths**: Large GWAS sample; novel multi-cell-type sc-TWAS framework; reproducible with publicly available data.
- **Limitations**: Exploratory analysis using healthy-donor PBMC sc-eQTLs, not tumor-derived immune populations; requires validation.
- **Conclusion**: Single-cell TWAS substantially outperforms bulk TWAS for BCC, revealing immune-cell-specific genetic risk mechanisms with therapeutic relevance.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1371/journal.pone.0354887 / PMID:42550790

## 3. Post-Chemotherapy Retroperitoneal Lymph Node Dissection for Patients with Residual Masses in Metastatic Pure Seminoma — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-04
- **Authors**: Jayalath VH et al.
- **Aims**: Define patient selection for pcRPLND and characterize outcomes in metastatic pure seminoma with residual masses.
- **Method**: Retrospective cohort study (n=52) at a single high-volume center (Memorial Sloan Kettering), 2000–2024; outcomes stratified by chemotherapy line and surgical indication.
- **Key findings**: Viable residual seminoma found in 23% after first-line and 6% after ≥2 lines of chemotherapy; 0% in patients receiving high-dose TI-CE; 25% cured by surgery alone.
- **Strengths**: Consecutive series from a high-volume reference center; long median follow-up (50 months); distinguishes consolidation vs relapse indications.
- **Limitations**: Small single-center retrospective cohort; selection bias inherent; limited generalizability.
- **Conclusion**: Benefit of pcRPLND is highly dependent on prior chemotherapy regimen, underscoring the need for improved post-chemotherapy diagnostics in metastatic seminoma.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag249 / PMID:42549863

## 4. The Real-World Use of McGill Interactive Pediatric OncoGenetic Guidelines (MIPOGG): A Cross-Sectional Survey — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-04
- **Authors**: Higuera-Ornelas J et al.
- **Aims**: Characterize self-reported real-world use of the validated MIPOGG cancer predisposition syndrome (CPS) screening tool across clinical, educational, and research settings.
- **Method**: Cross-sectional web-based mixed-methods survey of MIPOGG newsletter subscribers (n=90 respondents, 23 countries); descriptive quantitative + qualitative open-ended analysis.
- **Key findings**: 89.4% used MIPOGG clinically; 82.2% for guiding genetic referrals, 84.9% for testing decisions; 52.1% followed recommendations most of the time.
- **Strengths**: International sample; mixed-methods design captures qualitative use patterns; reveals real-world adherence dynamics.
- **Limitations**: Very small convenience sample (n=90 of 1,749 subscribers); self-reported outcomes; low response rate.
- **Conclusion**: MIPOGG is primarily a clinical decision-support tool used globally to guide cancer predisposition genetic referrals, underscoring the importance of algorithmic transparency and oversight.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag272 / PMID:42549886

## 5. Association Between Preoperative Chronic Steroid Use and Pulmonary Embolism in Adult Tumor Craniotomy: Linking Role of Platelet-to-White Blood Cell Ratio — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: PLOS ONE
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-04
- **Authors**: Gong B et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate the association between preoperative chronic steroid use and 30-day postoperative pulmonary embolism (PE) in tumor craniotomy, and explore the mediating role of PLT/WBC ratio.
- **Method**: Retrospective secondary analysis of the NSQIP database (n=15,274 tumor craniotomy patients); multivariable logistic regression and formal mediation analysis.
- **Key findings**: Chronic steroid use associated with elevated PE risk (OR=1.90; 95% CI: 1.24–2.90); PLT/WBC ratio significantly partially mediated the association (p=0.006).
- **Strengths**: Large national database; formal mediation analysis adds mechanistic epidemiological insight; clinically actionable finding.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective secondary analysis; residual confounding; limited causal inference; no long-term follow-up.
- **Conclusion**: Preoperative chronic steroid use increases postoperative PE risk in brain tumor surgery patients, partially through PLT/WBC-mediated coagulation effects.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1371/journal.pone.0355416 / PMID:42550807
