## 1. Hospital contacts before childhood cancer and the risk of early mortality: a Danish and Swedish population-based cohort study — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: British Journal of Cancer (Br J Cancer)
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-23
- **Authors**: Kenborg L et al.
- **Aims**: Examine whether pre-diagnostic hospital contacts are associated with risk of early mortality (0–90 days) after childhood cancer diagnosis.
- **Method**: Register-based cohort study; 14,089 children with cancer in Denmark and Sweden (1989–2014); logistic regression for ORs of early mortality.
- **Key findings**: Children hospitalised ≥14 nights before diagnosis had OR 4.6 (95% CI 3.2–6.4) for early mortality; blood disorders pre-cancer carried OR 3.3 (95% CI 2.1–5.1).
- **Strengths**: Large multi-national population-based register linkage covering 25 years; adjustment for maternal education.
- **Limitations**: Register-based design limits causal attribution; limited granularity on cancer subtype and treatment context.
- **Conclusion**: Pre-diagnostic hospital morbidity signals underlying fragility that substantially elevates early mortality risk in childhood cancer.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41416-026-03560-5 / PMID:42493601

## 2. Health Consequences of Spousal Health Shocks: Evidence from a Matched Event Study in the United States — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: American Journal of Epidemiology (Am J Epidemiol)
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-24
- **Authors**: Lee AR et al.
- **Aims**: Quantify population-level health spillover to US spouses/partners following a partner's first diagnosis of cancer, stroke, or heart disease.
- **Method**: Stacked event-study with matching; US Health and Retirement Study 1992–2020 (n=29,166); outcomes include self-rated health, ADL/IADL, depressive symptoms.
- **Key findings**: Partners showed modest persistent health decline: β=0.05 more doctor-diagnosed conditions (95% CI 0.01–0.08) and β=0.08 higher depressive symptoms (95% CI 0.01–0.16); effects larger for women with lower-educated or asset-poor spouses.
- **Strengths**: Rigorous quasi-experimental matched event-study design with a large, well-documented longitudinal cohort; heterogeneity analysis by sex and SES.
- **Limitations**: Cancer is one of three pooled diagnoses; cannot fully separate cancer-specific from other disease spillovers.
- **Conclusion**: Major diagnoses create measurable spillover health burdens on partners, motivating spouse-focused supportive interventions.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/aje/kwag178 / PMID:42496175
