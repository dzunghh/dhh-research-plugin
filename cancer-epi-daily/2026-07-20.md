## 1. Device-Measured Movement Behaviours and Cancer Incidence in UK Adults — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: BMC Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-18
- **Authors**: Mitchell JJ et al.
- **Aims**: To examine how 24-hour composition of movement behaviours (postures and intensities) relates to physical-activity-related cancer incidence in UK adults.
- **Method**: Prospective cohort; UK Biobank accelerometry subsample (n=59,218); 8-year median follow-up; Cox models; dual 24-hour compositional analyses; health registry linkage.
- **Key findings**: Replacing 15 min sedentary time with moving associated with HR 0.98 (95% CI 0.97–0.99); vigorous PA component of MVPA was the critical intensity.
- **Strengths**: Objective accelerometry in 59,218 adults with registry-linked cancer outcomes; novel dual compositional 24-hour framework.
- **Limitations**: Observational; predominantly older adults (mean age 61.7 y); residual confounding and reverse causation cannot be excluded.
- **Conclusion**: Any movement replacing sedentary behaviour reduces PA-related cancer risk; vigorous intensity and standing warrant particular attention.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1186/s12916-026-05069-3 / PMID:42471677

## 2. Timing of Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors and Overall Survival in Advanced Cancer — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: British Journal of Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-18
- **Authors**: Bosetti T et al.
- **Aims**: To determine whether time-of-day of ICI administration affects overall survival in patients with advanced cancer.
- **Method**: Retrospective single-centre cohort (The Christie, n=2,631); Cox models with time-dependent variables to minimise immortal time bias; 5 tumour types; 2018–2023.
- **Key findings**: Late-group median OS 13.1 vs 21.4 months (early group); time-dependent HR 1.30 (95% CI 1.16–1.44) for late administration.
- **Strengths**: Large cancer-centre cohort; time-dependent Cox model explicitly addresses immortal time bias; multiple tumour types.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective single-centre; residual confounding by performance status and scheduling; no randomised data.
- **Conclusion**: Earlier time-of-day ICI infusion consistently associated with longer OS; randomised trials are required to confirm causal effect.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41416-026-03494-y / PMID:42471484

## 3. Programme-Level Determinants of CRC Screening Participation in Italy — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: BMC Public Health
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-17
- **Authors**: Marmo R et al.
- **Aims**: To identify organisational factors jointly determining participation in Italian population-based colorectal cancer screening programmes.
- **Method**: National cross-sectional survey; 58 CRC programmes across 17 regions; target population 5.9 million; multivariable fractional logistic regression; validated web questionnaire.
- **Key findings**: Northern location (OR 2.03), programme age ≥16 years (OR 1.87), unified regional management (OR 1.45), and coordinated FIT-positive communication (OR 1.44) independently predicted higher participation; range 6.5%–67.1%.
- **Strengths**: National scope covering 5.9 million individuals; validated instrument; accounts for multiple organisational domains simultaneously.
- **Limitations**: Ecological cross-sectional design; single-year (2022) participation outcome; individual-level confounders unmeasured.
- **Conclusion**: Multiple organisational domains jointly determine CRC participation; decentralised systems require long-standing, coordinated programme structures.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1186/s12889-026-28525-4 / PMID:42469726

## 4. Prediction Model for False-Positive Nodal Staging in <4-cm NSCLC — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: BMC Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-17
- **Authors**: Isaka T et al.
- **Aims**: To develop and validate a model identifying patients at high risk of false-positive clinical nodal (cN+) staging in <4-cm non-small cell lung cancer.
- **Method**: Multicenter retrospective development cohort (n=251) and temporal external validation cohort (n=108); multivariable logistic regression; C-index and calibration assessed.
- **Key findings**: 28.7% of cN+ patients were pathologically node-negative; five-factor model achieved C-index 0.77 in validation, specificity 96.3%, PPV 70.0%.
- **Strengths**: External temporal validation with good calibration; five clinically available predictors; directly actionable for surgical vs neoadjuvant decisions.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective; small validation cohort (n=108); single-country (Japan) limiting generalisability.
- **Conclusion**: A five-variable preoperative model accurately identifies false-positive cN staging and may support upfront surgery in selected patients.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1186/s12885-026-16580-4 / PMID:42469687

## 5. Diagnostic Delay as Health System Navigation in Childhood Cancer in Burkina Faso — Score 3/6

- **Journal**: BMC Cancer
- **Pub date**: 2026-07-18
- **Authors**: Ida A et al.
- **Aims**: To examine how diagnostic delay accumulates across care pathways for children with cancer in Burkina Faso using a health system navigation perspective.
- **Method**: Sequential explanatory mixed-methods; 30 children (quantitative) + 20 caregiver interviews (qualitative); multivariable regression; health system navigation framework applied.
- **Key findings**: Median diagnostic delay 103.5 days (IQR 58–196); more provider encounters independently associated with longer delay (β=0.22, 95% CI 0.02–0.41, p=0.03).
- **Strengths**: Novel health system navigation framework integrates qualitative trajectories with quantitative delay data; fills LMIC evidence gap.
- **Limitations**: Very small quantitative sample (n=30); exploratory associations only; single-country LMIC context limits transferability.
- **Conclusion**: Childhood cancer diagnostic delay in Burkina Faso reflects cumulative navigation complexity; improving referral coordination and diagnostic capacity is essential.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1186/s12885-026-16462-9 / PMID:42471650

## Appraisal notes

The week's standout paper applies UK Biobank accelerometry within a novel dual 24-hour compositional framework, establishing that vigorous intensity is the critical movement behaviour for cancer risk reduction — a methodological advance over binary MVPA analyses. A recurring theme across this digest is the importance of time-related exposures: the ICI chronomedicine study demonstrates that time-of-day treatment scheduling is actionable and that time-dependent Cox modelling is essential to avoid immortal time bias in this setting. The Italian CRC screening analysis exemplifies programme-level epidemiology, showing that governance maturity and coordination systematically outweigh isolated interventions for population participation rates.
