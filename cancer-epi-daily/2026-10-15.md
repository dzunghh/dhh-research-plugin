## 1. A health-system-scale, episode-resolved multimodal atlas of cancer cachexia — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Nature Medicine
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-12
- **Authors**: Castillon VJ et al.
- **Aims**: Characterize episode-resolved trajectories of cachectic weight loss and link them to outcomes, serology, and tumor genotypes across cancer types.
- **Method**: Retrospective cohort of 52,236 MSK-IMPACT patients (2010–2024); multivariate predictive model externally validated at Dana-Farber (n=5,785).
- **Key findings**: Cachectic episodes clustered near disease progression and predicted inferior outcomes across nearly all cancer types; TP53 mutation associated with more frequent cachexia onset.
- **Strengths**: Largest clinicogenomic cachexia cohort to date with prospective external validation across two major cancer centers.
- **Limitations**: Primarily a single health-system discovery cohort; retrospective; weight-loss definition reliant on documented measurements.
- **Conclusion**: Routine clinicogenomic data are a scalable resource for cachexia discovery and risk prediction in diverse malignancies.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1038/s41591-026-04689-9 / PMID:42864412

## 2. Estimate Optimal Dynamic Treatment Regimes with Partial Compatibility — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: American Journal of Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-13
- **Authors**: Si C et al.
- **Aims**: Develop adaptive weighting methods for estimating dynamic treatment regime values when strict compatibility yields few valid trajectories.
- **Method**: Proposed generalized compatibility weighting (GCW) and bootstrap compatibility windowing (BCW); illustrated using a HIV observational cohort in India (2013–2024).
- **Key findings**: GCW and BCW produced more stable estimated regime values and more reliable optimal threshold identification than standard IPW in sparse data.
- **Strengths**: Addresses a real instability problem in DTR estimation with practical tools and clear empirical demonstration.
- **Limitations**: Validated in one small observational site; generalizability to other clinical settings requires further study.
- **Conclusion**: GCW and BCW improve causal treatment rule evaluation in observational studies with limited sample compatibility.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/aje/kwag254 / PMID:42865085

## 3. Risk of second primary malignancies in older long-term survivors of breast, prostate, colon, and rectal cancer — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-13
- **Authors**: Kwaramba T et al.
- **Aims**: Quantify late second primary malignancy (SPM) incidence and demographic/clinical risk factors among older long-term survivors.
- **Method**: SEER-Medicare retrospective cohort of 90,261 survivors (≥5yr post-diagnosis, age 66+); restricted mean survival time regression; SPMs classified into hematologic, screen-detectable, and non-screen-detectable.
- **Key findings**: 11.2% developed a late SPM; cumulative incidence highest in prostate survivors; non-screen-detectable tumors comprised 47.8% of all late SPMs.
- **Strengths**: Large national linked dataset; rigorous RMST approach accounts for competing mortality; clinically actionable SPM subtype classification.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective Medicare design limits generalizability to younger or uninsured survivors; residual confounding from treatment intensity.
- **Conclusion**: Approximately one-in-nine older long-term survivors develops a late SPM, supporting active surveillance strategies extending beyond 5 years post-diagnosis.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag383 / PMID:42867138

## 4. Income Dynamics and Incident Esophageal Cancer: A Retrospective Cohort Study — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Cancer Epidemiology, Biomarkers & Prevention
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-12
- **Authors**: Yoo JE et al.
- **Aims**: Examine whether longitudinal income trajectories (variability, cumulative duration, direction) are associated with esophageal cancer risk beyond static SES.
- **Method**: Retrospective cohort of 3,361,091 Korean adults (2012–2022); income dynamics derived from National Health Insurance data; adjusted Cox regression over 9.3-year follow-up.
- **Key findings**: Lowest income group had 22% higher EC risk (aHR 1.22, 95% CI 1.07–1.38); income decline from high-to-low status associated with 39% higher risk (aHR 1.39).
- **Strengths**: Very large population-based cohort; novel income trajectory approach capturing temporal SES dynamics; long follow-up.
- **Limitations**: Single-country (Korea); ICD-10 coded outcomes; potential residual confounding from lifestyle risk factors such as alcohol and smoking.
- **Conclusion**: Income variability, persistent poverty, and downward mobility independently increase esophageal cancer risk, suggesting income trajectories should inform EC prevention strategies.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-26-0805 / PMID:42862061

## 5. Alignment with lifestyle recommendations and physical health in breast cancer survivors and women without cancer — Score 4/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-10-13
- **Authors**: Bodelon C et al.
- **Aims**: Assess whether adherence to ACS lifestyle guidelines relates to physical health across breast cancer treatment types and survivorship timepoints.
- **Method**: Prospective cohort (ACS Cancer Prevention Study-3); 1,610 BC survivors matched 1:10 to 16,100 cancer-free controls; ACS guideline score (0–8); linear regression on PROMIS Global Health Scale.
- **Key findings**: Chemotherapy-treated survivors with high guideline alignment had significantly better physical health (β=2.56, 95% CI 1.40–3.72 at ≥3yr post-diagnosis) vs. low-alignment survivors.
- **Strengths**: Prospective design with large matched comparison group; validated lifestyle and patient-reported health instruments.
- **Limitations**: Self-reported lifestyle exposures; observational design precludes causal inference; limited reporting of racial/ethnic diversity.
- **Conclusion**: High alignment with ACS lifestyle guidelines is associated with physical health outcomes comparable to population norms even in chemotherapy-treated breast cancer survivors.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag388 / PMID:42867136
