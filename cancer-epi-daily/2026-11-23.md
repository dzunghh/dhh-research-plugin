## 1. Medical Imaging and Pediatric and Adolescent Brain Tumor Risk — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: JAMA
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-19
- **Authors**: Miglioretti DL et al.
- **Aims**: Quantify dose-related brain tumor risk from ionizing radiation in medical imaging among children and adolescents.
- **Method**: Retrospective cohort of 3.7 million children across 6 US health systems + Ontario; Cox and linear dose-response models for cumulative brain radiation dose.
- **Key findings**: Brain tumor risk increased with dose (HR 3.85 at ≥150 mGy vs no exposure); population-attributable fraction 10.1%; ~1 excess tumor per 1,266 head CTs.
- **Strengths**: Largest pediatric imaging-cancer cohort to date with 45.7M person-years and dosimetry data.
- **Limitations**: Residual confounding from underlying conditions requiring imaging; short median follow-up for late-onset tumors.
- **Conclusion**: Dose-related increase in pediatric brain tumor risk supports careful clinical justification and dose minimization for ionizing-radiation imaging.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jama.2026.20441 / PMID:43056070

## 2. Chest Computed Tomography Receipt Beyond 5 Years After Lung Cancer Diagnosis and Survival — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-19
- **Authors**: Yoon DW et al.
- **Aims**: Evaluate whether CT surveillance beyond 5 years after lung cancer diagnosis is associated with subsequent cancer events and survival.
- **Method**: National population-based target trial emulation using Korean NHIS data; 55,694 survivors, monthly eligibility re-assessment, propensity-score matching.
- **Key findings**: CT receipt associated with increased LCE detection (HR 1.28) and 34% lower all-cause mortality (HR 0.66); benefit strongest in females and never-smokers.
- **Strengths**: Applies rigorous target trial emulation to address immortal-time bias; nationwide data with 2.6M person-trials.
- **Limitations**: Claims-based design limits clinical detail; residual confounding by indication possible.
- **Conclusion**: CT surveillance beyond 5 years post-diagnosis supports lower mortality and earlier resection after recurrence in selected lung cancer survivors.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamaoncol.2026.4659 / PMID:43056125

## 3. Using Probabilistic Bias Analysis to Adjust for Selection Bias in Retrospective Case-Control Studies — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: American Journal of Epidemiology
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-18
- **Authors**: Bashir H et al.
- **Aims**: Develop probabilistic bias analysis (PBA) methods to adjust for differential non-participation selection bias in retrospective case-control studies.
- **Method**: Simulation study comparing four probability distributions (uniform, triangular, trapezoidal, beta) for non-participant exposure prevalence; applied to passive smoking and breast cancer meta-analysis.
- **Key findings**: Trapezoidal distribution was most principled; applying it shifted the retrospective meta-analytic OR from 1.23 (1.00–1.51) closer to the prospective estimate (RR 1.07).
- **Strengths**: Combines rigorous simulation with a real applied example; identifies actionable distribution choice for practitioners.
- **Limitations**: Requires defensible assumptions about non-participant exposure prevalence, which may be unavailable in many settings.
- **Conclusion**: Trapezoidal-distribution PBA offers a principled correction for selection bias in retrospective case-control studies.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/aje/kwag295 / PMID:43049044

## 4. Computed Tomography Screening and Small Cell Lung Cancer Stage Shift and Survival — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: JAMA Oncology
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-19
- **Authors**: Potter AL et al.
- **Aims**: Investigate whether LDCT lung cancer screening shifts SCLC stage at diagnosis and improves survival in Medicare beneficiaries.
- **Method**: Retrospective cohort using Medicare-SEER linked data; 17,099 SCLC patients 2015–2021; propensity score-matched analysis.
- **Key findings**: Screened patients less likely to have distant disease (44% vs 70%); 3-year OS 28.0% vs 10.8% in unscreened; findings consistent in matched analysis.
- **Strengths**: Large national claims-registry linkage with propensity matching; first Medicare-based evidence for LDCT benefit in SCLC.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective design with healthy-screener bias; only 5.5% of patients were screened, limiting generalizability.
- **Conclusion**: LDCT screening was associated with stage shift and improved survival in SCLC, supporting consideration in guidelines.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1001/jamaoncol.2026.4667 / PMID:43056109

## 5. Integrating Genetic and Lung Tissue Proteomics Data to Identify Susceptibility Proteins for Lung Cancer — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: Journal of the National Cancer Institute
- **Pub date**: 2026-11-19
- **Authors**: Xu S et al.
- **Aims**: Identify lung cancer susceptibility proteins by integrating lung tissue proteomics with GWAS data and evaluate them as drug targets.
- **Method**: Proteomic GWAS of 200 lung tissue samples; models applied to 55,174 cases/1.3M controls; colocalization and summary-data-based Mendelian randomization; drug database cross-reference.
- **Key findings**: 29 risk-associated proteins identified (FDR<5%); 3 proteins (DSP, IL18, TYMP) show reproducible/causal evidence and are targeted by 6 FDA-approved or phase III drugs.
- **Strengths**: Large GWAS sample, cross-dataset validation, explicit drug repurposing angle.
- **Limitations**: Protein prediction models based on 200 tissue samples; limited cross-dataset reproducibility for many proteins.
- **Conclusion**: Lung tissue proteome-GWAS integration reveals actionable susceptibility proteins and drug repurposing candidates for lung cancer.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag424 / PMID:43055353
