## 1. Endocrine therapy in early-stage estrogen Receptor-Low breast cancer: a systematic review and Meta-Analysis — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: J Natl Cancer Inst
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-18
- **Authors**: Massa D et al.
- **Aims**: Assess efficacy of endocrine therapy in early-stage ER-low (1–9% expression) breast cancer.
- **Method**: Systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis of 17 studies (10,232 ER-low; 58,662 ER-rich patients); DFS and OS endpoints.
- **Key findings**: ET associated with 33% lower relapse/death risk (HR 0.67, 95% CI 0.54–0.85; I²=0%); ER-low tumours had 2× worse DFS vs ER-rich (HR 2.18).
- **Strengths**: Large multi-study sample with dual outcomes; consistent DFS effect with zero heterogeneity.
- **Limitations**: High heterogeneity in OS analysis (I²=95.8%); no randomised data; mostly observational studies.
- **Conclusion**: ET improves outcomes in ER-low disease, supporting inclusion of these patients in ET-based trials.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag281 / PMID:42613756

## 2. Estimating lead time and overdiagnosis in cancer screening programmes: the MOCCI method — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Int J Epidemiol
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-18
- **Authors**: Vratanar B et al.
- **Aims**: Develop a novel parametric method (MOCCI) to estimate lead time and overdiagnosis in population cancer screening.
- **Method**: Parametric modelling minimising incidence difference between invited vs. uninvited individuals; validated in Slovenian breast screening programme.
- **Key findings**: 30% of screen-detected breast cancers were non-progressive; 33% overdiagnosed; mean lead time 1.8 years (95% CI 1.3–2.9).
- **Strengths**: Accommodates multiple lead time distributions; separates progressive vs. non-progressive overdiagnosis sources.
- **Limitations**: Requires large samples for stable estimates; sensitive to assumed lead time model specification.
- **Conclusion**: MOCCI provides improved, transparent overdiagnosis estimation applicable to any cancer screening programme.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/ije/dyag167 / PMID:42615544

## 3. Elucidating the proteomic pathways linking childhood body mass index with type 2 diabetes: a Mendelian randomization and recall-by-genotype study — Score 6/6

- **Journal**: Int J Epidemiol
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-18
- **Authors**: Chen S et al.
- **Aims**: Identify proteomic mediators of the childhood BMI–T2D association using two-step Mendelian randomization.
- **Method**: Two-step MR (European GWAS; n>1M); 1,299 proteins screened; colocalization; recall-by-genotype cross-ethnic replication (Chinese cohort, n=250).
- **Key findings**: 36 proteins mediated BMI→T2D risk (6 colocalized, PPH4>80%); only SHBG replicated in East Asians; mediation ranged 1.87%–12.92%.
- **Strengths**: Exemplary two-step MR pipeline with colocalization; cross-ethnic validation rare in proteomics-MR literature.
- **Limitations**: European-centric discovery GWAS; recall-by-genotype replication cohort small (n=250).
- **Conclusion**: Highlights actionable protein mediators of adiposity-driven metabolic risk; limited generalisability beyond Europeans.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/ije/dyag165 / PMID:42613030

## 4. Leiomyosarcoma survival across anatomical sites identifies high- and low-risk patient groups to inform clinical trial design — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: J Natl Cancer Inst
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-18
- **Authors**: Oswald AJ et al.
- **Aims**: Characterise survival differences across leiomyosarcoma anatomical sites and identify independent prognostic factors.
- **Method**: Retrospective cohort of 11,751 patients; multivariable survival analysis; pooled sequencing analysis for ATRX mutations.
- **Key findings**: Black race, widowhood, lowest income independently associated with shorter survival; uterine/urinary sites were highest-risk; ATRX enriched in uterine cases (P-adj<0.0001).
- **Strengths**: Largest multi-site leiomyosarcoma cohort to date; molecular data integrated with sociodemographic factors.
- **Limitations**: Retrospective design; site-specific treatment variation may confound survival comparisons.
- **Conclusion**: Major outcome disparities across subtypes support site-stratified and equity-aware leiomyosarcoma trial design.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag284 / PMID:42613738

## 5. Development and validation of a predictive model of aromatase inhibitor-induced arthralgia among patients with early breast cancer — Score 5/6

- **Journal**: J Natl Cancer Inst
- **Pub date**: 2026-08-18
- **Authors**: Lapidari P et al.
- **Aims**: Develop and validate prediction models for aromatase inhibitor-induced arthralgia (AIA) at 1 and 4 years post-initiation.
- **Method**: Multivariable logistic regression with bootstrap validation; CANTO trial (development n=3,065; temporal validation n=1,313); biomarker sub-cohort (n=637).
- **Key findings**: BMI, prior joint disease, fatigue, and chemotherapy exposure predicted AIA; AUC 0.61–0.67; higher IL-8 inversely associated with year-1 AIA (OR 0.74).
- **Strengths**: Large prospective CANTO cohort with temporal validation and biomarker data; clinically actionable predictors.
- **Limitations**: Modest discrimination (AUC~0.65); IL-8 association needs independent replication.
- **Conclusion**: Baseline clinical features can stratify AIA risk and guide stepped-care interventions to optimise AI adherence.
- **ID**: DOI:10.1093/jnci/djag280 / PMID:42613747

## Appraisal notes

This week's digest is dominated by breast cancer methodology: a meta-analysis clarifying endocrine therapy benefit in the undercharacterised ER-low (1–9%) subgroup, and a CANTO-trial prediction model for treatment toxicity, together offering immediately actionable clinical guidance. The MOCCI method (Int J Epidemiol) is the standout methodological contribution, providing a parametric framework for separating progressive from non-progressive cancer overdiagnosis in screening programmes — directly relevant to ongoing debates around breast, prostate, and lung screening. The Chen et al. two-step MR + proteomics study exemplifies a growing epi-methodology trend of multi-instrument causal pathway analysis, though its limited cross-ethnic replication reinforces the urgent need for non-European GWAS resources.
